How the Wedding Planner Cost Calculator Works

Planner pricing has two anchor points in the industry: flat-fee packages set by market, and the percentage-of-budget convention for full service. This calculator starts from national tier ranges, then adjusts for your market and guest count, then shows the 10-15% rule alongside so you can see whether the two methods agree for your budget.

The formula

Fee = tier base range × market factor × guest factor. Tier bases are the national ranges in the table above, with typicals of $3,800 full-service, $2,600 partial, and $1,700 month-of at 100 guests in a mid-size metro. The market factor is 0.85 for small towns and rural areas, 1.0 mid-size, and 1.30 for major metros. The guest factor adds 10% per 50 guests above 100 — bigger weddings mean more vendor management, more hours, and more coordination staff.

How to use it

Enter your total budget and guest count, pick the closest market, and switch between tiers to see the spread. The 10-15% card shows what the percentage convention would charge on your budget; for full-service, a typical quote inside that band is a sign the fee is market-normal. For partial and month-of work, flat fees below the band are the norm, so don't read the comparison as a discount.

A worked example

Take a $40,000 wedding for 130 guests in a major metro, full service. The guest factor is 1 + (30 ÷ 50 × 0.10) = 1.06, and the market factor 1.30. Typical fee: $3,800 × 1.06 × 1.30 = $5,236, with a range of $4,134 (low) to $11,024 (high, luxury-tier planners). The 10-15% rule on $40,000 gives $4,000-$6,000 — the typical estimate sits inside it at 13.1% of budget. That agreement is what you want to see.

Now the other end: month-of coordination for a 90-guest wedding in a small town on a $25,000 budget. Guest factor 1.0 (under 100), market factor 0.85. Typical: $1,700 × 0.85 = $1,445, range $1,020-$2,975. That's 5.8% of budget — a flat fee doing its job, not a planner undercharging.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a wedding planner cost?

Couples who hire a full-service planner spend about $3,800 on average, per The Knot, with most falling between $3,000 and $10,000+. Partial planning typically runs $1,800-$6,000 and month-of coordination $1,200-$3,500. Luxury planners in major metros can charge $12,000 and up. The most common rule of thumb: full-service planning runs 10-15% of your total wedding budget.

What does a month-of wedding coordinator cost?

Most month-of coordinators charge a flat $1,200-$3,500, with The Knot citing a typical $1,500-$4,000 range in pricier markets. They take over in the final four to six weeks: confirming vendors, building the timeline, and running the rehearsal and wedding day. It's a flat fee, not a percentage, because the scope is capped.

Do wedding planners really charge 10-15% of the budget?

Full-service planners often do, either as a percentage or as a flat fee that lands in the same place. On a $40,000 wedding, that's $4,000-$6,000. Partial planning and month-of coordination are almost always flat fees, usually well under 10%. Note that The Knot's budget percentages show planners at about 4% of the average $34,200 wedding, because that average includes the majority of couples who never hire one.

What's the difference between partial and full-service planning?

A full-service planner runs the whole engagement: budget, venue search, vendor contracts, design, and day-of direction, meeting you monthly from booking to exit. A partial planner handles specific pieces you choose, often the venue and the tricky vendor categories, while you manage the rest. Partial typically costs $1,800-$6,000 versus $3,000-$10,000+ for full service.

Is a wedding planner worth the cost?

The honest math: venue and catering eat over half of the average $34,200 wedding, and planners negotiate those contracts for a living. Vendor discounts, avoided mistakes, and reclaimed weekends can offset much of the fee, but the real product is a single point of accountability. Many couples split the difference with partial planning early and a month-of coordinator to finish.

How can I spend less on wedding planning help?

Book a month-of coordinator instead of full service (saves roughly $2,000-$5,000), marry in the off-season when planners discount November-April dates, choose a partial package covering only venue and catering, or hire a newer planner building their portfolio. Destination weddings can also come out ahead: a local planner's vendor connections often beat what you could arrange from out of state.
