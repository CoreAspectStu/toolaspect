How Discount Pricing Works

This discount calculator lets you figure out sale prices, savings amounts, and stacked discounts. Enter the original price, choose your discount percentage, and instantly see the final price and how much you save.

How to Calculate a Discount

Savings = Price × (Discount% ÷ 100)

Sale Price = Price − Savings

Example: 30% off $150: savings = $150 × 0.30 = $45. Sale price = $150 − $45 = $105.

Stacking Multiple Discounts

When stores offer "extra 20% off already-reduced items," discounts apply sequentially, not additively. 20% off + 10% off ≠ 30% off.

Example: $100 with 20% off then 10% off: First → $80. Then → $80 × 0.90 = $72. Total effective discount = 28%.

Common Discount Types

  • Percentage discount: "25% off" — most common type
  • Fixed amount: "$10 off" — subtracts a flat amount
  • Buy one get one (BOGO): "Buy 1 get 1 50% off" — effectively 25% off per item
  • Tiered discount: "Spend $100 get 20% off" — threshold-based
  • Coupon stacking: Multiple coupons — each applies to the post-discount price

Psychology of Discounting

Stores use psychological pricing to make discounts feel bigger than they are:

  • "Was $200, now $99" sounds better than "Was $200, now $100"
  • "50% off" feels like a better deal than "Buy 2 for the price of 1"
  • Staged discounts (extra 10% off clearance) make you feel like you're getting a special deal
  • Always calculate the effective discount — "extra 50% off already 50% off" is 75% total, not 100%
