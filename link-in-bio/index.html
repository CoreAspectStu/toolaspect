What is a link-in-bio page?

A link-in-bio page is a single mobile-first page that gathers every important link a creator or business needs to share — social profiles, shops, booking pages, tip jars, latest content — behind one short URL placed in a social profile. It exists because most social platforms allow only one clickable link, so that one link has to carry everything. Building one well means thinking about hierarchy: the two or three actions you most want visitors to take go at the top, everything else stacks beneath, and the page should load instantly on a phone because nearly every visitor arrives from a mobile app.

What makes a link-in-bio page convert

Creators who get clicks tend to follow a few consistent patterns. The page opens with a clear name and face so visitors confirm they are in the right place instantly. The primary call to action — usually the newest video, product drop, or booking link — sits first, styled more prominently than the rest. Links use verbs that describe the outcome ("Watch the new video", "Book a session", "Shop the drop") rather than bare platform names. The page avoids dead ends: every button goes somewhere maintained. And the whole page fits one or two phone screens, because every extra scroll loses a portion of the audience.

Choosing a handle and domain

Consistency beats cleverness. The best handle is the same one you use everywhere else, because a link-in-bio page is an extension of an existing audience relationship, not a new brand. If your name is taken on some platforms, pick the closest stable variant and use it everywhere rather than optimizing per platform. Creators who later buy a custom domain usually point it at this same page, so choose a handle you would not be embarrassed to see on a business card in two years.

A note on doors this page opens later. Because the snippet is plain HTML, it ports anywhere: your custom domain, a Carrd page, a Notion site, or a developer-built page. Nothing about starting free locks you in, and nothing about upgrading later requires starting over — the content model (a name, a handle, an ordered list of labeled links) is the same at every scale of internet presence.

Design details that matter

Three small decisions carry most of the page's performance. First, button style: filled buttons with strong contrast outperform plain text links on mobile because they present a bigger tap target. Second, spacing: generous padding between buttons prevents mis-taps, which mobile users punish with instant back-button exits. Third, color restraint: one accent color from your existing content thumbnails ties the page to your channel visually. None of these require design skill — the preview above shows each change instantly, so iterate until it looks like yours.

Tracking which links get clicks

Once live, you will want to know which buttons earn their position. The simplest method is appending UTM parameters to each destination URL so your analytics show per-button clicks; more advanced users route through a short-link service with per-link counters. Reorder ruthlessly on evidence: if the bottom button out-clicks the top, swap them. Most creators find that two links collect the large majority of all clicks, which argues for keeping the page short and promoting the winners to the top slots.

Common mistakes to avoid

The recurring failures are all forms of clutter: embedding twenty buttons so nothing stands out, linking to profiles the creator abandoned, using vague labels like "click here", and forgetting to update the page after a launch ends. A stale link-in-bio quietly costs credibility with exactly the audience that clicked deepest. A monthly five-minute review — does every link still work, does the top button still point at the thing you most want people to do — keeps the page earning.
