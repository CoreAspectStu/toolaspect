Your Baby at Week 3

Week 3 is conception week. Sperm meets egg in the fallopian tube, and the fused cell begins dividing as it drifts toward the uterus. Your baby is a microscopic ball of cells this week, already carrying a complete genetic blueprint.

The fertilized egg becomes a zygote, then a morula, then a blastocyst as it travels. Around the end of the week it reaches the uterus and begins burrowing into the plush lining, a process called implantation that finishes early in week 4.

Gestationally, week 3 means you are 2 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant, day 15 to day 21 counted from the first day of your last period. This is week 3 of the first trimester (weeks 1–13). Prenatal appointments run about every four weeks in this stretch. Week 4 brings a positive home pregnancy test as hCG rises. If your cycle runs longer or shorter than the 28 days the due-date math assumes, your ovulation date and due date shift with it, which is why a first-trimester ultrasound remains the gold standard for dating. Every number on this page is an average across pregnancies; your own scan dates and your baby's measurements are what actually describe your week 3.

Your Body at Week 3

  • Light implantation spotting possible
  • Mild cramping
  • No obvious symptoms for most

What to Do This Week

Avoid alcohol, smoking, and high-mercury fish from now on; the neural tube and earliest organs form in the next two weeks, when the embryo is most sensitive to harm.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many months is 3 weeks pregnant?

Weeks of pregnancy don't map cleanly to calendar months, but the common division puts week 3 falls in month 1 of pregnancy (weeks 1–4). Week 3 is part of the first trimester, which runs from week 1 to week 13. Gestationally, week 3 means you are 2 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant.

What size is the baby at week 3?

At week 3 there isn't much to measure yet; the baby is on the scale of a poppy seed. Measurable length in inches begins with the embryo stage in the next few weeks.
