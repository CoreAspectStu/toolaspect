Your Baby at Week 1

Week 1 starts on the first day of your last period, which means you're not pregnant yet in any biological sense. Doctors count from here because the LMP is the one date most people can pinpoint. Conception is still about two weeks away.

Your body is shedding last cycle's uterine lining and starting fresh. Follicles in your ovaries are beginning to mature, one of which will release next cycle's egg. The pregnancy count that started this week will run 280 days to your due date.

Gestationally, week 1 means you are 0 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant, day 1 to day 7 counted from the first day of your last period. This is week 1 of the first trimester (weeks 1–13). Prenatal appointments run about every four weeks in this stretch. Week 4 brings a positive home pregnancy test as hCG rises. If your cycle runs longer or shorter than the 28 days the due-date math assumes, your ovulation date and due date shift with it, which is why a first-trimester ultrasound remains the gold standard for dating. Every number on this page is an average across pregnancies; your own scan dates and your baby's measurements are what actually describe your week 1.

Your Body at Week 1

  • A normal period
  • Mild cramping
  • No pregnancy symptoms yet

What to Do This Week

Start a prenatal vitamin with 400–600 mcg folic acid now if you're trying; the neural tube closes in the first month, often before a positive test.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many months is 1 weeks pregnant?

Weeks of pregnancy don't map cleanly to calendar months, but the common division puts week 1 falls in month 1 of pregnancy (weeks 1–4). Week 1 is part of the first trimester, which runs from week 1 to week 13. Gestationally, week 1 means you are 0 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant.

What size is the baby at week 1?

At week 1 there isn't much to measure yet; the baby is on the scale of not yet conceived. Measurable length in inches begins with the embryo stage in the next few weeks.
