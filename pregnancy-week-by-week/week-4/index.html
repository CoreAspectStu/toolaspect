Your Baby at Week 4

Week 4 is when the pregnancy becomes detectable. The blastocyst finishes implanting in the uterine lining, and the cells that will become the placenta start pumping out hCG, the hormone a home test measures. A sensitive test can turn positive now.

The embryo settles into two layers: the epiblast, which becomes your baby, and the hypoblast, which feeds it early on. The amniotic sac and yolk sac are forming. Size is still measured in millimeters, about a tenth of a centimeter.

Gestationally, week 4 means you are 3 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant, day 22 to day 28 counted from the first day of your last period. This is week 4 of the first trimester (weeks 1–13). Prenatal appointments run about every four weeks in this stretch. Week 6 brings the first heartbeat, usually visible on ultrasound. If your cycle runs longer or shorter than the 28 days the due-date math assumes, your ovulation date and due date shift with it, which is why a first-trimester ultrasound remains the gold standard for dating. Every number on this page is an average across pregnancies; your own scan dates and your baby's measurements are what actually describe your week 4.

Your Body at Week 4

  • Missed period
  • Light spotting (implantation)
  • Tender breasts
  • Fatigue

What to Do This Week

Take a home pregnancy test if your period is late; first morning urine holds the most hCG. Call to schedule your first prenatal visit, usually set for around week 8.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many months is 4 weeks pregnant?

Weeks of pregnancy don't map cleanly to calendar months, but the common division puts week 4 falls in month 1 of pregnancy (weeks 1–4). Week 4 is part of the first trimester, which runs from week 1 to week 13. Gestationally, week 4 means you are 3 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant.

What size is the baby at week 4?

At week 4 there isn't much to measure yet; the baby is on the scale of a poppy seed. Measurable length in inches begins with the embryo stage in the next few weeks.
