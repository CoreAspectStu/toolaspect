Your Baby at Week 30

Week 30 hits the ten-milliliter-of-amniotic-fluid-per-pound mark, and the baby is approaching three pounds. Red blood cell production shifts to the bone marrow, a sign of mature systems coming online.

The eyes can track light, and the baby can turn toward a bright source pressed near the belly. Lanugo continues disappearing, and toenails have reached the tips of the toes. Average daily gain now approaches half an ounce.

Gestationally, week 30 means you are 29 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant, day 204 to day 210 counted from the first day of your last period. This is week 3 of the third trimester (weeks 28–40). Appointments step up to every two weeks from week 28. Week 32 brings the Tdap vaccine window (27–36 weeks) winding down. From here the baby gains roughly half a pound a week on average, which is why the third trimester feels measurably heavier by the day. Every number on this page is an average across pregnancies; your own scan dates and your baby's measurements are what actually describe your week 30.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many months is 30 weeks pregnant?

Weeks of pregnancy don't map cleanly to calendar months, but the common division puts week 30 falls in month 3 of pregnancy (weeks 9–13). Week 30 is part of the third trimester, which runs from week 28 to birth. Gestationally, week 30 means you are 29 weeks and 0 to 6 days pregnant.

What size is the baby at week 30?

At week 30 the baby averages 15.7 in long and weighs about 2.91 lb, roughly the size of a cabbage. Sizes come from fetal growth charts (crown-to-heel length from week 20), and normal variation week to week is wide; your ultrasound measurements always outrank a chart.
