What is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. It breaks work into focused intervals (traditionally 25 minutes) separated by short breaks. These intervals are called "pomodoros," named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer Cirillo used as a student.

How to Use This Timer

  • Enter your task in the "What are you working on?" field
  • Press Start to begin a 25-minute focus session
  • When the timer rings, take a 5-minute short break
  • After 4 focus sessions, take a longer 15-minute break
  • Customize the durations in the settings above to match your workflow

Benefits of the Pomodoro Technique

  • Reduces mental fatigue — Regular breaks prevent burnout and keep your mind fresh
  • Improves focus — Short, defined work periods are easier to commit to than open-ended work
  • Fights procrastination — Starting a 25-minute timer feels manageable, reducing the urge to delay
  • Tracks productivity — Completed pomodoros give you a tangible measure of focused work
  • Prevents distractions — Knowing a break is coming makes it easier to defer interruptions
  • Builds momentum — The cycle of work and rest creates a sustainable rhythm for deep work

Tips for Getting the Most Out of Pomodoros

During focus sessions, close unnecessary tabs, silence your phone, and commit fully to one task. During breaks, step away from the screen — stretch, hydrate, or rest your eyes. Adjust the timer durations to find your optimal focus length; some people work best with 50-minute sessions and 10-minute breaks.
