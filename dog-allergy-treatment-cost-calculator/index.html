Cytopoint vs Apoquel vs immunotherapy, priced over 1-5 years

Answer: A Cytopoint injection costs $50-$200 by weight band, repeated every 4-8 weeks ($60-$90 for dogs under 20 lb, $90-$140 for 20-40 lb, $140-$200 for large dogs). Apoquel runs $50-$150 a month (~$2.50/tablet). Allergy testing is $200-$600 one-time and immunotherapy serum $50-$100 a month. Over 3 years, a 55 lb dog runs about $4,000 on Cytopoint (every 8 weeks), $4,265 on Apoquel, and $3,570 on immunotherapy — the expensive test makes immunotherapy the cheapest path from year two on.

How the Calculator Works

Chronic allergy treatment is a subscription, not a purchase. The bill that matters is the one three or five years out, because the cheapest first year is rarely the cheapest third year. This calculator runs all three mainstream options side by side to whichever horizon you pick.

The formulas

Cytopoint: injections per year = 52 ÷ weeks between shots (every 8 weeks = 6.5 a year). Yearly cost = injections × (shot price + exam fee), same every year. Apoquel: 12 months of tablets + a first-month double-dose adder in year one + two recheck exams a year. Immunotherapy: one-time test and consult in year one + 12 months of serum + two recheck exams a year; years after run on serum and rechecks alone.

How to use it

Pick your dog's weight band — the Cytopoint and Apoquel prices adjust to typical mid-band figures, which you can then override with real quotes. Choose the Cytopoint schedule your vet actually recommends; some dogs hold 8 weeks, others need 4. Set the horizon: seasonal itchers do well on a 1-year view, year-round cases on 3 or 5.

A worked example

A 55 lb dog with year-round environmental allergies, Cytopoint every 8 weeks at $160 a shot, $45 exam fees, Apoquel at $110 a month with a $35 first-month adder, and immunotherapy at $600 for test plus consult and $75 a month in serum.

Cytopoint: 6.5 visits a year × $205 (shot + exam) = $1,332.50 a year, $3,997.50 over three. Apoquel: year one $1,445 ($1,320 in tablets + $35 adder + $90 in rechecks), $1,410 each year after, $4,265 over three. Immunotherapy: $1,590 the first year, $990 each year after, $3,570 over three — cheapest at the three-year mark despite the most expensive start.

Two things flip that verdict. Seasonal dogs who need relief four months a year cut Apoquel and Cytopoint costs by two-thirds, while the immunotherapy test stays a sunk cost. And insurance changes everything: a policy that started before the itching did can reimburse most of any of these numbers — run your numbers through the pet insurance calculator linked below before paying list price for year three.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Cytopoint injection cost?

Nationally, $50 to $200 per injection, repeated every 4 to 8 weeks. Small dogs (under 20 lb) usually land at $60 to $90 per shot, medium dogs (20-40 lb) at $90 to $140, and large dogs can reach $180 or more because the price scales with the dog's weight band. A large dog on an 8-week schedule runs about $1,300 a year in injections and exam fees.

How much does Apoquel cost per month?

Apoquel typically runs $50 to $150 a month depending on dog size, around $2.50 per tablet. The first month costs more — about $110 for a typical dog — because dosing starts twice daily for 14 days before dropping to once daily. Year-round treatment on a mid-size dog runs roughly $1,000 to $1,200 a year.

Is allergy immunotherapy cheaper than Cytopoint or Apoquel?

Usually after the first year. Testing runs $200 to $600 (blood test $200-$400, intradermal skin test $300-$600, often with a $200-$400 dermatology consult), and serum runs roughly $50 to $100 a month. Year one often costs $1,500 or more, but years two onward drop to around $1,000 or less — and unlike daily drugs, immunotherapy is the only option aimed at actually retraining the immune response. Many dogs taper off it entirely after 1-3 years.

Does pet insurance cover allergy treatment for dogs?

Usually yes for accident-and-illness plans, because atopic dermatitis is a diagnosed illness — but only if the policy started before symptoms appeared. Allergies documented before enrollment count as a pre-existing condition and stay excluded. Coverage typically applies to vet visits, Cytopoint, Apoquel, and testing toward your deductible and reimbursement rate; wellness add-ons usually don't cover chronic treatment.

Why does Cytopoint cost more for bigger dogs?

Vets price Cytopoint by weight band — the product comes in fixed-size vials, so a larger dog needs a larger (or doubled) vial per visit. That pricing is per injection, not per milligram you calculate at home: the clinic quote is the number to plan around, which is why this calculator uses published per-visit prices by weight band.

What's the cheapest way to treat a dog's allergies?

If the itching is seasonal and mild, a shorter course of treatment each year costs far less than year-round dosing — the calculator's 1-year horizon shows that gap. If it's year-round, immunotherapy usually wins on total cost after year one, and some pharmacies discount Apoquel with quantity fills or manufacturer offers. Price-shop the injection itself: prices for the same shot vary $50 or more between clinics in the same metro.
