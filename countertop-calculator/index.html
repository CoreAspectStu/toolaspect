How the Countertop Calculator Works

Enter each counter run's length and depth (a standard kitchen counter with a 1.5 in overhang is 25.5 in deep), the number of identical sections, and a backsplash height if you want it counted. The calculator returns total square feet, square feet with waste, how many granite or quartz slabs you need (slabs run roughly 45–55 sq ft usable), and the linear feet of finished edge — the number that drives edge-profile pricing, since most fabricators quote edge treatment by the linear foot.

Formulas Used

  • Counter area: sq ft = length × (depth ÷ 12) × sections
  • Backsplash: sq ft = length × (backsplash height ÷ 12) × sections
  • With waste: total × (1 + waste%) — use 10–15% for granite, 15–20% if matching veining
  • Slabs needed: slabs = ceil(sq ft with waste ÷ slab size), slab ≈ 50 sq ft
  • Edge linear feet: length × sections (front edge) + depth/12 × sections (exposed ends — adjust for your layout)

Worked Example

An L-shaped kitchen with a 10 ft run and an 8 ft run, both 25.5 in deep with 4 in backsplash: counter area = (10 + 8) × 25.5/12 = 38.25 sq ft, backsplash = 18 × 4/12 = 6 sq ft, total 44.25 sq ft. With 15% waste that is 50.9 sq ft — two 50 sq ft granite slabs (vein matching almost always costs you part of the second slab). Finished edge runs about 18 linear feet plus exposed ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

How big is a granite slab? Typical slabs measure about 110 × 60 in, giving 45–55 usable sq ft after the cutouts and rough edges. Quartz slabs are similar at roughly 120 × 55 in.

How much waste should I add? 10–15% for straightforward runs, 20% or more when bookmatching veining, working around large islands, or when the slab has natural flaws.

Do sinks and cooktops reduce the square footage? Most fabricators charge for the full slab area including cutouts — the labor to cut them exceeds the material saved. Undermount sink holes do not come off the bill.

What does edge profile cost? Eased and bullnose edges are usually included; ogee, waterfall, and mitered edges add $10–30 per linear foot.
