How the Typing Test Works

This is a standard forward-only typing test. The timer starts on your first keystroke, every character you type is compared against the passage, and the run ends when the clock runs out or you finish the text. There's no backspacing, which mirrors how WPM exams and most employer tests score: errors count against you and you keep moving.

The formula

Gross WPM = all characters typed ÷ 5 ÷ minutes. Accuracy = correct characters ÷ characters typed. Net WPM = correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes. The divide-by-five convention treats an average English word as five characters, spaces included, so results are comparable across passages and keyboards.

How to use it

Pick a duration, 60 seconds is the standard, click the passage area, and type what you see. Keep your eyes on the text, not your hands. When the run finishes you'll get net WPM, gross WPM, accuracy, and error count. Take it two or three times; your first run is usually a few WPM under your steady pace.

A worked example

Say you type 348 characters in 60 seconds and 340 of them are correct. Gross WPM is 348 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 69.6, so about 70. Accuracy is 340 ÷ 348 = 97.7%. Net WPM is 340 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 68, which is the number to quote on a resume or in a hiring test. The 8 wrong characters cost you 1.6 WPM.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average typing speed?

The average adult types around 40 words per minute, with most large typing-test samples landing between 38 and 42 WPM at 90 to 95 percent accuracy. Reading is far faster, around 238 WPM in reading research, and speaking lands near 130 to 150 WPM. Anything at 60 WPM or above is faster than most office jobs require.

How is WPM calculated?

Industry standard treats every 5 characters, spaces included, as one word. Gross WPM equals all characters typed divided by 5 divided by minutes elapsed. Net WPM uses only the correct characters, and accuracy is correct characters divided by total characters typed. A 60-second run with 348 characters typed and 340 correct scores 69.6 gross WPM, 68 net WPM, and 97.7 percent accuracy.

Is 70 WPM a good typing speed?

Yes. At 70 WPM you type nearly twice the adult average and clear the 50 to 60 WPM range that most clerical and data-entry job postings ask for. Professional typists run 65 to 80 WPM, and competitive typists exceed 100 WPM, so 70 puts you solidly in proficient territory.

What typing accuracy should I aim for?

Anything above 97 percent is strong for everyday work, and transcription roles usually want 98 percent or better. Accuracy matters more than raw speed for most jobs: 65 WPM at 98 percent accuracy beats 80 WPM at 88 percent, because fixing errors costs more time than typing carefully in the first place.

What is the fastest typing speed ever recorded?

Stella Pajunas typed 216 words per minute on an IBM electric typewriter in 1946, still the commonly cited record for sustained typing. Barbara Blackburn averaged around 150 WPM and peaked near 212 WPM on a Dvorak layout in the 1980s. Court reporters exceed 225 WPM, but on stenotype machines that chord whole words, not regular keyboards.

How do I type faster?

Learn proper touch typing so each finger owns its keys and your eyes stay on the screen, then practice short daily sessions at a speed where accuracy stays above 97 percent. Hunt-and-peck averages around 27 WPM and plateaus there. Speed follows accuracy: most typists who drill at 95-plus percent accuracy gain 10 to 20 WPM within a couple of months.
