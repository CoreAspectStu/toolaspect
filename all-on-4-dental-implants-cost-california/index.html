What Moves a California Quote

On top of the base arch price: 3D CBCT imaging ($200-$600), IV sedation ($500-$1,000), and grafting where bone is short ($300-$800 minor; $1,500-$3,000 for sinus or major). With scan and sedation added, a California acrylic arch runs about $19,000-$38,200 all-in. Confirm whether extractions and the final prosthesis are bundled — coastal chain centers usually bundle; lowball ads often don't.

Geography is the bigger lever. Bay Area and LA quotes sit at the top of the range (or above it for premium prosthodontists); San Diego tracks slightly below LA; the Central Valley, Inland Empire, and Sacramento quote meaningfully lower — commonly $3,000-$6,000 less per arch for identical hardware. UCSF and UCLA dental schools treat patients at academic rates typically 30-50% under private practice, with longer appointment lead times as the trade.

Insurance and Financing Reality

A typical dental PPO covers major restorative work at 50% with a $1,000-$2,000 annual maximum — call it $1,500 of a $18,300-$36,600 arch, leaving roughly $16,800-$35,100. California Medicare Advantage dental riders cap in the same $1,000-$3,000 territory. The standard plays: phase the two arches across plan years to capture two annual maximums, pay from an HSA (saving 20-35% effectively at common brackets), and compare in-office financing APRs against a personal loan before signing a treatment contract.

Acrylic vs. Zirconia: What the Upgrade Buys

The gap between the two prosthesis materials — roughly $5,000-$6,000 per arch in California — is the biggest single decision inside a quote. Acrylic hybrids, denture teeth set in a gum-colored acrylic bar, are the standard offering at the $18,300-$36,600 end. Monolithic zirconia, milled from a single ceramic block at $24,400-$42,700, is the premium tier coastal practices push hardest. The honest tradeoffs:

What the First Year Actually Looks Like

Quotes describe a product; the first year is a schedule. Surgery day places the implants and a fixed immediate prosthesis — teeth you leave the office with. The next 3-6 months are osseointegration: implants fusing to bone, a soft-food diet, and soft-bristle hygiene around the prosthesis. Around month 3-6 come the impressions and try-in for the final prosthesis, with delivery shortly after. Confirm in writing that the quote includes both the immediate and the final prosthesis — "teeth in a day" pricing that excludes the final set is the oldest lowball in the category, and California's advertising rules don't stop it. After delivery, plan on professional cleanings three to four times a year; implant prostheses collect plaque like natural teeth, and the gum interface is where problems start.

The California-specific levers during that year: UCSF and UCLA aren't the only academic options — the University of the Pacific in San Francisco and Loma Linda both run implant clinics at academic rates typically 30-50% under private practice, and some Central Valley practices price at academic-adjacent levels already. If you're splitting treatment across two plan years for the insurance double-dip, schedule the second arch's surgery in the new benefit year, not just the payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do All-on-4 dental implants cost in California?

About $18,300 to $36,600 per arch, applying California's roughly 22%-above-average dental price level to the national $15,000-$30,000 range. Zirconia prosthesis arches run $24,400 to $42,700, and full-mouth treatment on both arches typically lands between $37,000 and $73,000 in California.

Why is All-on-4 more expensive in California than other states?

Lab and practice overhead: California's commercial rents, staff wages, and dental lab fees run well above national averages, and prosthodontist-heavy markets like the Bay Area, Los Angeles, and coastal San Diego price at the top. Inland markets — the Central Valley, Inland Empire, Sacramento — quote noticeably below the coastal metros for identical treatment.

Does insurance help with All-on-4 in California?

A typical dental PPO covers major restorative work at 50%, capped by a $1,000-$2,000 annual maximum — so about $1,500 of a $20,000+ arch. California Medicare Advantage plans with dental riders cap similarly at $1,000-$3,000. Many Californians combine an annual-max payout with HSA funds (20-35% effective savings) or in-office financing; phasing arches across two plan years doubles the insurance capture.

How can I pay less for All-on-4 in California?

Three proven levers: get quotes inland as well as coastal (the same drive that saves $3,000-$6,000 per arch is worth it for many), consult the UCSF or UCLA dental schools (typically 30-50% below private practice), and compare the acrylic vs zirconia tier honestly — acrylic at $18,300-$36,600 is the value play; zirconia is an upgrade, not a requirement.

What is included in a California All-on-4 quote?

A fair quote bundles the four implant posts, the immediate prosthesis, the final prosthesis, extractions, imaging, and follow-up visits. The lines that get excluded in lowball advertising: the final (permanent) teeth, IV sedation ($500-$1,000), 3D CBCT imaging ($200-$600), and bone grafting. Always ask whether the number covers the final teeth, not just the surgical day.

What is the monthly payment for All-on-4 in California?

On an $18,300 California acrylic arch: about $762 a month on a 24-month 0% promotional plan, $1,525 on 12 months, or roughly $434 a month on a 60-month plan at 14.9% APR (total about $26,064 — financing adds $7,764). Compare the total paid, not just the monthly figure, and watch for deferred-interest clauses on promotional plans.
