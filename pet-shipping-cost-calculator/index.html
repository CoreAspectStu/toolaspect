How the Pet Shipping Cost Calculator Works

Pet transport pricing looks like auto transport pricing with different constraints: a falling per-mile curve for ground moves, size multipliers instead of vehicle classes, and paperwork that cars don't need. This calculator models all three so a quote from a transporter lands inside a range you already understand.

The formula

For shared ground, cost = distance × the per-mile tier rate × a size factor (0.90 for small pets up to 1.30 for extra-large dogs, because bigger crates eat a proportional share of the van). Private service multiplies the shared result by 2.0 to 2.3. In-cabin flight is a flat $95 to $150 each way. Air cargo is size-banded: roughly $500 to $900 for a small pet, $700 to $1,300 medium, $900 to $2,000 large, and $1,200 to $2,500 for extra-large, higher once a full-service shipper arranges the whole move, with route driving the bigger half of the range. Extras stack on top: an interstate health certificate runs $100 to $300 and an airline-grade crate $50 to $220.

How to use it

Pick the mode you're considering, set distance and pet size, and toggle the paperwork you'll actually need (ground interstate moves need the health certificate; air needs it plus the crate). The result is a low-to-high band, not a quote: use it to judge whether a transporter's number is fair, cheap for a reason, or padded.

A worked example

A 65-pound dog moving 1,250 miles by shared ground. The 1,250-mile tier prices at $0.60 to $1.00 per mile, and the large-dog size factor is 1.15. Low end: 1,250 × $0.60 × 1.15 = $862.50. High end: 1,250 × $1.00 × 1.15 = $1,437.50. Add the health certificate ($100 to $300) and a travel crate ($50 to $220) and the all-in estimate is $1,013 to $1,958. Upgrading to private transport puts the same move at roughly $1,725 to $3,306 before extras. If a $2,500 shared quote arrives for this run, it's 74% above the top of the band, and it's time for more quotes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to ship a dog across the country?

Shared ground transport, the most common choice, runs roughly $0.50 to $1.60 per mile, so a coast-to-coast move for a medium or large dog lands between $700 and $2,500. A 1,250-mile move for a large dog prices out around $863 to $1,438 shared. Private door-to-door ground service runs about double, and air cargo for a large dog adds $900 to $2,000 plus crate and health certificate.

Is ground or air transport better for pets?

Ground suits most dogs: no airline crate requirements, no breed restrictions, and temperature limits matter less because drivers manage rest stops. Air makes sense for long timelines or Hawaii and international moves. Flat-faced breeds like French Bulldogs, English Bulldogs, and Persians are banned from cargo on most airlines because heat and stress are dangerous for them, which pushes those pets to ground transport regardless of distance.

What papers do I need to ship a pet?

Interstate ground transport generally needs a certificate of veterinary inspection, a health certificate issued within 10 to 30 days of travel depending on the state, plus current rabies vaccination records. Air travel is stricter: an IATA-compliant crate with correct sizing and hardware, a health certificate within 10 days, and sometimes an acclimation certificate. Airlines and states each add requirements, so confirm with the transporter before booking.

Why do pet transport quotes vary so much?

Like auto transport, pet shipping is a broker-heavy, lead-generation vertical. Many sites sell your contact info to several transporters, and the cheap quote often excludes crate, paperwork, or door delivery, or it assumes your pet shares a route that is not yet built. Collect three to five itemized quotes, confirm USDA registration for ground transporters, check IPATA membership for air, and never pay a large deposit before the route and driver are confirmed.

Can I fly with my pet in the cabin instead?

If your pet is small enough, yes, and it is by far the cheapest option: US airlines charge roughly $95 to $150 each way for an in-cabin pet, typically limited to pets under about 20 pounds that fit in a carrier under the seat. Slots per flight are limited, breeds are restricted, and summer heat embargoes can block cargo but rarely cabin travel.

How far in advance should I book pet transport?

Two to four weeks for shared ground transport, longer for summer and snowbird routes, and four to eight weeks for air cargo with a professional shipper. Last-minute bookings exist but cost more and compress the health-certificate window, which is only valid for a limited time before travel.
