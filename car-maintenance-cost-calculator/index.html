How the Car Maintenance Cost Calculator Works

Every manufacturer spaces deep services at 30,000-mile intervals: 30K, 60K, 90K, 120K, and so on. What your car actually costs depends on which of those milestones your odometer crosses, and when. This calculator lines your mileage up against the schedule and prices both views: the cash you'll spend in the next 12 months, and the smoothed 5-year average.

The formula

First it finds every 30,000-mile milestone between your current mileage and your mileage a year from now; those are the major services due. Each is priced from the table above, scaled by your pricing tier. Routine care is annualized separately: oil changes at your interval, rotations, filters, battery, wipers and alignment spread over their lifespans, about $380 a year at typical pricing before tier scaling. Total = routine + majors due. The 5-year average applies the same math across the next five years of milestones.

How to use it

Enter your odometer reading and roughly how far you drive in a year. Pick your pricing tier: economy for a trusted independent, typical for chain-shop pricing, dealer for franchise rates. The results show which major services are due next, what the year totals, and your per-mile figure to compare against the $0.03-$0.07 mainstream band.

A worked example

A 2019 CR-V at 42,500 miles driving 12,000 a year. Nothing crosses a milestone in the next 12 months (54,500 is still short of 60,000), so the year is routine care only: about $381, or $0.032 a mile. But the 5-year window catches both the 60,000 and 90,000-mile services, so the averaged cost is $531-$781 a year. Budget the average, not the light year, or year four will ambush you.

Now the same car at 57,000 miles, dealer serviced. The 60,000-mile service falls inside the next 12 months: $390-$1,040 at dealer pricing on top of routine care, so the year totals $885-$1,535, briefly $0.074 a mile. That spike is normal, and it's exactly why dealers quote maintenance plans against averaged numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does car maintenance cost per year?

A mainstream car driven 12,000 miles a year typically runs $380 to $800 a year for scheduled maintenance once the 30/60/90K services are averaged in. Light years (no major service due) run closer to $380; the year a 60,000 or 90,000-mile service lands, the bill jumps $300 to $1,200. Luxury brands run two to three times these figures.

What does a 60,000-mile service include?

The usual list: oil and filter, tire rotation, engine and cabin air filters, brake fluid flush, coolant inspection or flush, and transmission fluid if the schedule calls for it, plus a multi-point inspection. At a typical shop that runs $300-$800; dealers sit 20-40% above that. Spark plugs usually wait for 90,000 miles or 100,000 on iridium.

Do I have to go to the dealer to keep my warranty?

No. Under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, any qualified shop can perform scheduled maintenance as long as the work matches the manufacturer's intervals and you keep receipts. Skipping documented maintenance entirely is what gives a manufacturer grounds to deny a warranty claim on a related part.

Which maintenance items are upsells?

Fuel system cleanings, engine flushes, 'winterization' packages, and most fluid services scheduled more often than your manual specifies. The honest list is short: oil and filter, tire rotation, engine and cabin air filters, brake fluid, coolant, transmission fluid, spark plugs, and wear items (brakes, tires, wipers, battery) when measured low.

How much should I budget for maintenance per mile?

About $0.03 to $0.07 per mile for scheduled maintenance on a mainstream car up to roughly 100,000 miles. Past that, add a repair reserve of $0.05 to $0.10 per mile and your all-in ownership cost for maintenance and repairs settles near $0.10 to $0.20 per mile.
