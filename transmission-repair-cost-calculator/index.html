Rebuild vs replace vs used, priced against your car's value

Transmission work runs $150-$1,200 for minor repairs (fluid, solenoid, seal), $1,200-$2,800 for a used unit installed, $1,500-$3,500 for a rebuilt automatic, $3,000-$5,000 remanufactured, and $4,000-$8,000+ for new OEM. CVTs usually aren't rebuilt at all, so figure $3,500-$5,500 for a reman CVT. SUVs and trucks run about 10-15% more than sedans; luxury and European vehicles about 50-60% more. The decision rule most mechanics use: if the repair stays under half the car's repaired value, fix it. A $2,640 rebuild on a 2016 SUV worth $9,000 fixed is 29% — an easy yes. The same job on a $3,500 car says sell it to a junkyard while it still moves.

How the Transmission Repair Cost Calculator Works

Transmission quotes scare people because the range is huge: the same symptom can be a $200 valve body solenoid or a $4,000 replacement. This calculator narrows the range with national parts-and-labor bands for each repair path, scales them for your vehicle class, and then runs the number that actually decides the repair: cost as a share of the car's repaired value.

The formula

Estimate = base national range for the repair path x vehicle-class multiplier (sedan 1.0, SUV/van 1.1, truck 1.15, luxury 1.6). The share-of-value check compares the midpoint against the car's value after the repair; under 30% is usually an easy repair decision, 30-50% depends on the rest of the car, and over 50% is where replacing the vehicle starts winning.

How to use it

Pick your vehicle class and transmission type, then the repair path your shop is proposing. Enter the car's value once repaired (KBB or Edmunds private-party is fine). If the verdict says the repair is questionable, toggle between used, rebuilt, and remanufactured to see how each path moves the percentage.

A worked example

Example 1: a 2016 Ford Explorer, 3.5L automatic, starts slipping and the shop finds burnt fluid. Rebuild estimate on an SUV: base $1,500-$3,500, times 1.1 = $1,650-$3,850, midpoint $2,640. The Explorer is worth about $9,000 fixed, so the rebuild is 29% of value: fix it, and the car has years left.

Example 2: a 2012 Nissan Sentra CVT with 160,000 miles does the same thing. CVT reman replacement on a sedan: $3,500-$5,500, midpoint $4,300. The Sentra is worth maybe $4,500 fixed, so the repair is 96% of value. That's a different car-shopping conversation, and possibly a call to our junk car value calculator instead.

Example 3: a 2018 German luxury sedan needs a reman automatic: $3,000-$5,000 base x 1.6 = $4,800-$8,000, midpoint $6,240. On a car worth $18,000 after repair, that's 35%: doable, but this is where you get a second quote, because dealer and independent transmission shops differ by thousands on exactly the same job.

All figures are labeled national ranges, not quotes. Labor rates run $100-$180 an hour depending on region, parts availability swings the used-unit market weekly, and a good independent transmission shop will show you the teardown before the big number is final.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to fix a transmission?

National installed ranges: minor repairs (fluid, solenoids, seals) run $150-$1,200; a used transmission installed runs $1,200-$2,800; a rebuilt automatic runs $1,500-$3,500; a remanufactured unit runs $3,000-$5,000; and a new OEM transmission from a dealer runs $4,000-$8,000 or more. CVTs are the exception: they usually aren't rebuilt, so replacement runs $3,500-$5,500. Luxury and heavy-duty vehicles add 50% or more.

Is it worth rebuilding a transmission?

The common rule of thumb: repair when the cost stays under about half the car's repaired value. A $2,640 rebuild on a 2016 SUV worth $9,000 after the fix is 29% of value, usually worth doing. The same repair on a car worth $3,500 is 75%, which pushes you toward a used unit or a different car. Also weigh the rest of the vehicle: tires, suspension, and rust are the next bills if they're coming.

Rebuild, used, or remanufactured transmission?

Rebuilt in-shop ($1,500-$3,500, 1-3 year warranty) keeps your own case and suits a car you're keeping. Used from a salvage yard ($1,200-$2,800, 30-90 day warranty) is the budget play for older cars, with real risk the unit has the same wear. Remanufactured ($3,000-$5,000, 2-3 year warranty) is factory-refreshed to spec, the safest long-term option if the car justifies it. CVTs generally go reman or nothing.

Can you drive with a slipping transmission?

Short distances to a shop, gingerly, maybe. Hard acceleration, towing, or highway trips while it slips turns a $300 solenoid into a $3,000 rebuild, because the clutches destroy themselves as they slip. If the fluid is burnt or there's metal in the pan, stop driving it entirely. Get it diagnosed before the tow bill becomes the small part of the invoice.

How long does a transmission rebuild take?

Typically 3 to 5 days in the shop: a day to remove, a day or two to rebuild or wait on the replacement unit, and a day or two to reinstall, refill, and road-test. Back-ordered parts or a busy transmission shop can stretch it to a week or two. Remanufactured units often ship within a few days, which is why some shops prefer them over in-house rebuilds.
