What a California Speeding Ticket Really Costs

California runs the country's most famous fine multiplier. The legislature writes small base fines ($35 for 1-15 over), then penalty assessments, state surcharges, and court fees multiply it to $234 or more, roughly a 6x markup. Go 16-25 over and the all-in number is about $367; cross 100 mph and it's a mandatory court appearance, around $800-1,000, and two negligence points instead of one.

Points matter more here than in most states. One speeding point is rated by insurers for about three years (24% average increase, about $552 a year on a $2,300 premium, $1,656 total). Four points in 12 months, six in 24, or eight in 36 brings a negligent-operator suspension. A second major violation moves you toward nonstandard-carrier pricing.

Traffic school is how Californians dodge most of this. You still pay the fine and roughly $64-100 for an approved course plus a small administrative fee, but the point never reaches your insurer, which deletes the $1,656 surcharge from the math. Eligibility runs once per 18 months for eligible 1-point violations. The economics: school costs about $300-400 total to avoid $1,656. The exception is the 100+ mph ticket, where school isn't available and the fight itself is the only lever; officers' speed measurement (pace, radar, lidar) becomes the battleground. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a speeding ticket in California?

A $35 base fine for 1-15 mph over becomes about $234 with penalty assessments; 16-25 over runs about $367 and 26+ about $490. The assessments stack state, county, and court surcharges that multiply the base roughly 5-6x. Add the three-year insurance increase of roughly $1,656 and the true cost approaches $1,890.

Does traffic school remove a California ticket from insurance?

It masks the point from your insurer, which is what pricing cares about. You pay the fine plus about $64-100 tuition and a small admin fee, complete an 18-month-eligible course, and the DMV holds the conviction confidential from your carrier. The violation still counts internally toward negligent-operator points.

What happens if you get caught going 100 mph in California?

Mandatory court appearance, fines around $800-1,000, two negligence points (double a normal ticket), likely license suspension if it stacks with priors, and no traffic school. Insurers treat it as a major violation, with increases well beyond the typical 24%.

How much does insurance go up after a speeding ticket in California?

About 24% at renewal on average, per ValuePenguin, LendingTree, and The Zebra analyses, lasting roughly three years. On a $2,300 premium that is roughly $552 a year. California insurers price primarily on your motor vehicle record, which is why masking the point via traffic school is so valuable there.
