How much does dog surgery cost? Common canine procedures run $2,000–$5,000 for foreign-body removal, $4,500–$6,500 for TPLO knee surgery on a large dog, $1,500–$4,500 for patella repair, and $2,500–$6,000 for bloat. Size, region (metros run 25–40% above average), and add-ons like imaging and rehab move the bill; pet insurance typically reimburses 80–90% after the deductible on a covered condition.

How the Surgery Cost Calculator Works

Every surgical estimate has three layers: the procedure itself (priced by dog size — anesthesia, drugs, and implant scale with weight), the add-ons around it (workup, imaging, meds, follow-ups), and where you live. This calculator stacks all three, then shows what insurance would pay.

The formula

Procedure range for your dog's size × regional factor (0.85 low-cost, 1.0 average, 1.25 high-cost metro, 1.4 top metro) + add-ons at national prices. Insurance reimbursement = (all-in cost − annual deductible) × reimbursement percentage on a covered condition. The calculator prices the middle of each range for the insurance split; real bills land anywhere in the band.

How to use it

Pick the procedure and your dog's size, set your region, and leave the common add-ons ticked (bloodwork, X-rays, pain meds, and exam are near-universal before surgery). If you have insurance, switch it on and enter your deductible and reimbursement level — 90%/80% are the most common plans. For TPLO and other orthopedics, tick the rehab course: post-op physical therapy at $350–900 is standard of care at many referral hospitals now, not an upsell.

A worked example: TPLO on a lab in a big city

A 75-lb labrador tears a cruciate ligament and needs a TPLO in a high-cost metro. The large-dog procedure runs $4,500–6,500 nationally; ×1.25 for the metro gives $5,625–8,125. Add the standard stack — exam ($50–85), bloodwork ($100–250), X-rays ($150–400), pain meds ($30–100), and a rehab course ($350–900), about $1,050 all-in — and the total lands at $6,675–$9,175.

With insurance: the midpoint claim is $7,925. Subtract the $500 deductible, apply 90%: the insurer reimburses $6,683 and the owner pays $1,243. Without insurance the same bill is a $7,925 charge on a credit card — which is the entire pitch for buying a policy while the dog is young and healthy, before the first lameness becomes a pre-existing condition.

What shifts the number most

Referral vs general practice: a board-certified surgeon often runs $1,000–$2,000 more, and for TPLOs, FHOs, and airway surgery, that premium buys measurable experience. Complications: enterotomy sites that leak, infections, and re-laparotomies can double a foreign-body bill — one honest reason quotes are ranges. And timing: septic patients that present late cost more to stabilize, which is why "wait and see" on a vomiting dog is a wager, not a plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does TPLO surgery cost for a dog?

A TPLO for a cruciate-ligament tear runs $3,000–$4,500 for a small dog, $3,800–$5,500 for medium, $4,500–$6,500 for large, and $5,000–$7,500 for giants — before add-ons like bloodwork, X-rays, and rehab, which add $500–$1,000. All-in, most owners pay $4,000–$7,500, and referral hospitals in expensive metros can exceed $8,000. The cheaper lateral-suture repair ($1,200–$3,500) still works well for small dogs.

How much does surgery cost if my dog swallowed something?

If the object is still in the stomach, endoscopic removal runs $800–$2,500. If it has reached the intestines, surgery runs $2,000–$5,000, and a complicated case with bowel resection or damaged tissue can pass $6,000. Time matters more than money here — a linear foreign body (string, tinsel, floss) can plicate the intestine, and every hour of delay worsens the odds.

How much does patella luxation surgery cost?

Patella luxation repair runs $1,500–$3,000 for small dogs (where the problem is most common), $1,800–$3,500 medium, and $2,500–$4,500 for large. Most luxating-patella patients are small breeds, so the realistic bill with workup is $2,000–$3,500. Grade 1 and many Grade 2 luxations are managed medically with weight control and exercise modification rather than surgery.

Does pet insurance cover dog surgery?

Accident-and-illness plans (the standard policy, $600–$750 a year average for dogs) cover surgery for covered conditions, reimbursing typically 80–90% of the bill after your deductible — on a $7,925 surgical claim with a $500 deductible and 90% reimbursement, you get back $6,683. Wellness plans do not cover surgery, and no insurer covers a condition that showed symptoms before the policy started, which is why cruciate tears often end up owner-paid: the other knee's lameness predates enrollment.

Why do vet surgery prices vary so much between clinics?

Three levers: who does it (general practice vs board-certified surgeon at a referral hospital, often $1,000–$2,000 apart), where it is done (high-cost metros run 25–40% above the national average), and what is in the quote (some bundle anesthesia, fluids, monitoring, and rechecks; others itemize them). Ask each clinic for an all-in written estimate that includes anesthesia, take-home pain meds, and recheck visits before comparing.

How can I pay for a $5,000 dog surgery?

In order of cost: pet insurance bought before the problem (reimburses 80–90%), an emergency fund, Scratchpay or CareCredit financing (read the deferred-interest terms — the promotional 0% can retroactively charge ~27% APR on the whole balance if missed), a low-interest personal loan, and vet-school teaching hospitals or nonprofit funds for qualifying owners. Many clinics also accept payment plans on smaller procedures if you ask before the bill exists.
