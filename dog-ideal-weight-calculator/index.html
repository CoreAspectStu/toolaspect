🐕 Dog Ideal Weight Calculator

Breed range + body condition score = your dog's healthy weight

There's no single right weight for a breed — a healthy Labrador spans 55–80 lb — but there is a right condition. Vets score body condition on a 9-point scale, where each point above 5 means roughly 10% of ideal body weight in excess. Divide your dog's current weight by that factor: a 90 lb Lab at score 7 is about 20% heavy, so their ideal weight is 90 ÷ 1.2 = 75 lb. This calculator does that math, shows the breed's healthy range, and computes daily weight-loss calories (a 75 lb target gets roughly 986 kcal/day).

How the Calculator Works

Two inputs do the work: where your dog is, and what condition they're in. Breed supplies a healthy range from AKC standards (or enter your own for a mixed breed). The body condition score supplies the correction factor, using the veterinary convention that each point above 5 carries about 10% of ideal body weight: ideal weight = current weight ÷ (1 + 0.1 × (BCS − 5)). From there the calculator derives excess pounds, the daily calorie target for weight loss, and a realistic timeline.

A worked example

A 90 lb Labrador scored 7/9 at the vet. Each of the two points above ideal adds 10%, so 90 lb = ideal × 1.2, and ideal weight = 90 ÷ 1.2 = 75 lb — inside the breed's 55–80 lb range, near the top. That's 15 lb to lose, about 17% of current body weight.

For feeding: RER at the 75 lb target (34.0 kg) is 70 × 34.0^0.75 ≈ 986 kcal. A weight-loss plan feeds 1.0 × RER at ideal weight, so roughly 986 kcal a day — noticeably less than the 1,578 kcal a neutered dog maintains 75 lb on. At the safe pace of 1–2% of body weight per week, 90 → 75 lb takes about 9–18 weeks. Weigh every two weeks; if nothing moves, cut 10% more.

Underweight dogs work the same way in reverse: a dog at BCS 3 is roughly 20% under ideal, so a 50 lb dog at 3/9 has an ideal weight near 50 ÷ 0.8 = 62.5 lb. See your vet — sudden weight loss has a different differential than gradual gain.

Why the scale reading isn't the answer

Two dogs of the same breed can differ by 20 lb and both be perfect. Build matters: a narrow-chested Labrador at 80 lb is overweight while a broad Bernese at 80 lb is mid-range. That's why vets score condition by hand (ribs, waist, tuck) rather than reading a chart, and why this tool leads with BCS and uses the breed range as a sanity check rather than a verdict. When the BCS math and the breed range disagree hard — say the math says your "55 lb Beagle" should be 45 — trust the hands-on score and confirm with your vet.

For the feeding side of the plan, the dog food calculator converts calories into cups of your specific food, and the raw dog food calculator does the same for raw and fresh diets at 2–3% of target body weight. Weight-loss dog food (high protein, low calorie density) lets your dog eat a bigger volume for the same calories — useful when the bowl suddenly looks empty.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should my dog weigh?

Start from the breed range - Labradors 55-80 lb, Beagles 20-30 lb, Chihuahuas 4-6 lb - then adjust for body condition. Feel the ribs and look for a waist. A dog at body condition score 5 out of 9 (ribs palpable, visible waist from above, abdominal tuck from the side) is at a healthy weight, and the scale reading at that condition is that dog's ideal weight.

How do I calculate my dog's ideal weight if they're overweight?

Divide current weight by a factor for their body condition score, using the vet convention that each point above 5 is about 10% of ideal body weight: a 90 lb Labrador at BCS 7 is roughly 20% overweight, so ideal weight = 90 ÷ 1.2 = 75 lb. BCS 8 divides by 1.3, BCS 9 by 1.4.

How many calories should my dog eat to lose weight?

Veterinary weight-loss plans feed 1.0 x RER at the target weight, where RER = 70 x (ideal kg)^0.75. A dog whose ideal weight is 75 lb (34 kg) has an RER of about 986 kcal, so roughly 986 kcal per day - not the 1,578 kcal a neutered dog maintains 75 lb on. Weigh every two weeks and aim for 1-2% of body weight lost per week.

Is there a BMI chart for dogs?

Not a useful one. Dogs vary too much in build for a single height-weight formula, so vets use the 9-point body condition score instead: a hands-on check of ribs, waist, and abdominal tuck that works for a Greyhound and a Bulldog alike. BCS plus the breed range does what BMI tries to do, better.

How fast should a dog lose weight?

1-2% of body weight per week. Faster risks muscle loss and a stalled metabolism; slower drags the diet out for months. A 90 lb Labrador dropping to 75 lb takes roughly 9-18 weeks at that pace. Recheck weight and BCS every 2 weeks and adjust calories by about 10% if the scale isn't moving.

Does being overweight really shorten my dog's life?

Yes. In a 14-year Purina study of 48 Labrador Retrievers, dogs kept lean lived a median of 13.0 years versus 11.2 for their heavier littermates - about 1.8 extra years - and went 3 years longer before needing arthritis medication. See our dog life expectancy calculator for breed-by-breed numbers.
