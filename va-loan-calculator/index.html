How the VA Loan Calculator Works

VA loans let eligible veterans and service members buy with zero down and no monthly mortgage insurance, which is the single biggest cost advantage in American mortgage lending. The catch is a one-time funding fee, usually financed into the loan, that most other calculators ignore. This tool applies the correct current fee for your situation and shows the payment you'll actually make.

The formula

Base loan = home price − down payment. The funding fee is a percentage of that base loan, set by your down payment and whether it's your first VA loan: 2.15% for first use with less than 5% down, 3.30% for subsequent use, dropping to 1.50% at 5% down and 1.25% at 10%. Total loan = base + fee. Monthly principal and interest uses the standard amortization formula, M = P × (r/12) ÷ (1 − (1 + r/12)−n), on the total loan. There's no PMI or MIP line at all; the monthly payment is just P&I plus taxes and insurance divided by 12.

How to use it

Enter the price and your down payment (percent and dollar fields stay in sync). Choose first or subsequent use, and tick the exemption box if you receive VA disability compensation, which zeroes out the fee. Results update as you type. Try adding a 5% down payment to see how much the fee rate drop saves you; on subsequent use it cuts the fee by more than half.

A worked example

A $400,000 home with $0 down on a first VA loan carries a 2.15% funding fee: $8,600 on the $400,000 base, for a total loan of $408,600. At 6.25% over 30 years, principal and interest is $2,516 a month. Add $250 in property tax and $125 in insurance and the full payment is about $2,891. A conventional buyer with the same numbers would be paying PMI on top; a VA borrower isn't.

How does a VA loan compare to FHA and conventional?

The zero-down feature gets the headlines, but the real advantage is what's missing: monthly mortgage insurance. FHA charges an upfront premium plus an annual one that never comes off with less than 10% down, and conventional loans below 20% down carry PMI until you build enough equity. The VA replaces all of that with the one-time funding fee you see above.

Read the last column with the down-payment column together. The VA borrower pays the smallest or comparable monthly payment while bringing $0 to the table — everyone else spent $14,000 to $20,000 in cash to get there. Exempt veterans do even better, skipping the $8,600 fee entirely. The trade-offs are real but narrow: the funding fee is wasted money if you never benefit, VA appraisals can be stricter about property condition, and some sellers bristle at closing-cost concessions. For a deeper look at the fee itself, the VA funding fee guide breaks down every rate and exemption.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible for a VA loan?

Veterans, active-duty service members, many National Guard and Reserve members, and some surviving spouses. The usual thresholds are 90 days of active service during wartime, 181 days during peacetime, or six years in the Guard or Reserves. You prove it with a Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which lenders can usually pull online in minutes.

Can you have two VA loans at the same time?

Yes. VA entitlement can be split, so plenty of people keep one VA loan on a former home and use remaining entitlement to buy the next one, often after a military move. The second loan counts as subsequent use, so the funding fee jumps to 3.30% if you put less than 5% down.

Is there a VA loan limit?

Not if you have full entitlement, and that's been true since 2020. The VA will back a loan of any size with zero down; your income, credit, and the lender's appetite set the practical cap. If you have reduced entitlement from an existing VA loan, county conforming limits come back into play.

Can the funding fee be refunded?

Yes, in one common case: you paid the fee, then later received a service-connected disability rating with an effective date before your loan closed. The VA refunds the fee, and if it was financed, the refund is applied against your loan balance. Contact your lender or regional VA loan center to start the claim.

How much is the VA funding fee now?

2.15% of the loan amount for first-use purchases with less than 5% down, 3.30% for subsequent use, 1.50% at 5% to 9.99% down, and 1.25% at 10% or more down. Those rates took effect April 7, 2023 and haven't changed since. Borrowers receiving VA disability compensation, certain Purple Heart recipients, and eligible surviving spouses pay nothing.

Does a VA loan ever need a down payment or PMI?

No down payment is required with full entitlement, and VA loans never carry monthly mortgage insurance of any kind — the funding fee exists precisely to fund the program in PMI's place. Putting 5% or 10% down is still worth considering, though, because each step drops the fee rate and shrinks the balance the fee is charged on.
