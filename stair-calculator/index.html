How the Stair Calculator Works

Measure your total rise — the vertical distance from the finished lower floor to the finished upper floor — and enter your preferred tread depth. The calculator divides the rise by a target riser height near 7¼", rounds to a whole number of risers, then computes the exact riser height, tread count, total run, and the stringer length using the Pythagorean theorem. It also checks the result against IRC residential stair code automatically.

Formulas Used

  • Riser count: n = round(total rise ÷ target riser)
  • Exact riser height: total rise ÷ n
  • Tread count: n − 1 (the top tread is the landing/floor itself)
  • Stringer length: √(total run² + total rise²) — Pythagoras on the stair triangle
  • IRC code (residential): riser 7¼" max (min 4"), tread 10" min, risers within ⅜" of each other

Worked Example

Deck stairs with a total rise of 108": 108 ÷ 7.25 = 14.9 → round to 15 risers. Exact riser height = 108 ÷ 15 = 7.20" — code compliant (under 7¾"). Treads = 14. With 10.5" treads the total run is 14 × 10.5 = 147". Stringer length = √(147² + 108²) = √(21,609 + 11,664) = √33,273 ≈ 182.4" ≈ 15.2 ft — buy 16 ft 2×12 stringer stock.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum riser height allowed? IRC residential code allows a 7¾" maximum riser and requires a 10" minimum tread (measured nosing to nosing). Commercial IBC stairs are stricter at 7" risers and 11" treads.

Why do I get one fewer tread than risers? The upper floor or landing serves as the top step, so a 15-riser stair has 14 cut treads on the stringer.

How long should stair stringers be? Use the Pythagorean result plus 12–18" of extra stock for trimming and attaching to the frame. For rise over ~60", use a middle stringer on 36"+ wide stairs.

What size lumber for stringers? 2×12 for risers up to 7¾" with 10–11" treads; never cut a 2×10 so deep that less than 5" of uncut depth remains.
