About the Sod Calculator

This sod calculator converts your lawn's square footage into rolls, pallets, and cost. Enter the area or dimensions and price per pallet, and the tool works out how much sod to order and roughly what it will weigh.

Sod is sold two ways: individual rolls laid by hand, and full pallets delivered by truck. Knowing both counts prevents over- or under-ordering, and adding a small waste factor covers irregular edges.

How Sod Quantities Are Calculated

Sod rolls in the US typically cover 9 or 10 square feet — a common roll size is 16 by 60 inches (6.67 sq ft) for small rolls, while the widely used contractor roll covers about 10 square feet. Square feet per pallet vary by region and farm: standard pallets are commonly 450 or 500 square feet.

The formulas are simply: rolls = area ÷ roll coverage, and pallets = area ÷ pallet coverage. Add 5 to 10 percent for cutting and irregular shapes. Weight matters for delivery planning — fresh-cut sod holds a lot of moisture, and pallets commonly weigh between 1,500 and 3,000 pounds depending on moisture and thickness, so a forklift or pallet jack is usually needed at delivery.

Measuring Your Lawn Area

For rectangular lawns, multiply length × width in feet. For odd shapes, divide the yard into rectangles and circles, compute each (circle area = π × radius²), and add them up. Subtract large beds, patios, and walkways.

Order sod only when you can lay it immediately: harvest-to-install within 24 hours is the standard recommendation, because sod heats as it decomposes and begins to yellow. Prepare the soil first — till, rake smooth, and moisten — so the sod goes down on ready ground the day it arrives.

Delivery, Installation, and First-Year Care

Pallets left stacked in the sun generate internal heat that kills grass within hours; if you cannot lay sod immediately, break down pallets into shaded single layers and keep them moist. Measure your area the day before and re-confirm the farm's pallet coverage so the truck brings what your numbers say.", 'Installation is straightforward but physical: stagger rolls like brickwork, butt edges tightly without stretching, and cut rolls in half lengthwise to start alternating rows. On slopes steeper than about 2:1, pin rolls with biodegradable stakes. Roll the finished lawn with a half-full roller to press roots into soil, then soak to a depth of several inches immediately.', "The first two weeks decide everything: keep the top inch of soil consistently moist, watering daily or twice daily in heat. Mow only once the sod resists lifting — usually two to three weeks in — cutting no more than a third of the blade height. Hold off on fertilizer until the roots establish, following your local extension office's seasonal schedule."]

Costs to budget beyond the sod itself: delivery fees often apply under a few pallets, installation labor typically runs roughly double the material price if you hire it out, and a starter fertilizer at laying time is cheap insurance. Soil amendments matter too — if you are tilling in compost before installation, figure about 1 cubic yard of compost per 1,000 square feet worked into the top few inches, which improves rooting and reduces first-summer watering.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square feet is a pallet of sod?

Standard pallets commonly hold 450 or 500 square feet of sod, depending on the farm and region. Mini pallets and big rolls also exist, so confirm the coverage with your supplier before ordering.

How many sod rolls do I need for 1,000 square feet?

At the common 10 square foot roll, 1,000 square feet is exactly 100 rolls — 2 pallets at 500 square feet each. Add 5 to 10 percent extra if your lawn has curved borders.

How much does a pallet of sod weigh?

A full pallet typically weighs between 1,500 and 3,000 pounds depending on soil moisture, sod thickness, and roll size. Plan for equipment to move it; it is not a one-person carry.

How much extra sod should I order for waste?

Add 5 percent for simple rectangular lawns and up to 10 percent for curved edges, slopes, or first-time installers. Offcuts along borders are unavoidable, and sod is cut fresh per order so returns usually aren't accepted.

When is the best time to lay sod?

Cool-season grasses like fescue and bluegrass root best in fall and spring; warm-season grasses like Bermuda and zoysia establish fastest in late spring to summer. Whenever you install, lay sod within 24 hours of harvest and water daily for the first two weeks.
