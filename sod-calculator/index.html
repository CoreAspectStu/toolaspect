How the Sod Calculator Works

Measure the lawn area you want to sod and enter the dimensions (or enter the total square footage directly as length × 1). The calculator adds a small waste allowance for trimming and irregular edges, then converts to pallets and rolls. Sod is sold two ways: by the pallet — slab pallets typically cover 450–500 sq ft, big-roll pallets 500–600 sq ft — and by the individual roll or slab, commonly 10 sq ft (a 2 × 5 ft slab) or 16 × 24 in pieces. Always confirm your farm's pallet coverage, it varies by region and cutter.

Formulas Used

  • Area: sq ft = length × width
  • With waste: sq ft × (1 + waste%), 5% is standard for trimming loss at edges
  • Pallets: ceil(sq ft ÷ sq ft per pallet), pallet ≈ 450–500 sq ft
  • Rolls/slabs: ceil(sq ft ÷ sq ft per roll), typical slab = 10 sq ft

Worked Example

A 40 × 30 ft front yard = 1,200 sq ft; with 5% waste that is 1,260 sq ft. At 500 sq ft per pallet you need 3 pallets (1,500 sq ft delivered — extra sod is cheap, running short is not, and leftover pieces patch edges). In 10 sq ft slabs that is 126 pieces. Sod at $450/pallet runs about $1,350 in material; installed price with prep typically lands at $1.50–2.50 per sq ft.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square feet in a pallet of sod? Most slab pallets cover 450–500 sq ft (roughly 45–50 slabs of 10 sq ft); big-roll pallets cover up to 600 sq ft. Slab pallets weigh 1,500–3,000 lb depending on moisture.

How much waste should I order? 5% for clean rectangles, up to 10% for curved beds and irregular borders. Sod must be installed within 24–48 hours of cutting, so ordering 10% extra "to be safe" wastes money unless you can use it immediately.

How much sod do I need for 1/4 acre? A quarter acre is 10,890 sq ft — about 22–24 pallets. Most farms deliver free above 4–6 pallets.

Seed or sod? Sod gives an instant lawn and erosion control at 3–5× the cost of seed. Seed wins for large areas and shade-tolerant blends; sod wins for slopes, new construction, and immediate results.
