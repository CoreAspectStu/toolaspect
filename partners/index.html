Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bartender cost per hour?

Professional bartenders through staffing agencies and mobile bar companies run $30-$70 an hour, with $50 typical. Thumbtack's national band runs $40-$60 an hour, and major-metro specialists quote up to $80. Two-hour minimums and billed setup time are standard, so a four-hour reception realistically bills 4.5 hours per bartender.

How many bartenders do I need for my event?

Follow the one-per-50-75-guests rule: one bartender per 75 guests for a beer-and-wine-only bar, one per 60 with a signature cocktail added, and one per 50 for a full cocktail menu. At 100 guests that means one or two bartenders; at 150 on a full bar, three. The extra bartender matters most in the 30 minutes after the toast, when everyone orders at once.

What's included in a mobile bar package?

The typical $300-$800 package brings the physical bar (a portable counter, skirting, sometimes a vintage trailer or tow-behind), ice, mixers, garnishes, cups, and small equipment. You supply the alcohol, which keeps it cheaper than a venue's open-bar package. Labor is often billed on top at the hourly rate, so compare quotes on the all-in total.

How much does a wedding bartender cost total?

When you supply the alcohol, expect $480-$1,200 total for a 100-150-guest wedding, per Insurance Canopy's published band. A typical build: 100 guests, full bar, 4 hours plus setup, two bartenders at $50 an hour ($450 labor), a $500 mobile bar package, and 18% gratuity ($81) — about $1,031 all-in, with a realistic range of $619-$1,543.
