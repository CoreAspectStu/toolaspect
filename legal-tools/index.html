How These Legal Calculators Work

Most people's first question after an injury, a denial, or a termination is simple: what is this worth? The twenty-nine tools in this directory answer that question the same way attorneys do at a first consultation — by applying a recognized method to your numbers. Car accident and pain-and-suffering values come from the multiplier and per-diem methods. Workers comp settlements multiply an impairment rating by scheduled weeks. Wrongful termination adds up back pay, front pay, and benefits. SSDI back pay is computed from your onset date through the waiting period. None of it is a prediction, and none of it is legal advice — it is the arithmetic behind the headlines, run instantly and free in your browser.

Each calculator pairs with a plain-language guide (seventeen in the Guides section below) that explains where the numbers come from, what varies by state, and which factors move a result the most. Use the calculator to get the figure, then read the guide to understand what an adjuster or judge would actually scrutinize.

Injury and Settlement Calculators

Ten tools cover the injury categories with the most published data. The car accident settlement calculator applies the multiplier method with fault adjustment and attorney fees. The pain-and-suffering calculator contrasts the multiplier method against per-diem valuation. Dog bite settlements weigh severity, scarring, and fault; slip-and-fall tools apply comparative fault rules that reduce awards by your share of responsibility. Mesothelioma claims are estimated from trust funds, settlements, and VA benefits. Motorcycle accidents factor rider injuries, gear, and helmet rules; Uber and Lyft accidents map the three app periods against the $1 million coverage that applies during active trips. Medical malpractice applies damages multipliers with state caps, and nursing home abuse settlements are tiered by harm level with reporting guidance.

Disability, Family Law, and Employment

Beyond injury law, the directory covers the calculations people need in difficult administrative and employment situations. The SSDI Denial and Back Pay calculator shows appeal odds by level, the waiting period, the fee cap on attorney compensation, and the back-pay amount implied by your onset date. The child support calculator implements the income-shares model along with the distinct Texas, New York, and California formulas. The lawsuit loan calculator exposes the true cost of pre-settlement funding — compound monthly fees can consume a startling share of a recovery, and the tool makes that visible before you sign. On the employment side, two wrongful-termination calculators add up back pay, front pay, lost benefits, and 401(k) value within statutory caps; the workers comp premium calculator estimates employer cost by class code; and the overtime calculator verifies time-and-a-half pay owed under the FLSA.

Why Estimates Differ from Outcomes

Every method here has real-world error bars. Multipliers range widely — insurers argue for 1.5, plaintiff attorneys push toward 5, and the same injuries price differently across venues. Comparative fault rules differ by state, with some barring recovery entirely above a threshold. Damage caps in medical malpractice and employment cases cut awards regardless of what the math says a harm is worth. Settlement timing matters too: a case valued early, before treatment finishes, will price lower than the same case after maximum medical improvement. These calculators show the structure of the math so you can see which levers matter — but the value of a lawyer is knowing how far each lever moves in your courthouse. Treat results as orientation, not expectation.

Reading the Guides

The seventeen guides below each take one question — how much is a mesothelioma settlement, how are workers comp settlements calculated, how do lawsuit loans work — and answer it from published data: NPDB payout records for malpractice, trust-fund disclosures for mesothelioma, rider survey data for motorcycle accidents. They are the background reading that turns a calculator's single number into an informed conversation with counsel. Like the calculators, they are free, require no signup, and are written to be read before you sign anything.

Finding the Right Tool for Your Situation

Start with the event, not the tool. A traffic collision points first to the car accident settlement calculator; if injuries dominated the crash, follow it with the pain-and-suffering calculator to sanity-check the general-damages component. A crash involving a rideshare driver is its own category — the Uber and Lyft tool maps the three app periods because coverage of $1 million attaches only during an active trip, and the difference between periods can be the whole case. A workplace injury runs through the workers comp settlement calculator, while an injury from a dog attack or a store fall uses tools built around severity tiers and comparative fault. Long-latency claims — mesothelioma from asbestos exposure decades ago, nursing home harm discovered in a facility's records — have dedicated calculators that reflect how those claims are actually valued: trust-fund schedules and harm tiers rather than simple multipliers. On the employment side, start with the wrongful termination settlement calculator for an overview, then the compensation calculator for the detailed wage-and-benefits stack. Denial letters from Social Security start with the SSDI tool, which shows why the appeal path usually beats refiling and what back pay accrues while you wait.

What Every Calculator Has in Common

Three rules hold across the whole directory. First, every tool is free and instant — no signup, no lead form, no call from a "case evaluator" afterward. Second, every tool shows its method: the multiplier, the rating times the scheduled weeks, the app-period table, the DIME-style stack, so you can audit the arithmetic and change the inputs. Third, every tool stops short of legal advice. Statutes of limitations, damage caps, comparative-fault thresholds, and filing windows are state-specific, deadlines are unforgiving, and no calculator can see the facts an attorney finds in a police report or personnel file. Use these tools to arrive informed; use a licensed attorney to act.
