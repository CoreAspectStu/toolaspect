How the Bridal Hair & Makeup Calculator Works

Beauty pricing is per-person, per-service, with a market multiplier and a short stack of fees. This calculator prices the bride and each attendant at your market's day-of rates, adds the bride's trial, optional lashes for everyone, and the artist's travel fee — the four lines that make up virtually every bridal beauty invoice.

The formula

Bride day-of = 2 × the per-service bride rate for your market. Bridal party = headcount × 2 (or 1, or 0) × the party per-service rate. Trial = 75% of the bride's day-of price at typical, running 50-100% across the range. Lashes = $15-$25 per person. Travel = flat $0-$100. The low and high estimates swap in the ends of each band.

How to use it

Set the market honestly — a wedding 40 minutes outside a major metro prices like the major metro. Count only the people actually getting services done; mothers and grandmothers often join late and can be added by bumping the count. If your artist quotes a per-mile travel fee instead of a flat one, pick the closest flat band. And if you're salon-bound instead of on-location, set travel to none and watch the minimum-booking problem disappear.

A worked example, three ways

The full party, mid-size metro: bride ($430 day-of) + four attendants at $210 each ($840) + bride's trial at 75% ($323) + lashes for five ($100) + $50 travel. Typical total: $1,743, with the range running $1,330-$2,315. That single line is 5% of a $34,200 wedding — more than most couples guess.

Bride only, small market: $340 day-of plus a $255 trial, no party, no lashes, salon visit with no travel fee. Total: $595 ($420-$800 range). The custom floor for a smaller celebration.

Major metro, big party: bride $560 + six attendants at $270 ($1,620) + trial ($420) + lashes for seven ($140) + $100 travel. Total: $2,840 ($2,125-$3,595). This is why NY/NJ couples quote four figures without pausing — and why the per-service math is worth knowing before you ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does bridal hair and makeup cost?

The bride pays $300-$600 for day-of hair and makeup combined (The Knot's 2025 band; WeddingWire's average runs about $300 with a $150-$600 range). Bridal party members run about $110 per service per person — roughly $210 for both hair and makeup. In a mid-size market, a bride plus four attendants getting both services comes to about $1,270 before trials, lashes, and travel.

How much is a bridal hair and makeup trial?

Trials usually cost the same as or slightly less than the day-of service — expect 50-100% of the day-of rate, with 75% typical. For a mid-market bride paying $430 for day-of hair and makeup, the trial adds about $323. Book one trial per service for the bride; attendants rarely need them.

Do bridesmaids pay for their own hair and makeup?

Usually yes, by custom — but the couple often covers it as the gift instead. Either way, budget the line: at about $105 per service in a mid-size market, four attendants getting both services is $840. Many artists require a minimum booking total (often $400-$600) to come on-location, so a small bridal party can pay near-large-party rates.

What is a travel fee for a wedding hair and makeup artist?

On-location artists charge for distance: commonly $0.50-$1 per mile beyond a set radius, or a flat $25-$75 fee — $50 typical. Destination weddings and early-morning call times push it higher. Some artists waive it against a minimum booking, so ask how the fee interacts with your party size before booking.

How much should I tip my hair and makeup artist?

15-20% of the service total is the standard thank-you, in cash on the day. False lashes are often billed per person ($15-$25) on top of the service price — confirm whether they're included, along with touch-up kits and on-site standby time, which some artists bill at $75-$200.

How do I save on wedding hair and makeup?

Three levers work. Cut the party's services, not the bride's — attendants can do their own makeup while the bride gets both. Use a salon visit instead of on-location service to erase the travel fee and minimum-booking requirements. And book a talented artist in their first 2-3 years, who typically charges 20-30% below established pros in the same market.
