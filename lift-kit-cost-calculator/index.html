How the Lift Kit Cost Calculator Works

Three products get called a "lift kit" and their price tags barely overlap. A leveling kit adds spacers or slightly taller front springs to fix the factory rake. A body lift raises the cab and bed on pucks without touching the suspension. A true suspension lift relocates the whole front end — and the taller it gets, the more control arms, brake-line extensions, and driveshaft corrections join the parts list. This calculator prices whichever one you're actually quoting.

The math

Parts estimate = the typical band for your lift type and truck class, positioned by your parts tier (budget picks the low end, mid the middle, premium the high end). Labor = typical shop hours for that lift type × your shop's rate. Add-ons stack on top: alignment, tires, wheels, and speedometer recalibration each add their typical cost when checked.

How to use it

Pick the lift type and truck, set your local labor rate (independent 4x4 shops commonly run $75–$100 an hour, dealers $125–$150), and check the add-ons you actually plan. Switching installation to DIY zeroes the labor line — fair for a leveling kit, optimistic for a 6-inch suspension lift, where spring compressors and brake-line handling eat the savings fast.

A worked example

A half-ton F-150, 4-inch suspension lift, mid-tier kit, at a $110 shop rate. Parts: $1,350. Labor: 12 hours × $110 = $1,320. Installed subtotal: $2,670 — right in the middle of the $1,500–$3,500 band for a half-ton. Add the mandatory alignment at $125 and you're at $2,795. The owner then adds 35-inch tires at $1,200, bringing the real out-the-door number to $3,995. That last jump is why the tires belong in the budget, not the surprise column.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a lift kit?

Depends which product you mean. A leveling kit runs $250 to $900 installed, a body lift $400 to $1,000, a mild 2-to-4-inch suspension lift $1,000 to $3,500, and a tall 6-inch suspension lift $3,000 to $7,000 or more. Most of that spread is kit quality and labor rate, and it moves further by truck: Jeeps sit at the cheap end, heavy-duty trucks at the top.

How much does a 4-inch lift kit cost installed?

Typically $1,500 to $3,500 installed on a half-ton like an F-150, Silverado 1500, or Ram 1500. Jeep Wranglers run closer to $1,000 to $3,000 and heavy-duty trucks $2,000 to $5,000. Budget brands with an independent 4x4 shop land near the low end; premium kits through a dealer or specialty shop run past the high end.

How much does a leveling kit cost?

$100 to $400 in parts and $250 to $900 installed. A leveling kit just adds spacers or slightly taller front springs to even out the factory rake, and it clears up to about 33-inch tires. Budget the alignment afterward either way — another $100 to $150.

What does a 6-inch lift cost?

Roughly $3,000 to $7,000+ installed. Tall kits need new control arms, track-bar brackets, brake-line extensions, and driveshaft corrections, which is why both parts and labor hours jump: expect 15 or more shop hours at $75 to $150 an hour.

What extra costs come after installing a lift kit?

Three, usually. The alignment is mandatory at $100 to $150. Bigger tires add $800 to $2,000, and if the new rubber needs wider wheels that's another $600 to $1,600. Go tall enough and the speedometer needs recalibration — a $100 to $300 programmer on most trucks.

Is it cheaper to install a lift kit yourself?

A leveling kit is a realistic driveway job — 2 to 4 hours with jack stands and hand tools — so DIY saves the $225 to $600 of labor. Full suspension lifts are different: spring compressors, torque specs, brake-line and ABS handling. Shops routinely finish half-done driveway jobs at $150 an hour, so price the install honestly from the start.

Will a lift kit void my warranty?

No blanket void. Under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act the dealer must show the modification caused the specific problem to deny that repair. A lifted truck with a failed infotainment screen is still a warranty claim; a destroyed CV joint after a 6-inch lift and 37s may not be.
