How the Cat Litter Cost Calculator Works

Litter is a per-pound commodity sold in confusing bag sizes, so the only comparisons that mean anything are dollars per pound and pounds used per month. A $23 40-lb bag of premium clay is 57.5 cents a pound; the same brand in 14-lb boxes can pass 90 cents. Substrates differ more than brands do: the cheapest silica costs triple what the priciest clay does per pound, but you use a quarter as much of it.

The formula

Price per pound = bag price ÷ bag size (overrides the $/lb field when you edit it). Monthly cost = cats × pounds per month for your substrate × price per pound. Subscription discount (Chewy Autoship 5%, Amazon Subscribe & Save 5-15%) multiplies the monthly and yearly figures, and the savings line is the discount times twelve. Per cat simply divides by your headcount.

How to use it

Pick your substrate — the calculator preloads a typical per-pound price and the planning pounds-per-month figure for it. Enter the actual bag you buy and its size, and set your subscription discount to 0 if you buy off the shelf. Two cats? Change the count; the math stays linear, which slightly overstates multi-cat cost and is the conservative budget.

A worked example

Two cats on premium clumping clay at $0.55 a pound: 2 × 25 lb × $0.55 = $27.50 a month, $330 a year, $13.75 per cat. Adding 5% autoship drops it to $26.13 a month and saves $16.50 a year — convenience money, not life-changing money. Swapping to pine pellets at $0.22 a pound (20 lb per cat) cuts the same household to $8.80 a month, $105.60 a year, a 68% cut.

Now the subscription question done honestly: PrettyLitter at $4 a pound and 6 lb per cat is $48 a month for two cats — within 9% of PrettyLitter's own two-cat price of $44, which tells you the DTC brands price right at the retail math. The health-monitoring crystals and doorstep delivery are what you're buying, not a discount.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cat litter cost per month?

For one cat: budget clay runs $9-$14 a month, premium clumping clay (the Dr. Elsey's tier, about 55 cents a pound) about $14-$17, pine and paper pellets $4-$9, and silica subscriptions $22-$48 (PrettyLitter charges $24 for one cat). Add a second cat and clay roughly doubles; most multi-cat households spend $25-$60 a month on litter.

What is the cheapest cat litter per pound?

Pine pellets are the floor at 18-35 cents a pound (equine bedding pellets at the hardware store are under 20 cents). Non-clumping clay runs 25-45 cents, clumping clay 35-60 cents, wheat and grass 90 cents-$1.60, tofu $1.40-$2.20, and silica gel $1.75-$4 — the premium silica subscriptions like PrettyLitter work out to about $4 a pound ($24 per 6-lb bag).

How long does a 40-lb bag of cat litter last?

For one cat scooping daily, a 40-lb bag of clumping clay lasts about 6-7 weeks, or roughly 25 pounds a month, because you top off what the scoop removes and dump the box monthly. Non-clumping clay full-change weekly uses closer to 30 pounds a month, so the same bag lasts 5-6 weeks. With two cats, expect a 40-lb bag every 3-4 weeks.

Are litter subscriptions worth it?

The discount is small and the convenience is real. Chewy Autoship takes 5% off and ships free, Amazon Subscribe & Save runs 5-15%, and direct brands like PrettyLitter ($24/month per cat) and Boxiecat (about $1.20 a pound) price convenience into the product itself. On $30 a month of clay litter, autoship saves $18 a year — worth it for the door delivery, not as a bargain.

How much litter do you use per cat?

Planning figures by substrate, per cat, per month: 25 lb of clumping clay, 30 lb of non-clumping clay, 6 lb of silica gel (one bag a month, per the makers), 20 lb of pine or paper pellets, 12 lb of wheat or grass, and 8 lb of tofu. Real usage tracks box size and scooping discipline more than brand.

Does more expensive litter save money?

Sometimes, but the math is about pounds used, not price per pound. Silica and tofu use a quarter of the volume clay does, so $4-a-pound silica can land in the same monthly range as 55-cent clay — that's why PrettyLitter's own two-cat price ($44) sits within a dollar of the generic math ($48). What actually costs more is buying litter in small bags: per-pound prices drop 30-50% from 14-lb boxes to 40-lb bags.
