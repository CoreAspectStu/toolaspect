How the Daycare Calculator Works

Daycare is sold three ways, and the structures only make sense against each other. The same dog at the same facility can cost $6,200 a year on walk-in pricing or $6,600 on an unlimited pass it barely uses — or $5,300 on the right package. This calculator finds your number.

The formulas

Days per month = days per week × 4.33. Walk-in monthly = days × day rate. Packages apply their discount to the rate before multiplying. The pass costs its flat price no matter how many days you use. Pass break-even = pass price ÷ full-day walk-in rate — the visit count where the pass becomes cheaper than paying per day, also shown in days-per-week terms (÷ 4.33).

How to use it

Enter your facility's full-day rate and how many days a week your dog would actually attend — be honest; optimism is how packages expire half-unused. Pick the structure you're considering, and swap the default $550 pass for your facility's real price. Half-day mode estimates the discounted rate at 60% of full day if you don't have the real number.

A worked example

A $40-a-day facility, three days a week. That's 3 × 4.33 = 13 days a month, $519.60 walk-in, $6,235 a year. The 20-day package at 15% off brings the effective rate to $34: $441.66 a month, saving $77.94 monthly — $935.28 a year. The $550 unlimited pass? Break-even sits at 550 ÷ 40 = 13.75 visits a month, or about 3.2 days a week. At exactly three days, the package wins; at three and a half or more, the pass takes over and the annual bill freezes at $6,600 while walk-in keeps climbing with every extra day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does dog daycare cost per day?

Nationally, $25 to $65 for a full day, with an average around $40. Half days (usually under 5-6 hours) run $20 to $30 — about 55-65% of the full-day rate. Urban markets trend toward $45-$55+, and some facilities price by dog size. Monthly spend lands between $300 and $1,000 depending on how many days a week you go.

Are daycare packages worth it?

Usually, if you'll actually use the days. Prepaid packages typically save 10-15% versus walk-in rates — one facility's 30-day pack drops the daily rate from $37 to $26.67. The catch is expiration: most packs expire in 6-12 months, so a 20-day pack for a once-a-week dog can lapse half-used. At one or two days a week, walk-in plus occasional half days is often the smarter buy.

When does a monthly unlimited pass pay off?

At three-plus days a week. Break-even is pass price divided by the walk-in day rate: a $550 pass at $40 a day needs 13.8 visits a month (about 3.2 days a week) to beat walk-in pricing. Below that, you're subsidizing the gym members who never come. Above 4 days a week, the pass is almost always the cheapest structure available.

Why is doggy daycare more expensive in big cities?

Rent and staffing ratios. A daycare needs industrial square footage in or near a downtown, insurance, and enough attendants to keep dog-to-staff ratios safe for group play. Urban facilities commonly charge $45-$55+ per day versus $25-$35 in smaller markets, and franchise campuses in Miami, LA, and NYC sit at the top of the range.

Do daycares charge by dog size?

Some do. Size-based tiers are common at group-play facilities — one Miami campus charges $45 for small dogs and $55 for medium — because bigger dogs need more floor space per dog in a play group. Others charge one rate regardless of size and sort dogs into play groups by size and temperament instead.

What does daycare require before the first day?

Current vaccinations — rabies, DHPP, and bordetella at minimum, plus canine influenza at many facilities — and a temperament evaluation before joining group play. Some daycares also require or strongly suggest a fecal test and flea prevention. The eval is usually free or cheap and scheduled in advance, so book it before you need care, not the week of.
