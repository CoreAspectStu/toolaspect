How the IELTS Band Score Calculator Works

IELTS reports a band score from 0 to 9 for each of the four skills, then combines them into one overall band. The combination is deliberately simple: add the four section scores, divide by four, and round the result to the nearest half band.

The rounding rule

This is the part people get wrong. IELTS doesn't round like a calculator. If the average ends in .25, it rounds up to .5. If it ends in .75, it rounds up to the next whole band. Anything else goes to the nearest half band. So 6.25 becomes 6.5, 6.75 becomes 7.0, but 6.125 rounds down to 6.0 because the nearest half band is 6.0. The rule can only ever help you: both of the tie cases round in your favor.

How to use it

Enter the band score for each section from your Test Report Form. Scores snap to half-band steps, since that's how IELTS awards them. The calculator shows your overall band, the unrounded average underneath it, which rounding rule fired, and where the result sits on the CEFR scale. Practicing with mock tests? Enter your expected section scores to see which combinations clear your target, and read the guide on how IELTS scores are calculated for the full picture.

A worked example

Say you scored Listening 7.5, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.0, Speaking 6.5. The average is (7.5 + 7.0 + 6.0 + 6.5) ÷ 4 = 6.75. That ends in .75, so it rounds up to an overall 7.0 — a C1 result.

Now a tighter case: 6.5, 6.5, 6.0, 6.0. The average is exactly 6.25, which rounds up to 6.5. And a case where rounding goes the other way: 6.5, 6.0, 5.5, 5.5 averages 5.875. The nearest half band is 6.0, so the overall is 6.0 — no boost, because only .25 and .75 tie-break upward.

Notice what this means strategically. If three sections are locked in at 6.5, a 5.5 in the fourth averages 6.25 and still delivers a 6.5 overall. Half a band in one skill can move your whole result, which is why test-takers target their weakest section in the final weeks of prep.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the IELTS overall band score calculated?

The overall band is the average of your four section scores, rounded to the nearest half band. Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking carry equal weight. For example, 7.5 + 7.0 + 6.0 + 6.5 averages 6.75, which rounds up to a 7.0 overall.

Why did my 6.25 average become 6.5?

IELTS rounds averages ending in .25 up to the next half band, and averages ending in .75 up to the next whole band. So 6.25 becomes 6.5 and 6.75 becomes 7.0. This rounding-up rule works in your favor both times it can apply.

Can my overall band be 6.25 or 6.75?

No. Overall band scores are only awarded in whole and half bands, so possible overalls end in .0 or .5. Any .25 or .75 in the raw average gets rounded up before it's reported. Section scores themselves also only come in half-band steps.

What CEFR level is IELTS band 7?

Band 7.0 is C1 on the Common European Framework. The published alignment runs: 4.0-5.0 is B1, 5.5-6.5 is B2, 7.0-8.0 is C1, and 8.5-9.0 is C2. Band 3.5 and below sits below B1.

What IELTS score do universities require?

A 6.5 overall meets most postgraduate minimums, with 7.0 common at competitive programs and for fields like medicine, law, and teaching. Undergraduate requirements usually sit at 6.0 to 6.5. Many programs also set a per-section minimum, often 6.0 in each skill, so a strong overall can't always rescue a weak Writing score.

Do Listening and Reading count less than Writing and Speaking?

No. All four sections carry identical weight in the overall band average, even though Writing and Speaking are marked by examiners while Listening and Reading are marked against answer keys. A 7.0 in Listening lifts your average exactly as much as a 7.0 in Writing.
