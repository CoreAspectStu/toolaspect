How the Photo Booth Calculator Works

Operators quote a package price for a standard 3-hour block, then bill every additional hour at a booth-type rate and bolt on à-la-carte extras. This calculator mirrors that quote structure, so the number it shows is the number a written quote should land near.

The formula

Total = 3-hour package rate + (hours − 3) × extra-hour rate + digital sharing (hours × $235 typical) + custom backdrop ($150 typical) + travel ($50 typical). Low and high estimates swap in the ends of each band, so the spread is honest, not decorative.

How to use it

Pick the booth type before anything else; it's 70% of the bill. Match hours to your real peak window: for weddings that's roughly cocktail hour through the last hour of dancing, for proms the first ninety minutes after doors open. Tick the sharing kiosk only if guests will actually want clips on their phones; a print-only booth drops the total by about $235 an hour.

A worked example, both ways

The wedding: open-air booth, 4 hours, digital sharing kiosk. Base $620, one extra hour $200, sharing 4 × $235 = $940. Total: $1,760 typical, range $1,290-$2,440. Drop the kiosk and the same booth rents for $820 ($550-$1,300) — prints still included.

The prom: 360 spinner, 3 hours, custom backdrop with school colors. Base $1,170, backdrop $150. Total: $1,320 typical ($725-$2,750). That's the format students line up for, and why PTAs fundraise for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a photo booth?

Most standard 3-hour rentals run $500-$1,200, with the national average around $619. A 4-hour staffed rental typically costs $800-$1,500. Booth type moves the number more than anything else: open-air booths start around $400-$1,000, mirror and other premium formats run $900-$1,400, and 360 booths average $1,170 for 3 hours with a range of $675-$2,500.

How much is a 360 photo booth rental?

360 booths average $1,170 for a 3-hour rental nationally, roughly $390 an hour, with the full range running $675-$2,500 per event. Hourly rates land between $200 and $500. A prom-priced example: 3 hours plus a custom backdrop comes to $1,320 typical, range $725-$2,750. Operators charge for the attendant and the video-editing software that makes the slow-motion clips, so the floor price rarely drops below $600.

What is usually included in a photo booth rental?

Most packages include the booth, an attendant, unlimited or high-count photo strips, a basic backdrop, and props, for 2-4 hours of runtime. The common extras: digital sharing stations and social kiosks run $185-$285 an hour (Thumbtack's band), custom branded backdrops commonly quote $50-$250, and travel beyond the operator's radius adds $25-$150. A 4-hour open-air booth with a sharing kiosk totals $1,760 typical ($1,290-$2,440).

How many hours should I book a photo booth for?

Three to four hours covers most weddings and proms: usage peaks in the two hours after dinner or after the first dance, then tapers. Additional hours run $150-$300 for standard booths and $200-$500 for 360 booths, so extending a 3-hour package to 4 adds roughly $200-$390. Booking idle time so the booth opens before cocktail hour costs less per hour than runtime but is rarely free — ask before scheduling a 6 p.m. setup for an 8 p.m. event.

Do corporate events pay more for photo booths?

Standard corporate rentals price like weddings, $600-$1,300. What costs more is the brand-activation tier: custom booths wrapped in company graphics, data capture, and instant GIF delivery run $1,200-$3,200+ in major markets like Los Angeles. If you need lead capture or a step-and-repeat with a logo wall, budget toward the top of that band and expect the operator to quote a 4-hour minimum.

How can I save money on a photo booth rental?

Three levers work. Book the shortest package that covers your peak hours (3 hours, not 5) and skip idle-time setup. Choose an open-air booth over a mirror or 360 model; the photos of your guests being ridiculous look the same. And book off-peak: operators price Friday and Sunday events, and November-through-March dates, below Saturday nights in peak season. Digital-only packages (no prints) shave a bit more.
