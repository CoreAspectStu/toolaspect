How the Gutter Calculator Works

Enter your building footprint and the calculator works out gutter runs for a 2-sided (gable) or 4-sided (hip) roof, sizes the downspout count from your actual roof area (footprint × pitch multiplier), and counts hangers at your chosen spacing. Adjust the pitch multiplier to convert flat footprint area to true roof area — 1.1 for a 4/12 pitch, 1.25 for 8/12, up to 1.4+ for steep roofs.

Formulas Used

  • Gutter LF: 2 × building length (gable) or 2 × (length + width) for hip roofs, + waste
  • Roof area: length × width × pitch multiplier
  • Downspouts: ceil(roof area ÷ 600 sq ft) — the industry standard drainage capacity per standard downspout
  • Hangers: ceil(gutter LF ÷ spacing) + 1 per run; code/manufacturer standard is 24–36" spacing

Worked Example

A 40 × 30 house with a hip roof: gutters = 2 × (40 + 30) = 140 LF, +5% = 147 LF. Roof area at a 6/12 pitch (×1.12) = 40 × 30 × 1.12 = 1,344 sq ft → 1,344 ÷ 600 = 2.24 → 3 downspouts. Hangers at 3 ft: 147 ÷ 3 ≈ 49 + extras ≈ 53. Installed at $9/LF plus $90 per downspout: $1,323 + $270 ≈ $1,593.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many downspouts do I need? One standard 2×3 or 3×4 downspout per 600 sq ft of roof area as a rule of thumb; 3×4 oversized downspouts handle roughly 800–1,000 sq ft each and clog less.

What gutter size do I need? 5" K-style handles most residential roofs up to ~40 ft run per downspout; step up to 6" for steep roofs, long runs, or areas with heavy downpours.

How far apart should gutter hangers be? Every 24–36 inches for spikes/ferrules or hidden hangers; closer (24") in snow regions where ice loads the gutter.

What's the right gutter slope? ¼" of fall per 10 ft of run toward the downspout. Runs over 40 ft should slope from the middle to a downspout at each end.
