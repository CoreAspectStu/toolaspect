How This Content Calendar Template Works

A calendar fails when it asks you to make decisions on a schedule. So this one makes exactly three things configurable — platforms, cadence, times — and handles placement for you. Each platform's weekly posts fill the weekdays first, in order (5 posts land Mon–Fri, 7 lands every day, 3 lands Mon–Wed), which keeps daily load level instead of dumping five posts on Tuesday.

How the schedule is built

For each active platform, its N weekly slots spread across the week round-robin. Every slot carries the platform's default time, seeded from the best-times table and editable per platform. The month view rolls the weekly pattern forward: 10 posts a week averages 10 × 52 ÷ 12 ≈ 43 posts a month, and the ICS export writes one calendar event per slot for the next 30 days from your start date, with the platform name in the event title so your calendar is readable at a glance.

How to use it

Tick your platforms, set a cadence you can actually sustain, and nudge the default times if you know your audience skews early or late. Click Export calendar and open the .ics in Google Calendar (Settings → Import) or just double-click it on desktop. The Copy as text button gives you a markdown week you can paste into Notion or a doc for topic planning. Write your topics against the slots — the calendar owns when, you own what.

A worked example

A creator runs Instagram 5×/week, TikTok 3×/week, and YouTube 2×/week: 10 posts weekly, 10 × 52 ÷ 12 = 43.3, so roughly 43 posts a month. Instagram lands Mon–Fri at 1:00 p.m., TikTok Mon–Wed at 3:00 p.m., YouTube Mon/Tue at 9:00 a.m. Monday and Tuesday are the heaviest days at 3 posts each, Wednesday carries 2, Thursday and Friday 1 each, and the weekend stays clear — balanced without thinking about it.

Now the payoff math. Planning 22 posts (a typical 30-day month at 5/week) ad hoc at ~30 minutes each costs about 11.0 hours a month, because every session starts cold. Batching — one 3-hour monthly planning session plus ~8 minutes of execution per scheduled post — runs 5.9 hours for the same output. That's 304 minutes, about 5.1 hours, handed back every month. The template's job is making that batch session bearable; the export makes the plan survive contact with a busy week.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a content calendar?

A content calendar is a schedule of what you will publish, where, and when — typically a weekly or monthly grid with one row or card per post. It answers three questions in advance: the platform, the topic or format, and the posting time. Teams that plan in batches publish more consistently than teams that decide day by day.

How many posts should I plan per week?

A sustainable starting cadence is 3 to 5 posts per week on one primary platform, plus 1 to 3 repurposed pieces on a second. Ten posts a week across three platforms works out to about 43 posts a month (10 x 52 / 12). Consistency beats volume: a calendar you actually keep outperforms an ambitious one you abandon in week three.

What are the best times to post on each platform?

Aggregated engagement data (Sprout Social's 2025-2026 analyses of customer posts) points to midweek midday as the strongest window almost everywhere: Instagram Mondays 2-4 p.m., Tuesdays 1-7 p.m., Wednesdays 12-9 p.m.; TikTok Tuesdays-Thursdays 2-6 p.m.; Facebook Tuesdays-Wednesdays 12-8 p.m.; LinkedIn Tuesdays 11 a.m.-5 p.m. Treat these as starting points and let your own audience analytics override them after a few weeks.

Can I export this calendar to Google Calendar?

Yes. The Export ICS button downloads a standard calendar file with one event per planned post. Open it in Google Calendar, Outlook, or Apple Calendar and the slots appear as regular events you can move, color, and get reminders for. The file uses local floating times, so slots land at the times you set regardless of time zone.

How far ahead should I plan content?

Plan topics a month ahead and lock the schedule one to two weeks ahead. A month is far enough to balance content pillars and campaigns, but close enough to react to what is working. Batch the planning into one session: a 3-hour monthly session plus about 8 minutes per post at execution beats deciding fresh every day.

What should a content calendar template include?

Columns for date, platform, content pillar (educational, engaging, promotional, entertaining), format (reel, carousel, short, article), caption hook or working title, asset status, and posting time. The grid itself can be a spreadsheet, a Notion board, or calendar events — the fields matter more than the tool.
