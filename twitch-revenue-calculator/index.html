How the Twitch Revenue Calculator Works

Twitch monetizes through several channels — subscriptions, Bits, ad revenue, and sponsorships — and this calculator estimates all of them. The inputs are your Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 subscriber counts, Bits cheered per month, monthly ad revenue, monthly tips, and your revenue split (50/50 standard or 70/30 partner). The output applies the publicly documented payout splits so you can see how much of each dollar actually reaches the creator.

The split is the part many viewers underestimate. Twitch subscriptions pay affiliates and partners 50% of the purchase price — a Tier 1 sub at $4.99 returns about $2.50 to the streamer, with Twitch retaining the rest. Bits, the platform's cheering currency, return roughly 1 cent per Bit to the streamer (viewers buy Bits at a higher price than their payout value). Partners in some cases negotiate different terms, and Twitch has tested alternate splits for some partner tiers, but 50/50 sub revenue and ~$0.01 per Bit remain the widely documented baseline.

Payouts run monthly with a $100 minimum threshold — earnings below $100 roll over to the next month. Twitch also requires tax information and pays in supported currencies via the creator's chosen method, all documented in the official Twitch help pages.

Subscriptions, Bits, and Ads Compared

Each revenue line has different economics. Subscriptions are recurring and predictable: a Tier 1 sub is $4.99, of which roughly $2.50 goes to the affiliate; Tier 2 ($9.99) and Tier 3 ($24.99) exist but convert at a tiny fraction of Tier 1 volume. Bits are one-off and event-like, spiking during hype moments. Ad revenue, available to affiliates and partners, depends on hours streamed and ad density, with reported RPMs varying widely by channel size, season, and region — no reliable flat rate exists, which is why this tool does not guess it.

A useful planning identity: $2.50 per Tier 1 sub means $500 of monthly sub revenue requires roughly 200 active Tier 1 subs. At typical small-channel conversion patterns — where monthly active subs often sit near one to two per average concurrent viewer — a stream averaging 10 viewers might support 10–20 subs, i.e., $25–$50/month before any Bits or ads. That is the honest scale of the affiliate stage; growth compounds it.

Affiliate and Partner Requirements

To become a Twitch Affiliate, the published requirements are: at least 50 followers, 8 hours streamed in the last 30 days, 7 distinct broadcast days, and an average of 3 viewers. Affiliate unlocks subs and Bits. Partner status — with its expanded toolset and potentially negotiable terms — requires a sustained, larger audience and is granted by application. Neither tier changes the math this calculator performs: your revenue is still tiered subs times your split, plus Bits at about a cent each, plus whatever ads and tips you enter. If you are pre-Affiliate, use the calculator to model what your channel would earn once subs unlock — many streamers set a target of 50–100 Tier 1 subs as their first meaningful milestone, which at the standard 50% split is $125–$250 of monthly sub income before Bits, ads, or outside tips. Both programs' terms are published on Twitch's official help pages and can change; verify before planning income around them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Twitch affiliate make per sub?

Affiliates receive 50% of a Tier 1 subscription's $4.99 price — about $2.50 per sub per month. Tier 2 and Tier 3 pay roughly $5.00 and $12.50 respectively at the same split, though partners may have different negotiated terms.

How much are Bits worth to a streamer?

Roughly one cent per Bit to the streamer. Viewers buy Bits at a higher price than their payout value, and the difference is Twitch's share. A 1,000-Bit cheer is worth about $10 to the creator.

What are the Twitch Affiliate requirements?

As published by Twitch: 50 followers, 8 hours streamed in the last 30 days, 7 distinct broadcast days, and an average of 3 viewers. Meeting these unlocks subscriptions and Bits on your channel.

When does Twitch pay out?

Monthly, once accrued earnings reach the $100 payout threshold; amounts below $100 roll over to the following month. Payouts require completed tax and payment information.

Can this calculator predict my ad revenue?

Not by itself. Ad RPM on Twitch varies widely by channel size, audience region, season, and ad density, and Twitch publishes no flat rate. The calculator includes an Ad Revenue ($/mo) field that you fill from your own past statements or estimates — it applies the publicly documented splits to subs and Bits and simply adds your ad figure to the total.
