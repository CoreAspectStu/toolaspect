How the GPA Scale Converter Works

US schools compress a 100-point grade into a 4.0 number using letter bands. The converter above holds the standard 13-band table and works in all three directions: give it a percentage and it returns the letter and point value; give it a letter and it returns the percent band and points; give it a point value and it finds the letter whose bracket contains it.

How to use it

Pick a direction with the tabs, then type. The course-type dropdown applies the standard weighting — +1.0 for AP or IB, +0.5 for honors — so a 92% (A-, 3.7) in AP converts to 4.7. For a GPA value, brackets sit at the midpoints between letter points: anything from 3.85 up maps to A/A-, 3.5 to 3.84 to A-, 3.15 to 3.49 to B+, and so on.

A worked example

Five courses, standard scale: 92%, 85%, 78%, 95%, 88%. Band by band: 92 is an A- (3.7), 85 a B (3.0), 78 a C+ (2.3), 95 an A (4.0), 88 a B+ (3.3). Average the points: (3.7 + 3.0 + 2.3 + 4.0 + 3.3) ÷ 5 = 16.3 ÷ 5 = 3.26 unweighted. Now weight it — say the 95 is an AP course and the 88 is honors: (3.7 + 3.0 + 2.3 + 5.0 + 3.8) ÷ 5 = 17.8 ÷ 5 = 3.56 weighted. Same grades, half a point of difference, both numbers on one transcript.

The percent→GPA step is where most errors happen, because band edges are unforgiving: the converter rounds first, so 86.9 becomes 87 — a B+ (3.3) — while 86 on the nose stays a B (3.0). When a transcript lists integer percents, that one point at a boundary is worth 0.3 GPA — which is why teachers who round can genuinely change a class rank.

Frequently Asked Questions

What GPA is a B+?

On the standard 4.0 scale, a B+ is 3.3, earned for 87-89%. Neighbors: B is 3.0 (83-86%) and A- is 3.7 (90-92%). One percentage point decides it: 86 is a B, 87 is a B+, 89 is still a B+, and 90 jumps to A-. Weighted, a B+ in an AP course counts 4.3 and in honors 3.8.

What percentage is a 4.0 GPA?

A 4.0 needs A-level work: 93-100% on the standard scale with pluses and minuses (A = 4.0, A+ = 4.3 at schools that cap A+ higher), or 90-100% at schools using the flat 90=A scale. If your school doesn't distinguish A from A+, any grade from 93% up converts to 4.0.

How do I convert a percentage to GPA?

Match the percentage to its letter band, then take that letter's point value: 90-92 is an A- (3.7), 87-89 a B+ (3.3), 83-86 a B (3.0), 80-82 a B- (2.7), and so on down to F (0.0) below 60. For a cumulative GPA, average the point values across courses (weighted: add +1.0 per AP/IB and +0.5 per honors first). The converter above does both directions instantly.

What is the highest GPA on a weighted scale?

5.0 at most US high schools: an A (4.0) in an AP or IB course adds 1.0. Schools that also weight an A+ at 4.3 can reach 5.3, and a few use 6.0 scales for college-level dual enrollment. Colleges recalculate unweighted GPAs for comparison, which is why a 4.4 weighted and a 3.9 unweighted often appear on the same application.

Do all schools use the same GPA scale?

No. The most common US scale is the 13-step one here (A+ through F with pluses and minuses), but many schools use a flat scale where 90-100 is simply an A = 4.0, some skip D grades, some cap A+ at 4.0, and others don't calculate weighted GPA at all. Check your school's grading key — the table above is the version most transcript-evaluation services treat as standard.

Is a 3.7 GPA an A or an A-?

An A-. On the standard scale, A (93-96%) is 4.0 and A- (90-92%) is 3.7. A cumulative 3.7 means your average grade sits right at the A-/A boundary — roughly 92% — which many colleges treat as an A- average.
