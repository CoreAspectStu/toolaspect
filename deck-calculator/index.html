How the Deck Calculator Works

Enter your deck's length and width, joist spacing, and decking board width. The calculator estimates the number of decking boards, the number of joists (including a rim joist at each end), concrete footings based on beam spans, and a lumber cost estimate. Board width should include the fastener gap — a standard 5.5" composite or 5.5" wood board spans 5.5" installed with gaps.

Formulas Used

  • Decking boards: ceil(deck width ÷ (board width ÷ 12)), boards run parallel to the length
  • Joists: ceil(deck length ÷ (spacing ÷ 12)) + 1, plus rim joists
  • Footings: 2 per beam line; beams every ~8 ft of joist span (4 max for typical decks)
  • Deck area: length × width, for fasteners and stain estimates

Worked Example

A 16 ft × 12 ft deck with 5.5" boards running the 16 ft direction: board rows = 12 ÷ (5.5/12) = 26.2 → 27 rows. Using 16 ft boards, that's 27 boards. Joists at 16" OC spanning the 12 ft width: ceil(16 ÷ 1.333) + 1 = 13 joists × 12 ft each. With beams at mid-span and ledger, figure 4–6 footings depending on beam config. At $8/board and $14/joist: decking ≈ $216, joists ≈ $182.

Frequently Asked Questions

What joist spacing should I use? 16" OC is standard for wood decking. Use 12" OC for composite (check manufacturer specs — many require it perpendicular to house) and for diagonal layouts; 24" OC only for 2×6 wood boards.

How far can a 2x8 joist span? Roughly 12–14 ft at 16" OC for Southern pine; check the IRC span tables or your local code for your species and grade.

How many footings do I need? Typically one every 4–8 ft along each beam line, sized by soil bearing capacity. Most ground-level decks need 4–8; elevated multi-beam decks need more.

Should decking run parallel or perpendicular to the house? Boards normally run perpendicular to joists and parallel to the house for a conventional look — the calculator assumes boards run parallel to the deck length.
