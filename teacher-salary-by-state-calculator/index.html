How the Calculator Works

Two calculators in one page. The state lookup reads from the NEA's 2024-25 Rankings and Estimates table above. The projector models the step-and-lane salary schedule that most public school districts use.

The formula

Step schedules pay salary × (1 + step%)^(years − 1), with an optional lane multiplier for graduate credits or certifications: × (1 + lane%). Cumulative earnings sum every year's salary. The hourly rate divides by your contract hours per week times contract weeks, defaulting to 40 × 38, which is a standard 190-day contract at 8 paid hours a day.

How to use it

Pick your state for the published average. Then set your district's actual starting salary (the national average starting salary is $48,112, but districts range widely), your step percentage from the salary schedule on your district's HR page, and any lane bump. Teachers in step-schedule states typically see steps between 1% and 5%; some years districts freeze steps entirely, which the tool lets you model by setting step to 0.

A worked example

Start at the national average starting salary, $48,112, with 3% annual steps and no lane change. Year one pays $48,112; year ten pays 48,112 × 1.03^9 = $62,775. Over ten years the teacher earns $551,550 gross. On a 190-day, 8-hour contract that's 1,520 paid hours a year, so the hourly rate climbs from $31.65 to $41.30.

Add a 10% master's lane at hire and every figure scales: year-ten salary becomes $69,053, and the ten-year cumulative becomes $606,705, a $55,155 difference for the same decade of work. That's why lane changes are the single biggest lever inside a salary schedule, often worth more than a state-average raise.

For context against the state table: a teacher earning $62,775 in year ten is already above the 2024-25 average salary in fifteen states, from Oklahoma ($61,931) down to Mississippi ($54,975), states where the average includes 25-year veterans.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which state pays teachers the most?

California, at an average of $103,552 for 2024-25, the only state above $100,000. New York is second at $98,655, Washington third at $96,589, the District of Columbia fourth at $95,077, and Massachusetts fifth at $93,554. Mississippi is lowest at $54,975, with Florida ($56,663) and Louisiana ($56,785) just above it.

What is the average teacher salary in the US?

The national average public school teacher salary for 2024-25 is $74,495, up 3.5% from $71,985 the year before, per the NEA's Rankings and Estimates. The national average starting salary is $48,112. Averages blend everyone from first-year teachers to 30-year veterans, which is why they sit well above starting pay.

How much do teacher salaries rise each year?

Statewide one-year increases averaged 3.5% in 2024-25. The biggest movers were Nevada at 11.8%, the District of Columbia at 9.7%, and Delaware at 7.6%; Oklahoma raised just 1.0%. Within a career, most districts use step schedules that add roughly 1% to 5% per year of experience, plus larger lane jumps for graduate credits, which is what the projector above models.

Do teachers with a master's degree earn more?

Usually, yes, through lane differentials in the salary schedule. The premium varies widely by district, commonly 5% to 15% at each step and often compounding across a career. Enter your district's lane bump in the projector to see the career difference; a 10% lane premium applied at every step is worth several hundred thousand dollars over 30 years.

NEA average vs BLS median: which teacher salary number is right?

Both are real; they measure different things. The NEA collects average salaries from state education agencies ($74,495 for 2024-25, all public K-12 teachers). The Bureau of Labor Statistics surveys employers and reports medians by role: $72,040 for high school teachers, $64,370 for middle school, and $63,970 for elementary (May 2025). Averages run higher than medians because veteran salaries pull the mean up.
