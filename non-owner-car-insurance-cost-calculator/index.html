How the Non-Owner Insurance Calculator Works

Non-owner car insurance is a liability-only policy for people who drive but don't own a car — frequent renters, people who borrow family vehicles, drivers keeping coverage continuous between cars, and anyone who needs an SR-22 without a vehicle to insure. Because there's no collision or comprehensive layer, it costs a fraction of a full policy.

The formula

Estimate = state average × age factor. The state average is what a 40-year-old with a clean record pays for liability-only non-owner coverage in your state, and the age factor is your age's national average divided by the age-40 national average ($486). Monthly = annual ÷ 12; six-month term = annual ÷ 2. If you check the SR-22 box, the filing fee is added once to the first year.

How to use it

Pick your state and age. The number that comes back is a planning estimate for a clean record — violations, especially a DUI, push premiums up sharply, and the SR-22 fee is just the paperwork, not the surcharge. Compare the result against the full-coverage average for your state to see the spread you're capturing by not insuring a car you don't have.

A worked example

An Ohio driver, age 30. Ohio's average non-owner premium is $291, and the age-30 factor is 508 ÷ 486 = 1.045. Estimate: $291 × 1.045 ≈ $304 a year, about $25 a month, $152 a six-month term. Full coverage in Ohio averages $1,783, so the non-owner policy saves about $1,479 a year over insuring an owned car comprehensively.

Now a harder case: a 22-year-old in New Jersey. $1,141 × (645 ÷ 486) ≈ $1,514 a year, or $126 a month — more than triple the national average before any surcharge. Add an SR-22 with a $30 filing fee and the first year is $1,544. In expensive states at young ages, it pays to quote several carriers: the company table above shows a $619 spread for identical coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does non-owner car insurance cost?

Non-owner car insurance averages $486 a year nationwide, about $41 a month. Most drivers pay somewhere between $200 and $1,150 a year depending on state, with South Dakota the cheapest at $216 and New Jersey the priciest at $1,141. Your age moves the number too: a 16-year-old averages $1,660 while drivers in their 50s average around $460.

What does non-owner car insurance cover?

It is liability-only coverage: it pays for injuries and property damage you cause to other people while driving a borrowed or rented car. It does not cover damage to the car you are driving, because it carries no collision or comprehensive coverage. It also usually includes the state-required minimum liability limits, and it keeps your insurance history continuous between vehicles.

Who needs non-owner car insurance?

Four common cases: you rent cars often, you regularly borrow cars from friends or family, you are between cars and want to avoid a coverage lapse that raises future rates, or you need an SR-22 filing to reinstate a license but do not own a vehicle. If you drive the same household member's car weekly, you should usually be added to their policy instead.

Does non-owner insurance cost more than regular insurance?

Less, typically much less. Because it excludes collision and comprehensive, a non-owner policy runs a fraction of full coverage: nationally $486 a year versus $2,000-plus for full coverage on an owned car. Even compared with a liability-only owner policy it is usually a bit cheaper because low-mileage occasional drivers present less risk.

How much does a non-owner SR-22 add to the cost?

The SR-22 filing itself costs only $15 to $50, a one-time fee your insurer charges to submit proof of coverage to the state. What raises the premium is the violation that triggered the SR-22, not the form. Filing typically stays on your record about three years, and not every insurer writes SR-22s, so shopping among carriers that do matters more than the filing fee.

Can I get non-owner insurance with a suspended license?

Sometimes. Many states require an SR-22 or equivalent proof of financial responsibility before reinstatement, and a non-owner SR-22 policy is the standard path for drivers who do not own a car. Availability depends on the insurer and state rules, so expect to compare a few carriers.

This calculator is an estimate based on published industry averages, not a quote or insurance advice.
