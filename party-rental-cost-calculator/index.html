How the Party Rental Calculator Works

Rental yards price the tent by size and everything else by the piece, so the calculator works the same way. It sizes the tent from your guest count using the standard planning rules, then stacks the furniture at per-item rates you can override.

The formula

Tent need = guests × 12 sq ft (seated at rounds) + dance floor × guests × 3 sq ft + 200 sq ft for the head table and bar when there's dancing, then the smallest standard size that clears the need. Total = tent + (tables × $12) + (chairs × $2) + (linens × $12) + (dance sq ft × $2) + delivery, with low and high estimates swapping in the ends of every band.

How to use it

Start with a realistic guest count, not the invitation list — expect 80-85% attendance at a backyard party. Say yes to the dance floor unless you've honestly never seen your crowd dance. Then compare the recommended size against the chart above; if you're between sizes and the yard prices them close, take the bigger tent. Tight tents are the number one regret in backyard event reviews.

A worked example, both ways

The 100-guest backyard reception: 100 guests seated at rounds need 1,200 sq ft, plus 300 sq ft of dance floor and 200 for the head table and bar = 1,700. That overflows a 40x40, so the calculator recommends the 40x60 at $1,400 typical. Stack: 10 rounds ($120), 100 chairs ($200), 10 linens ($120), 300 sq ft floor ($600), local delivery ($75). Total: $2,515 typical, range $1,765-$3,980.

The 30-guest graduation cookout: 30 × 12 = 360 sq ft, no dance floor, so a 20x20 ($325) covers it. Three rounds ($36), 30 chairs ($60), 3 linens ($36), delivery included by the yard down the road. Total: $457 typical ($330-$604). That's $15 a head for shade and a seat, which is why backyard parties stay popular.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent party supplies?

For a 100-guest backyard reception, expect about $2,515 typical for the rental stack: a 40x60 tent ($1,400 typical, $1,000-$2,000), 10 round tables ($120), 100 chairs ($200), 10 linens ($120), a 300 sq ft dance floor ($600), and delivery ($75). A 30-guest cookout with a 20x20 tent runs $457 typical ($330-$604). Most yards quote the tent, tables, and chairs as one order; linens and dance floors are where the bill climbs.

What size tent do I need for 100 guests?

Seated at 60-inch rounds, plan 10-12 square feet per guest, so 100 seated diners need about 1,200 sq ft. Add a dance floor at 2-4.5 sq ft per guest (300 sq ft typical) and a head table and bar push the total to roughly 1,700 sq ft, which points to a 40x60 tent (2,400 sq ft). A 40x40 fits 100 seated only if there's no dancing; most rental charts seat 160-200 under a 40x60 with room for the floor.

How much do table and chair rentals cost?

Chairs run $1.25-$3 each (resin, white or black), so 100 chairs cost about $200 typical. Round 60-inch tables rent for $8-$18 each (about $12 typical), and 8-foot banquet tables run $8-$15. A table, chair, and linen bundle is commonly quoted at $12-$20 per guest, which is worth comparing against item-by-item pricing.

How much is linen and dance floor rental?

Basic polyester linens rent for $6-$20 each (about $12 typical for a 120-inch round cloth); premium fabrics run $15-$40. Dance floors rent for roughly $1.50-$4 per square foot, so a 16x20 floor (320 sq ft) lands around $640 in many markets and full installs range $400-$2,000. Size the floor at 2-4.5 sq ft per guest: 100 guests means about 300 sq ft.

Is setup and delivery included in tent rental prices?

Sometimes. Many yards include delivery, setup, and teardown within a home radius (often 15-25 miles); others charge $50-$150 by distance. Ask two questions before booking: is staking possible on your surface (stakes on grass, weights or water barrels on pavement), and does the quote include teardown the same night or next morning. Budget $50-$300 extra if your town requires a tent permit.

When is renting a tent cheaper than booking a venue?

A full backyard setup for 100 guests runs about $2,500 in rentals. Venue rental alone often starts at $3,000-$6,000 for a Saturday night in mid-size markets, so the tent wins on price once you own (or can borrow) the yard. The tent loses its edge fast once you add restrooms, catering warmers needing power, and a rain plan: frame tents cost more than pole tents, and sidewalls or heaters add real money in shoulder season.
