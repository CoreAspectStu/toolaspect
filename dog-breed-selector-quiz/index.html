How the Dog Breed Quiz Works

Breed matching fails when it starts from the dog instead of the life. This quiz starts with you: seven questions covering the four factors that drive most surrendered dogs — size, energy, shedding, and household (kids, apartment, experience) — then scores all 34 breeds against your answers.

The scoring

Each breed carries ratings from 1 to 5 for size, energy, shedding, kid-friendliness, apartment suitability, novice-friendliness, and grooming needs. Your answers build the same profile, and the score blends the differences: size and energy mismatches cost 15 points per step of separation, shedding beyond your tolerance costs 20 per step, grooming beyond your willingness 10, and the household factors (kids, apartment, first dog) weigh the breed's ratings directly. Weights: 20% each for size, energy, and shedding; 15% kids; 10% each apartment and experience; 5% grooming.

How to use it

Answer honestly, not aspirationally. If your real day is one 30-minute walk, choose the moderate-energy answer — the dog you get has to fit the life you actually have. "Kids" means children under ten in the home. "Apartment" means no fenced yard. First-time owner means you've never had primary responsibility for a dog. When the results land, treat the top five as a shortlist to research, not an order form.

A worked example

A first-time owner in an apartment with young kids, wanting a small-to-medium dog (size 2), moderate energy (3), low shedding (tolerance 2), open to some grooming (3). The scoring runs: Boston Terrier matches at 98% — it's small (2), moderate energy (3), light shedding (2), 5/5 with kids, 5/5 in apartments, 4/5 for novices. Havanese follows at 97%, Miniature Poodle at 96%, French Bulldog 95%. The cost column flags the trade-offs: $750-1,500 a year for the Boston versus $800-1,600 for the Poodle, whose coat needs professional grooming every 4-6 weeks.

Flip the profile to an active experienced household with a yard, wanting a large athletic dog (size 4, energy 5, tolerant of shedding), and the quiz returns Golden Retriever and Labrador at 94%, with German Shepherd, Border Collie, and Australian Shepherd close behind. Same engine, different life, completely different kennel — which is the point.

Frequently Asked Questions

What dog breed is right for me?

The breed that matches your daily life, not the one you like photos of. Four factors do most of the work: size that fits your space, energy that matches your exercise routine (a 30-minute walk suits a Greyhound; a Border Collie needs hours), shedding you can live with, and kid-suitability if children are involved. Take the 7-question quiz for a ranked shortlist — for a first-time owner in an apartment with kids, it typically returns Boston Terrier, Havanese, and Miniature Poodle at 95%+ match.

What is the best dog breed for an apartment?

Surprisingly large breeds can work — Greyhounds are famous couch potatoes and score 4/5 for apartment suitability. The consistent winners are smaller, lower-energy, quieter types: Havanese, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Boston Terrier, Shih Tzu, Bichon Frise, and French Bulldog all rate 5/5. The real apartment disqualifiers are high energy and noise, not size: Huskies and most herding breeds (Border Collie, Australian Shepherd, Australian Cattle Dog) rate 1/5.

What is the best first dog for a beginner?

Forgiving, biddable breeds: the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel (5/5 novice rating), Havanese (5/5), Bichon Frise (4/5), Golden Retriever and Labrador (4/5), and Miniature or Standard Poodle (4/5). Avoid breeds that test a novice's limits on day one: Border Collie, Siberian Husky, Australian Cattle Dog, and Rottweiler all rate 1-2/5 for first-time owners because their intelligence turns into mischief without experienced handling.

How much does a dog cost per year?

National planning ranges for food, routine vet care, and supplies: roughly $500-$1,200 a year for a small breed like a Chihuahua or Maltese, $700-$1,600 for medium breeds, and $1,100-$3,200 for large and giant breeds like a Labrador or Great Dane. That excludes pet insurance (typically $600-$750 a year for accident-and-illness coverage), professional grooming, and emergencies, which run four figures — a knee surgery alone is commonly $3,500-$6,000.

Are there truly hypoallergenic dogs?

No dog is 100% hypoallergenic — allergies come from dander and saliva proteins, not fur length. But low-shedding breeds spread far less dander around the house. Poodles (all sizes), Bichon Frise, Maltese, and Havanese rate 1/5 on shedding and are the usual recommendations for allergy sufferers; heavy shedders like a Bernese Mountain Dog or Husky (5/5) are the worst choices. Spend time with the specific breed before committing if allergies are the driver.

Should I choose a breed or a rescue mix?

Both work if you match energy honestly. Purebred puppies give predictable size, coat, and temperament ranges, which matters in apartments and with kids. Rescues are cheaper up front, often already spayed and vaccinated, and adult rescues show their real personality — what you see is what you get. Many shelters perform the same lifestyle matching this quiz does, so bring your quiz results with you.
