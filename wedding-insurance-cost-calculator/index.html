How the Wedding Insurance Cost Calculator Works

Wedding insurance bundles two coverages that have almost nothing to do with each other. Liability protects the venue and your guests: if a guest trips over a speaker cable or a candle sets a drapery alight, the policy pays. Cancellation coverage protects your deposits: if the vendor goes under, the venue floods, or the mother of the bride lands in the hospital, you get the money back. Pricing them separately is how carriers quote, so it's how this calculator works.

The formula

Estimated premium = liability premium (fixed by limit tier) + cancellation premium (fixed by coverage tier) + liquor add-on ($75 typical) − 10% bundle discount when you carry both liability and cancellation. WedSafe advertises up to 15% off for bundling; 10% is a conservative middle. The range shown runs roughly 20% below to 25% above the typical figure, which brackets how much carriers and states vary.

How to use it

Set cancellation coverage to your total non-refundable commitments — venue, caterer, photographer deposits, attire, not the whole dream budget. Pick your liability limit from the venue's contract ($1M is the standard requirement; skip it only if your venue doesn't ask and you're comfortable self-insuring). Add the liquor add-on unless you're dry; hosts serving alcohol without it wear the liability personally.

A worked example

Take the average $34,200 wedding. Round the insured deposits to $30,000, add the $1M liability your venue contract requires, and include liquor: $280 (cancellation) + $140 (liability) + $75 (liquor) = $495. The bundle discount takes it to $445.50, or about 1.3% of the insured costs — one canceled vendor would repay the premium many times over.

The lean version matters too. A backyard wedding with $7,500 of deposits and a $500K liability requirement prices at roughly $130 + $100 = $230, less $23 with the discount: $207. Insurance for the price of two guest meals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does wedding insurance cost?

Wedding insurance runs $75 to $550 for most couples. Liability-only coverage (the kind venues require) starts at $75 to $100 and rarely exceeds $250. Cancellation coverage starts around $130 at the $7,500 level and scales to roughly $575 at $100,000 of coverage. Bundling both, most couples land between $200 and $350 total.

What does wedding insurance cover?

Two different coverages ride under one policy. Cancellation and postponement coverage reimburses lost deposits when a vendor goes bankrupt, the venue closes, extreme weather forces a change, or a key person (couple, immediate family, officiant) falls seriously ill or is military-deployed. Liability coverage pays when a guest is injured or property is damaged, and most venues require $1 million of it in your contract.

Does wedding insurance cover cold feet?

No. A change of heart is the standard exclusion on every wedding cancellation policy — carriers will not pay because one partner backs out. Coverage is for circumstances outside the couple's control, not a change of decision.

Does my venue require wedding insurance?

Many do, especially hotels, estates, and public spaces. The typical requirement is $1 million of event liability coverage naming the venue as an additional insured, and it exists because their own policy does not extend to your event. A liability-only policy at $75 to $185 satisfies almost every venue clause.

When should I buy wedding insurance?

Buy cancellation coverage as soon as you start paying non-refundable deposits — the policy needs to be in force before a covered loss, not after. Some carriers require the policy to be purchased at least two weeks before the wedding, so don't leave it to the final week. Liability coverage can be added any time before the event.

Does wedding insurance cover illness or a pandemic?

Serious illness of the couple or immediate family is a covered reason on standard cancellation policies, usually with documentation. Broad pandemic or government-mandated shutdown coverage is generally excluded — if a public-health order closes your venue, some policies pay only if the venue itself is unavailable, so read the exclusions before buying.
