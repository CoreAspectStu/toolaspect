About this keyboard test

This tool listens to keydown events in your browser and highlights the matching key on a visual QWERTY layout. A key that lights up every time you press it is electrically healthy; a key that never lights up is likely dead, and one that flickers or double-fires may have a worn switch. The counter tracks how many unique keys you have verified, and repeated presses of an already-tested key get a softer highlight so you can spot chatter (unwanted repeated signals).

While the test is active, keys like Tab, Escape, Space and arrow keys are intercepted so the page does not move focus or scroll away — your keystrokes stay in the test area. Nothing you type is stored or transmitted; the page only reads key codes, never text fields.

Tips for a full keyboard check

Work across each row left to right, then test both Shift keys, both Ctrl keys, Alt, Enter, Backspace and the space bar — these are the most-used keys and the most likely to wear out first. If a key only fails sometimes, press it 20–30 times in a row; intermittent faults show up quickly under repetition. For laptop keyboards, a dead key after a spill is often residue under the keycap rather than a broken switch, and may be fixable with careful cleaning.
