How the Crown Molding Calculator Works

Enter your room dimensions or measured wall perimeter, the number of inside and outside corners, and your molding stock length. Because coped inside corners eat extra material — you cut away a coping profile and often lose a foot or more per joint — the calculator lets you set a separate waste percentage that should run 15–20% for coped work versus 10% for simple mitered outside corners. It returns total linear feet to buy, stick count, and cost.

Formulas Used

  • Perimeter: 2 × (length + width)
  • Buying LF: perimeter × (1 + waste%)
  • Sticks: ceil(buying LF ÷ stock length)
  • Spring angle: the angle between the molding back and the wall — set your miter saw bevel/crown stops to the matching spring angle so the profile nests flat against wall and ceiling

Understanding Spring Angle

Crown molding installs sprung between wall and ceiling, touching each at a different angle. The spring angle is measured off the wall: most colonial and ogee profiles are 38°/52° (38° against the wall, 52° against the ceiling), while many contemporary and cove profiles are 45°/45°. When cutting crown lying flat in a miter saw, you tilt and rotate using these angles (for 38° crown: bevel 33.9°, miter 31.6°); when cutting it nested against the saw fence, just set the miter to 45° for a 90° corner and let the saw's crown stops hold the spring angle.

Worked Example

A 16 × 12 room: perimeter = 2 × (16 + 12) = 56 LF. With 4 coped inside corners and 15% waste, buy 56 × 1.15 = 64.4 → 65 LF. In 12-ft sticks that's 6 pieces (72 LF on hand). At $3.50/LF: about $225 in molding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is crown molding waste so high? Coped inside corners require cutting a reverse profile with a coping saw, and each corner typically consumes an extra 6–12" of stock. Short offcuts also pile up fast on long profiles.

Cope or miter inside corners? Always cope inside corners on anything but a perfectly square, never-moving room — copes absorb seasonal wood movement and stay tight; miters open up.

What miter angle for crown corners? Nested in the saw at the correct spring angle, a standard 90° corner is a 45° miter. For 38° crown cut flat (bevel-and-miter method): 31.6° miter / 33.9° bevel.

How do I measure for crown? Measure each wall independently at the ceiling, wall to wall. Never scale up from a perimeter if the room isn't rectangular — bay windows and jogged walls change everything.
