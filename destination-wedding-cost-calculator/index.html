How the Destination Wedding Calculator Works

A destination wedding quote covers the package. Your card covers the package, plus legal paperwork, plus your own flights and room nights, plus the photographer, plus the party. And underneath it all, every guest is quietly buying a $1,300 vacation. This calculator prices both views: what you sign, and what the whole event actually costs.

The formula

Package = tier base + max(0, guests − included) × overage rate. Couple's travel = 2 × airfare + room nights × nightly rate. Legal, photographer, welcome events, and welcome bags (× guests) stack on top. Per-guest cost = airfare + 3 room nights at a $280 group rate; true total = couple's total + guests × per-guest.

How to use it

Start with the destination — it presets legal fees, airfare, and nightly rates; overwrite any of them with real quotes. Pick the package tier closest to your resort's menu (the tier table below shows what each typically bundles). Guests means the count you actually expect; only 40–60% of invited guests usually attend a destination wedding, so invite generously but budget realistically.

A worked example

Jamaica, standard package, 30 guests, resort photographer, $800 welcome party, $500 flights, 5 nights at $350.

Package: $4,600 with all 30 guests included — no overage. Legal and documents: $325. Flights for two: $1,000. Room: 5 × $350 = $1,750. Photographer $1,200, welcome party $800, welcome bags $8 × 30 = $240. Couple's total: $9,915.

Now the other ledger. Each guest pays roughly $500 airfare plus 3 nights at a $280 group rate = $1,340; 30 guests together contribute $40,200. True total: $50,115 — five times what the couple's contract shows. Upgrade to the premium package with a flown-in photographer and 50 guests and the couple's bill alone reaches $22,975, with a true total just under $90,000. The cheap-looking wedding never is; it's just split across more credit cards.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a destination wedding cost?

Couples typically spend $9,000-$25,000 total on Caribbean and Mexico destination weddings, with many landing near $10,000: a $4,600 resort package for 30 guests, $325 in legal fees, $1,000 in flights, five room nights, a photographer, and a welcome event. That is against roughly $33,000-$36,000 for the average US wedding. But guests pay their own way at a destination wedding, so the full cost across everyone runs $50,000+ for 30 guests.

Is a destination wedding cheaper than a local wedding?

For the couple, usually yes — guest lists shrink (40-60% attend on average), resort packages bundle venue, food, decor, and coordination, and many resorts comp or credit the package against room-night minimums. The catch is you are also paying your own travel, room nights, and legal documentation, and premium packages with flown-in photographers can push past $20,000, closing the gap.

What do guests pay for a destination wedding?

Typically $1,100-$1,700 each: airfare ($300-$600), three to four nights at group rates ($180-$400 a night all-inclusive), plus transfers and extras. At $1,340 a head — a realistic Jamaica number — 30 guests collectively contribute $40,200. That is why the true total of a destination wedding usually dwarfs what the couple signs for.

What are the legal requirements to marry in Mexico or Jamaica?

Jamaica is the easy one: 24 hours on the island, a license around US$75 arranged through your planner or the Registrar General's Department, certified birth certificates, no blood test, no apostille. Mexico requires a civil ceremony by a judge (religious-only ceremonies are not legally binding), apostilled and translated birth certificates, a blood test in some states, and witnesses — figure $650-$1,000 all-in with translations. Many couples do the paperwork at home and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad.

What hidden costs come with resort wedding packages?

Per-guest overage fees once you exceed the package's included headcount ($25-$130 each), room-night minimums before the package is comped, vendor travel and resort subvendor fees, private-event dinner upcharges ($40-$90 per person to buy out a restaurant), and documented legal steps if you want the marriage valid at home. Read the package's inclusions table line by line before booking.
