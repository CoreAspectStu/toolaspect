How the Topsoil Calculator Works

Enter the area you need to cover and the depth of topsoil, and this calculator returns cubic yards — the unit landscape suppliers sell by — plus the equivalent in 2 ft³ bags and 40 lb bags for the bagged-product comparison, and a delivered cost estimate. A compaction allowance is included because loose-screened topsoil settles: what looks like 3 inches fluffs down to 2-1/2 after rain and raking, so most pros order 10% extra.

Formulas Used

  • Volume: ft³ = length × width × (depth ÷ 12)
  • Cubic yards: yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27
  • 2 ft³ bags: bags = ceil(ft³ ÷ 2) — typical bagged topsoil/compost size
  • 40 lb bags: bags = ceil(ft³ ÷ 0.75) — a 40 lb bag of topsoil yields ≈ 0.75 ft³
  • With compaction: order yd³ × (1 + waste%)

Worked Example

Topdressing a 20 × 20 ft lawn at 3 inches: 20 × 20 × 0.25 = 100 ft³ = 3.7 yd³. With 10% compaction, order 4 yd³. In bags that same 100 ft³ is fifty 2 ft³ bags or about 134 forty-pound bags — at bulk prices around $30/yd³ delivered, the yard wins decisively once you need more than about a yard.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep should topsoil be? Topdressing an existing lawn: 1/4–1/2 in. New lawn from sod: 4–6 in of good topsoil. Garden beds: 6–12 in. Grass seed over bare subsoil needs at least 3–4 in.

How much does a yard of topsoil weigh? Screened topsoil runs about 2,000–2,400 lb per cubic yard depending on moisture — a full-size pickup safely carries about one yard.

How many bags equal a yard? One cubic yard = 27 ft³ = about 36 bags of 0.75 ft³ (40 lb) or 13.5 bags of 2 ft³. Bagged soil costs 3–5× bulk per yard; buy bags only for small patches.

Topsoil, garden soil, or compost? Topsoil is the structural base; blend 2–3 inches of compost into it for beds. 100% compost as a lawn topdressing is too rich — mix 50/50.
