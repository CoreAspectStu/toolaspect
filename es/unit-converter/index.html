Preguntas frecuentes

¿Cuántos centímetros hay en una pulgada?

Exactamente 2,54: la pulgada ha sido definida como 2,54 cm por acuerdo internacional desde 1959. Doce pulgadas son exactamente 30,48 cm y un pie es exactamente 0,3048 metros.

¿Cómo convierto kilogramos a libras?

Multiplica por 2,20462 (o divide las libras por 0,45359237, que es la definición exacta de libra en kilogramos). 10 kg ≈ 22,05 libras.

¿Cuál es la fórmula de Celsius a Fahrenheit?

°F = °C × 9/5 + 32. Entonces 20 °C = 68 °F. Invierte con °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. A -40 grados las dos escalas marcan el mismo número.

¿Es lo mismo un galón estadounidense que un galón británico?

No. Un galón estadounidense equivale aproximadamente a 3,785 litros; un galón imperial (Reino Unido) equivale aproximadamente a 4,546 litros. Las onzas líquidas estadounidenses e imperiales también difieren (29,57 ml frente a 28,41 ml). Confirme siempre qué sistema utiliza la fuente.

Acerca del convertidor de unidades

Unit conversion looks trivial until you meet it in the wild: a European recipe in grams, a torque spec in newton-meters on a car built for foot-pounds, a shipping weight in kilograms on a runway measured in feet. This converter handles the everyday families — length, weight, volume, temperature, area, speed — with exact conversion factors, so the output is the same number you would get from a reference table, not a rounded approximation of it.

The foundation of modern conversion is exactness. Since 1959, the international yard and pound agreement has defined 1 yard as exactly 0.9144 meter and 1 pound as exactly 0.45359237 kilogram, which makes every imperial–metric conversion in length and mass a matter of multiplying a fixed rational number. Volume units trace to the liter (1 L = 0.001 m³) with the US gallon defined as exactly 231 cubic inches. Temperature is the odd one out: it converts by formula, not factor, because the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales have different zero points.

Lo que esto significa en la práctica es que un convertidor puede y debe ser exacto. A continuación se muestran los factores exactos o estándar que utiliza la herramienta, los mismos que publican los institutos nacionales de metrología como el NIST en su publicación especial sobre conversión métrica.

Using it is simple: pick a category, choose the units on each side, and type a value — the result updates instantly, and you can flip the direction with one click. Because the factors are exact, converting back and forth loses no precision, which matters for recipes, medication doses, engineering specs, and customs paperwork where a rounded conversion can compound into a real error.

One caution: US and imperial (UK) units diverge where volume is concerned. A US gallon is 3.785 L; an imperial gallon is 4.546 L. A US fluid ounce is about 29.57 mL; an imperial one about 28.41 mL. Recipes and fuel economy figures are the classic victims — always confirm which system the source used before converting.

Significant figures deserve a mention too. A factor may be exact, but your input rarely is: converting 150 pounds to kilograms gives 68.0388555 kg on paper, yet a bathroom scale reading of 150 lb justifies no more than three significant figures of output. Report what the measurement supports, not everything the arithmetic produces — the tool gives you the exact number and leaves that judgment to you.

Una nota sobre la notación: el convertidor acepta decimales y fracciones simples donde las unidades se escriben tradicionalmente de forma fraccionaria, como 5,5 o 5 1/2 pulgadas en carpintería.

Temperatura: el caso de la fórmula

Temperature cannot be converted by a single factor because the scales do not share a zero. The exact relations are: °F = °C × 9/5 + 32, and K = °C + 273.15. The 273.15 is exact by definition of the kelvin. Water freezes at 0 °C / 32 °F / 273.15 K and boils at 100 °C / 212 °F at standard pressure; −40 is the single point where Celsius and Fahrenheit numerically agree — a handy fact for checking any temperature converter.

Dónde realmente duelen los errores de conversión

History's memorable unit mistakes are instructive. The 1999 Mars Climate Orbiter was lost because one team used pound-force seconds and another newton-seconds — a factor of about 4.45 between software systems. Medicine dosing errors between milligrams and grains, and aviation fuel miscalculations between kilograms and pounds (the 1983 Gimli Glider incident), show the same lesson: the arithmetic is easy, but the discipline of naming the unit before converting is what prevents the error. Type the number, confirm both units, then trust the answer.
