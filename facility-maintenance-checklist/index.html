How the Checklist Generator Works

Choose the systems your building actually has and the generator assembles a checklist grouped by frequency — weekly, monthly, quarterly, semiannual, annual, and multi-year — with the governing standard noted on each line. Facility type swaps in a few space-specific tasks: dock doors for warehouses, playground equipment for schools, automatic entrances for retail.

How to use it

Put the printed sheet on a clipboard that lives where the work happens. Date and initial each line as it's done; a checklist nobody signs is a list, not a record. Weekly and monthly rounds suit in-house staff; the annual and multi-year rows are mostly contractor visits — schedule them at the start of the year rather than when something fails. The CSV export drops straight into a CMMS or spreadsheet if you track work orders digitally.

A worked example

A default office building with all seven systems switched on generates 48 tasks: 3 weekly, 11 monthly, 8 quarterly, 5 semiannual, 17 annual, and 4 multi-year. Switching the type to a warehouse adds the quarterly dock-door and leveler round (49 tasks); a retail store adds refrigeration and automatic-door checks (50); a school swaps in playground and rigging inspections (50). The weekly round is about ten minutes — flush the eyewash, eyeball unsupervised sprinkler valves, check the diesel fire pump if one exists. The monthly round is a 30-60 minute walk if nothing turns up. The annual bucket is where budgets live: sprinkler ITM, fire alarm test, extinguisher maintenance, 90-minute battery test, backflow tests and the generator load test together typically run a few thousand dollars a year for a mid-size building, and skipping them trades a predictable bill for an unpredictable one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often do fire extinguishers need to be inspected?

NFPA 10 requires a visual inspection when an extinguisher is placed in service and at least monthly after that — anyone can do it, checking access, pressure gauge, tamper seal and condition. A certified technician must do a full maintenance check annually, stored-pressure dry chemical units need an internal teardown every 6 years, and most types need hydrostatic retest every 12 years.

How often is emergency lighting tested?

A 30-second functional test every month and a full 90-minute test at least annually, per NFPA 101 (section 7.9). The monthly check catches dead batteries and burned-out lamps; the annual test proves the batteries can actually carry the load for the code-required duration. Write down the date and result each time — the log is what an inspector asks for.

What does NFPA 25 require for sprinkler systems?

The common cadence: gauges on wet systems read monthly (dry and preaction weekly), control valves checked weekly when unsupervised, waterflow alarms tested quarterly, a full inspection and main drain test annually, and an internal pipe examination plus gauge replacement every 5 years. Your water supply and occupancies can add requirements, so the annual ITM visit with a licensed fire protection contractor is the anchor.

How often should HVAC filters be changed?

Check monthly and expect to replace them every 1 to 3 months in most commercial buildings — the right interval depends on filter depth (2-inch and 4-inch filters run longer than 1-inch pleats), MERV rating, and how dirty the space is. A filter that has gray, matted media is overdue regardless of the calendar.

What should a facility maintenance checklist include?

The durable spine is life safety: extinguishers, emergency and exit lighting, sprinklers, alarms, fire doors, and eyewash stations on their code-driven schedules. Around that sits the mechanical plant — filters, belts, coils, boilers, cooling towers — then electrical (generator runs, IR scans), plumbing (backflow tests, water heaters), and the envelope (roof, drains, sealant). Group tasks by frequency so the same person can knock out a whole monthly round in one walk.
