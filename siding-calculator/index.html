About the Siding Calculator

This siding calculator converts your home's wall dimensions into squares — the siding trade's unit — and adds a waste factor so you order enough without a pallet of leftovers. It handles gables, openings, and the main rectangular walls. Everything computes in your browser.

Siding estimating has one core conversion (square feet to squares), one core adjustment (waste factor), and a handful of details — soffits, trim, starter strip — that separate a clean order from a frustrating one.

How Siding Quantities Are Calculated

One square of siding covers 100 square feet. Wall area for each face is width × height; gables add half the base × gable rise (a triangle). Total the faces, subtract openings only partially — doors and windows create cutting waste that often cancels what they remove — then divide by 100 and multiply by your waste factor.

Waste factors run 5 to 10 percent for simple single-story rectangles with few openings, 10 to 15 percent for two-story or homes with many windows, and 15 to 20 percent for complex layouts with diagonal cuts and multiple material transitions. Trim, Accessories, and Underlayment

The accessory list matters as much as the field material: starter strip along the base, J-channel at windows, doors, and inside corners, corner posts outside, drip cap, and fascia/soffit stock. A practical rule is one accessory square for every three field squares when starting out — the mix refines with each elevation drawing.

Behind the siding, house wrap (weather-resistant barrier) covers the sheathing at roughly the same area as the walls, sold in 9- or 10-foot × 100-foot rolls (covering 900 or 1,000 square feet per roll with overlap). Rigid foam insulation board, if used, adds its own square count at whatever thickness your climate zone requires.

Material Choices at a Glance

Vinyl is the least expensive and most common US residential siding, chosen for low maintenance rather than authenticity. Fiber cement offers fire resistance and paintability with wood-like profiles at a higher installed cost. Engineered wood hits a middle price with good durability if kept painted. Natural wood demands the most upkeep. Metal panel systems dominate commercial and modern residential work.

Whichever material you choose, buy all field material from a single production run — dye lots vary visibly in vinyl and fiber cement — and store it flat, dry, and cool until installation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate how many squares of siding I need?

Total each wall's width × height, add gable triangles (half base × rise), keep most openings un-subtracted to cover cutting waste, then divide by 100. A 2,000 sq ft wall area is 20 squares; add 10 to 15 percent before ordering.

How much waste should I add for siding?

Use 10 percent for simple one-story layouts, 10 to 15 percent for two-story homes or many windows, and up to 20 percent for complex elevations with diagonal cuts. Each window and door intersection creates offcuts that waste factor absorbs.

Should I subtract windows and doors from siding area?

Only partially or not at all. The cutting waste around openings usually consumes nearly what the opening saves. Many estimators keep the full wall area and let the waste factor cover openings — you'll have usable offcuts, not a shortage.

What is a square of siding?

A square is 100 square feet of wall coverage — the standard unit for ordering siding, matching roofing's convention. A house with 2,400 square feet of wall area needs 24 squares plus waste.

How much house wrap do I need?

House wrap coverage roughly equals wall area: a 1,000 sq ft roll covers a 1,000 sq ft house less overlap, so figure one roll per 900 to 1,000 square feet of wall. Seal seams with manufacturer tape at penetrations and openings.
