How the Elopement Cost Calculator Works

An elopement is a wedding with the guest list deleted. What's left is a short stack of vendor lines, and because none of them scale with headcount, the total stays in four digits while the average wedding runs to five. This calculator itemizes the stack so you can see which lines carry the day.

The formula

Tick the lines you're buying, set each to low, typical, or high, and the calculator sums the selected tier values. Travel & lodging and the all-inclusive package are off by default: the first applies to destination elopements, the second replaces the officiant, photographer, florals, and location lines (uncheck those if you take the package). The comparison row divides your total into the $34,200 average wedding to show savings and percentage.

How to use it

Start from the typical column and adjust the lines you care about. Most couples protect the photographer line — the images are what survives the day — and save on location (public land) and attire (non-bridal). If you're weighing a package, check the package row — the four lines it replaces (officiant, photographer, florals, location) dim and drop out of the total automatically, so the comparison is honest: at typical tiers the itemized stack is $1,350 against a $3,500 package, so the package price buys hours, deliverables, and coordination, not a discount.

A worked example

A couple marries at a state park: $60 license, $250 officiant, $800 photographer for two hours, a $200 special-use permit, $500 attire, $100 bouquet, $150 hair and makeup, $200 dinner. Total: $2,260 — $31,940 under the average wedding, 6.6% of it. Slide every line to high — a $2,500 photographer, $1,500 attire, $600 venue fee — and the total reaches $6,175, still under a fifth of the average. Add travel and lodging at $500 and the destination version totals $2,760 at typical tiers.

The courthouse floor is lower still: a $60 license plus a $100 civil ceremony fee and dinner is a few hundred dollars all-in. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to elope?

A bare courthouse or mountain-top elopement runs $150-$2,500 once the license, a modest officiant fee, and a celebration dinner are counted. A typical full elopement with a photographer, celebrant, florals, and attire lands in The Knot's $5,000-$10,000 range. Destination elopements with travel and lodging run above it. The average traditional US wedding, for comparison, was $34,200 in The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study.

Is eloping cheaper than a wedding?

Usually by 70-90%. The itemized middle-of-the-road elopement — license, officiant, two-hour photographer, permit, attire, florals, hair and makeup, dinner for two — totals around $2,260, which is about 93% less than the $34,200 average wedding. Even a top-of-band elopement with travel ($7,000 or so) comes in at roughly a fifth of the average wedding, mostly because the guest-count-driven lines (venue, catering per head, bar) disappear.

What does an elopement package include?

Most packages bundle an officiant, a photographer for 1-3 hours, florals, and help with location permits; many add hair and makeup or a picnic dinner. Published package bands run about $1,500-$8,000 depending on hours, images delivered, and destination. A package competes with roughly $1,350 of separately-booked typical-tier vendors (officiant, two-hour photographer, florals, location), so compare inclusions hour by hour before paying for convenience.

Do you need witnesses to elope?

Most US states require one or two adult witnesses at the ceremony, though a handful require none. Elopement officiants, venues, and photographers routinely serve as witnesses for free or a small tip ($20-$50 each), and some states let the officiant's staff witness. Rules and license fees vary by state — confirm with the county clerk where you will marry. This calculator is a budgeting tool, not legal advice.

What is the cheapest way to elope?

A courthouse ceremony: a $25-$125 license (state-dependent), a $25-$100 civil ceremony fee where charged, and dinner after. That's $100-$400 all-in before attire and rings. Free public land — many national forests allow small ceremonies without a permit or with a $50-$200 special-use permit — keeps the location line near zero. The photographer is usually the first line couples refuse to cut, at $400-$800 for a short shoot.

Can you elope now and have a reception later?

Yes, and plenty of couples do exactly that: marry privately, then host a party months later. The costs shift rather than vanish — a per-guest reception still runs near the $292-per-guest average — but you control the guest list and skip the ceremony-day stack of venue, officiant-on-site, and full-day photography.
