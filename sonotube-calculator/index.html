How the Sonotube Calculator Works

Sonotubes (concrete form tubes) create round piers for decks, pergolas, sign posts, and pole barns. Enter the tube diameter, the pour height, and how many piers you need — the calculator returns the concrete volume of one tube using the cylinder formula πr²h, the total for all tubes in cubic feet, cubic yards for the ready-mix order, and the 80 lb bag count if you're mixing by hand. A waste factor covers spillage and over-dig at the bell of the hole.

Formulas Used

  • Volume per tube: V (ft³) = π × (diameter ÷ 24)² × height — radius in feet
  • Total: ft³ × number of tubes × (1 + waste%)
  • Cubic yards: yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27
  • 80 lb bags: bags = ceil(ft³ ÷ 0.6) — each bag yields ≈ 0.6 ft³

Worked Example

A 12 in diameter tube poured 4 ft deep: π × 0.5² × 4 = 3.14 ft³ per tube. Six footings = 18.8 ft³, and with 10% waste that is 20.7 ft³ = 0.77 yd³ — order 1 yd³ from the mini-mix truck or mix 35 eighty-pound bags (a strong argument for the truck). Remember to include any flared footing bell below the tube.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much concrete in a 12 inch sonotube per foot? Exactly π × 0.25 × 1 = 0.785 ft³ per vertical foot — roughly 1.3 eighty-pound bags per foot of height. An 8 in tube uses 0.35 ft³/ft; a 10 in uses 0.55.

How deep should footings go? Below the frost line for your region (from 12 in in warm climates to 48+ in up north) and into undisturbed bearing soil. Check your local building department's frost depth map.

Do I need rebar in sonotubes? Most decks call for two #4 vertical bars in each pier, or a post anchor embedded in the concrete. Tall or heavily loaded piers may require full cages — follow plans.

Can I pour above grade? Yes — extend the tube 6 in above grade to keep post bases out of soil moisture, and backfill around the protruding tube.
