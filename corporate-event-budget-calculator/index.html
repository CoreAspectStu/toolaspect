How the Corporate Event Budget Calculator Works

Corporate event costs are quoted two unhelpful ways: a giant total from an event-planning deck, or a per-person price from a venue that covers nothing but the room. The workable unit is the per-attendee stack — every line priced per head, then multiplied by headcount. That's how finance teams sanity-check event spend, and how you spot the line that's out of band.

The formula

Each event type loads a per-person stack: venue, food and beverage, bar or breaks, entertainment or facilitation, AV, transport, lodging and flights for travel events, swag, and planning coordination. Venue and F&B scale with your market tier; everything else is flat per head. Subtotal = stack × attendees. Total = subtotal × (1 + contingency).

How to use it

Choose the event type closest to yours — the presets are mid-range figures, so treat them as a starting grid, not a quote. Set headcount honestly, including plus-ones for a holiday party if you're inviting them; per-person costs assume they eat and drink like everyone else. Once real quotes arrive, replace the whole-line approach: the catering calculator prices F&B by service style, and the hotel room block tool handles lodging attrition clauses.

A worked example

Holiday party for 120 employees, standard metro, 10% contingency. The stack: venue and setup $30, catering $55, bar package $25, entertainment $12, decor $8, AV $6, transportation $6, swag $6, planning coordination $12 — $160 per person. Subtotal: 120 × $160 = $19,200. Contingency: $1,920. Total: $21,120, or $176 a head all-in. That sits just above ezCater's $75–$150 standard band once the bar, transport, and coordination lines are included — which is the point of a stack: the published band usually covers food and venue only.

Compare the offsite mode: 50 people, two nights, flights included. $1,950 per person × 50 = $97,500, plus contingency = $107,250. The travel lines ($700 lodging + $500 flights) are 61% of the stack, which is why hybrid schedules — one travel day, two meeting days — get popular when budgets tighten.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a company spend on a holiday party per person?

The industry standard runs $75-$150 per person for an evening reception (ezCater), and a common rule of thumb is $75-$200 per employee, with seated dinners costing more than receptions because of staffing ratios. A realistic planning stack for 120 employees lands near $21,120: about $160 a head covering venue, catering, a bar package, entertainment, decor, AV, transport, swag, and coordination, plus a 10% contingency.

How much does a company offsite cost per person?

Multi-day offsites typically run $2,000-$3,000 per person all-in (BoomPop's 2025 budgeting guide), driven by flights ($500), lodging ($700 for two nights), food and beverage (about $300), meeting space, AV, and a team activity. A 50-person, two-night offsite prices out around $107,250 with contingency. One-day versions avoid the travel lines and run closer to $290 a head.

What percentage should be contingency in an event budget?

Add 5-10% to any event budget, leaning toward the high end for custom production, outdoor venues, or first-time vendors. Food and beverage is the line that blows up first - meeting F&B costs rose about 6% in 2025, the steepest line in corporate event budgets, while overall meeting costs rose 4.3%.

How do you budget for team building?

Plan roughly $65-$380 per employee for a team-building event (2025 planning bands), with facilitated workshops and off-site activities at the top of the range. A typical one-day session for 40 people runs about $115 a head - activity or facilitation, lunch, materials, transport, swag - or $5,060 with a 10% contingency. Many companies cap total corporate entertainment (holiday party plus outings) around $200 per employee per year.

What is the biggest cost line in corporate events?

Food and beverage, consistently. It's the steepest-rising line in 2025 (+6%), and for a one-day meeting it runs about $110 of the roughly $290 per-person stack. Venue comes second, then AV and production - the line that surprises first-time planners, since full production for a conference adds $120 a head or more.
