How the Plastic Surgery Cost Calculator Works

Every surgical cosmetic bill is really three bills stapled together: the surgeon's fee, the facility fee, and the anesthesia fee. Quotes that look wildly different usually differ in which of the three they include. This calculator starts from national total-cost ranges and adjusts them for the three levers that actually move your number.

The formula

Estimate = national range for your procedure × state price level × surgeon tier multiplier × facility multiplier + extras. The multipliers: budget surgeons run about 12% under the average board-certified price, while highly sought-after surgeons run about 15% over; office-based operating rooms price below accredited surgery centers, and hospital operating rooms run roughly 25% above baseline. Extras — pre-op labs, compression garments, prescriptions — default to $300.

How to use it

Choose your procedure and the state price band that matches where you'd actually have surgery. Be honest about the surgeon tier: "highly sought-after" is the surgeon with a three-month waitlist and a strong revision record, not merely the most expensive. Then compare the low and high estimates against written all-in quotes — a quote below the low estimate is either a surgeon-fee-only number or a corner being cut.

A worked example

Breast augmentation in California with a highly sought-after surgeon at an accredited surgery center: $6,500-$12,000 national range × 1.20 state × 1.15 surgeon = $8,970-$16,560, plus $300 of extras — call it $9,270 to $16,860 all-in. The same procedure in Texas with an average-demand board-certified surgeon in an office-based OR: $6,500-$12,000 × 0.96 × 1.00 × 0.92 = $5,741-$10,598 plus extras. Same implants, a ceiling about a third lower.

And a warning worth repeating: the budget tier exists in this calculator because it exists in the market, not because we recommend it. For BBLs especially — the highest-risk routine cosmetic procedure — surgeon selection is the one variable that shouldn't be optimized for price.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does plastic surgery cost?

National total-cost ranges (surgeon + facility + anesthesia): breast augmentation $6,500-$12,000, rhinoplasty $5,000-$15,000, liposuction $3,500-$9,000 per area, tummy tuck $8,000-$15,000, facelift $9,000-$20,000, eyelid surgery $3,500-$7,000, breast lift $6,500-$12,000, breast reduction $7,000-$13,000, BBL $6,000-$14,000, and mommy makeover $10,000-$25,000. Where you live moves the number as much as 20% in either direction.

Why do plastic surgery prices vary so much between surgeons?

Three fees hide inside every quote: the surgeon's fee (varies with experience and demand), the facility fee (hospital versus accredited surgery center versus office OR), and anesthesia (typically $600-$1,000 per hour). A budget office-based quote and a hospital quote from a sought-after surgeon can differ by 2x for the same procedure — and the cheap end of the range is where most of the horror stories come from.

Does insurance ever cover plastic surgery?

Only when the procedure treats a functional problem: breast reduction for documented back and shoulder pain, eyelid surgery blocking vision, septoplasty inside a rhinoplasty, or reconstruction after trauma or cancer. Purely cosmetic procedures are excluded. Even 'covered' cases often require photos, physical therapy records, and prior authorization.

What is the cheapest safe way to pay less for plastic surgery?

Choose an accredited surgery center over a hospital operating room (the facility fee drops meaningfully), pick a mid-market metro instead of a coastal one, and use a board-certified surgeon — never cut that corner. Surgery tourism abroad can cost half as much and carries its own revision and complication economics; if something goes wrong, your local surgeon bills you full price to repair it, and that money was never in the vacation quote.

What fees are usually NOT in the headline price?

Pre-operative labs and clearance ($100-$400), post-surgical garments ($50-$150), prescriptions ($50-$200), follow-up visits (usually included for the first year), and any revision surgery. Some quotes bundle anesthesia and facility; others quote only the surgeon's fee, which can be barely half the real total. Always ask for the all-in number in writing.

How much does breast augmentation cost in California?

Applying California's typical price level (about 1.2x the national band) to the $6,500-$12,000 national range gives roughly $7,800-$14,400 before surgeon tier and facility add-ons. A highly sought-after surgeon operating in a hospital can push past $17,000; an average board-certified surgeon in an accredited surgery center lands near $8,100-$14,700 with extras.
