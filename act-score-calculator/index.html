How the ACT Score Calculator Works

The ACT reports every section on a 1-36 scale, and your composite is nothing more exotic than their average, rounded to the nearest whole number. The rounding detail matters at the margin: a 27.5 average becomes a 28, because ACT rounds halves up. Sub-scores and the optional Writing test never touch the composite.

The formula

Composite = round((English + Math + Reading + Science) ÷ 4) on the classic test, with halves rounding up. On the enhanced format the average runs over English, Math, and Reading only, since Science moved out of the composite in 2025. Section scores themselves come from equating: your raw count of correct answers converts to the 1-36 scale through a table calibrated for your specific test form.

How to use it

Pick your format and enter either scaled section scores or raw correct counts. In raw mode the calculator converts through a representative recent scale; in enhanced-format raw mode it maps your percentages onto that scale, which makes those section scores estimates rather than conversions. The benchmark flags under each section update live.

A worked example

Scores of 29, 25, 27, and 24 sum to 105, and 105 ÷ 4 = 26.25, which rounds to a composite of 26. That lands around the 82nd percentile nationally. Now watch the rounding rule earn you a point: 30, 28, 27, and 25 average exactly 27.5, and ACT rounds that up to 28, near the 89th percentile.

Raw mode works the same way one step earlier. Say you count 60 of 75 right in English, 41 of 60 in Math, 30 of 40 in Reading, and 26 of 40 in Science. On the representative scale that's 24, 24, 28, and 25, averaging 25.25 for a composite of 25. On your actual form those raw counts might scale a point differently in either direction, which is the honest uncertainty in any pre-official estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the ACT composite score calculated?

The composite is the average of your section scores rounded to the nearest whole number, with .5 rounding up. A student scoring 29, 25, 27, and 24 averages 26.25 for a composite of 26. Each section is scored 1-36 through equating, which converts your raw correct answers to a scaled score calibrated for that test form.

What is a good ACT score?

The national average composite has run about 19.5-20 in recent graduating classes. A 20 clears the average, a 24 lands near the 75th percentile, a 28 puts you around the top 10 percent of testers, and 30 or better sits in roughly the top 5 percent. The right target depends on the middle-50 percent range at colleges you're considering.

Is the ACT curved?

No, it's equated. A curve would set your score against everyone testing that day. Equating calibrates each form's raw-to-scaled conversion in advance so a given raw score earns the same scaled score regardless of when you test or how the country performs. That's why the conversion table moves a couple of raw points between test dates.

How many questions can I miss and still get a 25?

On a recent representative form, a 25 in English allowed about 61-62 of 75 correct (13-14 missed), in Math about 44-45 of 60 (15-16 missed), in Reading about 25 of 40 (15 missed), and in Science about 26 of 40 (14 missed). Exact allowances shift by a few questions between forms because of equating.

What changed with the enhanced ACT in 2025?

The ACT rolled out a shorter format through 2025, with online national dates first and all testers moved over in fall 2025. Sections have fewer questions, and Science became optional: the composite now averages only English, Math, and Reading, with Science reported separately (paired with Math for a STEM score). The 1-36 scale and the round-average composite rule did not change.

Do ACT section scores matter separately?

Yes. Colleges see every section score alongside the composite, and placement decisions often key off a single section. ACT's published College Readiness Benchmarks, the scores tied to a decent shot at passing first-year credit courses, are 18 in English, 22 in Math, 22 in Reading, and 23 in Science.
