How the Wedding Hashtag Generator Works

A good wedding hashtag does one job: it lets every guest's photos pile up in a single searchable feed. Getting there is mostly arithmetic on names, which is what this tool automates across four styles.

The method

Mashups split each surname at its last syllable and graft one name onto the other's ending — Smith takes Johnson's "son" and becomes #Smithson; Starling + Moss cross-breed into #Mossling. Classics keep both names intact (#SmithJohnson, #TheSmiths, #MeetTheJohnsons) with proper plurals (Berry → #TheBerries). Puns match your surnames against 40-odd sound rules: names ending in -son get #JohnAndOnly, -bell gets #ForWhomTheBellTolls, -wood gets #WoodYouBeMine. First names run the simple Forever/SayIDo patterns when surnames are unwieldy.

How to use it

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make a wedding hashtag?

Start with both surnames and work through the reliable formulas: the mashup (Smith + Johnson → #Smithson), the classic join (#SmithJohnson, #TheSmiths), a pun if either name puns (#JohnAndOnly for names ending in -son), or first names (#AlexAndJordanForever). Check the winner on Instagram and TikTok before printing anything, and keep it short enough to type without errors.

Whose name goes first in a wedding hashtag?

There's no rule. Test both orders out loud (#SmithJohnson vs #JohnsonSmith) and pick the one that flows and doesn't spell anything unfortunate. If one surname is shorter or puns, it usually leads. Many couples also mint both orders and let guests pick.

How do I check if a wedding hashtag is already taken?

Search it on Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook and look at both the hashtag results and the top posts. If recent posts from an unrelated couple dominate, add your year or locale (#SmithJohnson2026) to make yours unique. Also check the plain-word collision: #Wilson reads as a common name, so engagement posts from strangers will drift in regardless.

Do wedding hashtags still matter in 2026?

For guest photos, yes — a hashtag is still the cheapest way to collect everyone's shots in one feed instead of chasing group texts. What changed is where: TikTok and Instagram Reels carry most of the volume now, and some couples add a shared album or a QR code on table cards as backup, since not every guest hashtags reliably.

Where should we share our wedding hashtag?

Put it on your wedding website, save-the-dates or invitations, a sign at the welcome table, drink stirrers or napkin cards, and the photo-booth strip footer. One clear placement near the bar and one near the guest book covers most guests; repeated small reminders beat one big banner.
