Mind Map vs Outline vs Concept Map

ToolStructureBest For
Mind mapRadial, one center, hierarchical branchesBrainstorming, note capture, essay planning
OutlineLinear, numbered hierarchyWriting the thing once structure is settled
Concept mapNetwork; any node can link to any node, links labeledExplaining relationships, studying systems
FlowchartDirected, decision-basedProcesses and logic

If your ideas link to each other more than they branch from one center, you want a concept map. If they run in sequence, a flowchart.

What the Map Does With Your Text

The parser is deliberately simple, which makes the rules easy to remember. Everything below was read from the page's own code, so it's what actually happens rather than what a mind map tool usually does.

You typeWhat the map does
A line with no indentNew main branch with its own color
A line indented 2 spaces (or a tab)Sub-branch of the nearest main branch above it
A line indented 4 or more spacesStill a sub-branch of that main branch. The map draws one sub-level
A line starting with #Ignored, so you can leave notes to yourself
A blank lineIgnored
An indented line before any main branchDropped, because there's nothing to hang it from
More than 8 main branchesColors start repeating from the first one
Topic over 24 charactersCenter label is cut at 24
Main branch over 22 charactersLabel is cut at 22
Sub-branch over 26 charactersLabel is cut at 26

Those character caps are a good discipline anyway. A branch label that needs more than about 25 characters is usually a sentence, and sentences belong in the outline you write afterwards.

Getting a Clean Map Out of It

A few habits make the output look deliberate rather than dumped. Keep the center topic short, because a long one pushes every branch outward and the longest sub-branch labels can run off the right or left edge of the 980-pixel canvas. Balance the sides by ordering branches so heavy and light ones alternate. And put related branches next to each other in the list, since neighbors in the text end up as neighbors on the map.

The map saves itself in your browser's local storage every time it redraws, so closing the tab doesn't lose your work. That storage is per browser, though. Open the page on your phone and you'll see the sample map, not the one from your laptop. Download the SVG when you're done, and if you want to keep editing later, paste your branch list into a notepad too, since the SVG can't be loaded back in.

If your ideas link to each other more than they branch from one center, a mind map is the wrong shape. A flowchart suits steps and decisions, and an org chart suits strict hierarchies. Once the map has done its job, a kanban board turns branches into tasks.

How the Mind Map Maker Works

Give it a central topic and an indented list. Top-level lines become main branches, which the tool splits across the left and right of the center automatically; indented lines become sub-branches of the branch above them. Each main branch gets its own color, links are drawn as curves, and the download is an SVG that stays crisp at any size.

How to use it

Write fast and judge slowly: get every branch down first, then prune and regroup. Use keywords, not sentences, because a branch that reads like prose means you're outlining, not mapping. When one branch grows much fatter than the others, that branch is probably its own mind map; spin it off. The sample button loads an essay-planning map to show the shape that works: three to five argument branches, each with evidence hanging off it.

A worked example

The default map plans a podcast launch with four main branches: Format & length, Guests, Production, and Growth. The tool puts the first half of your list on the right and the rest on the left, both running top to bottom. So Format and Guests sit on the right, and Production and Growth sit on the left. Read the right side top to bottom, then the left side top to bottom, and you get the project in order: decide the format, line up guests, set up production, plan growth.

The numbers behind it are small. Format has 2 sub-branches and Guests has 3, so the right side needs 5 rows. Production and Growth have 3 each, so the left needs 6. The taller side sets the height: 6 rows at 34 pixels plus 80 pixels of margin gives a 980 × 284 pixel SVG. That's the at-a-glance size a mind map should be. If you have to scroll to read yours, it's trying to do too much.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a mind map used for?

Capturing and organizing ideas as they arrive, before structure exists. The radial layout mirrors how association works: you put the topic in the middle, throw branches outward as they occur to you, and the structure emerges instead of being imposed. Classic uses: lecture notes, essay planning, meeting brainstorms, and unpacking a decision.

How is a mind map different from an outline?

An outline forces hierarchy and sequence from the first keystroke; a mind map defers both. On a map you can add a branch anywhere, connect related ideas across the tree, and see the whole shape at a glance. Outlines are better once the structure is settled and you're writing; mind maps are better while you're still discovering what you think.

What are the rules of good mind mapping?

Keep it to one topic per map, use keywords rather than sentences on branches, aim for 4 to 8 main branches, and keep sub-branches shallow. On paper that can mean 2 or 3 levels; this tool draws one level of sub-branches, which forces the same discipline. Color-code main branches so related ideas cluster visually. If a branch starts feeling like a paragraph, it's a new map.

Does mind mapping actually work for studying?

The evidence is mixed but leaning positive: reviews of classroom studies find mind mapping often outperforms plain note-taking and reading, especially for retention, though the effect is smaller than its evangelists claim. The mechanism that matters is generation: producing the map, not owning it. Redrawing a messy map from memory is where the studying happens.

How many branches can this map have?

There's no hard limit, and a few dozen render fine. Readability gives out before the tool does: past about 8 main branches or 3 levels of depth, split into one map per major branch. A map you have to zoom into stops doing the at-a-glance job it exists for.

Can I add a third level of branches?

Not in this tool. Any indented line becomes a sub-branch of the nearest main branch above it, however far it's indented, so the map always has two levels. If a sub-branch needs its own children, make it a main branch or give it its own map.

Is my mind map saved?

Yes, in this browser. The topic and branch list are written to local storage each time the map redraws, so they come back when you reopen the page. Nothing is sent to a server, which also means the map won't appear on another device. Download the SVG and keep a copy of your branch list to be safe.

Can I download my mind map as a PNG or PDF?

The download is an SVG, which stays sharp at any size. To get a PNG, open the SVG in a browser and take a screenshot, or convert it in any image editor. For a PDF, open the SVG in your browser and print it to PDF.
