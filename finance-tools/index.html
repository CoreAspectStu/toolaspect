Free Finance Calculators for Loans, Paychecks, Taxes & Retirement

Money decisions come with numbers attached, and those numbers are usually amortizing, compounding, or taxed — three things human intuition handles badly. This collection of over thirty free finance calculators exists so you can replace gut feel with arithmetic before you sign anything: a mortgage, a car loan, a refinance, a retirement plan, a quarterly tax payment. Every calculator runs instantly in your browser, free and without an account, and each one shows its work — payment schedules, interest totals, break-even points — so you can see where the number came from, not just what it is.

The directory is organized into four working groups. Loans & credit covers borrowing of every flavor — conventional, FHA, VA, refinance, HELOC, credit cards. Income & salary handles paychecks, taxes, and retirement readiness. Business & real estate prices investments, rent, property tax, and net worth. Investment tools project compounding and returns over time.

Loans & Credit: Payments, Insurance, and Payoff Dates

The borrowing group starts with the classic: the Mortgage Calculator turns loan amount, rate, and term into a monthly payment with a full amortization schedule, so you can see exactly how much of payment one goes to interest versus payment two hundred. Around it sit the specialty variants that answer the questions the generic tool can't. The FHA Loan Calculator adds the upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) and annual MIP that FHA borrowers pay; the VA Loan Calculator folds in the VA funding fee; the PMI Calculator prices conventional mortgage insurance and shows when it can be removed; and the Closing Cost Calculator estimates purchase costs state by state.

Two decision tools deserve special mention. The Refinance Break-Even Calculator divides refinancing costs by monthly savings to find how many months until the refinance pays for itself — the number that decides whether a refi makes sense at all. The Credit Card Payoff Calculator simulates month-by-month payoff given your balance, APR, and payment, turning an open-ended debt into a concrete debt-free date. The DTI Calculator computes the debt-to-income ratio lenders scrutinize before any of this, so you know where you stand before you apply.

Income & Salary: From Paycheck to Retirement

The income group converts between the ways pay is quoted. The Salary Calculator and Hourly to Salary Calculator translate between annual and hourly figures in both directions — essential when comparing a contract rate to a salaried offer. The Overtime Calculator applies time-and-a-half math to extra hours, and the Payroll Calculator estimates take-home pay with taxes and deductions.

The tax tools serve the employed and self-employed alike. The Income Tax Calculator estimates federal and state liability; the Self-Employment Tax Calculator applies the 15.3% SE tax and identifies the deductible half; and the Quarterly Tax Calculator lays out the four estimated-payment deadlines with safe-harbor guidance. On the retirement side, the Retirement Calculator projects whether your savings trajectory will fund your goal, the Social Security Calculator estimates benefits, and the Roth Conversion Calculator plans conversions across the 2026 bracket ladder. The HSA and FSA tools track contribution limits and spend-by deadlines for the tax-advantaged accounts.

Business, Real Estate & Investing: The Bigger Picture

The final group zooms out. The Compound Interest Calculator shows growth over time — the engine behind every long-term projection on this page. The ROI Calculator measures return on any investment against its cost. The Rent Calculator checks how much rent your income supports, the Property Tax Calculator estimates annual property tax, and the Net Worth Calculator totals assets minus liabilities for the single number that summarizes a household's position. The Savings Calculator projects progress toward a goal, while the Sales Tax, Tip, and Discount calculators handle the day-to-day money math.

How to Use This Collection

  • Run the DTI Calculator before applying for any loan — it's the first ratio a lender will compute
  • Compare a refinance with its break-even months against how long you'll keep the property
  • On credit card payoff, experiment with the monthly payment field — small increases move the debt-free date dramatically
  • For taxes, treat results as planning estimates; your filing's final numbers depend on deductions and credits these tools can't see
  • Re-run retirement projections yearly — the answer changes as markets and contributions do

Free, Private, Instant

Every calculator here computes in your browser — no account, no fees, nothing stored. The inputs you'd rather keep private (income, debt, balances) never leave your machine. Start with the number you know: a payment you can't quite afford, a payoff date you want to see, a retirement you want to confirm. There is a calculator on this page built for it.
