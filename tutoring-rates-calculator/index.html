How the Tutoring Rates Calculator Works

The calculator is a pricing model, not a wage survey. It anchors each subject to its published national range, then adjusts for the four factors that actually move what families pay.

The formula

Rate = subject base × credentials multiplier × level multiplier × format multiplier × market multiplier × experience factor. Credentials run from 0.65 for a college student to 1.3 for a subject specialist. Level runs 0.8 at elementary up to 1.1 for college coursework. Online tutoring applies 0.95, small towns 0.85, major metros 1.2. Experience adds 2% per year, capped at ten years, because a tutor's twelfth year prices like their tenth. The rate band shows ±15% around your number, which is the normal spread you'll see shopping the same profile across a metro.

How to use it

Set the six profile fields to match your real offer, then read the hero number as your list rate. The package row applies the common 5% off 8 sessions and 10% off 12 or more; the monthly figure uses 4.33 weeks a month at your stated hours. If you teach through a platform, price your list rate so the net-of-commission figure still clears your target, and remember self-employment tax applies to all of it.

A worked example

A certified teacher tutoring high school math in person in a suburb, four years in: the model gives 45 × 1.15 × 1.08 = $55.89 an hour, with a defensible band of $47.51 to $64.27. Twelve sessions at the 10% package discount is $603.61 instead of $670.68. At six hours a week that's about $1,452 a month gross, or $1,089 net of Wyzant's 25% commission ($41.92/hr effective).

Shift the same profile to SAT prep as a subject specialist, online, in a major metro, seven years in, and the model returns $101.37 an hour. That sounds aggressive until you check the market: elite test-prep firms charge $79 to $349 an hour, so a $101 independent rate undercuts the firms while more than doubling the general-subject rate. The premium tracks the stakes, not the hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge for tutoring per hour?

Most independent US tutors land between $25 and $80 an hour. Where you fall inside that band depends on four things: subject (math averages about $46 an hour and SAT prep about $62 on Wyzant), your credentials (a certified teacher prices 10-20% above a general tutor), the market (major metros run about 20% above suburban rates), and experience (roughly 2% more per year, topping out after a decade). The calculator above turns those into one number.

What do most tutors charge on Wyzant?

Wyzant tutors set their own prices, and the platform reports a typical range of $35 to $60 an hour, with subject specialists above that. Remember the take rate: Wyzant keeps a flat 25% commission, so a $55 listed rate pays you $41.25. Preply starts new tutors at a 33% commission that falls to 18% with volume, and keeps 100% of a new student's first lesson.

How much does SAT tutoring cost?

SAT prep runs about $62 an hour on Wyzant, roughly 63% more than language tutoring. The broader private market is $50 to $150 an hour, and elite firms charging brand-name-tutor rates run $79 to $349 an hour. Most families buy 10 to 20 sessions, so a mid-range package lands between $600 and $2,000.

Should I charge less for online tutoring?

Slightly, yes, if you are competing in a national market: online rates typically run about 5% below in-person because you compete with tutors everywhere and save the commute. But if your online students are local and would otherwise book you in person, there's no reason to discount at all. The travel time you save is yours.

Should I offer tutoring packages?

Packages smooth your income and cut no-shows, and a modest discount buys commitment: 5% off an 8-session package and 10% off 12 or more is common. A $55.89 rate makes a 12-session package $604 instead of $671, and you get the calendar locked in advance. Set an expiry (six months is typical) so old packages don't haunt you after you raise rates.
