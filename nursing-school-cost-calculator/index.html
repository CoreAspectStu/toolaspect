How the Nursing School Cost Calculator Works

Nursing is unusual among degrees: the cheapest path and the priciest path lead to the same license and the same median paycheck. That makes the cost decision genuinely mathematical, and worth doing before you pick a program rather than after.

The formula

Tuition = credit hours × rate per credit. Fees = semesters × per-semester fees. Total = tuition + fees + supplies + licensure − grants. All-in per credit = total ÷ credit hours. Loan payment uses the standard amortization formula: payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r the monthly rate and n the number of payments. Payback = total ÷ (salary ÷ 12) — how many months of RN work cover the whole program.

How to use it

Choose your degree path and the calculator loads typical credits, tuition rate, semesters, fees, and supplies for that program type — then replace any field with your program's published numbers, which is the whole point. Enter grants and scholarships to see net cost, and use the loan section to model repayment on what you actually borrow. Starting salary defaults to the national RN median ($97,550, BLS May 2025); swap in your region's rate for sharper payback math.

A worked example

Community college ADN: 70 credits at $200, four semesters at $500 in fees, $2,500 in supplies, $450 licensure. Tuition: 70 × $200 = $14,000. Fees: 4 × $500 = $2,000. Total: $14,000 + $2,000 + $2,500 + $450 = $18,950, which is $270.71 per credit all-in. Borrow it all at 7% over 10 years and the payment is $220.03 a month. Against the RN median salary of $97,550, the entire degree equals 2.3 months of gross pay.

Now the expensive route: a private BSN at 120 credits × $1,400 = $168,000 tuition, plus $8,000 fees, $3,000 supplies, $450 licensure — $179,450 total, or $1,495.42 per credit. Financed at 7% over 10 years, that's roughly $2,084 a month, and the payback stretches to about 22 months of median RN pay. Both paths end at the same NCLEX-RN and the same license; the calculator makes the $160,500 gap between them impossible to wave off.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does nursing school cost?

It depends entirely on the degree path. A two-year ADN at a community college runs $6,000 to $20,000 in tuition and fees — the cheapest route to an RN license. A four-year BSN at a public university costs roughly $40,000 to $80,000 in-state, while private BSN programs run $100,000 to $200,000. Accelerated BSNs for career changers land between $33,000 and $93,000, and an online RN-to-BSN bridge costs $5,000 to $30,000.

Is it cheaper to get an ADN then a BSN?

Usually, yes. The ADN-at-community-college-then-online-RN-to-BSN route can cost $12,000 to $35,000 total, versus $40,000 to $80,000-plus for a straight four-year BSN — and you earn an RN salary one to two years sooner. The trade-off is sequencing and employer preferences: some hospital systems, especially Magnet facilities, prefer BSN nurses and some will subsidize the bridge after hiring you with the ADN.

How much is an accelerated BSN program?

Accelerated BSNs for people who already hold a bachelor's degree typically cost $33,000 to $93,000 and run 11 to 18 months. Public universities in-state sit at the low end; private universities dominate the upper half — one well-known private ABSN charges about $28,000 per semester across three semesters. Financial aid can be thinner for these programs since they're second degrees.

What extra costs come with nursing school beyond tuition?

Plan on $2,000 to $4,000 a year in program extras: scrubs and clinical uniforms ($150-$300), a stethoscope and diagnostic kit ($80-$350), books and access codes ($500-$1,500 per year), drug screens and background checks ($50-$150), CPR certification, travel to clinical placements, and licensure at the end. The NCLEX exam itself costs $200 through early 2027, then $350.

Do ADN and BSN nurses earn the same salary?

Both take the same NCLEX-RN and hold the same RN license, and the median RN wage was $97,550 in May 2025. Pay differences between ADN and BSN nurses at the same bedside are usually small; the BSN matters more for hiring screens, Magnet hospital requirements, and progression into leadership or graduate study. Nurses who continue to nurse practitioner, midwife, or anesthetist roles earn a median of $134,920.

Is nursing school worth the cost?

The payback math is unusually fast. A community-college ADN costing $19,000 all-in equals about 2.3 months of median RN pay — you clear the entire cost within your first quarter of working. Even a $179,000 private BSN equals about 22 months of median pay, which is a far better ratio than most graduate degrees. The caveat: you have to finish, pass the NCLEX, and stay in the field.
