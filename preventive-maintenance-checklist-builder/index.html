How the Preventive Maintenance Checklist Builder Works

A PM checklist fails in one of two ways: it's so generic nobody uses it, or it's so dense nobody reads it. This builder starts from the middle — an equipment-specific task list drawn from common OEM schedules — then prices the whole year so you can defend the program with a number, not a vibe.

The formula

Each task carries a frequency and an average completion time in minutes. Occurrences per year: daily × operating days (default 250), weekly × 52, monthly × 12, quarterly × 4, semiannual × 2, annual × 1. Annual PM hours = Σ (task minutes ÷ 60 × occurrences). Annual PM labor = hours × technician rate. The checklist renders grouped by frequency with checkboxes, ready to print.

How to use it

Pick your equipment type, set operating days and shop labor rate, and the checklist, hours, and cost update live. Edit any task row to match your OEM manual, then print or save as PDF — the sheet carries a QR code linking back to this builder. Operating days matter more than you'd think: a 7-day plant doubles the daily-task minutes versus a 250-day shop, all by itself.

A worked example

Rotary-screw air compressor, defaults: 2 daily tasks at 5 min total × 250 days = 1,250 min; 1 weekly × 52 = 520 min; 2 monthly at 35 min × 12 = 420 min; 3 quarterly at 60 min × 4 = 240 min; 1 semiannual × 2 = 60 min; 3 annual at 135 min = 135 min. Total 2,625 minutes = 43.75 hours of PM a year, which is $3,718.75 at $85/hr. At 8 run-hours a day across 250 days the machine logs 2,000 hours — exactly the conventional-fluid oil-change interval, so the annual service and the calendar land together.

Contrast the rooftop HVAC preset: 6 tasks, 510 minutes, 8.5 hours and $722.50 a year. Lighter duty, lighter checklist — but skipping the $722 of filter and coil work is how you end up buying a compressor years early. Frequently Asked Questions

What should a preventive maintenance checklist include?

Three things per task: what to do, how often, and how long it takes. The best checklists group tasks by frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annual), stick to inspect-measure-replace actions a technician can verify, and carry the OEM interval next to any task tied to run-hours. Our builder generates all of that from your equipment type.

How many hours a year does preventive maintenance take?

It scales with how hard the asset runs. A typical rotary-screw air compressor needs about 44 technician-hours a year — 12 tasks spanning daily condensate drains to an annual oil and separator change — which is roughly $3,700 of labor at $85/hr. A rooftop HVAC unit runs lighter, about 8.5 hours and $722 a year.

How do I choose between calendar and run-hour intervals?

Use whichever comes due first. An asset that runs hard (a forklift at 6 hours a day) hits its 250-hour service long before a calendar limit; an asset that sits (a backup generator) ages by the calendar even with low hours. Oil, filters, and seals degrade with time as well as use, which is why OEM schedules list both.

Is preventive maintenance worth the cost?

The math is rarely close. A year of PM on an air compressor costs about $3,700 of labor; unplanned manufacturing downtime is often benchmarked at $260,000 an hour (an Aberdeen figure cited across the industry since 2016). Skipping the $30 oil filter to risk a seized air end is a bad trade — PM spend buys availability, not just longer machine life.

Can I print the checklist or post it on the machine?

Yes. The builder renders a print-ready checklist with a QR code linking back to this tool — print it, save it as PDF, and tape it inside the equipment panel so the checklist travels with the machine. Everything runs in your browser; no account needed.
