What laser vision surgery actually runs, by technology and provider type

Direct answer: LASIK costs about $2,250 per eye nationally ($2,632 in NVision's pricing research), or $4,500-$5,300 for both eyes. Where you buy it moves the number more than anything else: national chains average $1,500-$2,500 per eye, independent surgeons $2,000-$3,200, and premium or academic centers $2,500-$4,000+ for custom and topography-guided treatment. The $490-per-eye ads apply to the lowest correction tier on select prescriptions.

How the LASIK Cost Calculator Works

LASIK is one of the few elective surgeries where the same 15-minute procedure has a 3x price spread depending mostly on where you buy it. This calculator prices the four levers that matter: the technology tier, the type of provider selling it, one or both eyes, and the extras that show up after the advertised price.

The formula

Per-eye price = technology base price × provider multiplier. Chains price about 15% under independent surgeons on average; premium and academic centers run about 20% above. Both-eyes total = per-eye price × 2 + post-op meds. If you pay with FSA or HSA dollars, the effective cost drops by your marginal tax rate, since laser vision surgery is an eligible expense.

How to use it

Pick the technology from your consultation (candidates for SMILE or PRK usually learn this there), then price the same procedure across all three provider types to see the spread. Quotes should be written and inclusive: ask whether post-op visits, meds, and enhancements are covered, and for how long.

A worked example

Custom wavefront LASIK at an independent surgeon: base $2,400 per eye, so both eyes run $4,800 plus $75 in post-op drops, or $4,875 all-in. Paid through an FSA at a 24% bracket, the effective cost drops to about $3,705 — a $1,170 tax benefit most people forget to claim.

Same procedure class at a national chain: conventional LASIK prices near $1,700 per eye, both eyes $3,400 plus drops, about $3,475. That's the real gap between chain and independent pricing — roughly $1,400 on both eyes — not the $490 ad versus $4,000 horror stories. At the top end, SMILE at a premium center prices around $3,360 per eye, $6,795 both eyes with meds.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does LASIK cost per eye in 2026?

Most-quoted national averages run about $2,250 to $2,632 per eye, roughly $4,500 to $5,300 for both eyes. National chains typically land at $1,500 to $2,500 per eye, independent surgeons at $2,000 to $3,200, and premium or academic centers at $2,500 to $4,000 or more for custom and topography-guided treatment.

Is the $490 per eye LASIK advertised on TV real?

The teaser price is real but almost nobody qualifies. It applies to the lowest correction tier on select prescriptions, usually conventional (not custom) treatment, and often one eye on a financing contract. Quotes for average prescriptions at the same chains run $1,500 to $2,500 per eye, so treat the ad as a foot-in-the-door price, not a menu price.

Why do independent surgeons charge more than LASIK chains?

Chains run high patient volume with tiered pricing and heavy upsell paths, which pushes base prices down. Independent and premium-center pricing typically bundles custom wavefront or topography-guided technology, more surgeon time, and a longer post-op care window. Whether that's worth $500 to $1,500 more per eye depends on your prescription and how much you value surgeon continuity.

Does insurance cover LASIK?

Almost never, because it's elective refractive surgery. Two real discounts exist: LASIK is FSA and HSA eligible, so you can pay with pre-tax dollars (a 22-35% effective discount at typical tax rates), and some vision plans negotiate 10-15% off at networked providers. A few employers offer voluntary LASIK benefits worth checking.

What's the cheapest safe way to get LASIK?

Pay with FSA or HSA funds (saves your marginal tax rate on up to the annual limit), get consultations at both a chain and an independent to compare the actual written quote, and ask each to match the other. Ask what the quote excludes: post-op meds run $50 to $150, and enhancement policies differ, with some centers charging full price for touch-ups after a year.

Is PRK or SMILE cheaper than LASIK?

PRK usually prices slightly below comparable LASIK, around $1,500 to $2,800 per eye, because it skips the flap-cutting laser step. SMILE typically runs $2,200 to $3,500 per eye, at or above LASIK, since it uses a dedicated platform fewer centers own. Cost shouldn't drive that choice though; corneal thickness and prescription shape usually decide which procedure you're a candidate for.
