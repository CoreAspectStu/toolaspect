How the Car Wrap Calculator Works

A wrap price is mostly labor: 20 to 40 hours of skilled hands stretching film around bumpers, mirrors, and door handles. The vehicle class sets how much surface and how many difficult curves there are, the finish multiplies film cost and effort, and the installer tier prices the talent. This calculator multiplies those three together the way shops quote.

The formula

Cost = base range for the vehicle class × finish multiplier × installer tier, plus $500 to $1,500 for removal of an old wrap when you need it. Partial coverage multiplies the base by the covered share: roof about 22 percent of a full wrap, hood 18 percent, stripes 8 percent. Matte and satin films carry a premium because they show every seam and contaminate, so installers slow down; chrome is a specialty product that takes double handling and rarely leaves a shop for less than 2.5 times gloss.

How to use it

Pick your vehicle class honestly (the calculator prices surface area, not badges), choose the finish you actually want, and set the installer tier to match your market: standard for most shops, premium for certified show-work installers whose seams disappear. If there's an old wrap coming off first, add removal. The DIY kit line shows what material alone costs at retail, roughly $600 to $900 for a sedan, which is how much of a wrap quote is labor.

A worked example

A satin color change on a midsize crossover at a standard shop. Base range $2,500 to $4,500; satin's 1.12 multiplier for the trickier film brings that to $2,800 to $5,040, with the midpoint near $3,920. Same car in gloss at a budget shop runs $2,125 to $3,825 (0.85 tier), and at a premium shop in color-shift, $4,725 to $8,505. Bump up to a full-size pickup in matte at a standard shop and you're at $3,450 to $6,325, which is why truck owners so often wrap just the roof and bed accents instead.

These are national planning ranges, not quotes. Big-city shops with certified installers price above them; small-town general sign shops price below, sometimes with calendered film you don't want. Ask every bidder for the film brand and series, whether they disassemble trim, and photos of their seam work on your kind of car.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to wrap a car?

A full color-change wrap runs $2,000-$3,500 on a sedan, $2,500-$4,500 on a midsize SUV, and $3,000-$5,500 on a full-size SUV or pickup, installed in cast vinyl by a standard shop. Exotics and commercial vans run $5,000-$10,000. Specialty finishes push higher: matte and satin add 10-15 percent, color-shift about 35 percent, and chrome runs 2.5 times a gloss wrap or more. Published 2025-26 installer ranges.

Is wrapping a car cheaper than painting?

A quality wrap and a quality paint job cost about the same, $2,000-$5,000, but a wrap preserves the factory paint underneath and reverses, while a respray is permanent and can hurt resale on collectible cars. Cheap maaco-style paint starts under $1,000 and beats wrapping on price alone; a proper base-clear respray on a sedan starts around $5,000. The wrap's edge is reversibility and three-to-seven-day turnaround versus weeks in a booth.

How long does a car wrap last?

Cast vinyl wraps last 5 to 7 years outdoors, with premium films and garaged cars at the top of that range and sun-belt cars parked outside at the bottom. Matte and textured finishes show age sooner; chrome fails fastest in heat. Most installers warranty 1 to 3 years against peeling and adhesive failure, not against rock chips or neglect.

Does a wrap damage the paint?

On healthy factory paint, no; removal by a professional with heat leaves the clear coat intact. The exceptions are fresh repaints (wait 30+ days, and some repaints lift), repainted panels with cheap resprays, and wraps left on far past their life, when adhesive separates from the film and removal takes far longer. Delivered-from-factory paint is the best surface a wrapper can ask for.

What's the difference between cast and calendered vinyl?

Cast vinyl is poured thin (2 mil) and conforms to curves without shrinking back; it's what every reputable shop uses on vehicles and what the ranges in this calculator assume. Calendered vinyl is extruded thicker and cheaper, works for flat sign panels, and on a car it shrinks, cracks at edges, and fails within a couple of years. If a quote seems too good, ask which film it includes.

How much does wrap removal cost?

$500-$1,500 for a passenger car depending on age and film condition, billed by the hour because baked adhesive multiplies the work. Fresh film under two years old comes off in a day; six-year-old film in an Arizona sun can take two. Budget removal before any rewrap, and treat it as the cost of changing your mind.
