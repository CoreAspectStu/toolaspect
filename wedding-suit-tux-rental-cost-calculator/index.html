How the Suit & Tux Rental Calculator Works

Formalwear pricing looks like a single number and behaves like a party math problem. Each person pays a package price plus a damage-and-handling fee (about $12 everywhere), group promotions can zero out the groom, and the rent-versus-buy decision depends entirely on how often you'll wear formalwear again.

The formula

Per person = package price + $12 fee. Party total = per person × (groomsmen + groom, minus the groom if the free promo applies). On the buy side: total cost = purchase price + alterations; break-even wears = total cost ÷ per-person rental; cost per event at your attendance rate tells you the long-run number.

How to use it

Set the package tier to match what your chain quoted (basic packages carry lighter fabrics and fewer accessories; premium adds shoes and nicer wools). Enter your groomsmen count and leave the groom-free toggle on until the shop confirms. For the buy comparison, enter a realistic purchase price — $400 for a decent suit, $850 for a mid-range tuxedo — plus alterations.

A worked example

Standard package: $185 + $12 = $197 per person. Groom plus five groomsmen is six rentals; with the groom comped, five pay: 5 × $197 = $985 for the party. Without the promo, 6 × $197 = $1,182 — the difference between asking and not asking one question.

Now the groom alone, deciding whether to buy. A mid-range tuxedo at $850 plus $75 of alterations is $925 all in. Divided by the $197 rental, that's 4.7 — so buying wins at the fifth wedding or black-tie event. At one wedding a year, that's a five-year payback; attend three formal events a year and the tux pays for itself inside two. A $400 suit with alterations breaks even at just 2.4 rentals, which is why the standard advice is: rent the tux, buy the navy suit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a tuxedo?

Most wedding rentals run $150 to $250 per person including the standard damage-and-handling fee, with the full market between $99 and $300. Men's Wearhouse packages span $99-$249 and Jos. A. Bank lists jacket-and-pants at $169.99 plus a $12 fee, with a $289.99 nine-piece package. Prom rentals usually price lower, $100 to $180.

Is it cheaper to rent or buy a tuxedo?

Renting wins for one or two wears. Buying a mid-range tuxedo ($500-$1,000 plus $50-$150 of alterations) breaks even against a typical $197 rental at about the fifth wearing — the same 4-5 event rule Jos. A. Bank publishes. If you're in weddings regularly or attend black-tie events, buy; for one wedding, rent.

Does the groom get his tux rental free?

Often, yes. National chains commonly comp the groom's rental when four or more groomsmen rent paid packages, and group discounts of $30-$40 per tux are routine. It's a promotion, not a guarantee — ask when the party registers, because the free groom slot can also cover a father of the bride.

How much does a groomsmen tux rental cost for the whole party?

At the typical $197 per person (standard package plus fee), a groom and five groomsmen run about $1,182. With the common groom-free-at-five-rentals promotion, the same party pays $985. Budget $150-$250 per person when you ask groomsmen to rent.

What's included in a tux rental package?

Standard packages include the jacket, trousers, shirt, and a vest or tie; premium tiers add shoes, cufflinks, studs, and a pocket square. Everyone pays a damage-and-handling fee of about $12. Fittings are included — out-of-town groomsmen can get measured at any branch of the same chain.

How far in advance should groomsmen order rentals?

Reserve the party's styles and get everyone measured at least two months out, with final fittings one to two weeks before the wedding when alterations are turned around. Prom season (March through May) is the squeeze — reserve early or the popular cuts sell out in sizes.
