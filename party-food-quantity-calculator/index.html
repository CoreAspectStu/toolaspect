How the Party Food Calculator Works

Every party-food mistake is the same mistake: guessing. This calculator replaces the guess with the planning rules caterers actually use — per-person quantities scaled by party length and adjusted for how many guests are children.

The formulas

Appetizers: 6 pieces per adult when a meal follows, or 12 pieces for a two-hour party where appetizers are the meal, plus 3 pieces for each hour beyond two. Mains: 8 oz of protein per adult for a dinner, 10 oz for a BBQ, 7 oz for a plated reception. Sides: 6-10 oz per adult depending on format. Desserts: 1-2 servings. Drinks: 2 servings in the first hour plus 1 per additional hour. Children under 12 count as half an adult throughout, and the optional cushion adds 10% to everything.

How to use it

Pick your party type, enter adult and kid counts, and set the length. The 10% cushion is on by default — turn it off if you're buying for a crowd that reliably under-eats, like a late-evening reception after a big lunch. Numbers update as you type. For the alcohol share of the drink count, the wedding alcohol calculator splits servings into beer, wine, and spirits with bottle counts.

A worked example

Take a graduation open house: 30 adults, 10 kids, three hours. Appetizers at 15 pieces per adult (12 base + 3 for the third hour) with kids at half portions works out to 525 pieces, and the 10% cushion brings the shopping number to 578 pieces. Desserts at 2 per adult: 70 servings, or 77 with the cushion. Drinks: 4 servings per adult over three hours → 140, or 154 servings (about 14.4 gallons) with the cushion.

Now a dinner party for 12 adults, three hours, no kids. Six appetizer pieces each is 72; with the cushion, 80 pieces. The main course is 8 oz × 12 = 96 oz = 6 lb of protein, or 6.6 lb with the cushion — two whole chickens or a 7-lb pork shoulder covers it. Drinks come to 48 servings, 53 with the cushion — about five gallons total. A case of soda (24 cans) plus two gallons of lemonade covers 45 of them, so add a six-pack of seltzer and you're set.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many appetizers should I plan per person?

For a party with a full meal, 6 pieces per person before the meal. For a cocktail party or open house where appetizers ARE the meal, 12 pieces per person for two hours, plus about 3 more pieces for each additional hour. Kids under 12 eat roughly half of an adult portion.

How much meat should I buy per person for a party?

Plan 8 ounces of protein per adult at a sit-down dinner, about 10 ounces for a barbecue where burgers and dogs are the main event, and 7 ounces at a wedding reception with multiple courses. In pounds: 8 ounces per person means one pound feeds two guests.

How many drinks do I need per guest?

The standard catering rule is two drinks per guest for the first hour and one drink for each additional hour. At a three-hour party that's four servings per adult, or roughly 160 twelve-ounce servings for 40 guests.

How much extra food should I buy for a party?

Add about 10 percent. It covers the friend who brings a plus-one, second helpings at the buffet, and the inevitable spill. Running short is the one party mistake you can't fix mid-event, while leftovers keep.

How much food do I need for a graduation party?

Most graduation parties run three hours with grazing instead of a seated meal, so plan around 15 appetizer pieces per adult and 2 desserts each. For 30 adults and 10 kids, that's roughly 575 pieces and 75 dessert servings with a 10 percent cushion.

How much do kids eat at a party?

Count children under 12 as half an adult for mains, sides, and appetizers. A 10-year-old eats most of a slice of pizza but grazes far less than adults do, and the half-portion rule keeps you from buying a full adult share for every kid.
