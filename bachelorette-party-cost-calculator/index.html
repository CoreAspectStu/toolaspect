How the Bachelorette Party Cost Calculator Works

Bachelorette budgets break in two places: estimating the total (lodging plus everyone's spending) and splitting it (who pays, including whether the bride rides free). This calculator prices the trip as one lodging line plus four per-person lines, always counting the bride in the headcount since she eats, sleeps, and jet-skis too. The split then divides by the paying attendees, with a toggle for whether her share is covered.

The split math

Trip total = lodging + (attendees + 1) × (travel + food + activities + extras). If the crew covers the bride, each attendee pays total ÷ attendees. If she pays her own way, everyone including her pays total ÷ (attendees + 1). The "if split evenly instead" card shows the second number so you can see exactly what covering the bride costs each person.

How to use it

Start from the preset that matches your vibe and edit. Lodging is the total for the whole group over the whole stay, so a 3-room, 2-night, $300 room-night block enters as $1,800. The per-person lines are averages across the crew, so set food at what a typical member spends, not the biggest drinker. Flip the bride toggle to compare, and remember preset fields switch to Custom the moment you edit one.

A worked example

Eight friends, two nights, bride covered. The house is $1,800 (3 rooms × 2 nights × $300). Each of the 9 people spends $80 on rides and gas, $170 on food and drinks, $95 on a paddle-board class, and $40 toward decorations, a bride sash, and favors: $385 a head, $3,465 total. Add the house and the weekend is $5,265. Split 8 ways, that's $658.13 per person; the even 9-way split would be $585, so covering the bride costs each attendee $73.13.

The same crew going big changes the math fast: 3 nights in a $3,600 house with $350 flights, $260 of food, $200 of activities, and $45 of extras per person totals $11,295, or $1,411.88 each, right on The Knot's destination average. Staying local drops it to $2,715, or $339.38 each. Same friendship, wildly different Venmo requests.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bachelorette party cost per person?

The Knot's Bach Study puts the average attendee spend at about $1,300 per person for a one-to-two-day party, and $1,650 per person when it stretches to three or four days. A local one-night celebration can come in under $375 a head, while a three-night destination trip can top $1,400, so the range is genuinely wide.

Does the bride pay for the bachelorette party?

In most groups, no: the attendees split the bride's share along with everything else, which is why an 8-person weekend costs each person about $73 more than a straight even split. Some crews treat the bride's share as the maid of honor's gift, or cover just her dinner and activities rather than the whole weekend.

How do you split bachelorette costs fairly?

Divide shared costs (lodging, decorations, group activities) by the paying headcount, and let each person cover their own flights, rides, and personal spending. The friction points are room assignments and unequal beds: the clean fix is pricing each room separately and charging by the room, not splitting lodging evenly when one pair gets the master suite and others share a bunk.

Who pays for what at a bachelorette party?

The common pattern: the maid of honor (or party planner among the friends) books and fronts the deposits, attendees reimburse their share on a set deadline, and the bride's total gets divided among the group. Book refundable rates and collect money before the trip, not during, so nobody is chasing Venmo requests from a rooftop bar.

How can we keep the bachelorette cheap?

Three structural moves beat couponing: stay local and skip flights entirely, rent one house instead of hotel rooms (a 4-bedroom rental often halves the per-person lodging line), and travel off-season or Sunday to Thursday. A $2,715 local one-night plan and an $11,295 destination weekend are the same party in the bride's memory, roughly.
