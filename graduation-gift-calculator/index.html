How the Graduation Gift Calculator Works

Graduation gifting has no invoice to cover, unlike weddings. The bands come from relationship: how long you've known the graduate and how much their milestone means in your circle. This calculator applies the published etiquette bands, then adjusts for degree level, whether you're giving as a couple, and party attendance.

The formula

Start from the base band for your relationship at high school level: coworker $20–$40, friend $25–$50, relative $50–$100, grandparent/close family $100–$300. Multiply by 1.5 for a college graduation, or about 1.75 for a master's or doctorate. Multiply by 1.5 again if a couple is giving jointly, and subtract 25% if there's no party or you can't attend. Round to a clean bill: $25, $50, $75, $100. If a cap is entered, the suggested band is floored at what you can afford.

How to use it

Pick your relationship and the level, then let the calculator do the adjusting. The odd-number suggestion comes from the old cash-gift custom of giving amounts like $51 or $101 so the graduate can't be "evened out"; it's optional, a nice flourish on a card, never a requirement.

A worked example

Your partner's cousin is finishing a bachelor's degree, you're both going to the party, and you'd like to stay under $150. Relative band at college level: $75–$150. As a couple, ×1.5 gives $112.50–$225 on the raw math, but your $150 cap trims the top, so the calculator suggests $115 to $150 with a sweet spot near $130. In clean bills, $125 or $135 both read as generous.

A simpler one: your neighbor's kid graduates high school and you're invited to the open house. Acquaintance band: $20–$40. Individual, attending: no adjustments. Give $25 to $40, sweet spot $30, and a card that names what you're proud of.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money should you give for a graduation gift?

The conventional bands: $20 to $50 for a friend or classmate, $50 to $100 for a niece, nephew, or cousin, and $100 to $300 if you're a grandparent or close family. For a college graduation, move up about 50%: a friend's college gift runs $40 to $75.

How much should parents give their graduating child?

There's no etiquette band for your own child because parents typically give a milestone gift rather than cash in a card: a used car, a laptop for college, a trip, or a deposit to their 529 or Roth IRA. Families who do give cash commonly land at $100 to $500, but the gift itself matters more than the number.

Do you give more for a college graduation than high school?

Yes, generally about 50% more at each relationship tier, since it's a rarer milestone and often ends a longer relationship with the graduate's family. A $25 to $50 friend gift for high school becomes $40 to $75 for college. For a master's or doctorate, another 15% to 25% on top is common.

Should I give less if I'm not invited to a graduation party?

A smaller gift is completely acceptable when there's no party and you won't see the graduate. Scaling back about 25% is a reasonable convention, and for acquaintances, a heartfelt card with $20 beats a strained $75.

Is cash or a gift card better for a graduate?

Cash or a check remains the most useful gift for both high school and college graduates, and etiquette writers now fully endorse it. Venmo or a similar app works well for younger graduates; for a college grad with student loans, cash toward loans is one of the most appreciated gifts there is.

How much should a couple give for a graduation gift?

Roughly 1.5 times the individual band is the sweet spot, since a couple is two givers but usually gives jointly. A couple giving to a friend's college graduation would land around $60 to $110 rather than doubling the $40 to $75 single band.
