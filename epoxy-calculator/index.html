About the Epoxy Garage Floor Calculator

This epoxy calculator estimates how much coating you need for a garage floor or slab, based on its dimensions, the number of coats, the product's coverage rate per gallon, a waste allowance, and your price per gallon. It converts total coated area into gallons to buy and into 2-car epoxy kits (250 ft² per kit is the typical full-system coverage — primer or color coat plus topcoat — stated on retail garage kits).

Epoxy coverage follows a simple rule: gallons = area × coats ÷ coverage per gallon. The nuance is that 'coverage' means different things per product — a thin rolled color coat may cover 350–400 ft² per gallon, while a 100%-solids self-leveling epoxy at 10 mils wet covers about 160 ft² per gallon, the default this calculator uses. Always check your product's technical data sheet and use its published figure.

This page explains the math, shows how coverage varies by product type, works through a two-car-garage example, and answers the most common coverage questions.

How the Calculator Works

Step 1 — purchasing area: length × width × (1 + waste). The default 10% covers roller loss, edge cutting, and material left in the tray. Step 2 — gallons: ceil(purchasing area × coats ÷ coverage per gallon), rounded up because epoxy comes in fixed kits and mixed batches have a limited pot life — you cannot buy 4.3 gallons.

Step 3 — kit conversion: the tool divides purchasing area × 2 coats by 250 ft² per kit and rounds up, matching how retail 2-car garage kits are specified (each kit typically contains a color coat plus a clear topcoat, together rated for ~250 ft²). Step 4 — cost: gallons × price per gallon.

Coverage derives from film thickness: 1 U.S. gallon spread at 1 mil (0.001 in) covers 1,604 ft². At 10 mils wet, theoretical coverage is ~160 ft² per gallon — the calculator's default. A thinner 7-mil coat yields ~230 ft²; a self-leveling 20-mil coat yields ~80 ft².Surface preparation drives real-world coverage as much as thickness does. Porous, broom-finished concrete absorbs the first coat: many installers seal with a water-based primer so the expensive high-solids coat is not soaked into the slab. If your concrete is old or visibly porous, raise the waste factor to 15% or budget a dedicated primer coat sized by the primer's own published coverage, not the topcoat's.

Two numbers on the label deserve a final read before purchase: recoat window and cure time. Most two-part systems demand a second coat within 12 to 24 hours of the first, and full chemical cure takes about 7 days — vehicle traffic waits a week even when the floor feels hard overnight. Buy all gallons at once, since even the same product from different production batches can shift shade.

Coverage by Product Type

The table below shows theoretical coverage at common wet film thicknesses (1 gallon = 1,604 ft² at 1 mil) alongside typical two-coat kit equivalences. Solids content drives the difference: a 100%-solids epoxy doesn't shrink as it cures, while a 50%-solids water-based product loses half its volume to evaporation, so its coverage at equal wet thickness is effectively half after cure.

Read the wet-film-thickness or coverage-per-gallon line on your product's data sheet, find the nearest row, and enter that number in the calculator's coverage field rather than accepting the default. Solids content explains the rest of the spread between products. A 100%-solids epoxy loses no volume as it cures, so a 10-mil wet film stays 10 mils dry. A 50%-solids water-based product loses half its film to evaporation — equal wet thickness cures to half the build. Manufacturers state solids on the data sheet; when in doubt, compare solids percentages across products at the same coverage claim.

Worked Example: Two-Car Garage

A standard two-car garage is about 20 ft × 20 ft = 400 ft². With 10% waste: 440 ft² to purchase. For a 2-coat system at 160 ft²/gal: ceil(440 × 2 ÷ 160) = 6 gallons. In retail kits: ceil(440 × 2 ÷ 250) = 4 two-car kits. At $50/gal the material runs about $300.

The same floor with a high-solids product covering 320 ft²/gal needs ceil(440 × 2 ÷ 320) = 3 gallons — half the material of the 160 ft² default, which is why checking the data sheet matters before budgeting. Pot life is the constraint the gallon math hides. Once mixed, two-part epoxy begins curing — typical pot life is 30 to 45 minutes in summer heat, less in direct sun. That is why rounding up matters: it is better to keep a sealed spare gallon than to rush the final 200 ft² and drag a half-cured roller through it. Mix whole kits, never partial batches by eye, and stage your tools before you start the clock.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much epoxy do I need for a 2-car garage?

A 20×20 ft garage is 400 ft²; with 10% waste, plan for 440 ft². A two-coat system at 160 ft²/gallon takes ceil(440 × 2 ÷ 160) = 6 gallons, or 4 retail 2-car kits rated at 250 ft² per kit.

How many square feet does a gallon of epoxy cover?

It depends on film thickness: 1 gallon covers 1,604 ft² at 1 mil wet, so about 160 ft² at 10 mils, 230 ft² at 7 mils, and 400 ft² at 4 mils. 100%-solids epoxies are typically applied at 8–12 mils per coat.

Why do I need two coats of epoxy?

Two coats build sufficient dry film thickness for durability and hide the concrete's color variations. Most garage systems pair a color/base coat with a clear topcoat; the 250 ft² rating on retail 2-car kits already assumes both coats together.

How much waste should I add for epoxy?

10% is the standard planning allowance — it covers roller absorption, edge work, tray loss, and the mixed batch you can't fully spread before pot life ends. On heavily sloped or porous concrete, 15% is safer since porous slabs absorb the first coat.

Can I use this calculator for epoxy countertops or table pours?

Yes, with the right coverage figure. Casting epoxies are poured thick — a 1/8 in pour is 125 mils, covering about 13 ft² per gallon. Set coats to 1 and enter the coverage for your pour depth; for a full flood coat on a table, thickness — not coats — drives volume.
