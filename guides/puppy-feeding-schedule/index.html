Puppy Feeding Schedule by Age: How Much and How Often

💡 27K searches/mo💰 CPC: $0.90⏱️ 8 min read

Every new puppy owner asks the same two questions — how much, and how often — and the answers are steadier than the internet makes them look. Veterinarians size a puppy's food as RER × an age factor: 3× resting calories under 4 months, 2× from 4 to 12 months. Meals run four a day, then three, then two. Here's the whole schedule, the portions behind it, and the handful of places where the standard advice bends.

Meal frequency steps down as the puppy's stomach grows and growth slows:

The schedule is doing two jobs. Obvious one: fuel. Less obvious: scheduled input is what makes house training work, because a puppy that eats at 7, 1, and 7 needs to go out on a rhythm you can learn. Free-feeding — leaving the bowl down — breaks both jobs and hides the single most useful early health signal a puppy has: a dog that suddenly skips a scheduled meal is a dog worth watching.

How much: the RER math behind the bag chart

Resting energy requirement — the calories a dog burns doing nothing — is 70 × (weight in kg)0.75. Growth multiplies it. Puppies under 4 months are building bone and tissue so fast they need about 3× RER; from 4 to 12 months that drops to 2×; large breeds still growing past a year sit around 1.6×. In calories per day:

To turn calories into cups, divide by your food's kcal-per-cup, printed on the bag (puppy formulas typically run 375–460). A 25-lb 4-month puppy at 865 kcal eats about 2.2 cups of a 400 kcal/cup food — roughly ¾ cup per meal across three meals. Two caveats keep the math honest: the puppy's current weight sets RER, not its adult weight, and the result is a starting ration, not a prescription. Adjust ±10% based on body condition, not the chart.

The large-breed exception that matters

For dogs headed over 50 pounds, the schedule's last steps stretch out: growth finishes at 18 months for large breeds and up to 24 for giants, and the multiplier holds at 1.6 through that window. The rule that matters more than any number, though, is keep them lean. Slow growth protects developing joints, and a large-breed puppy kept at body condition 4–5 out of 9 — ribs easily felt, waist visible — grows into sounder orthopedics than a roly-poly one fed to the top of every chart. Feed a large-breed puppy formula, which caps calcium; extra calcium doesn't build bigger bones, it builds wrong ones.

Treats, water, and the switch to adult food

Treats count against the daily total, and the ceiling is 10% of calories. On that 865-kcal day, that's about 85 kcal — fifteen 5-kcal training treats, which a single puppy class can burn through. Count them, or subtract a matching scoop from dinner.

Water is free-choice from day one; never ration it. And the puppy-food-to-adult-food switch lands around 12 months for small and medium breeds, 12–18 months for large, up to 24 for giants — done gradually over 7–10 days. Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I feed my puppy a day?

Start from RER — 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75 — and multiply by age: 3× under 4 months, 2× from 4 to 12 months, 1.6× for large breeds still growing past a year. A 10-lb 3-month puppy needs about 653 kcal a day; a 25-lb 4-month puppy about 865; a 60-lb 8-month Lab about 1,668. Convert to cups with your food's kcal-per-cup, typically 375–460 for puppy formulas.

How many meals a day does a puppy need?

Four meals to 3 months, three from 3 to 6 months, two after that. Toy-breed puppies under 3 months genuinely need the four-meal clock because their blood sugar dips between feedings. Scheduled meals also drive house training: scheduled input means predictable output.

Should puppies eat more if they seem hungry?

Not automatically — puppies beg like professionals, and overfeeding is the more common mistake. Check the ribs instead: easily felt with light pressure and a visible waist from the side means the portion is right. Adjust by 10% at a time, and satisfy the after-dinner begging act with training treats carved out of the daily total.

When should a puppy switch to two meals a day?

Around 6 months for small and medium breeds. Large and giant breeds often do better holding three meals until 8 to 10 months because their daily volume gets large and their joints benefit from steady nutrition rather than two big loads. Two meals is the floor — one meal a day isn't appropriate for any puppy.

How many treats can a puppy have per day?

Keep treats under 10% of daily calories. On an 865-kcal day that's about 85 kcal of treats — roughly 15 small training treats at 5 kcal each. Track them against the day's total, or puppy classes alone can add a meal's worth of calories nobody counted.
