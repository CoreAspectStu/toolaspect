When Is the FAFSA Deadline?

🎓 Financial aid⏱️ 6 min read

The FAFSA deadline is three deadlines wearing a trench coat. The federal one (June 30 after the school year) is almost impossible to miss. The state and school ones will quietly cost you five figures if you treat them the same way. Here's every layer, the 2026-27 dates, and the October filing strategy that beats them all.

What are the three FAFSA deadlines?

Every aid cycle runs on three separate clocks:

  • Federal deadline: 11:59 p.m. Central Time on June 30 after the school year ends. For 2026-27, that's June 30, 2027, with corrections accepted until September 12, 2027. This one gates Pell Grants, Direct Loans, and work-study.
  • State deadline: set by your state's grant agency, and wildly variable — from January 15 in Texas to "we close when the money's gone" in a dozen states.
  • College priority deadline: set school by school, often February through April, controlling institutional grants and scholarships that are the largest aid pool at many private colleges.

The pattern to internalize: the federal deadline is the last date, not the target. Everything valuable happens months earlier.

When does the FAFSA open, and what's the soft launch?

The 2026-27 FAFSA opened October 1, 2025, returning the cycle to its traditional schedule after a couple of delayed years. The 2027-28 form is expected around October 1, 2026. Recent openings have run as soft launches: the form goes live, occasionally pauses for maintenance in the first days, and stabilizes quickly. Hitting a pause doesn't hurt you or your place in line — come back the next day.

Why file in the first six weeks? Because the FAFSA pulls your tax data straight from the IRS for a year that's already filed, there's no reason to wait for tax season. And the biggest state and school pools are first-come.

What if you've already missed a deadline?

File anyway, immediately. The federal window runs to June 30, 2027, so Pell and federal loans survive a late filing, and some states accept applications well past their priority dates with reduced awards. You lose only what was strictly first-come or hard-closed. Students in the middle of a cycle can also file for the next year's form on October 1 and be first in line rather than last in the current one.

What does the FAFSA actually get you?

The form is the gateway to every federal need-based program and most state and school aid: Pell Grants (up to roughly $7,400 a year for 2025-26 award year), state grants, work-study, and Direct Loans. It also produces your SAI (Student Aid Index), the number schools use to build your package — you can preview yours with the SAI calculator before filing. And no, there's no income cutoff to file: even families who expect no need-based aid must file to qualify for federal loans and most merit awards.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the FAFSA deadline for 2026-27?

The federal deadline is June 30, 2027 at 11:59 p.m. Central Time, with corrections allowed until September 12, 2027. State deadlines are much earlier — Texas gives priority to forms processed by January 15, 2026, Missouri by February 2, and California by the March 2 postmark. Your college's own priority date can precede both.

When should I actually file the FAFSA?

In the first six to eight weeks after the form opens on October 1. There's no benefit to waiting for taxes to be filed, since the FAFSA pulls income data automatically from the IRS for the year that's already done. Filing early preserves eligibility for first-come state grants, institutional aid, and work-study, all of which can run out.

Can I submit the FAFSA after the deadline?

Yes, until the federal cutoff — federal Pell Grants and Direct Loans remain available for the whole cycle, and the FAFSA stays open until June 30, 2027. What you lose after state and school deadlines are those specific pools of money. Late is always better than never, but early is worth thousands.

What happens during the FAFSA soft launch?

In recent cycles the form has opened through a short soft-launch period: the site goes live, then pauses briefly for maintenance or hits form limits during peak hours, then fully stabilizes within days. If you hit a pause on day one, nothing is wrong with your application — return the next day. The 2026-27 form opened October 1, 2025, back on the traditional schedule.

Dates reflect the published 2026-27 cycle; state agencies revise deadlines occasionally — confirm yours on the official state or studentaid.gov pages before planning.
