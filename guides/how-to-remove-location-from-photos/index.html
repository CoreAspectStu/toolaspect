How to Remove Location Data From Photos (Before You Share)

🔒 7 min readUpdated October 1, 2026

Most phone photos store the GPS coordinates of the spot where they were taken, usually precise enough to pick out a single house. To remove them, turn off Location in the iPhone share sheet's Options, use Remove Properties on Windows or Remove Location Info in Mac Preview, or run the photo through our free image metadata remover, which builds a clean JPEG in your browser without uploading anything.

Do photos store your location?

If location access is turned on for your camera app, yes. Every photo gets a small block of hidden data called EXIF, and on phones that block usually includes latitude, longitude and often altitude. It sits alongside the capture time and phone model, and you never see any of it when you look at the picture.

That's fine while the photo stays in your library. It's a problem when the original file leaves your hands. A marketplace photo of your couch or a selfie sent to someone you met online can carry the exact spot where it was taken, and for most people that spot is home.

How precise is the GPS in a photo?

Coordinates are stored as decimal degrees, and each extra decimal place makes the position about ten times more precise. Here's what each level means in distance, measured along latitude (and along longitude at the equator, where degrees are widest):

Phones typically store more than enough precision to pinpoint a house. Even a fix that's off by a few meters indoors still lands on the right property.

How can you check if a photo has GPS data?

The quickest way is to open the photo in our EXIF viewer. It reads the file in your browser, lists everything it finds, and shows a warning when GPS coordinates are present. Nothing is uploaded.

You can also check on your computer:

  • Windows: right-click the file, choose Properties, then open the Details tab. Scroll down to the GPS section.
  • Mac: open the photo in Preview, then choose Tools > Show Inspector. A GPS tab only appears when the file has location data.

How do you remove location from a photo on iPhone?

There are three controls, each doing a different job.

  • Per share (iOS 13 and later): select the photo, tap Share, then tap Options at the top of the share sheet. Turn Location off and tap Done. That copy goes out without GPS, and the photo in your library keeps it.
  • Per photo: open the photo in Photos, swipe up or tap the Info button, then tap Adjust next to the location and choose No Location. The location is removed from that photo in your library.
  • For all future photos: go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Camera and pick Never. New photos won't be tagged at all. You'll lose the Places map in Photos, which is the trade-off.

How do you remove location from a photo on Android?

Every Android maker ships its own camera and gallery apps, so the menu names below may not match yours exactly.

To stop tagging new photos, open your Camera app's settings and look for an option called something like Location tags, Save location or GPS. Turn it off.

For photos you've already taken, Google Photos lets you open a photo, swipe up to see the details, and edit or remove the location. The wording shifts between versions. If your gallery app has no option, the browser method below works on any phone.

How do you remove location data on Windows?

Windows has a built-in cleaner that's easy to miss. Right-click the photo, choose Properties, and open the Details tab. At the bottom, click Remove Properties and Personal Information. Pick Create a copy with all possible properties removed and click OK. Windows saves a new copy next to the original. Share that one.

The other option in that dialog lets you tick individual fields, such as just the GPS ones, and remove them from the original. Some file types don't support editing properties, so check the Details tab afterwards.

How do you remove location data on a Mac?

Open the photo in Preview and choose Tools > Show Inspector. Click the GPS tab (the one with the location pin), then click Remove Location Info at the bottom. Save the file. The rest of the EXIF data stays, but the coordinates are gone.

If the photo lives in the Photos app, select it and choose Image > Location > Hide Location. When you export, also look at the export options and make sure location information isn't included.

Do social media sites strip location data?

Major networks such as Facebook, Instagram and X strip EXIF data from the copies they publish. That only covers the copy the platform shows, though. Plenty of other routes hand over the original file as it left your phone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can someone find my address from a photo?

If the file still has GPS coordinates, yes. Phones typically record location precisely enough to pick out a single house, and anyone with the original file can paste the coordinates into a map. Removing the location before you share closes that gap.

Does emailing or texting a photo remove the location?

Email attachments usually carry the original file, GPS included. Chat apps differ in how they handle ordinary photo sends, but the send as file or document option generally passes the original through untouched. If you aren't sure, strip the location first.

Do Instagram and Facebook show where my photo was taken?

Major networks such as Facebook, Instagram and X strip EXIF data from the copies they publish, so other people can't read GPS from a downloaded post. A location tag you add yourself is different. That one is shown on purpose.

Does removing metadata reduce photo quality?

It depends on the method. Our browser tool re-encodes the image as a JPEG at 92% quality, which is a second lossy compression pass. On normal photos the difference is very hard to see, but keep your original if you need every bit of quality or a PNG with transparency.

Will a photo turn sideways after I strip its metadata?

Not with our tool. Modern browsers apply the orientation tag when they load the image, so the rotation gets baked into the pixels of the clean JPEG. It displays upright without needing the tag.
