Does Converting HEIC to JPG Keep Metadata? We Tested 6 Ways

📄 6 min readUpdated October 22, 2026

Converting HEIC to JPG sometimes keeps the photo's metadata and sometimes wipes it, depending entirely on the converter. In our test, ImageMagick carried over the GPS location, capture date and all 45 EXIF tags, while browser-based conversion, including ours, removed every one. Here's what we found, how to check your own files, and how to keep or remove the location on purpose.

Does converting HEIC to JPG remove metadata?

Sometimes, and you can't tell from the file name. HEIC stores EXIF the same way JPG does: camera model, capture date and, on most phone photos, GPS coordinates accurate to a few metres. Whether that survives conversion is a choice the converter makes. Some copy it across on purpose. Others decode the picture to plain pixels and write a fresh file, and the metadata never makes it.

So we tested it. We built a 4717 × 3145 HEIC carrying a full 45-tag EXIF block, including GPS coordinates and the original capture date, and converted it six ways:

Two clear groups. Command-line tools that treat metadata as part of the photo, like ImageMagick, kept everything, including the location, in both JPG and PNG. Tools that go through a pixel buffer, like a browser canvas or Pillow's default save, dropped all of it. Pillow keeps it only when the code passes the EXIF through deliberately.

One note on method: we didn't have an original iPhone file to hand, so the test HEIC was made from a GPS-tagged camera photo, with its EXIF written into the HEIC the standard way. iPhone photos store EXIF in the same place, so converters read it the same way. Because the test file started life as a JPEG, its output sizes aren't typical: for photos shot as HEIC, the JPG copy is usually the bigger file.

Does our HEIC to JPG converter keep location data?

No. Our HEIC to JPG converter decodes the photo with libheif inside your browser, draws it onto a canvas and saves a new JPG or PNG from that. A canvas holds pixels and nothing else, so the result has no EXIF: no GPS, no capture date, no camera details. In our test the JPG had zero EXIF tags. Chrome added a standard sRGB color profile, which says nothing about you or where the photo was taken.

That's useful if you're about to post the photo publicly. It's a drawback if you're converting a photo library and want to keep dates for sorting. Photo apps that sort by date taken will fall back to the file date, which is the day you converted it.

What about converting on an iPhone, Mac or Windows PC?

Apple's own conversions are built to keep metadata, so a JPEG made by your iPhone during a transfer or share usually still carries the date and location. These are the settings that decide what leaves the phone:

How do I check whether my JPG still has GPS?

Open it in an EXIF viewer. If you see a GPS section with latitude and longitude, the location is there. If there's no GPS block, it's gone. A file with a capture date and camera model but no GPS has had the location stripped selectively, which some apps and share options do. The check takes seconds and runs in your browser, so the photo isn't uploaded.

How do I convert HEIC to JPG and keep the metadata?

Use a converter that copies EXIF on purpose. On a Mac, Preview's Export usually keeps it, and the Photos app's export dialog has an option to include location information. On Windows, or for batches, ImageMagick (magick photo.heic photo.jpg) kept every tag in our test. If you've already converted with a tool that dropped it, the metadata is still in the original HEIC. Keep the originals until you've checked a few converted files.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does converting HEIC to JPG remove location data?

It depends on the converter. In our test, ImageMagick kept the GPS location and all 45 EXIF tags, while browser-based conversion and Python Pillow's default save removed every tag. Check the converted file in an EXIF viewer to be sure.

Does ToolAspect's HEIC to JPG converter keep EXIF data?

No. It decodes the HEIC in your browser and saves a new JPG or PNG from a canvas, which holds only pixels. The output has no GPS, capture date or camera details. The photo is never uploaded.

Does the iPhone keep location when it converts HEIC to JPEG?

Usually, yes. Apple's conversions during transfer or sharing are built to keep metadata, including location. When sharing, tap Options at the top of the share sheet and turn off Location to send the photo without GPS.

How do I keep the date taken when converting HEIC to JPG?

Use a converter that copies EXIF, such as ImageMagick or a desktop photo app's export, and check a converted file in an EXIF viewer before doing a whole batch. Keep the original HEIC files until you've confirmed the dates came across.

Is a JPG converted from HEIC lower quality?

Slightly. HEIC to JPG is a lossy re-encode, so some detail is lost, though it's hard to see at high quality settings such as 0.9. For photos shot as HEIC, the JPG copy is also usually larger, because HEIC compresses more efficiently.
