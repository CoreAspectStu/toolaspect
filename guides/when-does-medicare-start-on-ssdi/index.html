When Does Medicare Start on SSDI?

📄 7 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Medicare on SSDI starts in your 25th month of SSDI entitlement. Add the five-month wait before SSDI itself begins and most people get Medicare about 29 months after they became disabled. People with ALS or kidney failure don't wait that long. Here's how to work out your own start date, what Medicare costs in 2026, and how to stay insured in the meantime.

Why do you have to wait 24 months for Medicare on SSDI?

Because the law says so. When Congress extended Medicare to people with disabilities in 1972, it added a waiting period to keep costs down and to cover only long-term disability. You become eligible for Medicare in your 25th month of entitlement to SSDI cash benefits.

The catch is that the clock doesn't start when you stop working. It starts when your SSDI entitlement begins, and that already comes after a separate five-month waiting period. Stack the two and most people wait about 29 months from the date they became disabled. That's two and a half years with no Medicare, usually at the point where medical bills are highest.

When will my Medicare actually start?

Count five full calendar months from your disability onset date to find your SSDI entitlement month. Then add 24 months. Here's how that works out for a few onset dates in the middle of a month:

Your award letter shows your entitlement month, which is the one to count from. The 24 months don't have to be in a row, either. If you were on SSDI before, went back to work, and then qualified again, earlier months can count toward the wait as long as you return within five years.

Long appeals have one upside. SSDI entitlement can reach back up to 12 months before the date you applied, so if your case took two years to approve, you may already be past the 24th month when the approval arrives. In that case Medicare can start right away, and Part A can even be dated back.

Who doesn't have to wait 24 months?

Two conditions skip the wait, and SSI works differently:

If you have kidney failure, you don't need to be on SSDI at all. ESRD Medicare is its own route, and it ends 12 months after dialysis stops or 36 months after a successful transplant, unless you qualify another way.

Do I have to sign up for Medicare on SSDI?

No. Enrollment is automatic. Social Security mails your Medicare card about three months before your 25th month, and you're enrolled in Part A and Part B. Part A is free. Part B isn't, so the card comes with the option to turn it down.

Think hard before declining Part B. It's only worth it if you have group coverage through a spouse's current job at a large employer. Otherwise you can face a late-enrollment penalty of 10% for each 12 months you went without it. One break for people under 65: the penalty is wiped when you turn 65 and get a fresh enrollment period.

Drug coverage (Part D) and Medicare Advantage are not automatic. You have a seven-month window around your 25th month to choose a plan.

So an SSDI check of $1,500 a month drops to $1,297.10 once the Part B premium comes out. If your income and savings are low, your state's Medicare Savings Program can pay that premium for you, and Extra Help lowers drug costs.

Supplemental Medigap policies are harder to get under 65. Federal law only guarantees them to people 65 and older. Some states require insurers to sell Medigap to younger people on disability, often at a higher price. Others don't, which leaves Medicare Advantage as the main way to cap your costs.

What can I do for health insurance while I wait?

  • COBRA: if you left a job with health insurance, COBRA normally runs 18 months. If Social Security finds you disabled within the first 60 days of COBRA, you can stretch it to 29 months, which is just about long enough to bridge the gap. The plan can charge up to 150% of the premium for the extra months, and you have to tell the plan within 60 days of your SSDI decision.
  • Medicaid: in states that expanded Medicaid, adults with low incomes qualify regardless of disability. Your SSDI amount counts as income, so check your state's limit.
  • Marketplace insurance: you can buy an ACA plan, with premium tax credits if your income qualifies. Once your premium-free Part A starts, you lose those credits, so plan to switch.

Does Medicare stop if I go back to work?

Not quickly. After your nine-month trial work period ends, Medicare continues for at least 93 more months, even if your SSDI cash payments stop because your earnings pass the SGA limit ($1,690 a month in 2026). You keep paying the Part B premium yourself once the checks stop. At full retirement age your SSDI becomes a retirement benefit and your Medicare just continues; see what happens to SSDI at full retirement age.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long after SSDI approval does Medicare start?

Medicare starts in your 25th month of SSDI entitlement, not 24 months after the approval letter. If your entitlement date is far in the past because of a long appeal, Medicare can start immediately after approval.

Is the Medicare waiting period 24 or 29 months?

Both numbers are right. The law sets a 24-month wait after SSDI entitlement begins, and SSDI itself starts after a five-month waiting period. Counted from the date you became disabled, most people wait about 29 months.

Do I have to pay for Medicare on SSDI?

Part A is free. Part B costs $202.90 a month in 2026 and is deducted from your SSDI check. A Medicare Savings Program can pay the Part B premium if your income and assets are low.

Can I get Medicare before 24 months on SSDI?

Only with ALS, which starts Medicare the same month as SSDI, or kidney failure, which qualifies under separate ESRD rules starting in the fourth month of dialysis or earlier with home dialysis or a transplant.

Do SSI recipients get Medicare?

Not through SSI. SSI is a needs-based program, so it doesn't lead to Medicare. Most states give SSI recipients Medicaid automatically or after a short application.
