SSI and LIHTC Housing: Does a Tax Credit Apartment Count?

📄 6 min readUpdated November 12, 2026

A tax credit (LIHTC) apartment almost never counts as income for SSI, because no subsidy is paid for you and SSA's rent rule only applies below $351.33 a month in 2026. The harder part is the rent. It's set from your area's median income, not yours, so without a voucher it can take half your $994 check or more.

Does SSI count a tax credit (LIHTC) apartment as income?

In the usual case, no. Nobody pays a subsidy on your behalf. The tax credit goes to the building's owner, and you owe the whole restricted rent yourself. SSA's housing exclusion, in manual section SI 00830.630, covers help paid under five federal housing laws. The tax code isn't one of them, so the exclusion that protects Section 8 tenants doesn't reach a plain tax credit unit.

What matters instead is SSA's rent rule. Under 20 CFR 416.1130(b), when your required rent is at least the presumed maximum value, it's a business arrangement and nothing is counted. That value is one-third of the federal SSI rate plus $20, which is $351.33 a month for 2026 and $363 for 2027.

Below it, the regulation says SSA counts the gap between your rent and that figure, or the market rent if that's lower. The cheapest tax credit units can land there. But SSA's manual, at SI 00835.380, tells staff to look into a rental subsidy only when someone in the household is the parent or child of the landlord or the landlord's spouse. A tax credit owner is normally a company, so on the manual's terms the question shouldn't come up.

Here's what we couldn't confirm. None of the SSA sections we read today names tax credit housing. The manual treats a rent-control ceiling as the market rent, but doesn't say a tax credit limit works the same way. If SSA cuts your check over a low tax credit rent, ask for the reason in writing and point to part B of SI 00835.380.

Either way, tell SSA your new address and rent. If you're saving for a deposit and getting near the $2,000 resource limit, the net worth calculator adds things up.

How is rent set in a tax credit apartment?

By the unit, not by your income. Under 26 U.S.C. 42(g)(2), gross rent can't be more than 30% of an imputed income limit. A studio is priced as if one person lives there, and other units as if 1.5 people live in each bedroom. The limit is a share of area median income, such as 50% or 60%.

We ran that formula for three example area medians. They're made-up round numbers for a family of four, not real counties. We used HUD's family size factors (70% for one person, 80% for two) and the IRS method of averaging those two limits for a one-bedroom. The percentage is the share of a $994 SSI check.

How do you look up the real limit for your county?

HUD publishes Multifamily Tax Subsidy Project (MTSP) income limits each year on huduser.gov. The fiscal 2026 set took effect May 1, 2026. Find your county, take the one-person limit for a studio or the average of the one-person and two-person limits for a one-bedroom, multiply by 0.30 and divide by 12.

Your result won't match our table exactly. HUD rounds the 50% limits up to the nearest $50 and applies floors and caps, and a building's limit never drops below its first-year level. Ask the manager which limit your unit carries and what the utility allowance is.

HUD capped fiscal 2026 limit increases at 10%, and SSI's rise for 2027 is $35, about 3.5%.

What are 20% to 80% units?

Since 2018, an owner can pick the average income test. Each unit gets its own limit, from 20% to 80% of area median in steps of ten, and the building's average can't top 60%. Here's a studio at our $80,000 example.

Can you use a Section 8 voucher in a tax credit building?

Yes. Each tax credit building is bound by a recorded agreement that bars the owner from refusing you because you hold a Section 8 voucher (42(h)(6)(B)(iv)). The voucher payment isn't counted in gross rent, so your share follows your income: about $284 to $288 a month on $994, by the formula in our Section 8 guide. SSA excludes the voucher, as our HUD housing guide explains.

What about a USDA Section 538 apartment?

Section 538 is a USDA loan guarantee for rural rental buildings. It doesn't lower your rent the way Section 515 rental assistance does. Under 7 CFR 3565.203, a unit's rent, tenant-paid utilities included, can't pass 30% of 115% of area median income, adjusted for family size. The building's average rent can't pass 30% of 100%.

At our $80,000 example, before any family size adjustment, that's $2,300 a month for one unit and a $2,000 average. The first figure is 2.3 times an SSI check. USDA's website refused our requests today, so we couldn't read how its handbook applies the adjustment.

If the building also carries tax credits or rental assistance, each program's cap applies and the lowest one governs.

For SSI, Section 538 sits in title V of the Housing Act of 1949. SI 00830.630 lists that title, counts guaranteed loans as housing assistance and tells staff to assume anything involving USDA's Rural Housing Service is excluded. We found no SSA example that names Section 538. The Rural Development voucher is a separate program.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does living in a tax credit apartment reduce SSI?

Usually not. A required rent of at least $351.33 a month in 2026 is a business arrangement under 20 CFR 416.1130. Below that, SSA's manual has staff look into it only when the landlord is your parent or child.

Is LIHTC rent based on my income?

No. It's capped at 30% of an income limit tied to the unit's size and its share of area median income.

Can a tax credit apartment refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Not for that reason. Under 26 U.S.C. 42(h)(6)(B)(iv), the building's recorded agreement bars refusing to lease to someone because they hold a Section 8 voucher.

How much is rent in a USDA Section 538 apartment?

Section 538 alone caps a unit's rent at 30% of 115% of area median income. That's far above an SSI check, so ask if the building also has tax credits or rental assistance.

Can SSI income be too high for a tax credit unit?

For the lowest tier, yes. A year of federal SSI is $11,928 in 2026. That's over the one-person limit for a 20% unit unless the area's median income is about $85,200 or more.
