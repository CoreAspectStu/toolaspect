How Much Does Lumber Cost?

⏱️ 6 min read

Framing lumber traded around $521 per thousand board feet in late August 2026 — near the low end of its recent range — which shakes out to $4 to $6 for an untreated 2×4×8 stud at retail, $4.20 to $5.00 pressure-treated, and $5 to $9 per board foot for hardwoods like oak and maple. What you actually pay depends on species, grade, treatment, and how the commodity market felt that quarter. Here's how those layers stack, and the board-foot math to price any order.

How much does a 2×4 cost?

The 2×4×8 stud is the benchmark — every yard stocks it and its price tracks the commodity closely. Current retail, straight from the big boxes:

Commodity value of the wood~$2.785.33 board feet at $521/MBF — the rest is milling, drying, freight, retail

That commodity line explains the sticker. A stud contains 5.33 board feet (2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12), and the mill-gate composite price of about $521 per thousand board feet puts $2.78 of wood in the board. Retail adds sawing, kiln drying, planing, shipping, and the store's margin — roughly a 50%-to-115% markup over the raw commodity, which is normal for dimensional lumber and worth knowing when prices spike and everyone blames the trees.

Hardwood pricing runs on a different ladder entirely. Wholesale FAS red oak moved around $680-$750 per thousand board feet in 2026, but the retail rack prices it per board foot: red oak 4/4 at roughly $5-$6, hard maple 4/4 at $6-$9, and figured maple — quilted, curly — from $9 into the $20s and beyond. Grade matters as much as species; FAS (First and Seconds) yields long clear cuts, #1 Common is the cabinet-shop workhorse at a discount, and the price gap between them routinely runs 30-40%.

What does a whole order cost?

Board feet make any order priceable: BF = thickness × width × length ÷ 12, boards = linear feet ÷ board length rounded up, and the order is boards × board price. Priced at the retail equivalent of $0.80-$1.13 per board foot, with 10% waste added to the footage:

The joist line is a real 12×16 deck frame minus beams and posts, and it shows why lumber dominates framing budgets: 208 board feet before you've cut a single board. Buy by the board foot whenever your supplier allows it — a $1.10/BF quote on that joist package is $229, and comparing BF prices across suppliers is the only apples-to-apples comparison there is.

Why do lumber prices swing so much?

Lumber is a commodity, and it trades like one. Futures have ranged from the low-$100s to above $800 per thousand board feet across recent decades, with historic spikes during building booms when mill capacity simply can't chase demand. The 2026 market sits near the calm end: futures around $567 in early September, drifting down 2.2% over the month, with analysts expecting a $500-$600 average and spikes above $600 if tariff policy fully lands on Canadian imports. For a homeowner buying 30 studs, a $50/MBF move is about $8 on the order. For a builder framing 40 houses, it's a margin event — which is why framing bids carry lumber-price clauses and why your deck quote is only good for 30 days.

How can you pay less for lumber?

A few levers that don't involve waiting out the market. Buy full bundles when the order is big enough — the tally tag math is the same nominal-size board-foot formula this guide uses, and bundle pricing beats picking through the rack. Choose lengths that minimize offcuts: a plan needing 100 linear feet of plates takes thirteen 8-footers or ten 10-footers, and the 10s leave fewer joints to cut and stagger. Compare big-box per-board prices against a local yard's per-thousand quote once orders pass a few hundred board feet — the yard usually wins. And skip pressure-treated everywhere except genuine ground contact and weather exposure; the treatment premium buys nothing indoors. Whatever the order, add 10-15% for waste and culls rather than making a second trip on a Saturday.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a 2x4 cost in 2026?

An untreated SPF 2x4x8 stud runs about $4.25 at Menards, $4.65 for Home Depot's premium-cut stud, and up to $6 budgeted by contractors. Pressure-treated 2x4x8 runs $4.20-$5.00. A 2x4x8 contains 5.33 board feet, so at the late-2026 commodity price of about $520 per thousand board feet, the wood itself costs roughly $2.78 before retail markups.

How much is lumber per board foot?

Commodity framing lumber traded near $521 per thousand board feet in late August 2026, or about $0.52 per board foot, with futures around $567. Retail dimensional lumber works out to roughly $0.80-$1.13 per board foot once sawn, dried, and shelved. Hardwoods at retail run $5-$9 per board foot for oak and maple.

Why do lumber prices change so much?

Lumber is a traded commodity, and futures have swung between the low-$100s and high-$800s per thousand board feet across recent decades, with extreme spikes during building booms. Housing demand, mill capacity, Canadian duties, and tariffs all move the price; analysts in 2026 expect framing lumber to average $500-$600 with spikes above $600 if tariffs fully land.

How much extra lumber should I buy?

Add 10-15% for framing waste, offcuts, and culls — if the plan calls for 100 linear feet, buy 110-115. Enter the inflated figure in the lumber calculator so the cost estimate reflects the real order, not the drawing.

Do I use nominal or actual lumber dimensions for pricing?

Use nominal — the size you order, like 2x4. Board-foot pricing conventionally uses nominal dimensions even though a surfaced 2x4 actually measures about 1.5 x 3.5 inches. The board feet formula is thickness x width x length ÷ 12.
