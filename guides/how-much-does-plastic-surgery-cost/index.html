How Much Does Plastic Surgery Cost in 2026?

💉 ~50K searches/mo💰 CPC: $8⏱️ 8 min read

The honest totals — surgeon plus facility plus anesthesia — run $6,500–$12,000 for breast augmentation, $5,000–$15,000 for a rhinoplasty, and $8,000–$15,000 for a tummy tuck, nationally. Your state moves the band ±20%, the surgeon's reputation another −12% to +15%. Here's the table, the three-fee anatomy of every quote, and the questions that keep a "cheap" quote from becoming an expensive revision.

The three fees hiding in every quote

Ask any office "how much is a breast augmentation" and you'll get one of three numbers, and which one you get matters more than the number itself:

  • The surgeon's fee — the number ASPS publishes averages for, and the one most websites quote. It's often barely half the total.
  • The facility fee — the operating room, nursing staff, and supplies. Hospital ORs run roughly 25% above accredited surgery centers; office-based ORs price below both.
  • Anesthesia — typically $600–$1,000 per hour of operating time, billed by a separate anesthesiology practice.

A quote for "breast augmentation $4,900" and a quote for "$9,800 all-in" can describe the same surgery. Always ask for the second number in writing.

When insurance actually pays

Almost never for cosmetic intent — but three categories cross into covered territory: breast reduction with documented back, shoulder, or neck pain (insurers typically want photos, symptom history, sometimes physical therapy records), upper eyelid surgery when a visual-field test shows obstruction, and septoplasty for breathing when it rides along with a cosmetic rhinoplasty. Reconstruction after cancer or trauma is a different billing universe entirely. Documentation is the game: the surgeon's office fights the prior-authorization battle, but only if your records support it.

Financing: the trap in the brochure

Most practices hand you a CareCredit-style application with "0% for 12 months" on it. Two things to know: that 0% is deferred, not waived — miss the payoff window and interest at 26%+ retroactively applies to the entire original balance; and the approved credit limit often covers only the surgeon's fee, leaving the facility bill as a surprise. A general personal loan at 8–15% APR with a fixed payoff is usually the saner structure — our loan calculator prices the payment before you sign anything. Best option of all, when the calendar allows: save for six to twelve months. Elective surgery is the rare purchase with zero deadline pressure.

The questions that protect you

  1. Is this number all-in — surgeon, facility, anesthesia, garments, labs?
  2. Are you certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery? (Verify yourself at abplsurg.org — "board certified" alone can mean a different board.)
  3. Is the facility accredited (AAAASF, AAAHC, or JCAHO), and who provides anesthesia credentials?
  4. What's your revision rate and policy — if I need a touch-up, who pays?
  5. Who handles complications, and would a hospital visit bill separately?

That list costs nothing and filters out most of the market's bad actors. For BBLs specifically — statistically the highest-risk routine cosmetic procedure — surgeon selection is the one variable you should never optimize for price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost of plastic surgery?

Most common surgical procedures land between $3,500 and $20,000 all-in. The midpoints: breast augmentation $9,250, rhinoplasty $10,000, liposuction $6,250 per area, tummy tuck $11,500, facelift $14,500, eyelid surgery $5,250, breast lift $9,250, breast reduction $10,000, BBL $10,000, and mommy makeover $17,500. These are national totals — surgeon plus facility plus anesthesia — not just the surgeon's fee.

Why did my consult quote differ from the price I saw online?

Because most published averages are surgeon fees only, and the surgeon's fee is roughly half the bill. The facility fee (operating room, staff, supplies) and anesthesia (typically $600-$1,000 per hour) ride on top. Add pre-op labs, garments, and prescriptions and the all-in number commonly runs 1.5 to 2 times the advertised figure.

Is plastic surgery cheaper in some states?

Yes — price levels span roughly ±20% around the national band. California and New York sit about 20% above; Massachusetts and Washington 8-15% above; Texas, Florida, Georgia, and North Carolina a few percent below; Utah, Tennessee, Michigan, and Ohio about 10% below. Metro matters as much as state: Beverly Hills is not California average.

Can you finance plastic surgery?

Most practices offer third-party financing (CareCredit and similar), typically 0% teaser APRs for 6-24 months that convert to 26%+ retroactively if you miss the payoff window. A general personal loan at 8-15% is often the saner instrument — our loan calculators price both. The safer move is saving for 6-12 months: elective surgery has no deadline.

What questions should I ask before paying a deposit?

Ask for the all-in written number (surgeon, facility, anesthesia, garments, labs); whether the surgeon is certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery (verify it yourself, don't take the website's word); whether the facility is accredited; what the revision policy is and who pays if you need one; and who handles complications — hospital ERs bill separately from your surgeon.
