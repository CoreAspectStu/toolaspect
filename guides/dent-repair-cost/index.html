How Much Does It Cost to Fix a Dent in 2026?

🚙 ~20K+ searches/mo💰 CPC: $4⏱️ 7 min read

Dent repair has two price lists, and which one you're on depends on a test you can do with your eyes. Paint intact? Paintless dent repair runs $75–$500 by size, $100–$200 for a quarter-size ding. Paint cracked, creased sharp, or metal stretched? Body-shop pricing: $150–$400 to fill and blend, $400–$900 to repair and respray a door, and up to $4,000 to replace a welded quarter panel. Here's both lists, the size-by-size table, and the insurance math that decides whether you file or pay.

First, the two-minute paint test

Look at the dent in raking light, angled sunlight or a flashlight held flat against the panel. You're checking the paint film, not the metal:

  • Paint smooth and unbroken, dent shallow with soft edges: PDR candidate. The tech massages the metal back out from behind with rods, or pulls it with glued tabs, and the factory paint never comes off.
  • Any crack, chip, or white stress line at the crease: conventional repair. Once the film is broken, flexing the metal back would flake paint off the dent's ridge.
  • Sharp crease, torn metal, or dent sitting on a body line or panel edge: conventional repair, and the quote depends on which panel (see the second table).

The pattern to notice: doors, fenders, and hoods unbolt, so replacing one is a parts-and-paint job. Quarter panels and roofs are welded into the unibody, so "replace" means cutting the old panel out, welding the new one in, and blending paint into the neighbors, triple the price of the same dent one panel forward. The insurance math, honestly

Dents fall under comprehensive coverage (or collision, if you hit something). The typical comprehensive deductible runs $250 to $1,000. Run the numbers on a $200–$384 two-ding PDR job against a $500 deductible: the claim pays nothing until the estimate clears $500, and even a $900 door respray against a $1,000 deductible pays $0. Meanwhile a claim can nudge your rates for years, check what a surcharge actually costs with the rate increase calculator before filing anything marginal.

When claiming makes sense: damage that clears the deductible by a wide margin, especially multi-panel hail. Hail is its own insurance-claim process, insurers increasingly push PDR for it because it's cheaper than conventional repair, so the same size table above drives the estimate.

Ways people get burned

  • Pushing the dent out from behind with a plunger or rod: metal has memory, and over-stretching it turns a $150 PDR fix into a body-shop job.
  • DIY fill-and-spray kits on a visible panel: a rattle-can blend on a metallic paint is visible from 20 feet and costs more to redo than doing it right.
  • "PDR-able" quotes given without seeing the dent: legit techs quote from photos or in person, and tell you when it's not a candidate.
  • Ignoring a dent that holds water: a crease that traps water rusts from the inside, which turns a $400 respray into panel replacement.
  • Forgetting the bumper: plastic bumper scuffs and tears are priced differently from metal dents, the bumper repair calculator covers that tier separately.

Does fixing it pay at resale?

Rough rule: on cars under about five years old, visible unrepaired dents cost more in trade-in value than the repair would have, appraisers deduct for every visible flaw, and a dented panel invites the buyer's diminished-value arithmetic. On an older commuter, a cheap PDR fix ($100–$200) before listing is worth it; a $900 respray on a $4,000 car usually isn't. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to fix a dent in a car?

With intact paint, a paintless repair runs $75-$500 depending on size, $100-$200 for a quarter-size ding. With cracked or creased paint, body-shop pricing takes over: $150-$400 to fill and blend, $400-$900 to repair and respray a door, and $2,000-$4,000 to cut out and replace a welded quarter panel.

Is paintless dent repair cheaper than a body shop?

Dramatically, when the paint is intact. PDR on a 2-3 inch door dent runs $200-$350 versus $400-$900 for conventional repair and respray on the same panel, roughly a 40-70% saving published across the PDR industry. The catch is eligibility: PDR needs undamaged paint, reachable metal, and a dent that isn't sharply creased.

Should I claim a dent on my insurance?

Almost never for a single dent. PDR bands of $100-$500 sit below typical comprehensive deductibles of $250-$1,000, so the claim would pay little or nothing while a comprehensive claim can nudge future rates. Claim when damage clearly clears the deductible, multi-panel hail being the classic case, and get a PDR hail estimate first since many insurers prefer it to conventional repair.

Does a small dent hurt my car's value?

Small unrepaired dents show up at trade-in: appraisers price visible body damage into the offer, and on newer cars a dented panel starts the diminished-value conversation. On a 10-year-old commuter, a $150 ding you leave alone costs less than fixing it. On a 3-year-old car, a $200 PDR fix usually protects more than $200 of resale value.
