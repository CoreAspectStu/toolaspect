How Much Paint Do I Need? Gallons by Room Size

🧮 7 min readUpdated September 28, 2026

Most bedrooms need about 2 gallons of paint for two coats on the walls. The math is square feet × coats ÷ coverage per gallon. A 12 × 14 ft room with 8 ft ceilings has about 365 sq ft of paintable wall once you take off a door and two windows, and 365 × 2 ÷ 400 = 1.8 gallons. Round up to 2.

How do you work out how much paint you need?

There are only three numbers in the whole calculation: the area you're painting, the number of coats, and how far one gallon spreads.

Gallons = square feet × coats ÷ coverage per gallon

Then round up to what the store actually sells. That's the same formula our paint calculator runs, and it's worth knowing by hand because the inputs are where people go wrong, not the math.

  1. Measure the perimeter. Add up the length of every wall. A 12 × 14 ft room has a perimeter of 2 × (12 + 14) = 52 ft.
  2. Multiply by the wall height. With 8 ft ceilings that's 52 × 8 = 416 sq ft.
  3. Take off doors and windows. Allow about 21 sq ft for a standard door and 15 sq ft for an average window. One door and two windows brings it to 416 − 21 − 30 = 365 sq ft.
  4. Multiply by coats, divide by coverage. Two coats at 400 sq ft per gallon: 365 × 2 ÷ 400 = 1.8 gallons.

So you buy 2 gallons and have a little left for touch-ups.

How far does a gallon of paint actually go?

Most interior paint cans quote somewhere between 250 and 400 sq ft per gallon. The top of that range assumes smooth, primed, previously painted drywall and a decent roller. Real walls often come in lower.

  • Texture eats paint. Orange peel, knockdown and popcorn finishes have more surface area than flat drywall. Plan on 300 sq ft per gallon, or less for heavy texture.
  • Bare surfaces soak it up. New drywall, patched areas and raw wood absorb the first coat. Prime them first or expect the first coat to cover a lot less.

Do you need one coat or two?

Plan on two. One coat can work when you're repainting the same color, or a very close shade, over a clean wall in good shape. Everything else needs two for even color and sheen: a noticeable color change, patched spots, or a flat paint going over an eggshell.

Big jumps are harder. Covering a dark red or navy with a light color can take a primer coat plus two topcoats. A tinted primer, close to the new color, usually saves you a third coat. Should you buy gallons, quarts or a 5-gallon bucket?

A quart is a quarter of a gallon and covers about 100 sq ft at 400 sq ft per gallon. Quarts are good for trim, a small bathroom or topping up a job that's a little over a round number. They cost a lot more per ounce, though, so four quarts is almost never a better deal than one gallon.

A 5-gallon bucket covers roughly 2,000 sq ft per coat at 400 sq ft per gallon. That's a whole-house job or several rooms in one color. It's usually cheaper per gallon than singles and has one more advantage: every drop is the same batch, so there's no color shift between cans.

If you do buy several single gallons of the same color, pour them all into one big bucket and mix before you start. Painters call this boxing. It evens out tiny differences between cans, which otherwise show up as a faint line where you opened a new one.

How much does it cost to paint a room yourself?

Paint is the easy part to budget. At $40 a gallon, which is the calculator's default and a fair mid-range price, a typical 12 × 14 bedroom costs $80 for the walls. Add a gallon of ceiling paint and maybe a quart of trim paint and you're close to $125 to $150 in paint.

Rollers, brushes, tape and drop cloths can cost as much again on a first job, but they last. The bottom line

Measure the walls, subtract doors and windows, multiply by two coats and divide by 350 to 400. Most bedrooms land at 2 gallons. Round up, box your cans together and keep a labeled jar of the leftover for touch-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

About 2 gallons for the walls. A 12 × 12 room with 8 ft ceilings has 384 sq ft of wall. Take off a door and two windows and you're at roughly 333 sq ft. Two coats at 400 sq ft per gallon come to 1.67 gallons, so buy 2. The ceiling needs another gallon.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior paints quote 250 to 400 sq ft per gallon. You'll get close to 400 on smooth, primed walls, and 300 or less on textured or porous surfaces. If you're unsure, plan on 350.

How much paint do I need for two coats?

Double the area, then divide by the coverage. For 400 sq ft of wall at 400 sq ft per gallon, one coat takes 1 gallon and two coats take 2. Most jobs need two coats for even color, especially when you're changing color.

Is one gallon of paint enough for a bedroom?

Usually not for the walls. A gallon gives one coat on about 400 sq ft, and even a small 10 × 10 bedroom has about 284 sq ft of paintable wall, which needs about 1.4 gallons for two coats. One gallon is often enough for the ceiling, though.

Should I subtract windows and doors when calculating paint?

Yes, for anything bigger than a closet. Allow about 21 sq ft per standard door and 15 sq ft per window. Skip small items like outlets and switches, since they make no real difference.
