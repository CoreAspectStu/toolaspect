Patreon Fees 2026: What Patreon Really Takes

💰 Creator monetization⏱️ 8 min read

Ask a creator what Patreon takes and you'll hear one number: "8%," or lately "10%." Both incomplete. Patreon's cut is a stack of fees — platform fee, payment processing, sometimes currency conversion, sometimes Apple — and on a $1 tier the stack can reach 42.9% of the payment. Here's every fee and the math for what lands in your balance.

How much does Patreon take?

Two fees apply to every membership payment. First, the platform fee: 10% on the standard plan, or 5%, 8%, or 12% on legacy plans (more on that split below). Second, payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per charge for creators whose payout currency is USD. Patreon calculates the platform fee on the payment excluding sales tax, but processing on the total including any tax collected from the member.

Run it on a $5 tier at 10%: platform takes $0.50, processing takes $5 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.445, so $0.94 total. You keep $4.06 per member per month — an effective fee of 18.9%, nearly double the headline 10%. At $25 the effective rate falls to 14.1%, because the fixed 30 cents matters less against a bigger payment.

What changed on August 4, 2025?

Patreon moved new creators to a standard 10% platform fee. If you published your page after August 4, 2025, that's your plan. Pages published on or before that date keep legacy pricing — 5% (Lite), 8% (Pro), or 12% (Premium) — but only while they stay continuously published. Unpublish your page, for any reason, and you're moved to the standard 10% plan when you republish. Patreon's own guidance is to use the pause tool rather than unpublishing if you want to keep legacy rates.

Two adjacent details. The Founders rate — 5% for creators who joined before May 7, 2019 and never upgraded — still exists. And separately from fees, Patreon is migrating all creators off per-creation billing to subscription billing by November 1, 2026 — a billing-model change, not a fee change, though it lands in the same settings page.

How does payment processing work?

Processing is charged per successful charge at a rate that depends on payout currency (2.9% + $0.30 is the USD rate). Legacy plans have a second rate: tiers at $3 or less pay a micropayment rate of 5% + $0.10, because a flat 30-cent fee on a $1 pledge would be brutal. On the standard plan that rate is gone — 2.9% + $0.30 applies regardless of amount.

One quiet detail: when a member owes sales tax or VAT, processing is calculated on the tax-inclusive total. Where local law requires it, Patreon also collects VAT or GST on the platform fee itself. Neither shows up in headline percentages; both nudge real costs above sticker rates.

Why do cheap tiers cost so much more?

The fixed $0.30 component — on a $1 tier it's 30 cents on the dollar before percentages even start. Effective total fee by tier price, USD, no tax, conversion, or iOS mixed in:

What about Apple's 30% cut?

Since November 2024, new memberships started inside the Patreon iOS app run through Apple's in-app purchase system, which takes 30% of the price. Patreon skips its own processing fee on those transactions, but the platform fee still applies. It's the most expensive way to acquire a member: at a $5 tier with 10% platform, an iOS member costs $1.50 to Apple plus $0.50 to Patreon — you keep $3.00 instead of $4.06.

There's an American exception: after the Epic v. Apple rulings, US fans can complete purchases through Patreon's mobile web checkout, which skips the 30% entirely. Wherever you link to your page, sending fans to the browser instead of the app changes your revenue per member.

What else comes off the top?

Currency conversion: 2.5% of the full payment (tax included) whenever a member pays in a currency different from your payout currency — refunded if the member refunds. Payout fees: when your balance transfers out, Patreon deducts a transaction fee that varies by method and currency. Direct bank transfer adds nothing extra from Patreon; PayPal and Payoneer may add their own conversion or withdrawal costs. Your balance already reflects platform, processing, and conversion fees.

How do you pay less in Patreon fees?

  • Sell fewer, bigger tiers. The fee stack is regressive — $25 costs you 14.1%, five $5 memberships cost you 18.9% for the same revenue. Consolidating perks into a higher tier cuts your effective rate.
  • Link to the web, not the app. iOS-originated memberships hand Apple 30% forever; web checkout keeps that money in the transaction.
  • Stay published if you're legacy. The pause tool preserves 5–8% pricing; unpublishing forfeits it to the standard 10% on republish.
  • Price in your payout currency when your audience allows. A 2.5% conversion fee on every foreign-currency member is real money.
  • Check unit costs before discounting. A $1 supporter tier loses 42.9 cents on the dollar on the standard plan and may belong in a free newsletter instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Patreon's fee 10% or 8%?

Depends entirely on when you published. Pages published after August 4, 2025 pay the standard 10%; pages continuously published on or before that date keep legacy pricing at 5% (Lite), 8% (Pro), or 12% (Premium).

What percentage does Patreon take after fees?

Platform plus processing together run 13.5% to 18.9% on a $5–$50 tier at the standard 10% plan, and worse on cheap tiers — 42.9% at $1. Add 2.5% for foreign-currency members and 30 percentage points of Apple's fee for iOS-app memberships.

Does Patreon charge the fee on sales tax?

The platform fee excludes sales tax; processing is calculated on the total payment including any tax collected from the member. Where required, Patreon also collects VAT/GST on the platform fee itself.

Do patrons pay Patreon's fees?

The fees come out of the creator's share — deducted from what would otherwise be your balance, not added to the patron's price. Patrons see the tier price plus any applicable sales tax.
