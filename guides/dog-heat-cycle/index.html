How Often Do Dogs Go Into Heat?

🐕 8 min read🧮 Prediction formula included

The dog heat cycle confuses owners for one reason: it doesn't follow a season. Cats come in and out of heat on daylight cues, but a dog sets her own clock, cycling every 6 to 12 months year-round. Here's how the cycle works, the day-9-to-14 fertile window breeders plan around, and how to predict her next one from her last.

What is the dog heat cycle?

"Heat" is the fertile phase of the estrus cycle, the whole loop running from one heat to the next. Intact females start at 6-10 months old in small breeds and as late as 18-24 months in giants, then repeat on their personal interval for life unless spayed. Unlike livestock, there's no breeding season — a Labrador can cycle in January or July.

The full cycle has four stages, and only two of them are visible to most owners:

StageLengthSigns
Proestrus~9 days (3-17)Vulva swells, bloody discharge, attracts males, refuses to mate
Estrus~9 days (3-21)Discharge pinkish/straw, stands and "flags" for males, fertile
Diestrus~60 daysInterest ends whether or not she conceived; false pregnancy signs possible
Anestrus~4-5 monthsEverything quiet; body resets for the next cycle

Add it up — roughly 18 days of visible heat, 60 days of diestrus, 4-5 months of anestrus — and you get the 6-to-12-month interval, which is exactly what the dog heat cycle calculator projects from.

How often will MY dog cycle?

Breed size is the best first guess:

  • Small breeds (Chihuahuas, toys): every 5-8 months — some seem to be in heat constantly
  • Medium breeds (Beagles, Border Collies): every 6-8 months, the "twice a year" textbook rhythm
  • Large breeds (Labs, Shepherds): every 6-12 months
  • Giant breeds (Great Danes, Newfoundlands): about once a year, sometimes less

Two outliers cycle annually regardless of size: the Basenji, which comes into season in autumn like a wild canid, and the Tibetan Mastiff. And once a bitch matures — usually by her third cycle — her personal interval becomes remarkably consistent, which is why breeders keep a heat diary and average the last two or three intervals rather than trusting a breed chart.

When can she actually get pregnant?

Not during the bloody part most owners panic about. Heavy bleeding is proestrus, and she's not fertile or willing yet. The risk window is estrus, roughly days 9 to 14 counting from the first sign of swelling and discharge, when the discharge lightens, she becomes receptive, and ovulation happens.

The practical rule every vet repeats: assume the entire 3-4 weeks is dangerous. Sperm survives for days inside the tract, some girls ovulate on day 7, others on day 21, and a determined pair can mate through a fence gap or a diaper in about ninety seconds. If an accidental tie happens, call your vet within a few days — mismate injections exist but carry risks, and an ovariohysterectomy later is also an option early on.

Breeders flip the goal: instead of avoiding the window, they confirm it. Progesterone blood testing at the vet pins ovulation to within a day or two, which is how successful breedings happen on single ties. Calendar math narrows the testing days; chemistry makes the call.

How do I track cycles without losing my mind?

A note on your phone with three data points per cycle beats memory: first day of swelling/discharge, first day of receptivity, and total days until it ended. Do that for two or three cycles and you'll know her interval well enough to predict the next heat within a couple of weeks. The dog age calculator is worth a stop too, since cycle intervals stretch as dogs age — a senior bitch might stretch from 6 months to 9 or 10, while still being perfectly healthy.

Management during the heat itself: dog diapers indoors, leash-only toilet breaks (never unattended in a fenced yard — males will find a way), and skip the dog park for the full 3-4 weeks. Walk at odd hours if intact males roam your neighborhood. The smell carries roughly a mile, and suitors start showing up days before discharge is visible.

When should a cycle worry you?

Most irregularity in young dogs is normal — first heats can be silent (swelling with no visible discharge) or split, where proestrus starts, pauses, and restarts weeks later. But flag these for your vet:

  • No heat by 24 months in a dog that should have cycled by breed norms
  • More than 12 months between cycles in a previously regular dog (hypothyroidism is a common cause)
  • Discharge lasting past 3-4 weeks, or foul-smelling discharge at any point
  • Drinking lots of water + lethargy + vulvar discharge weeks after a heat — the classic pyometra presentation, a uterine infection that is a true emergency and kills fast
  • Cycles under 4 months apart persistently

Pyometra deserves the emphasis: it typically hits in the 4-8 weeks after a heat, when progesterone from diestrus primes the uterus. If you're ever choosing between "wait and see" and the emergency vet on a recently-in-heat female acting sick, go. Treatment usually means an emergency spay, and the vet visit cost calculator can help you brace for the bill — emergency surgery runs well above a routine spay.

Does heat timing matter for spaying?

Generally, vets prefer to spay 2-3 months after a heat ends rather than right after one. During and just after a cycle, the uterus is engorged and blood vessels enlarged, which makes the surgery longer and riskier. There's no fertility benefit to "one litter first" — that's myth — but large-breed dogs do benefit developmentally from waiting until maturity, a timing conversation worth having with your vet per breed. Keep in mind a dog can be spayed while in heat if needed; it just costs more and carries slightly more risk.

None of this replaces your vet: cycle oddities, breeding plans, and spay timing all have individual factors a checklist can't see. Treat this as the framework and your vet as the decision-maker.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often do dogs go into heat?

Every 6 to 12 months for most dogs. Small breeds average 5-8 months apart, medium breeds 6-8, large breeds 6-12, and giant breeds like Great Danes sometimes cycle only once a year. Basenjis and Tibetan Mastiffs are famous once-a-year breeds. Individual dogs settle into a consistent rhythm by age two or three.

How many days is a dog in heat?

Two to four weeks, typically around 18 days: about 9 days of proestrus (swelling, bloody discharge, refusing males) then about 9 days of estrus (lighter discharge, receptive and fertile). First heats are often shorter or irregular, and some dogs run 3 weeks or more without anything being wrong.

How can I predict when my dog will go into heat next?

Add her typical interval to the first day of her last heat. A dog that started March 10, 2026 on a 6-month rhythm is due around September 10, 2026. Predictions drift 2-4 weeks either way, so use them to know when to start watching for swelling and discharge, not as a calendar appointment.

Can a dog get pregnant while bleeding?

Usually not during the heavy-bloody phase — that's proestrus and she's not fertile or receptive yet. Conception risk peaks in the lighter-discharge phase, roughly days 9 to 14, when she ovulates and actively seeks males. Keep her separated from intact males for the entire heat, though, because sperm survives for days and some dogs ovulate early.
