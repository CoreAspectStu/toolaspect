How Much Does Wedding Hair and Makeup Cost? Rates, Trials, and the Travel Fee

💄 14K searches/mo💰 CPC: $4.00⏱️ 7 min read

The bride pays $300-$600 for day-of hair and makeup combined (The Knot 2025; WeddingWire's average sits near $300), and each bridal party member runs about $105 per service in a mid-size market — $210 for both. Stack the bride's trial (~$323), lashes ($15-$25 a person), and a $25-$75 travel fee, and a bride plus four attendants lands at about $1,743 all-in. Here's the rate grid and the levers that move it.

The fees beyond the chair

  • The trial — 50-100% of the bride's day-of price, 75% typical; worth every dollar for the bride, skippable for everyone else
  • Lashes — $15-$25 per person, often billed as an add-on rather than included
  • Travel — flat $25-$75 typical, or $0.50-$1/mile beyond the artist's radius; early call times and destinations push it up
  • Minimum bookings — many on-location artists won't come out for under $400-$600 total, which squeezes small parties
  • Touch-up kits and standby — $75-$200 if you want the artist on-site through the ceremony

That minimum-booking line deserves emphasis for small weddings: a bride-only booking that falls under the artist's $400 floor effectively pays the same as a party of three. The workaround is a salon visit — no travel fee, no minimum, and often lower listed rates for in-studio work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does wedding hair and makeup cost?

The bride pays $300-$600 for day-of hair and makeup combined — The Knot's 2025 band, with WeddingWire's average near $300 and a $150-$600 national range. Bridal party members run about $110 per service, roughly $210 each for both. A mid-market bride plus four attendants, both services, totals about $1,270 before the trial, lashes, and travel fee.

How much do you tip a wedding hair and makeup artist?

15-20% of the service total, in cash, on the day. On a $1,743 all-in booking that's $260-$350 — factor it into the budget line rather than discovering it at 7 a.m. Some studios auto-add gratuity for bridal parties, so check the contract before double-tipping.

Is a hair and makeup trial worth it?

For the bride, almost always. The trial costs 50-100% of the day-of price (75% typical — about $323 on a mid-market $430 day-of booking) and buys you a rehearsal of the exact look with photos in your lighting. Skipping it to save money risks redoing the whole look on the one day it can't be redone. Attendants rarely need trials.

Do bridesmaids pay for their own hair and makeup?

By custom, usually yes — but plenty of couples cover it as the attendant gift instead. Budget the line either way: four attendants getting both services in a mid-size market is $840. Note that many artists set a $400-$600 minimum booking to travel on-location, so a small party can end up paying large-party rates.

Why is bridal hair and makeup more expensive than regular salon prices?

You're buying wedding-day conditions, not a haircut: on-location service at 6 a.m., products formulated to survive tears and dancing for 12 hours, touch-up kits, and a schedule built backwards from your photographer's timeline. The premium buys reliability — the artist who flaked is the story you tell at every anniversary.
