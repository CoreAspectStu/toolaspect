How Much House Can I Afford?

💡 165K searches/mo💰 CPC: $11⏱️ 8 min read

The answer depends on your income, debts, down payment, and current interest rates. Lenders use two key ratios: the 28/36 rule and your debt-to-income ratio. Here is exactly how to calculate how much house you can afford.

The 28/36 Rule

Most lenders follow the 28/36 rule. Your monthly housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income. Your total debt payments (including the mortgage, car loans, student loans, and minimum credit card payments) should stay under 36%.

For example, if you earn $75,000 per year ($6,250/month), your housing payment should be at most $1,750/month and your total debt payments under $2,250/month.

Step-by-Step Calculation

Follow these steps to determine your maximum affordable home price:

  • Calculate your gross monthly income (annual pre-tax income / 12)
  • Multiply by 0.28 to get your max housing payment
  • Multiply by 0.36 and subtract existing debt payments for your true max
  • Use current interest rates to back-calculate loan amount from monthly payment
  • Add your down payment to get your maximum home price
  • Factor in property taxes (1.1% national average) and insurance ($1,500/yr average)
  • Remember closing costs: 2-5% of the purchase price

The Impact of Interest Rates

Interest rates dramatically affect how much house you can afford. A 1% rate increase can reduce your buying power by 10-15%. At a 6.5% rate on a 30-year mortgage, a $2,000/month payment supports a roughly $316,000 loan. At 7.5%, that drops to about $286,000.

This is why getting the best rate matters. Improve your credit score, save a larger down payment (20% eliminates PMI), and compare offers from multiple lenders.

Hidden Costs of Homeownership

Your mortgage payment is just the beginning. Budget for these additional costs:

  • Property taxes: 0.5-2.5% of home value annually (varies by state)
  • Home insurance: $1,000-3,000/year depending on location and coverage
  • Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI): 0.3-1.5% of loan if down payment under 20%
  • HOA fees: $200-500/month for condos and planned communities
  • Maintenance: 1-2% of home value per year ($3,000-8,000 for a $400K home)
  • Utilities: $200-500/month depending on size and climate
  • Closing costs: 2-5% of purchase price at signing

Rules of Thumb

Quick guidelines for estimating affordability:

  • Conservative: 2x your gross annual income
  • Moderate: 3x your gross annual income
  • Aggressive: 4x your gross annual income (riskier)
  • Down payment: aim for 20% to avoid PMI, minimum 3.5% for FHA loans
  • Emergency fund: keep 3-6 months of expenses separate from your down payment

Frequently Asked Questions

How much house can I afford with a $60,000 salary?

With a $60K salary, you can typically afford a home between $180,000 and $240,000. This assumes a 20% down payment, moderate debt, and current interest rates around 6.5-7%.

Can I buy a house making $40K a year?

Yes, but your options are limited. With $40K income, you might qualify for a $120,000-$160,000 home. Consider FHA loans with 3.5% down, first-time buyer programs, or dual-income purchasing.

How much do I need to make to afford a $400,000 house?

To afford a $400K home with 20% down, you need an annual income of approximately $100,000-$120,000, assuming a 6.5% interest rate and moderate debt levels.
