A full color-change wrap runs $2,000–$3,500 on a sedan, $2,500–$4,500 on a midsize SUV, and $3,000–$5,500 on a full-size truck, installed in cast vinyl. Matte and satin add 10–15%, color-shift about 35%, and chrome runs 2.5× or more. Here's the full price table, what partials and removal cost, and how the wrap-versus-paint math actually works.

What are you actually paying for?

Labor, mostly. The film for a sedan costs $600–$900 at retail; the quote is $2,000–$3,500 because a full wrap consumes 20 to 40 hours of skilled work. Every badge, mirror, door handle, and bumper aperture gets film stretched and tucked around it, and the visible difference between shops is where the seams sit and how deep the tucks go. The good shops remove trim and wrap around the edges; the cheap ones cut film to the panel and let edges lift within a year. That difference is the budget-versus-premium tier, roughly 0.85× to 1.4× on the same car.

Why do finishes change the price so much?

Finish premium is film cost plus installer patience. Gloss cast vinyl is the commodity. Satin and matte (×1.12 to ×1.15) show every seam, fingerprint, and dust speck, so installation slows down. Textured films (×1.25) demand heat discipline. Color-shift (×1.35) is pricier material whose visual point is flawless panel-to-panel alignment. Chrome (×2.6) is a specialty laminate that takes double handling, shows everything, and is quoted by shops that do few of them, at prices to match. On a sedan, chrome's $5,200–$9,100 range buys a lot of paint, which is exactly the comparison chrome-shoppers should make.

What do partial wraps cost?

A roof wrap on a sedan runs $440–$770, a hood $360–$630, and racing stripes $160–$280. Partials price as fractions of a full wrap (roof about 22%, hood 18%, stripes 8%) with a minimum job charge under everything, which is why stripes aren't $80. The commercial version wraps the same math around advertising: a printed design for a sedan or crossover runs $1,500–$3,000 plus $300–$800 of design work, and businesses treat it as media spend per impression rather than cosmetic cost.

What about paint protection film?

PPF is the other vinyl: thicker, clear urethane whose job is absorbing stone chips rather than changing color. Full-front coverage (bumper, hood, fenders, mirrors) runs $1,200–$2,500 and full-body $4,000–$8,000, with 5-to-10-year warranties against the damage a color wrap doesn't stop. Some owners stack a color wrap with PPF on the leading edges, which works but doubles the budget. If you're wrapping to protect a daily driver rather than restyle it, PPF is usually the better product.

Wrap or paint: which makes sense?

Wrap wins on reversibility, turnaround (3-7 shop days versus weeks), preserving factory paint for resale, and lease cars you must return stock. Paint wins on permanence, repairability (a wrapped panel that takes a scratch needs the panel rewrapped), and price at the bottom of the market: a discount respray starts under $1,000 where no professional full wrap exists. At the quality tier most people actually want, base-clear paint starts around $5,000 for a sedan, which is why wraps with a defined end date (leases, planned resale, change-of-mind protection) are the rational play. Leased-car logic especially: return-day removal runs $500–$1,500, so bake it into the plan.

How do you judge a wrap shop?

Ask three questions. Which film and series (cast from 3M, Avery, ORAFOL class, or walk away)? Do they disassemble trim and badges or wrap around them? Can you see seam photos of their work on your kind of car? Then weigh the quote against the ranges here: materially under budget-shop pricing usually means calendered film that will shrink and crack, skipped disassembly, or an installer still learning on your hood. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a full car wrap cost?

$2,000-$3,500 for a sedan, $2,500-$4,500 for a midsize SUV, $3,000-$5,500 for a full-size SUV or pickup, and $5,000-$10,000 for exotics and large vans, installed in cast vinyl by a standard shop. Matte and satin add 10-15 percent, color-shift about 35 percent, and chrome 2.5 times or more. Published 2025-26 installer ranges.

Is it cheaper to wrap or paint a car?

Quality-for-quality they cost the same, $2,000-$5,000, but the economics differ. A cheap respray starts under $1,000 and wins on price alone; a proper base-clear respray starts around $5,000 for a sedan and is permanent. A wrap preserves factory paint, reverses for resale, and takes 3-7 days instead of weeks.

How long does a vehicle wrap last?

Cast vinyl lasts 5 to 7 years outdoors, longer garaged and shorter for sun-belt cars parked outside daily. Matte and textured finishes show age sooner, and chrome fails fastest in heat. Installers typically warranty 1 to 3 years against peeling and adhesive faults.

Does wrapping a car ruin the paint?

Not factory paint, professionally removed. The risks are fresh repaints (some lift; wait 30+ days), cheap resprayed panels, and film left on years past its life, which turns removal into an hours-long adhesive cleanup. Factory-cured clear coat releases film cleanly with heat.

How much does it cost to remove a car wrap?

$500-$1,500 for a passenger car, billed hourly because age and sun exposure dominate the work. Film under two years old strips in a day; six-year-old film baked in desert sun can take two. Budget removal before any rewrap.
