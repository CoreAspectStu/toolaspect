How Much Does Spotify Pay Per Stream?

⏱️ 7 min read

Ask Spotify for its per-stream rate and the honest answer is that there isn't one. Royalties come out of a revenue pool — subscription fees plus ad money, divided market by market — and your rate is whatever your listeners' share of that pool works out to. In practice, statements land near $0.003 to $0.005 per stream, and the money at the top is enormous: Spotify's own Loud & Clear report shows it paid the music industry more than $11 billion in 2025, pushing lifetime payouts toward $70 billion. Here's how a stream becomes a deposit, and where your number can differ from the average.

How much does Spotify pay per stream?

Spotify distributes a share of its net revenue to recording rightsholders, market by market. Every country's subscription and ad revenue goes into that market's pool, and the market's streams share it. Three levers move your personal rate:

  • Country. A stream in the US or UK draws from a high-revenue pool. The same stream in a lower-revenue market pays a fraction of it.
  • Subscription type. Premium listeners generate more revenue per play than free, ad-supported listeners, so their streams pay more.
  • The year. As subscription prices rise and the listener mix shifts, the blended rate drifts — the $11 billion paid for 2025 was up more than 10% over 2024.

Put together, most statements land in the $0.003-$0.005 band — $3 to $5 per thousand streams. An artist whose audience sits in premium-heavy markets can clear the top of it; a globally spread audience usually blends toward the middle or below.

What is the 1,000-stream rule?

Since 2024, Spotify requires a track to cross 1,000 streams in the trailing twelve months before it earns any recording royalties at all. Below the line, the plays pay nothing — a policy aimed at fraud farms and hour-long noise tracks, but one that also sweeps up real songs with tiny audiences. If you carry a deep catalog, expect a handful of tracks earning exactly zero despite honest play counts. The threshold resets annually, so a track that catches an algorithm next year re-enters the system.

Who actually receives the money?

Spotify pays rightsholders, not artists. On the recording side, that's your label or distributor; what reaches you is whatever your contract says, often after recouping advances. On the songwriting side, publishers and collection societies receive a separate payout — which is why a self-written, self-released track earns two checks, and only one of them shows up in the distributor dashboard everyone quotes. It's also why two artists with identical stream counts can report wildly different income: geography, deal terms, and ownership do the rest.

How many streams does $1,000 a month take?

Divide the target by the band: at $0.005 you need 200,000 streams a month; at $0.003, about 333,000; call it a quarter million at the midpoint. That's monthly, forever, which is why durable streaming income is a catalog game — thirty tracks each earning modest, steady plays beat one track riding a playlist spike that decays in six weeks. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Spotify pay per stream?

There is no official fixed rate, but the blended figure most statements show lands near $0.003-$0.005 per stream — roughly $3 to $5 per 1,000 streams. Premium listeners and high-revenue countries pay toward the top of that band; free-tier listeners and lower-revenue markets pay below it.

How much is 1 million streams on Spotify?

Roughly $3,000-$5,000 in gross recording royalties at the commonly observed $0.003-$0.005 band. What reaches the artist is less: a distributor fee or label share comes out first, and songwriting royalties arrive separately through the publishing side.

What is Spotify's 1,000-stream rule?

Since 2024, a track needs at least 1,000 streams in the trailing 12 months before it earns recording royalties on Spotify at all. Tracks under the threshold accumulate nothing — a change aimed at fraud and noise tracks that mostly affects artists with large catalogs of barely-streamed songs.

Who gets paid by Spotify — the artist or the label?

Neither, directly. Spotify pays rightsholders: labels and distributors on the recording side, publishers and collection societies on the songwriting side. Independent artists with a distributor receive their share minus the distributor's fee or percentage; signed artists receive whatever their contract's royalty rate specifies.

How many streams does it take to make $1,000 a month on Spotify?

About 200,000-333,000 streams per month at the $0.003-$0.005 band — call it 250,000 at the $0.004 midpoint. Reaching that every month usually takes a catalog of tracks earning together rather than one song holding its numbers.
