How Much Does Rumble Pay Per 1,000 Views?

📺 Creator platforms💰 6 min read

Rumble's pitch to creators is simple: monetize from day one, keep a bigger share than YouTube pays. The honest version needs numbers attached. Rumble publishes no rate card, so this guide assembles what's verifiable — the analyst-reported earnings band, Rumble's own program documentation, and the payout mechanics — then runs the math on real channel sizes.

How much does Rumble actually pay per 1,000 views?

The working answer, from third-party 2026 analyses: $2 to $10 per 1,000 views, earned on a standard 60/40 ad revenue split — the creator keeps 60% of ad revenue. Where you land inside that band depends mostly on three things:

  • Niche. News and political commentary — Rumble's strongest categories — often exceeds $10 per 1,000. Entertainment and gaming typically land at $2 to $5.
  • Geography. US, UK, and Canadian viewers carry high advertiser demand; audiences elsewhere can monetize at a small fraction of those rates.
  • Ad delivery. Views with an ad blocker, or views where the viewer leaves before an ad serves, pay nothing regardless of the rate.

Until your first payout statement, the disciplined move is forecasting with the whole band, not a single hopeful number.

Three ways Rumble pays creators

Standard ads. The default: your videos run ads, you keep 60% of the revenue. No follower minimum — the part Rumble markets hardest against YouTube's 1,000-subscriber wall.

Licensing. When you upload, you choose rights options. Under exclusive video management, Rumble distributes your video to its publishing partners and you keep 90% of net earnings; one-time licensing payments for newsworthy clips from roughly $50 to over $5,000 have been reported. The non-exclusive player-only option keeps everything on Rumble.com at the 60% split.

The Creator Program. A separate, streaming-centered program paying from a share of Rumble Premium revenue. This is the one with formal requirements, below.

The Creator Program: the official requirements

From Rumble's own help page, updated July 1, 2026. To apply: 100 total followers, at least 1 hour streamed via Rumble Studio in the past 30 days, and an active Rumble Premium subscription that stays active. Then, every calendar month to keep earning:

  • Stream 30 total hours via Rumble Studio, including 5 hours to Rumble Premium
  • Accumulate 500 hours of total watch time
  • Count 75 chatters across your livestreams
  • Complete one raid in five separate livestreams (no viewer-count minimum)
  • Upload or clip 20 shorts

Payouts follow a formula of minutes watched (your share versus other enrolled creators), new Rumble signups attributed to your content, and new Premium subscribers you source. It's currently open to creators in Australia, Canada, El Salvador, Macedonia, South Korea, the UK, and the US; US creators file a W-9, and payment is calculated by the end of the following month. This is a livestreamer's program — a pure uploads channel won't meet the streaming bars.

Payout mechanics: the $50 floor

Whatever you earn, Rumble pays out at a $50 minimum balance, via PayPal or direct deposit. Small channels feel this most: at 8,000 monthly views and $3 per 1,000, you make $24 a month and your first payout lands in month 3, when the balance hits $72. Earnings roll forward until the threshold clears — nothing is lost, just delayed.

Rumble vs YouTube: the short version

Rumble's 60% share beats YouTube's standard 55%, and its no-threshold start beats YouTube's 1,000-subscriber wall. YouTube's counterpunch is scale: typical US-creator RPMs of $5-$15 with far more ad inventory and a much larger audience. The pattern serious creators converge on is dual-posting — Rumble monetizes the same uploads from day one while the YouTube channel grows toward its threshold, and the YouTube earnings calculator next to the Rumble calculator shows both columns of that strategy.

The bottom line

Rumble pays roughly $2 to $10 per 1,000 views on a 60/40 split, with no follower minimum, a $50 payout floor, and a separate Premium-funded Creator Program for streamers who can sustain 30 streamed hours and 500 watch-hours a month. Forecast with the band, replace it with your first real statement, and model both platforms before betting on either — the numbers are small enough that one honest table beats a dozen optimistic quotes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Rumble pay per 1,000 views?

Typically $2 to $10 per 1,000 views, per third-party 2026 analyses — Rumble itself publishes no fixed rate. News and political commentary often exceeds $10, entertainment and gaming usually lands at $2 to $5, and audience geography is the biggest single factor: US, UK, and Canadian viewers monetize several times higher than most other regions.

Is Rumble monetization easier than YouTube's?

On paper, yes. The standard ad route has no subscriber or watch-hour minimum — you can earn from your first upload — while YouTube requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours (or Shorts equivalents). The trade-off is that Rumble's audience, and therefore its ad inventory, is smaller, so the same video usually earns less there than on an established YouTube channel.

What do you have to do to join the Rumble Creator Program?

Per Rumble's page updated July 1, 2026: 100 total followers, at least 1 hour streamed via Rumble Studio in the past 30 days, and an active Rumble Premium subscription. Each active month then requires 30 streamed hours (5 to Premium content), 500 hours of watch time, 75 chatters, a raid across five separate livestreams, and 20 shorts. It pays from a share of Premium revenue, not a per-view rate.
