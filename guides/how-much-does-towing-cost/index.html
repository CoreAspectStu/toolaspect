Tow pricing is simple arithmetic wrapped in bad timing: a hook-up fee that covers the first few miles, then a flat rate per mile after. A dead battery 12 miles from home costs about $127. The trouble starts when you're standing on a shoulder quoting nobody. Here's what the math should look like, so you can sanity-check the bill before you sign it.

How is a tow bill calculated?

Three lines, occasionally four. The hook-up fee gets the truck to you and typically includes 5 to 7 miles. Mileage beyond that bills at the per-mile rate — and it's door-to-door miles, not straight-line distance. Then add-ons: after-hours or weekend service ($25-$50), winching if the car isn't on pavement ($100-$300 per hour), and storage if it lands in a yard ($20-$80 a day).

Worked example: 12-mile light-duty tow with a $95 hook covering 5 miles at $4.50 a mile after. That's $95 + (12 − 5) × $4.50 = $126.50. The same truck 40 miles out: $95 + 35 × $4.50 = $252.50. Notice the hook fee is 40% of the short bill but under 15% of the long one — short tows are about the truck coming out, long tows are about miles.

How much does a long-distance tow cost?

Past about 100 miles, most operators switch to flat long-haul pricing of $1.50 to $3.00 a mile, so a 100-mile passenger-car tow typically totals $250 to $600. But at that distance you should price a second option: car shipping on an open trailer often beats tow pricing at 200+ miles, especially if the car rolls and you can wait a day for pickup. Towing is for when the car needs to move now.

Is a roadside assistance plan worth it?

Almost always, if you drive more than occasionally. The break-even math, using real tow bills:

  • Insurance roadside rider, $15-$50/yr, short tows: one 7-mile tow ($75-$125) pays for it.
  • Basic standalone plan (AAA Classic class), $60-$80/yr, ~7-mile tows: pays for itself on the second short tow.
  • Mid-tier (AAA Plus class), $100-$140/yr, 100-mile tows: one 40-mile tow ($163-$300) covers the year.
  • Premium tier, $130-$180/yr, 200-mile first tow: one 100-mile tow ($250-$600) covers it twice over.

The catch with insurance riders: some carriers count roadside calls as claims, and a pattern of them can affect pricing at renewal. Standalone plans like AAA keep that history off your policy entirely, which is part of what the fee buys. Check your phone plan, credit card, and automaker coverage too — Toyota, Hyundai, and others include 2-3 years of roadside on new cars, and many people pay twice for the same truck.

What adds money to a tow bill?

  • After-hours dispatch: $25-$50 for nights, weekends, holidays.
  • Winching and recovery: off-pavement or in a ditch bills $100-$300/hour after a $50-$100 hookup.
  • Storage: $20-$80/day once the car sits in the yard; a weekend stay adds $60-$160.
  • Dead-wheel equipment: dollies or skates for cars that won't roll or steer, often $10-$50.
  • Second truck: if the first truck dispatched can't handle the job, some contracts bill both arrivals.

The countermeasure is asking one question before dispatch: "What's the out-the-door total for X miles, including everything?" Reputable operators quote it; the ones that won't, you don't want. For everything that happens after the tow, the car repair cost calculator prices the next surprise.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a 5-mile tow cost?

Most light-duty operators charge $75 to $125 for a short local tow, since the hook-up fee usually includes the first 5 to 7 miles. Distance barely matters until you pass the included miles; the truck coming out is most of the cost.

Why do tow truck prices vary so much between companies?

Towing is dispatched locally, often through rotation or police-contracted lists, so competition rarely operates on price. Rates differ by $1 to $2 per mile between operators in the same town, and impound-ordered tows after an accident run the highest rates because you don't choose the company. Phone two operators before you need one and save the numbers.

Does AAA pay the tow company directly?

Yes, for covered tows — you sign and drive away with no bill, as long as you're within your plan's mileage (about 7 miles on basic plans, 100 on Plus-class, 200 for the first tow on Premier-class). Beyond the covered distance you pay the truck's out-of-pocket rate for the excess miles.
