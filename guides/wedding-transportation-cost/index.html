What do couples actually spend?

WeddingWire's national average is $750, with most couples between $500 and $1,100. The Knot's regional data explains the rest of the spread: Midwest $934, South $1,039, Mid-Atlantic $1,443, Northeast around $1,500. You're mostly paying local labor rates — the same three hours costs the same three hours everywhere.

Do you need a guest shuttle?

Three situations make the shuttle non-optional:

  • Venues more than 15-20 minutes apart. Without a shuttle you lose guests to parking logistics and arrive at the reception to half a room.
  • Limited or paid parking. Urban venues, estates, and vineyards routinely have space for 40 cars when you invited 120 couples.
  • An open bar and no walkable hotel. This one is liability as much as hospitality; a shuttle is cheaper than any alternative if something goes wrong.

The capacity rule: one 30-50 passenger shuttle per 75-100 guests, running loops between the hotel block, ceremony, and reception during peak windows. At the typical 4-hour party-bus minimum ($840) plus an 18% tip across 120 guests, guest transport costs about $8.25 a head — less than the per-plate catering line, and the cheapest hospitality upgrade available. What hides in the contract?

  • Gratuity, built in or not. Many operators add 18-20% to the invoice; others leave it to the day. If it's not included, budget 15-20% in cash per driver — roughly $180-$240 on a $1,185 booking.
  • Deadhead and fuel fees. Some quotes charge for the driver's travel to your pickup point; others fold it in. Compare all-in totals, not hourly rates.
  • Overtime rates. Running 40 minutes past the window can bill a full extra hour at a premium rate.
  • Deposits. Expect $100-$200 to hold the date; it's usually credited, not refunded, if you cancel.

Book as soon as both venues are signed — the same handful of operators serves every wedding in town, and peak-season Saturdays commit their fleets months ahead. Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget for wedding transportation?

Plan on $500 to $1,100, which brackets most couples against the $750 national average. One 3-hour limo for the couple runs $225-$450; adding a 4-hour party-bus shuttle for guests takes the day to roughly $1,200-$1,400 with tip. Northeast weddings average about $1,500, Midwest weddings $934.

How much does a limo cost for a wedding?

Standard stretch limos run $75-$150 an hour with 3-hour weekend minimums, so $225-$450 before gratuity. Larger SUV and stretch limos (14-20 passengers) run $120-$300 an hour. Vintage and classic cars span $90-$500 an hour or flat wedding packages of $300-$1,100.

How much is a party bus for a wedding?

$140-$350 an hour by size and market, with most couples paying $175-$250 for a mid-size bus and 4-5 hour minimums applying. At the typical $210 an hour and a 4-hour minimum, a party bus day is about $840 before tip. Charter motorcoaches price closer to a $1,000 base plus $200 per extra hour.

Do wedding guests expect a shuttle?

When the ceremony and reception are more than 15-20 minutes apart or parking is tight, yes — and if the bar is open, a shuttle is the responsible move. Figure one 30-50 passenger shuttle per 75-100 guests running loops; the full transportation day typically lands near $8-$12 per guest all-in.

How much do you tip a wedding limo driver?

15-20% of the vehicle subtotal, in cash, on the day — about $180-$240 on a $1,185 booking. Check your contract first: many operators build an 18-20% gratuity into the invoice, and double-tipping is the most common mistake couples make on this line.
