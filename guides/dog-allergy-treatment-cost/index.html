Cytopoint vs Apoquel vs Allergy Shots: What Dog Allergy Treatment Costs

🐾 8 min read🧮 2025 national prices, 3-year math

The licking starts in April, the paws turn salmon-pink by June, and by the time you're at the vet you're being handed three price tags that work nothing alike: an injection every couple of months, a daily tablet forever, or a testing-and-shots program with a big first year. This guide lays out the real national prices for each and runs the multi-year math that actually decides which one your budget can live with.

What does each treatment cost up front?

Allergy testing$200-$400 blood / $300-$600 intradermalOne-time, often with a $200-$400 derm consult
Immunotherapy serumMixed into the test bill$50-$100 a month in shots or drops

Cytopoint's price tracks the dog, not the diagnosis: vets buy it in fixed-size vials, so the invoice for a 15-pound terrier ($60-$90) looks nothing like the one for a 90-pound Shepherd ($180+). Apoquel bills like a subscription — around $2.50 a tablet, and most dogs take one a day after a twice-daily first two weeks. A 2025 NCBI pricing study put real-world spend at roughly $2 per pound per Cytopoint dose and $3 per pound per month on Apoquel, which matches the clinic bands. Immunotherapy front-loads the cost into testing, then settles into the cheapest steady-state bill of the three.

The 3-year math on a 55 lb dog

Multi-year totals are where the ranking flips. Take a 55 lb dog with year-round environmental allergies, Cytopoint every 8 weeks at $160 a shot, $45 exam fees, Apoquel at $110 a month, and immunotherapy at $600 to test plus $75 a month in serum:

YearCytopointApoquelImmunotherapy
Year 1$1,333$1,445$1,590
Years 2-3 (each)$1,333$1,410$990
3-year total$3,998$4,265$3,570

Three findings fall out of that table. Immunotherapy wins despite the most expensive first year, because the test never repeats. Cytopoint and Apoquel land within a couple hundred dollars of each other for a large dog, so the choice between them is medical, not financial. And every line assumes year-round treatment — which brings us to the lever that matters more than any of them.

Seasonal dogs change everything

A dog who itches April through July doesn't need twelve months of anything. Four months of Apoquel runs about $440 a year. Four months of Cytopoint runs two or three injections, maybe $500 with exams. Meanwhile the immunotherapy test bill doesn't shrink with the season — $600 of testing to treat a four-month problem takes years to pay back. Rule of thumb: the shorter the flare window, the better the symptom medications look; the longer it runs, the more the test pays for itself. Run your own season length, weight band, and clinic prices through the dog allergy treatment cost calculator before committing to either path.

Does pet insurance cover any of this?

Usually yes, with one giant asterisk: the policy has to predate the itching. Atopic dermatitis is a diagnosed illness, so a standard accident-and-illness plan will typically cover the dermatology visit, testing, Cytopoint injections, and Apoquel prescriptions the same way it covers any other illness — minus your deductible and copay percentage. Document the itching after enrollment, though, and it's a pre-existing condition, excluded for the life of the policy and every renewal after it. Wellness riders sound relevant but almost never cover chronic medication; they're built for vaccines and dental. If you're weighing coverage, the pet insurance calculator prices what a policy costs for your dog's breed and age, which you can set against the $3,500-$4,300 a year of treatment above.

Four ways to cut the bill

  • Price-shop the injection. Cytopoint quotes for the same dog vary by $50 or more between clinics in the same metro. Call three; it's the same product.
  • Ask about quantity fills on Apoquel. Larger fills and manufacturer rebates routinely knock 10-20% off the per-tablet price. Vet clinics price-match Chewy and 1-800-PetMeds on request more often than people ask.
  • Combine strategies seasonally. Some vets run Cytopoint for the bad months and Apoquel as a bridge — priced per month, not per year.
  • Recheck the diagnosis. Food allergies and flea allergy get worked up differently and can masquerade as environmental allergies. A $200-$600 test beats three years of the wrong $1,400-a-year medication.

Whatever the mix, keep it a cost question and a medical question separately. This guide prices the menus; the dosing schedule, drug choice, and diagnosis belong to your vet or a veterinary dermatologist — the consult fee ($200-$400) is the cheapest line item on this page if it steers you off the wrong path.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Cytopoint shot cost at the vet?

$50 to $200 per injection depending on the dog's weight band and the clinic, repeated every 4 to 8 weeks. Under 20 lb usually runs $60-$90, 20-40 lb runs $90-$140, and large dogs commonly reach $180+. Across a year on an 8-week schedule that's 6.5 visits, roughly $850 to $1,350 with exam fees.

Is Apoquel expensive?

It's a monthly bill rather than a big one: $50 to $150 a month at around $2.50 per tablet, with the first month running about $110 for a typical dog because dosing starts twice daily. The pain shows up on the multi-year view — $1,400 to $1,700 a year for a large dog, every year, for as long as the allergies last.

Is it worth paying for allergy testing and shots?

For year-round allergies, usually yes on cost alone. Testing runs $200-$600 and serum $50-$100 a month, which makes year one the most expensive ($1,500+). But years two onward drop to around $1,000 or less, beating both Cytopoint and Apoquel — and immunotherapy is the only option aimed at retraining the immune response rather than suppressing symptoms, so some dogs eventually stop needing it.

Does dog insurance pay for allergy treatment?

Usually, if the policy started before the itching did. Atopic dermatitis is a diagnosed illness, so accident-and-illness plans typically cover vet visits, testing, Cytopoint, and Apoquel after your deductible. Allergies documented before enrollment are pre-existing and excluded forever. Wellness riders almost never cover chronic medication.

Can I switch between Cytopoint and Apoquel to save money?

They're often combined in bad seasons and either can carry the quiet months alone, so seasonal splitting genuinely cuts the bill — a dog needing relief April-July might pay for four months of tablets instead of twelve. But which drug suits your dog is a medical call (Cytopoint targets itch signaling; Apoquel dampens the immune pathway behind inflammation), so plan the mix with your vet, not just the price sheet.
