How to Plan 30 Days of Content in One Sitting

🗓️ 8 min read⚡ Generator linked inside

The month always looks manageable from the outside and impossible from the inside. The fix isn't discipline, it's compression: make every decision that can be made in advance, in one session, so the rest of the month is execution only. Here's the exact sequence — cadence math, pillar split, idea banks, export — and where to automate each step.

Step 1: Fix the cadence before anything else

Cadence is the load-bearing decision, because everything downstream — pillar counts, idea counts, filming volume — derives from it. The arithmetic:

Posts / weekPosts in a 30-day windowWho it suits
3~13Solo, service business, content is support
521–23 (22 from a Monday start)The sweet spot for most solo creators
730Daily drivers with batching infrastructure
10~43Teams, or one person repurposing heavily

Choose the number you could hit on your worst week of the month, then subtract one. A missed post costs momentum; a sustainable cadence compounds it.

Step 2: Split the month with the 40/30/20/10 rule

Take the total post count and divide it: 40% educational, 30% engaging, 20% promotional, 10% entertaining. For the standard 22-post month that's 9 educational, 7 engaging, 4 promotional, 2 entertaining. The rounding uses largest remainders so the counts always sum exactly — a small detail that matters when you're coloring a spreadsheet at 11 p.m.

Why these weights? Educational content is what earns the follow — it's the majority because it does the audience-building. Engaging posts harvest comments, which every algorithm reads as a quality signal. Promotion converts, but only works as a minority: audiences tolerate a brand that mostly teaches and occasionally sells, and churn on the reverse. Entertaining posts are the personality that keeps the other 90% from reading like a syllabus.

Step 3: Pull ideas from a bank, not a brainstorm

This is the step that eats the session when done from scratch. Instead, keep (or steal) an idea bank per niche: categorized prompts that map to your pillars. Ten educational, eight engaging, five promotional, five entertaining prompts is more than a 22-post month can consume — no repeats, no 2 a.m. idea droughts.

The social media calendar generator ships with banks for eight niches (fitness, food, real estate, SaaS, e-commerce, personal finance, beauty, travel) and does the whole assignment — dates, platforms, times, pillars, ideas — in one click. The output is a draft in the truest sense: you'll swap a third of the ideas, and that editing pass is where your voice enters. But editing a full calendar takes twenty minutes; building one takes an afternoon.

Step 4: Assign platforms and times mechanically

Rotate platforms round-robin across slots (Instagram, TikTok, Instagram, TikTok…) so no platform goes dark for a week, and set each platform's slot time from its best window: Instagram early afternoon, TikTok mid-afternoon, LinkedIn late morning, per Sprout Social's 2025–2026 engagement analyses. These defaults get you through month one; from month two, your own analytics outrank any national average.

One topic should feed multiple platforms — but as different cuts, not copy-paste. The carousel becomes a talking-head video becomes a text post. Plan the idea once, budget the formats separately.

Step 5: Export to where the month actually happens

A plan that lives in a document is a wish. Export the slots as calendar events so publishing sits next to dentist appointments and school pickup — the places you already look. Google Calendar imports .ics directly (Settings → Import & Export), Outlook and Apple Calendar open the same file, and the CSV route fits spreadsheet-first workflows.

Then set the weekly rhythm: one 30-minute review to move what slipped, one batch session to shoot or draft the coming week's assets. The content calendar template covers the weekly grid and ICS export if you'd rather own the structure by hand; pair it with the hashtag generator at publish time and the engagement rate calculator at review time, and the loop is closed.

What to do when the plan meets reality

  • A week collapses. Drop to minimum cadence rather than going dark; move posts, don't delete them.
  • A post flops. Note the pillar and format, not the shame. Patterns over singles.
  • A trend emerges. Trends slot into any educational or entertaining slot — the structure flexes, the rhythm doesn't.
  • A post overperforms. Schedule a follow-up within 10 days; sequels inherit the audience.

The plan is a floor, not a ceiling. Its job is guaranteeing the minimum happens — everything above that is bonus.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to plan 30 days of content?

About two to three hours from blank page to exported calendar if you generate the structure first: one hour picking platforms, cadence and pillars, one hour assigning ideas to slots, and 30 minutes building assets lists. Generators compress the first two steps to minutes, which is the point — the irreplaceable part is adapting ideas to your voice.

How many posts do I need for 30 days?

Five posts a week lands 22 posts in a 30-day window that starts on a Monday (21 to 23 depending on start day), three a week lands about 13, and daily is exactly 30. Anywhere from 15 to 30 posts a month is the sustainable band for one person producing solo.

What is the 40/30/20/10 content rule?

Split the month's posts into 40% educational, 30% engaging, 20% promotional and 10% entertaining. It keeps selling a minority of your output so the audience that promotion converts actually exists. For 22 posts the split rounds to 9/7/4/2.

Should I post the same content on every platform?

Same ideas, different cuts. A single topic can become an Instagram carousel, a TikTok talking-head video, and a LinkedIn post, but native formatting and hook style matter enough that copy-paste cross-posting underperforms everywhere. Plan one idea, budget three formats.

What do I do when the plan collides with real life?

Keep the slot, swap the content. The calendar's value is consistency of rhythm, so when a week collapses, move posts rather than deleting them — and collapse to your minimum viable cadence (even 2 posts) instead of going dark. A catch-up session at month end beats abandoning the plan.
