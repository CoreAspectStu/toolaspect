What Happens at an SSDI Continuing Disability Review?

📄 7 min readUpdated October 22, 2026

A continuing disability review is SSA's periodic check that you still qualify for SSDI. Depending on your case it comes every 6 to 18 months, every 3 years or every 5 to 7 years, and it usually starts with a form in the mail. Most reviews end with benefits continuing. Here's what SSA looks at, how to fill in the forms, and what to do quickly if it decides your disability has ended.

What is a continuing disability review?

It's SSA checking whether you still meet its definition of disability. Everyone on SSDI gets them; they're required by law, and getting a review letter doesn't mean anyone suspects you of anything. Most reviews end with benefits continuing. The ones that go wrong usually go wrong for avoidable reasons: a form not returned, an exam missed, or medical records that don't show the treatment you've actually had.

How often you're reviewed was set when you were approved. Your award letter puts you in one of three categories, and the category decides the timetable:

Category on your awardHow often SSA reviewsTypical examples
Medical improvement expected (MIE)Usually every 6 to 18 monthsA fracture still healing, recovery after surgery, a condition expected to respond to treatment
Medical improvement possible (MIP)At least once every 3 yearsConditions that might improve but where nobody can say when
Medical improvement not expected (MINE)Every 5 to 7 yearsPermanent conditions, often combined with older age

Those intervals come from the regulations (20 CFR 404.1590). Backlogs mean a review can arrive late, but it does arrive.

What triggers a review early?

The 24-month rule matters to anyone thinking about working. Once you've received SSDI for at least 24 months, SSA can't start a medical review just because you went back to work. It can still review your earnings to see whether they're over the substantial gainful activity limit ($1,690 a month in 2026), and your regular diary review still happens. The work rules themselves are covered in can you work while on SSDI.

What is the short form and the long form?

Many reviews start with the short form, the SSA-455 Disability Update Report. It's a few pages of questions: have you worked, have you seen a doctor, has your condition improved or got worse. SSA uses the answers, along with a statistical profile of your case, to decide whether a full review is needed. Plenty of people return the short form and hear nothing more until the next one.

A full medical review uses the long form, the SSA-454 Continuing Disability Review Report: every doctor, clinic and hospital you've used, your medications, and how your condition limits daily life. The state disability agency then requests your records and may send you to a consultative exam.

  • Answer by the date on the letter. Ignoring a review is the fastest way to lose benefits. SSA can stop payments for failure to cooperate, regardless of your health.
  • List every provider, with addresses. The decision is built from records. A specialist you forgot to mention is evidence that never gets read.
  • Go to any consultative exam. If you can't make the time, call and reschedule rather than missing it.

How does SSA decide whether to stop benefits?

It can't just look at you again as if you were applying for the first time. Under the medical improvement review standard, SSA compares your condition now with how it was at your last favorable decision, called the comparison point. To stop benefits it generally has to show that your condition has medically improved, that the improvement relates to your ability to work, and that you can now do substantial work. There are exceptions, such as an earlier decision obtained by fraud, but for most people this standard is real protection.

What it means for you: keep treating. Gaps in care can make a file look like improvement when it isn't. If cost stopped you seeing a doctor, say so on the form.

What happens if SSA says your disability has ended?

You'll get a cessation notice. Two deadlines start immediately, and the first is short. SSA assumes you received the notice five days after the date printed on it. Here's how that plays out for a notice dated March 2:

What you wantDeadlineNotice dated March 2What to know
Keep your checks during the appeal10 days after you receive the noticeMarch 17Ask for benefit continuation when you appeal
Ask for reconsideration60 days after you receive the noticeMay 6Leads to a disability hearing
Ask for a hearing with a judge (ALJ)60 days after the reconsideration decisionDepends on that letterChecks can continue through the ALJ decision
Appeals Council, then federal court60 days at each stepDepends on that letterChecks stop after the ALJ decision

Reconsideration of a cessation is different from an initial denial. You get a disability hearing, where you can meet the hearing officer, bring witnesses and add evidence. If that goes against you, the next step is a hearing with an administrative law judge.

Continuing your checks during the appeal is a real choice, not an automatic yes. If you lose, SSA treats the continued payments as an overpayment. You can ask for that to be waived if you appealed in good faith, and the steps are in what to do about an SSDI overpayment. Whichever way you lean, decide within the 10 days.

Do reviews ever stop?

Yes. At full retirement age your SSDI turns into a retirement benefit, usually for the same amount, and medical reviews end; see what happens to SSDI at full retirement age. Before then, a MINE category means reviews are spaced out, not gone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does Social Security review SSDI?

It depends on the category on your award. If medical improvement is expected, usually every 6 to 18 months. If it's possible, at least once every 3 years. If it isn't expected, every 5 to 7 years. Reviews can also start early if you report improvement or SSA gets new evidence.

What is the difference between the SSA-455 and the SSA-454?

The SSA-455 Disability Update Report is a short form asking about work, doctors and changes in your condition. SSA uses it to decide whether a full review is needed. The SSA-454 Continuing Disability Review Report is the long form used for a full medical review, listing every provider, medication and limitation.

Can I keep getting SSDI while I appeal a cessation?

Yes, if you ask within 10 days of receiving the cessation notice, which SSA counts as 15 days from the date on it. Payments can continue through the decision of an administrative law judge. If you lose, SSA can ask you to repay them, though you can request a waiver if you appealed in good faith.

Will going back to work trigger a medical review?

Not by itself once you've received SSDI for at least 24 months. SSA can still check whether your earnings are over the substantial gainful activity limit, $1,690 a month in 2026, and your scheduled reviews still happen. People using a Ticket to Work and making timely progress aren't given medical reviews.

What happens if I ignore a continuing disability review?

SSA can suspend and then stop your benefits for failure to cooperate, even if your condition hasn't changed at all. If a deadline is tight, call SSA, explain and ask for more time rather than letting it pass.
