Student Loans and Marriage: Should You File Jointly or Separately?

💍 8 min read📊 RAP payment math + 2026 tax brackets

Getting married doesn't merge your student loans — but it can merge the income used to price the payments, and that can cost thousands a year. Since July 1, 2026, the federal income-based plan is RAP: payments run 1-10% of AGI on a sliding scale, and joint filing plugs the household's combined AGI into that formula. Here's the arithmetic of the student loan marriage penalty, when filing separately wins, and the dual-borrower trap that doubles the damage.

How marriage changes a RAP payment

RAP's formula is one line: a percentage of your AGI (1% starting at $10,001 of income, rising a point per $10,000 band to 10% above $100,000), divided by 12, minus $50 per dependent, floored at $10. Marriage enters through the AGI. Filing jointly, a borrower's payment is computed on the couple's combined AGI. Filing separately, each spouse's payment uses only their own AGI.

The dual-borrower trap: income counted twice

When both spouses hold federal loans on RAP and file jointly, each borrower's payment is computed on the same combined AGI. The household's income gets counted twice. Our $85,000 + $45,000 couple with one dependent: filing jointly, each owes 10% of $130,000 ÷ 12 − $50 = $1,033.33 — $2,066.67 a month total. Filing separately: $516.67 and $150.00, $666.67 total. The difference is $16,800 a year, and no realistic tax adjustment offsets that. For two-borrower households on income-driven plans, filing separately is usually the default answer — check the tax side, but expect it to lose.

What you give up filing separately

  • Student loan interest deduction: gone outright — MFS filers can't claim it at all, on either spouse's loans.
  • Education credits: American Opportunity and Lifetime Learning credits are unavailable filing separately.
  • Child and dependent care credit: also excluded.
  • Roth IRA: contribution room phases out fast for separate filers — often to zero.
  • Bracket widths: MFS brackets are half the joint widths above the 10-12% bands, though for many income pairs the difference is modest (see row three above, where it nets to $0).

Questions people ask before the wedding

"Will their loans become mine?" No. Federal student loans are individual; marriage doesn't transfer them or expose your wages. What changes is payment sizing on income-driven plans and, in community-property states, some treatment of debts incurred during the marriage. "Should we delay the wedding for the loans?" Sometimes, briefly — marrying in January versus December changes which tax year's filing status prices the payment, and one year's timing can be worth thousands. That's a numbers question: run both dates. "What about PSLF?" Payment size doesn't change forgiveness speed — 120 qualifying payments count the same whether they're $117 or $1,033 — so minimizing the payment while pursuing PSLF is pure gain. Frequently Asked Questions

Does marriage affect student loan payments?

Only on income-driven plans, and only through the income they measure. RAP, the federal income-based plan since July 2026, prices payments as 1-10% of AGI. Married filing jointly: the payment is computed on combined AGI, so a working spouse raises the borrower's payment. Married filing separately: each spouse's payment uses only their own AGI. The standard 10-year plan ignores marriage entirely.

Is there a student loan marriage penalty?

Often, yes. Example: a borrower earning $85,000 married to a $45,000 earner with no loans pays $1,033/month on RAP filing jointly (10% of the $130,000 combined AGI, less one $50 dependent credit) versus $517 filing separately (8% of their own AGI). That's $6,200 a year — filing separately costs about $1,850 more in federal tax, so it still nets roughly $4,350 ahead.

Will I have to pay my spouse's student loans?

No. Federal student loans are individual obligations; marriage doesn't transfer them or expose your wages for a spouse's premarital debt. Private lenders also can't pursue a non-signing spouse, though they may pursue a deceased borrower's estate. Community-property states add nuances for debt incurred during the marriage — that's a question for a family-law attorney.

What do I lose by filing married filing separately?

The tax code charges for the privilege: no student loan interest deduction (MFS is excluded outright), no education credits, no child and dependent care credit, reduced (often zero) Roth IRA contribution room at higher incomes, and half-width tax brackets. Against a large payment delta those losses can be worth it; against a small one they aren't. Net both columns before choosing.

Do both spouses' loans double the marriage penalty?

Yes, dramatically. When both spouses are on RAP and file jointly, each payment is computed on the same combined AGI — the household income is counted twice. An $85,000 + $45,000 dual-borrower couple pays $2,067/month jointly versus $667 separately: a $16,800-a-year swing that no realistic tax difference offsets.
