One cat runs $9-$15 a month on clay (budget to premium), $4-$9 on pine pellets, and $22-$24 on silica subscriptions like PrettyLitter. The lever that matters isn't brand — it's price per pound times pounds used, and the two move in opposite directions across substrates. Here's the math that holds.

The only two numbers that matter

Litter is a commodity sold in deliberately confusing bag sizes, so normalize everything to dollars per pound and pounds used per cat per month. A $22 40-lb bag of premium clumping clay is 55 cents a pound; the same product in 14-lb boxes can top 90. Meanwhile the substrates themselves differ more than any two brands do:

Notice the pattern: expensive-per-pound substrates use less volume, so monthly costs compress into a $4-$19 band for almost everything except premium silica. That's why "what's the cheapest litter?" is the wrong question and "what's the cheapest monthly cost?" is the right one.

How long a bag actually lasts

For one cat with daily scooping, a 40-lb bag of clumping clay lasts about 6-7 weeks: you remove clumps, top the box back to 3-4 inches, and dump the remainder monthly, which works out to roughly 25 lb a month. Non-clumping clay needs weekly full changes and consumes closer to 30 lb. Two cats push a 40-lb bag to about three to four weeks. Silica and tofu follow the makers' one-bag-a-month claims per cat — six and eight pounds respectively.

Are subscriptions worth it?

The discounts are thin: Chewy Autoship takes 5% and ships free, Amazon Subscribe & Save runs 5-15%, and on a $30-a-month clay habit that's $18-$54 a year. The direct-to-consumer brands are a different product: PrettyLitter at $24 a cat ($44 for two) works out to about $4 a pound, and Boxiecat around $1.20 — both are convenience products priced right at the retail math, not below it. A useful sanity check: generic silica math for two cats says 2 × 6 lb × $4 = $48 a month, within 9% of PrettyLitter's own two-cat price. Nobody's getting a bargain; some people are buying crystals that change color with urine pH and a bag that appears at the door.

Where the money actually leaks

  • Small bags. Per-pound prices drop 30-50% from 14-lb boxes to 40-lb bags. If you can carry it and store it, buy the big bag.
  • Litter boxes that are too small. A cramped box spreads litter over the floor and out the door; box size drives waste as much as scooping discipline does.
  • Overfilling. Three to four inches is the spec; deeper boxes waste litter with every top-off.
  • Skipping the monthly dump. Clumping litter needs its full reset on schedule, or odor drives premature whole-box replacement.
  • The pine-pellet cheat code. Equine bedding pellets at the farm store are the same compressed pine at under 20 cents a pound — the cheapest legitimate cat litter in America, if your cat accepts pellets.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget for cat litter monthly?

One cat: $9-$11 on budget clay, $13-$15 on premium clumping clay (about 50-60 cents a pound at 25 pounds a month), $4-$9 on pine pellets, and $22-$24 on silica subscriptions like PrettyLitter. Two cats on premium clay run about $27.50 a month, $330 a year. Multi-cat households generally spend $25-$60 a month on litter.

How long does a 40 lb bag of litter last one cat?

About 6-7 weeks for clumping clay with daily scooping and a monthly box dump — roughly 25 pounds a month once you account for top-offs. Non-clumping clay changed weekly uses closer to 30 pounds a month, so 5-6 weeks per bag. With two cats, a 40-pound bag lasts about 3-4 weeks.

Is PrettyLitter worth the money?

As a discount, no; as a product, maybe. PrettyLitter works out to about $4 a pound ($24 for a 6-lb bag, one cat) versus 55 cents for premium clay — but silica uses a quarter of the volume, so the monthly gap narrows to roughly $24 versus $13-$15. You are paying for health-monitoring color crystals and doorstep delivery, not savings.
