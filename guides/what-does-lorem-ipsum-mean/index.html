What Does Lorem Ipsum Mean? (Origin, Translation and How Much to Use)

⏱️ 6 min readUpdated September 27, 2026

Lorem ipsum doesn't mean anything as written. It's a scrambled chunk of Latin from Cicero's De finibus bonorum et malorum, written in 45 BC. "Lorem" is the back half of dolorem (pain), and "ipsum" means "itself," so the opening is a broken piece of the phrase "pain itself." Below you'll find the original line, what each word came from, and how much filler to use for headlines, buttons and body copy.

What does lorem ipsum mean?

The standard passage is cut from a longer sentence in which Cicero talks about pleasure and pain. Here's the original Latin, from sections 1.10.32 and 1.10.33:

"Neque porro quisquam est, qui dolorem ipsum, quia dolor sit, amet, consectetur, adipisci velit, sed quia non numquam eius modi tempora incidunt, ut labore et dolore magnam aliquam quaerat voluptatem."

H. Rackham's 1914 Loeb Classical Library translation starts: "Nor again is there anyone who loves or pursues or desires to obtain pain of itself, because it is pain, but occasionally circumstances occur in which toil and pain can procure him some great pleasure."

Put the two side by side and the familiar filler jumps out. "Dolorem ipsum" became "lorem ipsum." "Tempora incidunt" became "tempor incididunt." "Magnam aliquam" lost its endings and turned into "magna aliqua."

Where does lorem ipsum come from?

For a long time nobody knew. The link to Cicero was found by Richard McClintock, a Latin scholar at Hampden-Sydney College in Virginia. He looked up consectetur, a rare word, and traced it to De finibus. He shared the find in a 1994 letter to the editor of Before & After magazine.

You'll often read that printers have used lorem ipsum "since the 1500s." That claim came from McClintock too, and he later said it was a guess with no evidence behind it. So treat the Renaissance story as folklore.

The firmer history is 20th century. Letraset, the British company behind rub-on dry-transfer lettering, printed lorem ipsum on its body-type sheets in the 1960s, and that's the version most of us know. It spread further with desktop publishing. Aldus PageMaker came out in 1985, and it's widely reported to have shipped templates filled with lorem ipsum, though we couldn't find a primary source for that detail.

One neat theory about the scrambling: in the 1914 Loeb edition, the Latin on page 34 breaks off at "qui do-" and resumes on page 36 with "lorem ipsum." Start copying at the top of page 36 and you'd get exactly the word "lorem."

Is lorem ipsum real Latin?

Half real. Words like dolor, sit, amet, consectetur and velit are proper Latin forms. Others aren't. "Lorem" isn't a word by itself, and "adipiscing" bolts an English-looking ending onto a Latin verb.

A Latin teacher can pick out words. Nobody can translate a full paragraph, because there's nothing coherent to translate.

Why do designers use lorem ipsum?

Because real words pull attention. Show a client real sentences and they'll edit the copy instead of judging the layout. Lorem ipsum looks like text, with a normal spread of short and long words, but gives the eye nothing to read.

It also beats typing "text text text," which makes every line identical and hides how a paragraph will wrap.

When should you replace it (and what are the alternatives)?

Replace lorem ipsum as soon as real copy exists, even a rough draft. Filler hides problems only real words expose, like a German heading twice as long as the English one, or copy that's longer than anyone planned.

Some options that work better than Latin once you're past wireframes:

  • Proto-content. Rough real copy at the right length, marked as draft.
  • Target-language text. For localized sites, test with actual German, Finnish or Japanese strings.
  • Worst-case strings. The longest name, title or price you expect, dropped into every tight spot.
  • Real labels, always. Keep buttons, form labels and alt text genuine from the start, since screen readers will read lorem ipsum aloud as-is.

The bottom line

Lorem ipsum is Cicero's line about nobody loving "pain itself," cut up until it stopped meaning anything. That's the point. Use it to judge layout, size it to the real content, and swap it out the moment real words show up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does lorem ipsum mean in English?

Nothing exact, because the text is scrambled. It comes from a line by Cicero that H. Rackham translated in 1914 as "Nor again is there anyone who loves or pursues or desires to obtain pain of itself, because it is pain." Lorem is the tail end of dolorem (pain), and ipsum means itself.

Is lorem ipsum real Latin?

Partly. Words like dolor, sit, amet, consectetur and velit are genuine Latin taken from Cicero. Others were chopped or bent out of shape: lorem is half of dolorem, and adipiscing is adipisci with an extra ending. As a whole, the passage doesn't form real sentences.

Who wrote the original lorem ipsum text?

The words come from Cicero's De finibus bonorum et malorum (On the Ends of Good and Evil), written in 45 BC, sections 1.10.32 and 1.10.33. Nobody knows who first scrambled it into filler text. The version most people use today was spread by Letraset dry-transfer sheets in the 1960s.

How many words are in the standard lorem ipsum sentence?

The classic opening, Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit, sed do eiusmod tempor incididunt ut labore et dolore magna aliqua, has 19 words and 123 characters including spaces and punctuation.

What can I use instead of lorem ipsum?

Use real draft copy whenever you have it, even rough notes. Other options are proto-content written at the right length, text in the target language for localized layouts, or deliberately long strings to stress-test buttons and headings.
