How to Calculate Your Net Worth

💡 15K searches/mo💰 CPC: $6⏱️ 7 min read

Net worth is the single most important number in personal finance. It tells you exactly where you stand financially — not what you earn, not what you spend, but what you actually own after everything is accounted for.

The Net Worth Formula

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

That's it. Everything you own minus everything you owe. If the number is positive, you're solvent. If it's negative, you have more debt than assets — which is common for young adults with student loans.

What Counts as an Asset?

Assets are things you own that have monetary value. For a net worth calculation, include:

Cash and equivalents: Checking accounts, savings accounts, money market funds, CDs, cash under the mattress.

Investments: Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, crypto, brokerage accounts. Use current market value.

Retirement accounts: 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, pension value, HSA.

Real estate: Primary residence at current market value (check Zillow or recent sales), rental properties, land.

Vehicles: Cars, motorcycles, boats — at current market value (Kelley Blue Book), not purchase price.

Valuables: Jewelry, art, collectibles, business ownership — only if you could realistically sell them.

What to exclude: Future income, expected inheritance, skills and education (human capital is real but not liquidatable).

What Counts as a Liability?

Mortgage balance: Remaining principal on your home loan, not the original loan amount.

Auto loans: Remaining balance on any vehicle loans.

Student loans: Both federal and private.

Credit card balances: Current balances, not monthly spending.

Personal loans: Money borrowed from banks, family, or friends.

Medical debt: Outstanding medical bills.

Tax debt: Any unpaid income or property taxes.

Other obligations: Timeshare balances, store credit accounts, payday loans.

Step-by-Step: Calculate Your Net Worth

  1. Gather your account statements. Log into every financial account. Note current balances for checking, savings, investments, retirement, and credit cards.
  2. Value your real estate. Check Zillow for a home value estimate. For rental properties, use comparable sales. Subtract the mortgage balance to get equity.
  3. Value your vehicles. Use Kelley Blue Book or Edmunds for accurate car values. Subtract any auto loan balance.
  4. Add up all assets. Include cash, investments, retirement accounts, property equity, vehicle values, and significant valuables.
  5. Add up all liabilities. Include all debts: mortgage, car loans, student loans, credit cards, personal loans, medical debt, tax debt.
  6. Subtract liabilities from assets. The result is your net worth. Track it quarterly to monitor progress.

Worked Example: 35-Year-Old Professional

Assets:
Checking/savings: $12,000
401(k): $85,000
Roth IRA: $22,000
Brokerage: $15,000
Home value: $350,000
Car value: $18,000
Total Assets: $502,000

Liabilities:
Mortgage balance: $280,000
Student loans: $25,000
Auto loan: $10,000
Credit card: $3,000
Total Liabilities: $318,000

Net Worth: $502,000 − $318,000 = $184,000

Why Tracking Net Worth Matters

Income doesn't equal wealth. Someone earning $200,000 but spending $210,000 is going backward. Someone earning $70,000 and saving $20,000 annually is building real wealth.

Net worth strips away illusions. It shows whether your financial decisions are compounding in your favor or against you. Tracking it quarterly gives you a scorecard that's immune to lifestyle inflation — you can't hide from the number.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average net worth by age?

Median net worth: age 25-34 is about $39,000; age 35-44 is $135,000; age 45-54 is $247,000; age 55-64 is $365,000; age 65+ is $335,000. These are heavily skewed by home equity.

What counts as an asset for net worth?

Cash, savings, investments, retirement accounts, real estate equity (value minus mortgage), vehicles at market value, and valuable items like jewelry or art.

What is a good net worth for a 30-year-old?

A healthy net worth for a 30-year-old is $25,000-$100,000. By 30, aim to have at least your annual salary saved/invested. Negative net worth is common due to student loans.

Should I include my car in my net worth?

Yes, at current market value, not what you paid. Use Kelley Blue Book. Subtract any auto loan balance. Be aware that cars depreciate quickly.

What if my net worth is negative?

A negative net worth means you owe more than you own. This is common for recent graduates. Focus on paying down debt and building savings. Your net worth will improve as debts decrease.
