How Much Does Hybrid Battery Replacement Cost?

🔋 20K searches/mo⏱️ 7 min read

Millions of 2010s Priuses, Camrys and Fusion hybrids are now old enough for their packs to fade, and the bill spans a wider range than almost any other repair: $900 for a junkyard pull to $12,000 for a luxury PHEV pack from the dealer. Here's what each path actually costs, how to tell if you're still under warranty, and when the repair stops making sense.

Plan on $1,500 to $6,500 installed for the models people actually drive. The spread within a single car is huge because there are three repair paths, not one:

Which repair path should you pick?

New OEM makes sense on newer or higher-value hybrids, or where an extended warranty subsidizes it. You get all-fresh cells and dealer backing, and that's what the top of every quote range buys.

Remanufactured is where most value lives. A good rebuilder tests every module, replaces the weak ones, and capacity-matches the rest before shipping. At roughly half the dealer price with a real warranty, it's the default choice for a 10-year-old Prius or Camry.

Used pulls are the gamble. A yard pack with documented mileage and cell balance can be a bargain; an untested one just moves the failure date. Your old pack isn't worthless either — rebuilders pay $250–$500 for dead Toyota NiMH packs as cores, which comes straight off the bill.

Are you still under warranty?

Before paying anyone, check the calendar and the odometer. Federal rules require hybrid batteries to be covered for 8 years or 100,000 miles from the original in-service date, whichever hits first, and the coverage follows the car to later owners. Several states that follow California emissions rules extend qualifying models to 10 years or 150,000 miles. Toyota and Lexus cover 2020 and newer hybrids nationwide for 10 years/150,000 miles, and Hyundai covers most of its hybrids for life for the original owner.

A 2019 Camry Hybrid placed in service in March 2019 with 68,000 miles on it in August 2026 is 7 years 5 months old and well under the mileage cap — that pack is a warranty job, not a $5,000 repair. The VIN and in-service date at any dealer gets you a definitive answer in minutes.

Is it worth replacing, or should you scrap the car?

Compare the repair bill to the value gap between a running hybrid and a dead one. A 2012 Prius with a good pack retails around $6,000–$8,000; the same car with a P0A80 code sells for $2,000–$3,000. Spend $2,275 on a reman pack and you've created $1,000–$3,000 of net value while keeping a known car. If the quote clears half the car's running value, or the hybrid battery is arriving alongside a transmission and a set of struts, get a junker valuation before deciding — the junk car value calculator gives you that floor number.

Remember what the battery buys, too. A 50 MPG hybrid driven 12,000 miles a year at $3.40/gallon burns $816 of fuel; a comparable 30 MPG gas car burns $1,360. That $544 annual difference compounds over the remaining life of the car — the same logic worth checking before any big repair with the car repair cost calculator.

How long do hybrid batteries actually last?

Ten to fifteen years and 150,000 to 250,000 miles covers the majority of packs, with Toyota's NiMH designs at the durable end. Degradation is gradual: fuel economy slides first, then a capacity warning, then a diagnostic code. If yours is aging gracefully at 12 years, budget for the eventual swap rather than treating it as a surprise — and note that heat, frequent deep cycling, and sitting unused for months are the three biggest accelerants.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace the battery in a Prius?

A 2004–2015 Prius runs $1,500 to $2,600 installed with a remanufactured pack, $2,800 to $4,900 new from a dealer, and $900 to $1,600 with a used salvage pull. Most owners pay around $2,000 to $2,500 going the reman route, which usually carries a 2-year warranty.

Is it worth replacing a hybrid battery?

Usually yes, if the car is otherwise sound. A 2012 Prius is worth roughly $6,000 to $8,000 running and $2,000 to $3,000 with a dead pack, so a $2,275 reman replacement restores more value than it costs. If the quote is over half the car's running value, or a second major repair is also due, the math flips toward selling or scrapping.

Does the hybrid battery warranty still cover me?

Possibly. Federal rules require 8 years or 100,000 miles of coverage from the original in-service date, whichever comes first, and it transfers to later owners. States following California emissions rules often extend qualifying models to 10 years or 150,000 miles, and Toyota covers 2020 and newer hybrids for 10 years or 150,000 miles nationwide.
