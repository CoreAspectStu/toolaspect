How Much Do Wedding Decorations Cost? The Fixtures-and-Rentals Answer

🕯️ 14K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3.00⏱️ 7 min read

Wedding decor and lighting — the fixtures and rentals, flowers excluded — runs about $4,420 for a mid-size production and spans $675 lean to $7,800+ full. The big lines: uplighting at $19-$75 per fixture, pipe-and-drape at $8-$20 a linear foot, ceiling draping at $1.50-$6 per square foot, linens at $10-$35 a table, and the delivery/install/strike fee that gets it all in and out. Lighting: the highest-impact dollar in decor

Nothing else reshapes a room for the money. Professional uplighting washes walls in color at $50-$75 a fixture — a 16-fixture ballroom costs about $1,040 installed — while the same fixtures rented DIY and placed by a groomsman run $400. Complete professional packages (uplights, pinspots on the cake, a monogram) typically land at $1,500-$2,500. The honest rule of thumb: blank ballrooms need lighting more than decorated ones do, and a room with warm existing light needs almost none.

Draping: floor level vs ceiling, a 10x difference

Pipe-and-drape is scaffolding and fabric at ground level — $8-$20 a linear foot, so an 80-foot perimeter or head-table run costs $640-$1,600. Ceiling draping is rigging: ladders or lifts, fire-rated fabric, venue-approved anchor points, and a crew before and after. It prices by area at $1.50-$6 a square foot, and real quotes bear it out — $4,000 for ten panels, $7,500 minimums from some installers. Covering a 1,200-square-foot ballroom ceiling runs $1,800-$7,200 before the rest of the decor. If the venue has a statement ceiling, beams, or string lights already, that line is the first one to cut.

Full production ($6,900): the mid room with 1,200 square feet of ceiling drape ($3,600) instead of the pipe run. The drape line triples and dominates the budget — which is exactly why installers quote it separately from everything else.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do wedding decorations cost?

Excluding flowers, expect $675 for a lean DIY setup to $7,800+ for a professionally installed production, with $4,420 typical for a mid-size room: 16 installed uplights, 80 feet of pipe-and-drape, linens and candles for 20 tables, chair sashes, an arch, signage, and the install fee. Counting flowers, The Knot's combined florals-and-decor category averages about $3,100 — roughly 9% of the average wedding.

How much does wedding uplighting cost per light?

DIY rentals run $19-$40 per fixture shipped; professional installation runs $50-$75 per fixture plus a delivery fee around $100. Sixteen fixtures cost about $400 DIY versus $1,040 installed, and complete professional lighting packages (uplights, pinspots, a monogram) typically run $1,500-$2,500 with a $300-$5,000 full range.

Why is ceiling draping so expensive?

You're paying for labor and rigging, not fabric. Ceiling work requires ladders or lifts, fire-code-rated fabric, attachment to venue-approved points, and a crew both before and after the event — so it prices by area covered, roughly $1.50-$6 per square foot. Real quotes run near $4,000 for ten panels, and some installers set $7,500 minimums. Pipe-and-drape at floor level, by contrast, runs $8-$20 a linear foot.

Do I need chair covers and sashes?

Only if the chairs are ugly. Chiavari or crossback chairs look finished bare, and venue-standard banquet chairs are often fine with a sash alone ($1-$5) — skip the cover ($2-$10) and you halve the line. For 160 chairs, covers-plus-sashes run about $560-$2,080 at typical rates; a sash-only look runs $160-$800.

What's the cheapest way to make a reception look decorated?

Candlelight and uplighting. DIY-rented uplights at $19-$25 a fixture repaint a room for $300-$400, and candle or lantern centerpieces run $10-$60 a table against $75-$300 for fresh florals. Combine both with the venue's existing architecture — a room with character needs a fraction of the dressing a blank ballroom does.
