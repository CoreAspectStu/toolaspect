How Much Does an Ignition Interlock Cost?

🔑 IID program costs⏱️ 7 min read

An ignition interlock device (IID) runs $60–$120 a month to lease, plus a $70–$150 installation, $20–$30 calibration visits every 30–60 days, and a $50–$100 removal. A 6-month first-offense program totals $610–$1,010 (mid-range: $760, or $126.67/mo effective); a 12-month term runs $1,045–$1,820. You pay every dollar of it — these are offender-paid costs.

⚠️ Informational estimate, not legal advice. IID terms and rules vary by state, offense level, and judge, and they change. Confirm your requirement, term, and approved providers with your DMV, court, or monitoring authority.

The four-part cost stack

Providers advertise the monthly lease because it's the smallest-looking number. The real budget has four parts:

  • Installation: $70–$150. One-time, paid to the local service center that wires the device into your ignition.
  • Monthly lease: $60–$120. Covers the device and the state-required monitoring/reporting. Most invoices land near $80–$90.
  • Calibrations: $20–$30 per visit. Every 30 days in some states, every 60–90 in others. Each visit downloads your data and verifies the device.
  • Removal: $50–$100. Paid at the end, only when your monitoring authority confirms the term is complete — remove it early without authorization and you can restart the clock.

The term matters more than the provider

Your state's required term drives the total harder than any pricing decision:

First-offense terms where mandated commonly run 6 months in California, Florida, New York, and Texas; 8 in Colorado; about 12 in Arizona, Pennsylvania, and Washington. But the details that actually set your term — BAC level, test refusal, prior offenses, judge discretion — vary enormously, and several states only mandate the device for elevated-BAC first offenses or repeats. This is a check-with-the-court number, not a chart number.

A worked example

Six-month California term, mid-range provider: $100 install + 6 × $85 lease ($510) + 3 calibration visits × $25 ($75) + $75 removal = $760. That's $126.67 a month effective, or $4.17 a day — well above the "$2.75/day" a quote might headline, because the fixed fees amortize over a short term. The same setup over Arizona's 12 months: $1,345, an effective $112.08 a month. Longer terms dilute the fixed costs; that's the one small mercy of a longer requirement.

The fees nobody mentions

  • Vehicle transfer: $100–$200 if you change cars mid-program — the device moves, a new install fee appears.
  • Lockout service calls. Missed calibrations or too many failed starts can put the device into a countdown that requires a service-center visit, at a fee.
  • Early removal violations. Pulling the device before your authority authorizes it can reset the requirement entirely.
  • Camera and GPS add-ons, required in some states, bundled invisibly in others — ask what your quote includes.

The IID is also only one line of the post-DUI budget. An SR-22 filing and the high-risk insurance tier usually cost more per month than the device itself — the SR-22 insurance cost calculator prices that stack, and the rate increase calculator estimates the surcharge itself. Before the fact, the BAC calculator is the cheaper tool by several thousand dollars.

Cutting the cost honestly

Three legitimate levers: ask about low-income fee reductions — several states have them and providers don't advertise them; keep every calibration appointment, since violations extend terms and lockouts cost fees; and quote two or three certified providers locally before signing, because install promos come and go. What doesn't work: skipping payments (the device reports it), shopping for a device online (states certify providers), or borrowing someone else's car without an interlock (a new, worse offense). Drive the term cleanly, pay the stack once, and it ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an ignition interlock cost per month?

$60-$120 per month for the lease, with most invoices landing near $80-$90. Teaser rates as low as $1.96/day are real but rare — real-world billing clusters at $2.60-$4.00 per day once monitoring and state fees stack on. Budget on the program total, not the headline rate.

What fees does an interlock add beyond the monthly lease?

Installation runs $70-$150, calibration or service visits run $20-$30 every 30-60 days, removal runs $50-$100 at the end, and vehicle transfers cost $100-$200. Missed appointments can trigger lockout service fees. A 6-month program's bookend fees add roughly $250-$350 on top of the lease itself.

Which interlock provider is cheapest?

Advertised ranges overlap heavily: Smart Start leases from about $60/month but charges from $150 to install; Draeger often bundles a low install fee into an ~$80/month lease; Guardian runs $50-$90/month. The spread between major providers on a 6-month program is usually $100-$200 — often less than the cost of switching mid-program, so service quality and a nearby service center usually matter more than price.

How long is a first-offense interlock requirement?

Where states mandate IIDs for a first offense, terms commonly run 6 to 12 months: California, Florida, New York, and Texas at roughly 6 months; Colorado at 8 months; Arizona, Pennsylvania, and Washington at about 12. Terms depend on BAC level, refusal, and judge discretion, and several states only mandate the device for higher-BAC or repeat offenses. Verify yours with the DMV or court.

Is there financial help for interlock costs?

Some states offer fee reductions or waivers for verified low-income participants, and a few credit part of the cost against fines. The programs exist but providers rarely advertise them — ask the court clerk or monitoring authority directly. What nobody offers is insurance coverage: auto policies do not pay for interlock devices.
