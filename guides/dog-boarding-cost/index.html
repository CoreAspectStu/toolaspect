How Much Dog Boarding Costs per Night (and What Quietly Adds Up)

🏨 8 min read🧮 2025 national rates + worked bill

The vacation is booked, the dog isn't. A quick search gives you nightly rates from $25 to $185, which tells you almost nothing, because the number that lands on your card depends on the tier you pick, the add-ons that stack per night, and whether any of your dates touch a holiday surcharge window. Here's what each tier of boarding actually costs nationally, and how to price a stay before you commit.

Those figures come from a mix of published facility price lists and Rover's September 2025 national booking data (sitter-home boarding averaged $49.01 a night; Seattle ran $64, Chicago $54, with independent in-home sitters clustering near $75 for true overnights). Metro matters as much as tier — the same national chain runs meaningfully different rates between a suburb and a downtown — and dog size adds a little at facilities that price by run size.

The add-ons that turn $55 into $76

Nightly rates cover the run and the potty breaks. Everything else is line-itemed: extra walks at $5-$10 a day, playtime sessions at $10-$15 a day (solo sessions more than group), medication administration at $5-$10 a day, and required "activity packages" at some facilities that bundle the lot whether or not your dog needs it.

Run a typical week: 7 nights at a $55 standard facility, daily playtime at $12, daily meds at $7, with 2 nights in the Christmas window carrying a 15% surcharge. Base: $385. Add-ons: $133. Surcharge: $16.50. Total: $534.50 — $76.36 a night all-in, roughly 40% over the headline rate. Booking around the holidays

Thanksgiving and Christmas are the boarding industry's peak weeks, and pricing behaves like it: surcharges of 10-25% on holiday nights (or a flat per-night fee), plus the quiet tyranny of sellouts. Good facilities fill 2-3 months out, and the inventory left in November skews to the expensive end of every tier. If your dates are flexible even by a day — flying Tuesday instead of Wednesday, for instance — the surcharge math alone can pay for the change fee.

Vaccines and the pre-stay bill

Practically every facility requires current rabies, DHPP, and bordetella, and many add canine influenza; group-play setups typically want a temperament evaluation too. If any of those are due, that's a vet visit before the boarding visit, and it belongs in the budget. Boarding facilities also send home the occasional cough regardless of vaccination — kennel cough circulates wherever dogs mix, which is worth knowing before you blame the facility.

How to cut the bill honestly

  • Match the tier to the dog. A mellow senior who sleeps 20 hours a day does not need a $110 suite with a webcam; a velcro dog with separation anxiety might genuinely need a sitter's home.
  • Untick the activity menu. Playtime and walk add-ons are the fastest 25-40% on any bill. One add-on, not three, is plenty for most dogs.
  • Check the app platforms' final totals. Rover-style services add fees at checkout and sitters set their own holiday rates — compare checkout prices, not listed ones.
  • Split the care. For a week, some dogs do better (and cheaper) with 2 or 3 weekday drop-in visits plus a shorter boarding stretch — the pet sitter rates calculator prices the drop-in alternative.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost to board a dog for a week?

Around $245 at a basic kennel ($35 a night), $385 at a standard facility ($55), close to $343 through a sitter-home app like Rover ($49-50 average), and $770 or more at a luxury pet hotel ($110). Add-ons stack on top at $5-$15 each per night, so a week with daily playtime and medication runs $530-$570 at a mid-range facility.

Is Rover cheaper than a kennel?

Sometimes. Rover's September 2025 national average for boarding in a sitter's home was $49.01 a night — cheaper than most standard facilities ($40-$75) but more than a basic kennel ($25-$50). City averages vary: about $64 in Seattle, $54 in Chicago. Apps also add service fees at checkout, and holiday-week sitters set their own peak pricing, so compare the final checkout total, not the listed rate.

Do boarding facilities charge extra for holidays?

Most do — typically 10-25% more on nights inside Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year windows, or a flat per-night fee in place of a percentage. Two Christmas nights at a $55 rate with a 15% surcharge add $16.50. The bigger holiday cost is scarcity: prime facilities book out 2-3 months ahead, and whatever is left skews expensive.

Why is my boarding quote higher than the website rate?

Because the website rate is the run, not the stay. Facilities layer on mandatory or opt-in extras: required activity packages, playtime at $10-$15 a day, walks at $5-$10, medication at $5-$10, and fees for thicker bedding or solo (rather than group) play. On a $55 headline rate, daily playtime plus meds pushes the true nightly cost past $70 — a 30%+ jump that only shows up on the final invoice.

Do boarders charge per medication or per night?

All three models exist: a flat $5-$10 per night for oral meds, per-administration tiers (about $3.50 for 1-2 medications and $5.50 for 3-4), and per-pill pricing that can reach $3 a pill for heavy schedules. Injections run $10-$30. A few facilities include basic oral medication free. Ask before booking if your dog takes anything daily.
