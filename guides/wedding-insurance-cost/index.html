What are you actually insuring?

Wedding insurance is two policies wearing one name. Event liability pays when a guest is injured or someone's property is damaged during your events — the DJ's speaker falls on an aunt, a guest backs into the venue's fountain. Cancellation and postponement coverage reimburses your deposits when circumstances outside your control force a change: a vendor goes bankrupt, the venue floods, extreme weather hits, or a key person (the couple, immediate family, the officiant) is hospitalized or military-deployed.

Most venues that require insurance want the first kind only — $1 million of liability naming them as an additional insured. Most couples who buy a policy on their own initiative want the second. They're priced separately, and our wedding insurance cost calculator prices them together.

Is wedding insurance worth it?

Run the ratio. If your non-refundable commitments total more than about $5,000, a cancellation policy in the $150-$280 range is cheap protection against a tail risk you can't control. Below that, liability-only coverage at $75-$185 still makes sense if your venue requires it or you're serving alcohol — a single guest-injury claim can run well into six figures.

Two honest caveats. First, a change of heart is excluded everywhere; nobody insures against you deciding not to marry. Second, pandemic-related shutdowns are broadly excluded on current policies — if a public-health order closes your venue, coverage depends on the venue being unavailable rather than the order itself. Read the exclusions page of any quote before paying.

When should you buy it?

The moment deposits start. Cancellation coverage must be in force before the loss, not after the hurricane forms; buy it with your first venue payment and it covers every deposit you make afterward. Some carriers require purchase at least two weeks before the wedding, which rules out the panic-buy the day before. Liability coverage technically can wait until the final week, but there's no discount for procrastinating.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is wedding insurance on average?

Most couples pay $200-$350 for a policy combining liability and cancellation coverage, with the full market running $75-$550. Liability-only policies (often all a venue requires) cost $75-$250 at $1 million limits. Cancellation coverage starts at $130 for $7,500 of insured costs.

Is wedding insurance worth it?

The math favors it once your non-refundable deposits pass a few thousand dollars. A $445 policy on $30,000 of insured costs is 1.3% — one bankrupt photographer or one hurricane weekend repays it many times over. For a small backyard wedding with $1,500 at risk, liability-only coverage at around $100 makes more sense than cancellation coverage.

What does wedding insurance not cover?

The big exclusion is a change of heart — no carrier pays because the couple calls it off by choice. Pandemics and government-mandated shutdowns are generally excluded too, and coverage for items like rings and attire often requires a special-events rider on a separate policy. Read the exclusions page before buying.

Do venues require wedding insurance?

Increasingly, yes. Hotels, estates, museums, and public parks commonly require $1 million of event liability naming the venue as additional insured. The requirement protects them, not you, but a $75-$185 liability-only policy satisfies nearly every such clause.

When should you buy wedding insurance?

As soon as non-refundable deposits start leaving your account, because cancellation coverage must be in force before a loss occurs. Some carriers require purchase at least two weeks before the event. Liability coverage can technically be bought the week of the wedding, but there's no discount for waiting.
