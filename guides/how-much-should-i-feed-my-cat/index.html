How Much Should I Feed My Cat?

🐱 15K searches/mo⏱️ 6 min read

Cats are small, which makes feeding errors easy: an extra half-cup of kibble a week is the feline equivalent of a daily donut. Roughly half of indoor cats are overweight, and portions measured by scoop instead of by numbers are the main reason. Here's the formula vets use, a table by weight, and the can-count and fresh-food math for whatever you're pouring.

What's the formula vets use?

The veterinary approach starts from the resting energy requirement: RER = 70 x (weight in kg)^0.75. That 0.75 exponent is Kleiber's law, and it's why a 20 lb cat burns about 2.8 times what a 5 lb cat burns, not four times. The same equation runs our dog food calculator; only the factors change.

The standard feline factors, from the charts veterinary nutrition centers use:

  • Kitten under 12 months: x2.5 — growth is expensive
  • Intact adult: x1.4
  • Neutered or spayed indoor adult: x1.2 — the default for most house cats
  • Active or outdoor access: x1.6
  • Weight gain: x1.8
  • Weight loss: x1.0 — RER alone, calculated at the target weight
  • Senior 7+: x1.1 — many seniors slow down and need less

Worked example: a 10 lb tabby weighs 4.5 kg. RER = 70 x 4.5^0.75 = 218 kcal. She's spayed and indoor, so 218 x 1.2 = 261 kcal a day. That's the entire calculation. Run your own cat's numbers, including treats and meals-per-day splitting, in the cat food calculator.

How much fresh food should I feed?

Fresh and gently cooked brands (Smalls, JustFoodForCats, Raised Right, Darwin's, and others) cluster around 28-40 kcal per ounce, roughly 1.0-1.4 kcal per gram, and ship portioned packets sized near a day's food for common weights. At 35 kcal/oz, our 261-kcal tabby eats 7.5 oz a day. Every packet prints its kcal, so divide daily calories by the packet figure and you're done. Fresh food's main practical edge for cats is moisture and portion control, not magic: the calorie math is identical.

How do I feed an overweight cat?

Factor 1.0, calculated at the target weight. A 15 lb cat whose lean weight is 11 lb gets RER at 11 lb = 234 kcal a day, even though maintaining a neutered 15 lb cat takes 354 kcal. That gap is the diet.

Two cat-specific cautions. Weight loss must be slow, about 0.5-1% of body weight per week, because cats that stop eating or diet too hard can develop hepatic lipidosis, a liver emergency. And a vet should confirm the target weight first; a 15 lb Maine Coon and a 15 lb domestic shorthair are very different patients. Weigh the cat every two weeks on the same scale.

Free feeding or meals?

A cat's natural pattern is 8-10 small meals a day, the hunting rhythm. The modern substitutes: measured meals two to three times daily, or a puzzle feeder that meters the same total into slow pursuit. Free feeding works only for the minority of cats who self-regulate; for the rest, a bottomless bowl is the direct path back to this article. If you track your own intake the way we do in the calorie calculator, the principle transfers exactly: total calories in, portions measured, adjustments in 10% steps.

When should I change how much I feed?

  • Spay or neuter: drop from 1.4 to 1.2, about a 14% calorie cut, timed with the procedure
  • Kitten to adult: at about 12 months, 2.5 drops to 1.2 — a bigger cut than most owners expect
  • Indoor to outdoor access: toward 1.4-1.6 depending on how much the cat actually hunts
  • Senior years: many cats settle near 1.1; some seniors with hyperthyroidism lose weight and need more, which is a vet conversation
  • New food: recalculate portions from the new label, every switch
  • Two to three weeks after any change: rib check, waist check, adjust 10%

The final scoreboard is the cat, not the formula. You should feel ribs under a thin fat layer, see a waist from above, and find minimal belly pouch from the side. That's a body condition score of 5 out of 9. Any big change, a weight-loss plan, or a kitten of a large breed should involve your vet; everything else, the calculator plus a scale handles.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cans of wet food should I feed my cat a day?

Divide daily calories by the kcal per can on the label. A 10 lb neutered indoor cat needs about 261 kcal a day; on a typical 95-kcal 3-oz can that's 2.7 cans, split across two or three meals. The same cat on a 5.5-oz can at 165 kcal gets 1.6 cans a day. The label's kcal figure beats any breed-based chart, because cans vary from 70 to 200 kcal.

How much dry food should I feed my cat?

By weight, not by scoop. A 10 lb neutered cat eating a 350 kcal/cup kibble gets 261 kcal, or 0.75 cups a day, which is only about 1.7 oz by weight. A level 8-oz measuring cup of dense kibble can hold 450 kcal, so a 'cup a day' habit overshoots by 70 percent. A $12 kitchen scale makes overfeeding almost impossible.

How much fresh food does a cat eat per day?

Fresh brands like Smalls, JustFoodForCats, Raised Right, and Darwin's run about 28-40 kcal per ounce (1.0-1.4 kcal/g). A 261-kcal cat needs 7-9 oz a day, at 35 kcal/oz about 7.5 oz. Start from your cat's RER x factor, divide by the packet's kcal, and adjust 10% after two weeks based on body condition.
