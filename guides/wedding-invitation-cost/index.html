How Much Do Wedding Invitations Cost? 2026 Prices + Postage

Invitations are the first wedding purchase where the numbers get away from people: $3 a piece sounds harmless until the suite, the inserts, and the stamps put a $518 average on a line most couples budget at $250. Here's the real price stack — printing tiers, the 2026 postage rates, and the five decisions that quietly double the bill.

What does a wedding invitation suite actually cost?

National anchors first: couples average $220 on invitations alone and $518 on the full suite (invitation, RSVP, save-the-dates, postage), with a standard 100-guest suite running $400-$600. The spread is enormous because printing is tiered:

TierPer setWhat you get
Budget digital$1.25 – $2.00Flat digital print, standard cardstock
Standard$2.00 – $5.00Heavier stock, liners, thermography
Foil / letterpress$5.00 – $10.00Foil stamping, pressed impressions
Luxury custom$10.00+Calligraphy, handmade paper, boxed sets

How much is postage in 2026?

Stationery postage is a rate stack, and every layer is avoidable if you know it exists:

  • Base letter rate: 82¢ for the first ounce (USPS raised the Forever stamp from 78¢ on July 12, 2026).
  • Each additional ounce: +29¢. A typical suite — invitation, RSVP card and envelope, details card — weighs about 2 ounces, so $1.11 to mail.
  • Nonmachinable surcharge: +49¢ for square, rigid, or wax-sealed envelopes. A 1-ounce square invitation costs $1.31, and the surcharge applies per piece even when the mailer is light.
  • RSVP return: +82¢ for a stamped reply envelope, or +65¢ if your reply card is a postcard — or free with an online RSVP.

Add it up and a traditional 2-ounce suite with a stamped reply envelope costs $1.93 per household in stamps alone. On 100 invitations, that's $193 before a single card is printed — and if you chose square envelopes, $242.

How many invitations do you need?

Count households, not guests. Couples and families at one address get one invitation, so a 100-guest list is typically 60-70 households. Order about 10% extra for parents' keepsakes, misprints, and the cousin who materializes six weeks out — reprinting a short run later costs multiples of the unit price. And take one finished, stuffed, sealed suite to the post office to be weighed: 0.9 ounces and 1.1 ounces are different prices.

What adds the most hidden cost?

  1. Square envelopes — 49¢ apiece, purely a shape decision. Rectangles are free.
  2. The RSVP envelope — card printing plus a second stamp per household. Online RSVPs eliminate both; postcards cut the stamp to 65¢.
  3. Insert creep — every extra card adds print cost and risks pushing the suite over an ounce boundary (29¢ per envelope across the whole list). Wedding websites hold directions, hotel blocks, and registries for free.
  4. Inner envelopes — a tradition most couples now skip; they double envelope cost and weight.
  5. Guest addressing — calligraphy at $2-$5 per envelope versus printing addresses directly for pennies.

Run the five moves together — rectangular, digital tier, online RSVP, no inserts, no inner envelope — and a 75-household suite comes in around $182 including stamps, against $707 for the traditional version with the same information inside. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost of wedding invitations?

The full stationery suite averages $518, with most standard 100-guest suites landing between $400 and $600 including postage. Invitations alone, without save-the-dates or extras, average $220. Budget digital printing can bring a suite under $200; letterpress and foil designs push past $1,000.

How much does it cost to mail wedding invitations?

In 2026, a 1-ounce invitation costs 82 cents; each additional ounce adds 29 cents, so a typical 2-ounce suite with an RSVP card costs $1.11 to mail. Square or rigid envelopes add a 49-cent nonmachinable surcharge, and a stamped RSVP envelope adds another 82 cents. Budget roughly $2 per invitation all-in.

How can I save money on wedding invitations?

Use rectangular envelopes to dodge the 49-cent surcharge, keep the suite at or under 2 ounces, switch RSVPs to a website or postcard to save the return stamp, order by household rather than guest count, and take the digital-print tier from an online printer — those five moves routinely cut the bill 40-60%.

When should wedding invitations be mailed?

Save-the-dates go out 6-8 months ahead (earlier for destination weddings), and invitations 8-10 weeks before the wedding, with the RSVP deadline about 3-4 weeks out so you can chase stragglers and give the caterer a final count.

Do you count guests or households for invitations?

Households. Couples, families, and roommates at one address receive one invitation, so a 100-guest list typically translates to 60-70 invitations. Order about 10% extra for keepsakes and last-minute additions.
