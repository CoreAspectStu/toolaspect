How Much Does a Wedding Planner Cost? 2026 Pricing by Tier

💡 18K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3.20⏱️ 7 min read

The wedding industry sells three different products under the title "wedding planner," and the price gap between them is enormous: month-of coordination from $1,200, partial planning from $1,800, and full-service planning averaging about $3,800 (The Knot), with most quotes between $3,000 and $10,000+ — roughly 10-15% of your total budget. Here's what each tier actually buys, and which one your wedding needs.

The three tiers, priced

Why the percentage for full service? The work scales with the wedding. A 200-guest, three-event weekend is quadruple the vendor management of a 40-guest dinner, and pricing as a share of budget tracks that automatically. Note that The Knot's budget data shows planners taking only about 4% of the average $34,200 wedding — that average includes the majority of couples who never hire one at all, which pulls the share down.

What changes the number

  • Market: major-metro planners (NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, Boston) run about 30% above mid-size markets; small towns run about 15% below.
  • Guest count: fees climb about 10% per 50 guests above 100, because day-of management scales with tables, vendors, and moving parts.
  • Complexity: multi-day events, destination weddings, cultural weddings with multiple ceremonies, and long gaps between ceremony and reception all push quotes up — destination work often adds 10-20% or travel billing.
  • Add-ons: rehearsal direction (~$400), design and styling boards (~$600), and post-wedding wrap-up (~$200) are commonly priced separately.
  • Luxury tier: high-end firms can charge 20% of budget or six-figure minimums, and they turn away smaller weddings.

Which tier does your wedding need?

Full service earns its percentage when complexity is real: 150+ guests, multiple events, a destination, or jobs that leave no planning hours. A couple with a $60,000 budget and 180 guests is buying genuine project management, and 10-15% ($6,000-$9,000) is defensible.

Most weddings need less. If you've already found your venue and you're organized, partial planning at $1,800-$6,000 covers the hardest stretch — the final months, when vendor contracts, timelines, and payments pile up. And if you have the planning handled but want your families to actually enjoy the day, month-of coordination at $1,200-$3,500 is the highest satisfaction-per-dollar line in most wedding budgets. Someone professional holding the timeline is the difference between hosting your wedding and working it.

What a planner fee should include

When comparing quotes, make sure the scope is written and covers: planning meetings, vendor sourcing and contract review, timeline building, rehearsal direction, setup and teardown management, and day-of emergency handling. Ask who attends your wedding — the planner you met, or an associate? Firms sometimes sell the founder and send a team member; that's fine if the contract says so and the price reflects it.

Payment structure varies: full-service planners often bill 50% at booking and 50% before the wedding; coordinators usually take a deposit ($300-$800) with the balance due a week or two out. Never pay the full fee before the wedding day.

Where the planner fee fits in the budget

Planning services run about 4% of the average $34,200 wedding — well behind venue and rentals (29%, $12,900 average), catering and drinks (24%), and photo and video (10%). The decision framework is simple: lock the venue, then decide whether your remaining time is worth more than $2,200 to you. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a wedding planner cost?

Couples who hire a full-service planner spend about $3,800 on average, per The Knot, with most quotes between $3,000 and $10,000+ — roughly 10 to 15 percent of the total budget. Partial planning typically runs $1,800 to $6,000 and month-of coordination $1,200 to $3,500. Luxury planners in major metros can charge $12,000 and up.

What's the difference between a wedding planner and a coordinator?

A planner works your entire engagement: budget, venue selection, vendor bookings, design, and timeline. A coordinator (usually sold as month-of or day-of) takes over 4 to 8 weeks before the wedding, confirms vendors, builds the final timeline, and runs the day itself. Planners charge a percentage of budget or a flat fee; coordinators charge a flat $1,200 to $3,500.

Should I tip my wedding planner or coordinator?

Tipping isn't expected for planners who own their company — a thoughtful review, testimonial, or referral is the standard thank-you. For an employed coordinator, $100 to $500 (or up to 10% for exceptional work) is a common gesture. Some couples also cover the planner's meal and a hotel room for late-night teardown.

When should I hire a wedding planner?

Full-service planners are hired 10 to 14 months out, often before the venue. Month-of coordinators are booked 3 to 6 months ahead, and popular ones are gone by wedding season. If you're hiring full service, book before you book anything expensive — the planner's vendor relationships can pay for part of their fee.

Can a wedding planner save me money?

Sometimes, indirectly. Planners know which venues and vendors deliver at each price point, negotiate contracts, and catch costly mistakes like double-booked vendors or minimums you can't meet. They won't usually beat the market on price — but they prevent the expensive errors that sink budgets, which on a $34,000 wedding can exceed their fee.
