How Much Siding Do I Need? Squares by House Size

🧮 7 min readUpdated September 29, 2026

Siding is ordered in squares, and one square covers 100 sq ft of wall. Multiply your perimeter by the wall height, add the gables, subtract large openings, divide by 100 and add 10 to 15% for waste. A two-story 30 × 50 ft house has about 2,805 sq ft of net wall, or 28 squares, so you'd order 32 with 12% waste.

What is a square of siding?

A square is 100 sq ft of wall coverage. It's the unit suppliers quote and the unit most siding is priced in, the same convention roofers use. So 1,500 sq ft of wall is 15 squares, and a quote of $350 a square works out to $3.50 per sq ft of wall for the material alone.

The job of estimating is turning your house into that one number, then adding enough extra that you don't run short on the last wall.

How do you calculate how much siding you need?

You need four measurements, and a tape measure and a notepad will get you all of them in half an hour.

  1. Perimeter. Walk the house and add up the length of every wall. A 30 × 50 ft house has a perimeter of 160 ft.
  2. Wall height. From the bottom of the sheathing to the soffit, not to the roof peak. About 9 ft for a one-story, 18 ft for a two-story.
  3. Gables. The triangles at the ends of a gable roof. Each one is ½ × width × gable height.
  4. Openings. Windows, doors and garage doors. Width × height for each, added up.

Then: (perimeter × height + gables − openings) ÷ 100 = squares. Multiply by 1 plus your waste factor and round up. That's exactly what our siding calculator does.

Worked through for a two-story 30 × 50 ft house: 160 × 18 = 2,880 sq ft of wall. Add two 30 ft gables at 225 sq ft total, take off 300 sq ft of windows and doors, and you're at 2,805 sq ft, or 28.05 squares. With 12% waste that's 31.4, so you order 32 squares.

How do you measure a gable for siding?

A gable is a triangle, so its area is half the base times the height. The base is the width of that end wall. The height depends on the roof pitch: gable height = (width ÷ 2) × (pitch rise ÷ 12).

How much waste should you add for siding?

Waste covers the cuts around windows, the offcuts at corners and gables, and the odd damaged piece. The more corners and openings, the more you throw away.

Going from 5% to 20% adds 4 squares here, which is about $1,400 at $350 a square. That sounds like a lot, but running short is worse. Siding is made in batches, and a reorder weeks later may not match the color of what's already on the wall, especially with vinyl and prefinished fiber cement.

Should you subtract windows and doors?

Partly. Big openings like garage doors and sliding patio doors should come off, because they're whole squares of wall you won't cover. Small windows are different. The pieces you cut to go around them usually can't be reused anywhere, so a 3 × 4 ft window saves much less than its 12 sq ft.

A common approach is to subtract garage doors and large glass fully, subtract ordinary windows, and keep your waste factor at the higher end of the range. Or skip subtracting small windows entirely and use a lower waste factor. Either gets you close. What you shouldn't do is subtract every opening and then use 5% waste.

What else do you need besides siding?

The field siding is only part of the order. Trim and accessories are sold by the piece or the linear foot, so measure them separately.

  • Starter strip along the bottom of every wall, roughly equal to the perimeter.
  • Outside corner posts at every outside corner, floor to soffit.
  • J-channel around windows and doors, along the gable rakes and under the soffit.
  • House wrap behind the siding, covering about the same area as the walls, plus overlaps.
  • Soffit and fascia if you're redoing the eaves. The soffit and fascia calculator covers that separately.

Installation details vary by material, and they matter for the warranty. Vinyl gets nailed in the center of the slots no more than 16 in apart, and the nail heads shouldn't be driven tight, so the panel can move with temperature. Fiber cement like James Hardie wants clearance off the ground (about 6 in) and off roof lines (1 to 2 in). Read the manufacturer's installation guide before you order fasteners, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many squares of siding do I need for a 2,000 sq ft house?

It depends on wall area, not floor area. A 2,000 sq ft two-story home might have 2,200 to 2,800 sq ft of wall, which is 22 to 28 squares before waste, or about 24 to 32 squares with 10 to 15% added. Measure the perimeter and wall height to get a real number.

How many square feet are in a square of siding?

One square of siding covers 100 sq ft of wall. To convert, divide your wall area in square feet by 100. So 1,485 sq ft of wall is 14.85 squares, and you'd order 17 with 12% waste.

How do I calculate the area of a gable?

Multiply the gable's width by its height and divide by 2. The height is half the width times the roof pitch rise over 12. A 30 ft wide gable on a 6/12 roof is 7.5 ft tall, so it's 30 × 7.5 ÷ 2 = 112.5 sq ft.

How much extra siding should I buy?

Add 10% for a simple one-story house, 15% for two stories or lots of windows, and up to 20% for complex rooflines with dormers and diagonal cuts. Round the total up to whole squares. It's cheaper than a reorder that may not match in color.

Do I subtract windows when measuring for siding?

Subtract large openings like garage doors and patio doors. Small windows save less than their size suggests, because the cut pieces around them are usually waste. Many estimators leave small windows in and use a lower waste factor, which lands in about the same place.
