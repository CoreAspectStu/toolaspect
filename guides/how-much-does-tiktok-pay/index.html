How Much Does TikTok Pay?

⏱️ 6 min read

TikTok does not publish a rate card. The Creator Rewards Program pays per qualified view on videos longer than one minute, and creators publicly report roughly $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views in the strongest markets, less elsewhere. A million qualified US views therefore tends to clear a few hundred to a thousand dollars from the program itself, with brand deals and commissions usually contributing more. Here is how each stream works and what the math really produces.

How much does TikTok pay per 1,000 views?

Rewards are calculated from qualified views, and TikTok itself states that payouts vary by video because they depend on views, engagement, audience region, and compliance with program rules. What circulates publicly is creator-reported RPM, and the consistent picture looks like this:

Treat those as planning bands rather than rates. The honest workflow is to divide your actual last payout by your actual qualified views, derive your real RPM, and use that going forward; it automatically bakes in your audience geography and content niche.

What are the Creator Rewards Program requirements?

In the US, as published by TikTok: be at least 18, hold at least 10,000 followers, and have at least 100,000 video views in the trailing 30 days. Only videos longer than one minute are eligible for rewards, and thresholds can shift and vary by country. Accounts below the bar can still earn through LIVE gifts, TikTok Shop commissions, and off-platform brand deals, none of which care about the follower minimum in the same way.

What counts as a qualified view?

This is where most payout surprises come from. The rewards program counts views that meet its quality criteria, generally views from the For You feed that run past the opening seconds, on eligible videos. The number on your profile is raw views. Three disqualifiers shrink the paid count:

  • Length. Videos under one minute earn nothing from rewards, full stop.
  • Originality. Reused, watermarked, or unoriginal content is disqualified, which bites hard on repost-heavy and compilation accounts.
  • Source. Views that do not come through the rewarded surface do not count, so a viral profile-page hit can pay like a modest For You performance.

What happened to the Creator Fund?

The old Creator Fund, which paid pennies per thousand views and became famous for exactly that, stopped accepting new US participants when Creator Rewards arrived, and creators who stayed reported sub-$0.05 RPMs against view counts in the millions. Rewards pays substantially better because it only rewards longer, better-watched videos in ad-valuable markets, which is TikTok aligning payouts with what advertisers actually pay for. If you read an article quoting "a few cents per 1,000 views," check the date; it is describing the dead program.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does TikTok pay per 1,000 views?

TikTok publishes no fixed rate. Creator Rewards payouts vary by video based on qualified views, engagement, region, and content compliance; creators commonly report roughly $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views, with lower figures in lower-ad-demand regions.

How many followers do you need to make money on TikTok?

For the US Creator Rewards Program: 18 or older, at least 10,000 followers, and at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, with only videos over one minute eligible. LIVE gifts and TikTok Shop have their own, different requirements.

Do TikTok LIVE gifts pay real money?

Yes. Viewers buy coins and send gifts during streams; gifts convert to diamonds, which redeem for cash, with TikTok keeping roughly half the value along the way. Gift income is real but unpredictable, and it scales with live hours more than follower count.

Is TikTok money taxable?

In the US, yes: Creator Rewards, gifts, Shop commissions, and brand deals are self-employment income, generally reported on Schedule C, and subject to self-employment tax as well as income tax. Set aside a meaningful share of every payout.

Why did my payout differ from my view count?

Because rewards count qualified views, not the raw counter. Videos under one minute do not earn rewards at all, and reused, watermarked, or unoriginal content is disqualified, so the paid view count is often far below the public one.
