How Much Does Crown Molding Cost?

⏱️ 6 min read

Installed crown molding runs $7 to $16 per linear foot on average, with the full market spanning $4 to $23 depending on what it's made of and how fussy the room is. Material alone starts at $1 a foot for MDF and tops $15 for stain-grade hardwood. The typical project lands near $1,173 total — a solid day of a finish carpenter's time plus a few sticks of trim.

How much is crown molding per linear foot?

Per-foot pricing splits into material and labor, and the two move independently. A cheap material in a difficult room can cost more to install than expensive material in a simple one. Here's the 2026 landscape:

Labor alone typically adds $3 to $6 or more per foot on top of material — the carpenter's cope cuts cost the same whether the stick behind them cost $2 or $14.

What does a whole room cost?

Room pricing starts with perimeter, adds cutting waste, and multiplies by the per-foot rate. Coping inside corners means joints overlap rather than meet, and splice cuts between sticks eat length too, so 15% waste is the standard allowance. Why does the labor cost more than the molding?

Crown is the hardest common trim to install because it's cut at a compound angle while nested against two surfaces at once. Inside corners are coped — one piece runs square into the corner, the other gets its profile sawn with a coping saw so it nests over the first — and a cope takes minutes per joint in skilled hands and much longer otherwise. Walls that bow and corners that aren't square turn each joint into custom work. Rooms also differ in ways per-foot pricing hides: vaulted ceilings need scaffolding, outside corner beads add miter joints, and every obstruction (a duct chase, a beam) adds two joints and a length of offcut.

That's also why quotes vary so much between bidders. A crew that has cut the same profile dozens of times quotes confidently; a generalist building it into a larger remodel pads for learning curve. On a small room, get two bids minimum — the spread routinely covers the material cost.

MDF, pine, or polyurethane — which one should you buy?

MDF is the default for painted trim: it's flat, stable, cheap ($1–$3 a foot primed), and machines cleanly. Its one weakness is moisture — it swells if it gets wet, so skip it in bathrooms. Pine costs about the same in small profiles ($1.35–$1.40 a foot in 16-foot sticks at big-box pricing) and suits both paint and lighter stains. Polyurethane and PVC foams run $2–$10 a foot, hold giant profiles at low weight, and shrug off humidity, which makes them the standard for baths and exterior trim. Hardwoods at $5 to $15+ a foot are for stain-grade work where the grain shows; they also demand the most careful handling, since a miscalculated cope wastes an expensive stick.

One more variable worth knowing before you shop: molding comes in 38°/52° and 45°/45° spring-angle profiles, and your saw settings depend on which you bought. The calculator above shows the miter and bevel pairs for both, cut flat or nested against the fence.

Can you install crown molding yourself?

Yes, and the savings are real — material plus a borrowed saw gets a bedroom done for under $250. The honest caveat is the learning curve lives entirely in the corners. Straight runs between walls are easy; the first coped inside corner takes an hour and the tenth takes ten minutes. Rent or borrow a compound miter saw, cut a foot of scrap into test joints before touching the good stock, and number each piece against a sketch of the room. If a room has more than six corners or ceilings over ten feet, price the pro quote anyway — the DIY math stops favoring you when the waste pile grows.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does crown molding cost per linear foot?

Installed, expect $7 to $16 per linear foot on average, with the full market range running $4 to $23 depending on material and room difficulty. Material alone runs $1 to $3 per foot for MDF, $2 to $6 for polyurethane, and $5 to $15+ for hardwood.

What does crown molding cost for an average room?

Most homeowners spend around $1,173 total. A 15×14 living room runs $460 to $1,100 installed, and a 16×12 room at $7 to $16 per foot works out to roughly $450 to $1,030 once the 15% cutting waste is included.

What is the cheapest crown molding material?

MDF, at $1 to $3 per linear foot primed. It cuts clean, takes paint well, and is what most production trim packages use. Pine is comparably cheap at about $1.35 to $1.40 a foot in 16-foot sticks; polyurethane and hardwood cost more but handle moisture and stain-grade work better.

Can you put crown molding in a bathroom?

Yes, but skip MDF and bare wood, which swell with humidity. Polyurethane or PVC crown at $2 to $10 a foot is the standard choice for bathrooms and laundry rooms — it's stable, moisture-proof, and paintable.

Why does crown molding labor cost more than the material?

Coping inside corners is skilled handwork that doesn't speed up much regardless of material price. Labor typically adds $3 to $6+ per foot over the material cost, and it climbs further with high ceilings, out-of-square walls, and rooms with many corners — all of which add cuts, not footage.
