What Is a Good Credit Score?

💡 200K searches/mo💰 CPC: $10⏱️ 7 min read

A good credit score opens doors to better interest rates, credit card offers, apartment approvals, and even job opportunities. But what counts as "good"? Here is everything you need to know about credit score ranges, how they are calculated, and how to improve yours.

Credit Score Ranges

FICO scores (the most widely used model) range from 300 to 850. Here is how lenders categorize them:

  • 300-579: Poor (subprime borrowing, high rates or denials)
  • 580-669: Fair (may qualify but with higher rates)
  • 670-739: Good (qualify for most loans at reasonable rates)
  • 740-799: Very Good (best rates on most products)
  • 800-850: Exceptional (top-tier offers and lowest rates)

What Is Considered a "Good" Score?

A score of 670 or higher is generally considered good. The average FICO score in the US is 716, which falls in the "good" range. With a 670+ score, you will qualify for most credit cards, auto loans, and mortgages, though the best rates require 740+.

Different lenders have different thresholds. Some premium credit cards require 750+. FHA mortgages accept scores as low as 580. The higher your score, the more options and better rates you get.

How Your Score Is Calculated

FICO scores are based on five factors, weighted by importance:

  • Payment History (35%): On-time payments are the single biggest factor
  • Credit Utilization (30%): Keep balances under 10% of your credit limit
  • Length of Credit History (15%): Older accounts help your score
  • Credit Mix (10%): Having different types of credit (cards, loans) helps
  • New Credit Inquiries (10%): Each hard pull drops your score 3-5 points

How to Improve Your Credit Score

Improving your score takes time but the steps are straightforward:

  • Pay every bill on time — even one late payment can drop your score 50-100 points
  • Keep credit card balances below 10% of your limit (pay mid-cycle if needed)
  • Do not close old credit cards — they help your average account age
  • Limit new credit applications — space them out by 6 months
  • Request credit limit increases (more available credit = lower utilization)
  • Dispute errors on your credit report at all three bureaus
  • Become an authorized user on a family member's old, well-managed card

How Long Does It Take to Build Credit?

Building credit from scratch takes about 6 months of credit history to generate a FICO score. Going from fair (620) to good (700) takes about 6-12 months of consistent good habits. Moving from good to excellent (780+) typically takes 2-5 years. Negative marks like late payments stay on your report for 7 years, but their impact diminishes over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

FHA loans require a minimum 580 FICO score. Conventional loans typically need 620+. For the best mortgage rates, aim for 740+. VA and USDA loans may accept scores as low as 580.

How can I check my credit score for free?

You can get a free credit report from each bureau (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) at AnnualCreditReport.com. Many credit cards and banks now offer free FICO score monitoring. Avoid paid services — the free options are sufficient.

Does checking my credit score lower it?

No. Checking your own score is a "soft inquiry" and does not affect your score. Only "hard inquiries" from lenders when you apply for credit temporarily lower your score by 3-5 points.
