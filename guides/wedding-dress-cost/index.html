How Much Does a Wedding Dress Cost? The Tag Is Only 63% of It

👗 24K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2.50⏱️ 7 min read

The average US wedding dress costs $2,000 — The Knot's 2025 Attire and Fashion Study, ~$2,100 in the Real Weddings data — and most brides spend $1,500-$3,000. But the real line runs about $3,175: alterations at $500 typical ($300-$800), a $150 veil, $250 of accessories, and $275 preservation. Here's every tier, and why the fitting bill doesn't care what the gown cost.

Alterations: the 10-20% rule and the floor beneath it

Shops quote alterations at 10-20% of the gown's price, and for expensive gowns that holds: 15% of a $4,500 designer dress is $675, near the $500-$1,000 most standard gowns actually cost (Zola's band: $300-$800). But a $600 online dress doesn't get a $90 fitting. Hemming starts near $100, taking in the bodice at $150, a bustle from $50, and the individual lines stack to a labor floor around $500 — the seamstress charges the hours, not the tag. That's the quiet truth of the budget path: the cheaper the dress, the bigger the alterations share.

  • Hem — from $100, more with lace or horsehair edges
  • Side seams / bodice take-in — $50-$200
  • Bustle — from $50; multi-point bustles on long trains run higher
  • Straps or sleeves added — from $100
  • Timeline — 6-10 weeks, two to three fittings, booked before the dress arrives if you can

Three brides, all-in

The average bride: $2,000 boutique gown, standard alterations at $500 (the floor beats the $300 percentage quote), $150 veil, $250 accessories, $275 preservation. $3,175 all-in, range $2,150-$5,100. The tag was 63% of the line.

The online bride: $600 made-to-order dress, same $500 of fitting work, same veil, accessories, and box. $1,775 all-in, range $900-$3,000. The $1,400 gap in tags survived intact because both paths pay the alterations floor — a savings, just not the one the price comparison implied.

The designer bride: $4,500 gown needing real reconstruction — 20% is $900, and now the percentage outruns the floor — plus cathedral veil, full accessories, preservation. $6,075 all-in, range $3,950-$10,900. At this tier alterations are tailoring a garment, and it prices like one.

The lines behind the tag

The veil runs $50-$400 (mainstream from $50, designers past $300, cathedral lengths at the top). Shoes, jewelry, and undergarments add $100-$500 — shapewear and the right bra are not the place to economize on a $2,000 silhouette. Preservation costs $200-$400 and matters more than most couples expect: invisible stains oxidize and yellow fabric within months, so it protects both the keepsake and the resale price. And budget the timing, not just the money: 8-10 months out for a standard order — production, shipping, then 6-10 weeks of fittings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the average wedding dress cost?

About $2,000, per The Knot's 2025 Attire and Fashion Study, with the Real Weddings Study just above at ~$2,100. Most brides spend $1,500-$3,000; online dresses typically run under $900; premium designers start near $3,000; and custom or couture work reaches $10,000+. Destination brides average about $2,900 — roughly $800 more than hometown brides.

How much should I budget for alterations?

$500-$1,000 for a standard gown — hem, side seams, bustle. Zola's published band is $300-$800; simple fixes start at $50 and beaded or reconstructed gowns pass $1,000. Shops quote 10-20% of the gown price, but labor floors mean a $600 dress pays nearly the same $500 as a $2,000 one. Start fittings 6-10 weeks out.

What does wedding dress preservation cost?

$200-$400 for professional cleaning and archival boxing, with entry packages at $200-$275 and complex gowns up to $700-$1,000. Turnaround runs 8-10 weeks. It matters because invisible stains — wine, sweat, sugar — oxidize and yellow the fabric within months, so preservation protects both keepsakes and resale value.

Is a cheap online wedding dress a bad idea?

Not if you budget the tailor. Online gowns typically run under $900, but sizing follows the brand's chart, so alterations are near-certain — about $500 at the standard tier. All-in, the online path runs roughly $1,775 versus $3,175 for the boutique path. Order early, order by measurements not street size, and find a seamstress before the dress ships.

When should I buy my wedding dress?

Eight to ten months out for a standard order: 4-6 months of production and shipping, then 6-10 weeks of alterations with two or three fittings. Rush production compresses to 8-12 weeks for a fee around $100-$300, and off-the-rack can compress the whole timeline to 3-4 months. Sample sales reward the flexible — but sample gowns are sold as-is, so inspect seams and zippers.
