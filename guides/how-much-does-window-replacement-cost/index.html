How Much Does Window Replacement Cost in 2026?

💡 ~22K searches/mo💰 CPC: $25⏱️ 7 min read

New windows run $325 to $850 each installed in vinyl, and a typical 10-window project lands near $5,500. That spread is real, not sales noise: frame material alone can move the bill 70%. Here's what each choice costs, and which upgrades actually earn their price.

What does one window cost installed?

Quoted installed, a standard 3×5 ft double-hung in vinyl with low-E argon glass runs $325 low, $550 typical, $850 high across national 2025-26 pricing. That number includes the unit, the labor ($100 to $300 per window), trim work inside, and haul-away of the old sash.

Window type matters less than you'd think until you hit the oddballs. Sliders and awnings come in slightly under double-hungs; casements slightly over. Bay and bow windows are the outliers at $1,600 to $4,100 installed because they're really three or more units plus structural framing and a head flashing.

Which frame material should you pick?

Vinyl owns about two-thirds of the replacement market for a reason: it's cheap, it insulates well, and it never needs paint. It also expands with heat and comes in limited colors, which is where the alternatives earn their keep.

  • Vinyl is the baseline. Best value, forgiving maintenance, mid lifespan of 20 to 30 years.
  • Aluminum runs about 5% more. Strong and slim, favored in modern and coastal builds, but it conducts heat.
  • Fiberglass costs about 40% more and shrugs off temperature swings. It can be painted and lasts 30 to 50 years.
  • Composite (about 55% more) mixes wood fiber and polymer for a wood look with less upkeep.
  • Wood costs about 70% more and needs repainting every few years, but nothing else matches it for historic homes. Clad-wood (wood inside, aluminum or vinyl skin outside) splits the difference.

Is the glass upgrade worth it?

Double-pane clear is the budget tier at roughly 5% less than low-E argon. Double-pane low-E with argon fill is today's default and the right call for almost everyone. Triple-pane adds about 22% and earns it in cold climates, on noise-facing walls, and in rooms that never feel comfortable; elsewhere the payback is slow.

For context: replacing clear single-pane glass with double-pane low-E typically saves $100 to $300 a year on heating and cooling. Real money, but on a $5,500 project the payback stretches past 20 years. Buy new windows for comfort, quiet, and operation, and treat the energy savings as a bonus.

Insert or full-frame: what's the difference?

A retrofit insert drops a new window into the existing frame in about an hour per opening. Full-frame replacement pulls the old frame out entire, replaces the sill and jambs, and rewraps the exterior trim. Insert is standard and cheaper; full-frame (about $175 more per window) is the honest answer when the sill is soft or you're changing the opening size.

How do you save money on window replacement?

  1. Do the whole house at once. One mobilization and 5% to 15% volume discounts beat three small projects.
  2. Stick to standard sizes. Custom units add 40% to 60%; stock sizes are mass-produced.
  3. Choose inserts when frames are sound. You skip trim, siding, and paint work.
  4. Get at least three quotes. Installed prices for identical windows vary 30% or more between installers in the same town.
  5. Skip the door-to-door "today only" pitch. One-day discounts on five-figure projects are pressure tactics, not savings.

While your walls are open, it's also the cheapest moment to handle adjacent work. If the siding around the openings is due, price it alongside the windows with a siding calculator instead of paying for two mobilizations later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace all the windows in a house?

A typical 10-window whole-house project in vinyl runs about $5,500 installed, with a national range of $3,250 to $8,500. Fiberglass or wood frames, triple-pane glass, or full-frame installation push the same job to $8,000 to $12,000 or more. Most installers discount 5% to 15% for whole-house volume.

What adds the most to window replacement cost?

Frame material first: fiberglass runs about 40% more than vinyl and wood about 70% more. Then glass: triple-pane adds roughly 22% over double-pane low-E. Full-frame installation (about $175 more per window), second-story access ($75 per window), and custom or oversize units (40% to 60% more) stack on top.

Are expensive windows worth the money?

Sometimes. The energy savings from upgrading single-pane to double-pane low-E run $100 to $300 a year, so pure payback can exceed 20 years. Pay up for better glass in harsh climates, for wood where appearance demands it, and for quality hardware and installers; cheap windows installed badly fail young.
