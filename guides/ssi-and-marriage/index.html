SSI and Marriage: What Changes When You Marry

📄 7 min readUpdated October 29, 2026

SSI is one of the few benefits where getting married can cost you money. Two people on SSI lose $497 a month between them, and if your spouse isn't on SSI, SSA counts part of their income and savings as yours. Here's how the 2026 rules work, with tables showing what your check would be at different spouse incomes.

Does SSI go down when you get married?

Often, yes, and how much depends on who you marry. SSI treats a married couple as one unit. If both of you get SSI, you share a couple rate. If your spouse doesn't get SSI, SSA counts part of their income and all of their countable savings as yours. Either way, the check you got as a single person isn't guaranteed.

The timing rule is strict. SSA looks at your status on the first moment of each month. Marry on the 1st and you're still single for that month, and the change starts the next month. The same works in reverse when a marriage ends.

What is the SSI marriage penalty for two people on SSI?

Two single people on SSI can each get $994 a month in 2026, $1,988 together. Marry, and the couple rate is $1,491. That's $497 less every month, or $5,964 a year, before any state supplement. The resource limit shrinks too: two singles can hold $2,000 each, $4,000 in total, but a couple gets only $3,000 between them.

That $1,000 matters more than it looks. If you each have $1,800 in the bank, you're both fine as singles. Married, your combined $3,600 is over the couple limit, and neither of you gets a check until it's spent down. How does a spouse's income affect SSI?

If your spouse doesn't get SSI, SSA uses a process called deeming. It doesn't count every dollar they earn, but it doesn't ignore them either. The steps go like this:

  1. Start with your spouse's monthly income.
  2. Take off $497 for each child in the home who isn't on SSI (less that child's own income).
  3. If what's left is $497 or less, nothing is deemed. You keep your own SSI as if you were single.
  4. If it's more than $497, SSA adds it to your income and works out a couple benefit: the $20 general exclusion, then $65 and half of the rest of any wages, subtracted from the $1,491 couple rate.
  5. You get whichever is lower: that couple figure, or your normal single amount. Deeming can only lower your check, never raise it.

The $497 figure isn't arbitrary. It's the gap between the couple rate and the single rate ($1,491 minus $994), so it goes up whenever SSI does.

Benefits count as unearned income, and unearned income only gets the $20 exclusion. So it cuts your SSI dollar for dollar once it's past about $517 a month:

Does living together count as marriage for SSI?

It can. SSA treats you as married if you live together and lead people to believe you're married. Sharing a last name, introducing each other as husband or wife, or signing a lease as a married couple can all count. Once that happens, every rule above applies even without a license.

If you live together and don't present yourselves as married, you're two individuals. But if your partner pays your rent, SSA may count that as in-kind support and cut your SSI by up to about a third. Food no longer counts for that since September 30, 2024. Shelter still does.

What happens to SSI if you separate or divorce?

Deeming stops from the month after you separate or the marriage ends, and you go back to being an individual with the $994 rate and the $2,000 limit. "Separated" means living apart. Separate bedrooms in the same home don't count. Report the change to SSA within 10 days after the month it happens, and do the same for the marriage itself. Late reporting is one of the most common causes of SSI overpayments, and the SSI overpayment waiver guide covers what to do if one lands.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I lose my SSI if I get married?

Not always. If you marry someone else on SSI, you share the couple rate of $1,491 instead of $1,988. If your spouse isn't on SSI, SSA deems part of their income to you. Your check only drops once their income after allocations is over $497 a month, and it can stop if they earn or receive enough.

How much is SSI for a married couple in 2026?

The federal SSI rate for an eligible couple is $1,491 a month in 2026, compared with $994 for one person. Some states add a supplement. The couple resource limit is $3,000.

Does my spouse's 401(k) count against my SSI?

No. When SSA deems an ineligible spouse's resources to you, it excludes their pension funds, which means IRAs and work retirement plans such as a 401(k) or a Keogh. Their bank accounts and other countable assets do count toward the $3,000 couple limit.

Can I live with my boyfriend or girlfriend and keep SSI?

Yes, as long as you don't present yourselves as married. If you do, SSA can treat you as a married couple. Even if you don't, a partner who pays your rent or mortgage can reduce your SSI as in-kind support. Food has not counted since September 30, 2024.

When does SSA start counting my spouse's income?

Your marital status is set at the start of each month, so the change starts the month after the wedding. For the payment amount, SSA usually uses your spouse's income from two months earlier.
