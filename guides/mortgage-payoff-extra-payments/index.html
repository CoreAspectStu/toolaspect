A 30-year mortgage doesn't have to take 30 years. Adding even small extra payments can shave years off your loan and save tens of thousands in interest. Here's exactly how it works — and how to calculate the savings yourself.

Why Extra Payments Are So Powerful

In the early years of a mortgage, most of each payment goes toward interest, not principal. On a $300,000 loan at 7%, your first payment of $1,996 sends $1,750 to interest and only $246 to principal. That ratio improves slowly over time.

When you make an extra payment directly to principal, you permanently reduce the base that interest is calculated on. Every dollar of principal you eliminate early saves you interest every month for the rest of the loan term. The effect compounds — pay down $1,000 in year 2, and you save interest on that $1,000 for 28 more years.

Three Strategies for Paying Off Your Mortgage Faster

  1. Biweekly payments. Instead of 12 monthly payments, pay half your mortgage every two weeks. There are 52 weeks in a year, so you make 26 half-payments — equal to 13 full monthly payments. That one extra payment per year is pure principal.
  2. Fixed monthly extra. Add $100, $200, or $500 to your regular payment, designated for principal. Even $200/month on a $300,000 loan at 7% cuts 7+ years off the term and saves over $100,000 in interest.
  3. Annual lump sum. Use tax refunds, bonuses, or other windfalls to make one large principal payment each year. A $5,000 annual lump sum on a 30-year $300,000 mortgage saves roughly $80,000 in interest.

Worked Example: $300,000 Loan at 7% APR, 30-Year Fixed

Standard payment: $1,996/month. Total interest paid over 30 years: $418,560.

+ $100/month extra: Saves ~$62,000 in interest. Pays off in ~25 years (5 years early).

+ $200/month extra: Saves ~$104,000 in interest. Pays off in ~22.5 years (7.5 years early).

+ $500/month extra: Saves ~$178,000 in interest. Pays off in ~17 years (13 years early).

Biweekly (one extra payment/year): Saves ~$57,000 in interest. Pays off in ~26 years (4 years early).

Critical: Make Sure Extra Payments Go to Principal

This is the most common mistake homeowners make. Some lenders automatically apply extra payments toward next month's interest — which gives you zero benefit. You want the extra money applied directly to your principal balance.

Here's how to make sure:

1. Use your lender's online portal and look for a checkbox or option that says "apply to principal" or "principal only."

2. If paying by check, write "apply to principal" in the memo line and include a separate note.

3. Check your next statement. The principal reduction should appear immediately, and your next regular payment amount should stay the same.

Should I Pay Off My Mortgage or Invest Instead?

This depends on your mortgage rate and expected investment returns. The general framework:

If your mortgage rate is above 7%: Paying extra is hard to beat. Getting a guaranteed 7% return (by avoiding that interest) is better than most risk-adjusted investments.

If your mortgage rate is 3-4%: Long-term market returns average 8-10%. Investing the extra money likely comes out ahead over 20+ years, though with more risk.

If your rate is 5-6%: It's a closer call. A blended approach — some extra payments, some investing — offers a balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does one extra mortgage payment a year save?

On a $300,000 loan at 7% for 30 years, one extra payment per year saves approximately $57,000 in interest and pays off the loan about 4 years earlier.

Is it better to pay extra on mortgage or invest?

If your mortgage rate is 7% and you can earn 8-10% in the market, investing the difference may come out ahead long-term. But paying off the mortgage is a guaranteed return with zero risk.

Does paying biweekly really save money?

Yes. Biweekly payments result in 26 half-payments per year, which equals 13 full monthly payments instead of 12. This extra payment goes entirely to principal.

Should I make a lump sum payment on my mortgage?

If you have an emergency fund and no higher-interest debt, a lump sum payment on your mortgage can save significant interest. Always ensure your lender applies it to principal.

How do I tell my lender to apply extra to principal?

Most online payment portals have a 'apply to principal' checkbox. Otherwise, include a note with your payment. Verify on your next statement that the principal was reduced.
