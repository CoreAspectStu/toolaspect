How Much Does a Photo Booth Rental Cost? Booth Types, Hours, and Add-Ons

📸 10-20K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2-6⏱️ 7 min read

A photo booth rental costs $500-$1,200 for a standard 3-hour package (national average about $619). Premium formats change the math: mirror booths run $900-$1,400 and 360 booths $675-$2,500 (average $1,170). Extra hours bill at $150-$500 depending on type, and the digital sharing kiosk is the sleeper fee at $185-$285 an hour. A loaded 4-hour wedding booth lands near $1,760.

What a photo booth costs by type

Booth type is the price lever. Everything else — hours, event, extras — moves the number by hundreds, not thousands.

The open-air booth (a camera on a stand, a ring light, and a backdrop) is what most weddings book. Mirror booths — a full-length mirror that's secretly a touchscreen — carry the gala premium. The 360 booth is a platform your guests stand on while a camera orbits them; it produces the slow-motion clips that dominate social feeds, which is why prom committees pay for it.

Weddings, proms, and corporate events price differently

Weddings run $600-$1,200 for standard formats, $900-$1,400 for premium ones. Corporate parties price like weddings at $600-$1,300 — until you ask for brand-activation gear: custom booth wraps, lead capture, and instant GIF delivery push the bill to $1,200-$3,200+ in major markets. Proms skew toward mirror and 360 formats, and schools facing 400 students often book two booths instead of one long shift.

Private birthdays are the value lane: 2-3 hour open-air packages at $400-$800, often digital-only.

The hours question nobody asks until the quote arrives

Operators quote a 3-hour base and bill beyond it hourly. The trap is the fifth hour: booth usage peaks in the two hours after dinner (weddings) or after doors open (proms), then tapers hard. Extending a 3-hour open-air package to 4 adds about $200; paying for 5 hours at a corporate gala that empties by 9 p.m. is the classic wasted line item. If you want the booth open during cocktail hour but running only after dinner, ask about idle-hour rates before signing — they're cheaper than runtime, but rarely free.

The add-ons that stack

  • Digital sharing station, $185-$285/hr — guests text or post their clips; the most common upsell and the easiest to skip if your crowd prints
  • Custom backdrop, $50-$250 — commonly quoted for branded or color-matched setups; basic backdrops are included
  • Travel, $25-$150 — beyond the operator's home radius, priced by distance
  • Props and prints — almost always included; unlimited strips are standard in competitive markets

A worked wedding quote: open-air booth, 4 hours, sharing kiosk. Base $620, one extra hour $200, kiosk 4 × $235 = $940. Total $1,760, range $1,290-$2,440. The same booth without the kiosk rents for $820 — and the prints still come home with your guests.

Three ways to pay less without regretting it

Book 3 hours, not 5, timed to your peak window. Book the open-air model unless the mirror or 360 format is the point of the rental. And ask the operator what's actually included before comparing quotes — two quotes $300 apart usually differ by a kiosk, a backdrop, or idle-time terms, not by the booth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a photo booth for 3 hours?

Most standard 3-hour rentals run $500-$1,200, and the national average sits near $619. Open-air booths start at $400-$1,000, enclosed and inflatable booths at $500-$1,200, mirror booths at $600-$1,400, and 360 booths at $675-$2,500 (average $1,170). Packages almost always include an attendant, props, a basic backdrop, and prints.

Why do 360 photo booths cost more?

A 360 booth averages $1,170 for 3 hours, about $390 an hour, versus $620 for an open-air classic. You're paying for the platform, the operator who runs it, and the software that stitches slow-motion clips and pushes them to guests' phones. Hourly add-ons run $200-$500, the highest of any booth type.

How many hours of photo booth do I need?

Three to four hours covers weddings and proms; usage peaks in the two hours after dinner or after doors open, then falls off. Extra hours cost $150-$300 for standard booths and $200-$500 for 360s, so a fifth hour is often money spent on an empty booth. A 4-hour open-air wedding rental with a digital sharing kiosk totals $1,760 typical, range $1,290-$2,440.

What add-ons do photo booth companies charge for?

The big one is the digital sharing station: $185-$285 an hour to let guests text or post their clips, per Thumbtack's published band. Custom branded backdrops commonly quote $50-$250. Travel outside the operator's home radius runs $25-$150. Props, a basic backdrop, and prints are usually baked into the package.

Is a photo booth worth it at a wedding?

At $600-$1,200 for the formats most weddings book, a booth costs about what a tier of bar service or a floral installation does, and it produces the most-shared photos of the night. The honest math: if your crowd dances, the booth gets heavy use in hours two and three. If your crowd leaves at 9 p.m., book the 2-hour package and save.
