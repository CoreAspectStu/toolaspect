What are the parts of a VIN?

Since 1981, every vehicle sold in the US carries a 17-character VIN in three chunks. Positions 1-3 form the World Manufacturer Identifier: who built it and where the company is based. Positions 4-8 are the Vehicle Descriptor Section, where each maker encodes model, body, engine, and restraint details however it likes. Position 9 is the check digit. Position 10 encodes the model year, position 11 the assembly plant, and positions 12-17 are the production serial that makes the whole string unique.

The first character alone gives you the country: 1, 4, and 5 are the United States, 2 Canada, 3 Mexico, J Japan, K Korea, L China, W Germany, V France or Spain, S the UK, Z Italy, and Y the Nordic countries. One caveat worth knowing: the WMI encodes where the manufacturer is headquartered, not always where your specific car was bolted together. Plenty of "J" Toyotas and Hondas sold in the US were assembled in Kentucky or Ohio; position 11 plus the live decode tells you the actual plant.

How does the check digit work?

Position 9 is a math test the rest of the VIN has to pass. Convert every character to a number, with letters worth 1 through 9 (A=1, B=2, ... H=8, J=1, K=2, skipping I, O, and Q, which never appear in a VIN at all). Multiply each by its position's weight: 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 10 for the first eight positions, 0 for position 9 itself, then 9 down through 2 for the last eight. Add everything up and take the remainder when divided by 11. That remainder is the check digit, and a remainder of 10 is written as X.

Run 1HGCM82633A004352 through that arithmetic and out pops 3, exactly what's stamped in position 9. The payoff: a single mistyped character almost always breaks the match. How do I read the model year letter?

Position 10 cycles through a fixed alphabet that skips I, O, Q, U, and Z along with the digits in between. A is 1980 or 2010, B is 1981 or 2011, and so on through Y, which covers 2000 and 2030; the digits 1 through 9 fill 2001 through 2009. The code repeats every 30 years, so H means 1987 or 2017, and you pick the right one by looking at the vehicle. A 2026 model carries T, a 2025 carries S, and an R is the 1994/2024 pair. For anything built in the last three decades, context settles the ambiguity in a heartbeat.

What can a VIN tell me when I'm buying used?

  • Match the physical car to the paperwork. Dashboard VIN, door jamb, title, and insurance record should all agree. Mismatches are disqualifying.
  • Verify the model year. Sellers rounding a model year up is a classic listing inflation; position 10 doesn't round.
  • Catch clones and reassignments. A valid check digit with mismatched attributes (a "V6 truck" VIN on a 4-cylinder sedan) means the VIN or the car isn't what's claimed.
  • Check open recalls. NHTSA's recall database is public and keyed by make, model, and year; the decoder on our site queries it directly.
  • Price the running costs. Once you know the exact model, quotes for car insurance, an auto loan, and even an extended warranty all sharpen from "a 2010-ish sedan" to a specific VIN-level vehicle.

What can't a VIN tell me?

Mileage, accident history, number of owners, and current title brand. Those live in separate databases (odometer records, insurance claims, state title systems) that commercial vehicle-history reports stitch together from the VIN as a key. The VIN itself is a birth certificate, not a biography. It also won't give you trim colors and option packages beyond what the maker chose to encode; a build sheet or window sticker does that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I look up who owns a car by its VIN?

No, and any site claiming to hand out current owner names from a VIN is either lying or trafficking in illegally obtained data. The Driver's Privacy Protection Act bars DMVs from releasing personal records tied to a VIN except for permitted uses like litigation, insurance, or employer checks. What a VIN legitimately unlocks: specifications, equipment, open recalls, and, through paid vehicle-history products, accident and title records.

Do two identical cars have similar VINs?

They'll share the first chunk: same WMI, same body and engine codes, maybe the same plant. The serial number in positions 12-17 is what makes every VIN unique, and it's assigned in production order. That's also why VINs of sequentially built cars differ only in the last few digits, something worth knowing when you're copying one off paperwork by hand.

What does it mean if a VIN check digit fails?

Almost always a transcription error: one character typed or transcribed wrong. Re-read the VIN from the dashboard and door jamb and compare against the title. If the physical VIN on the car itself fails the check digit math, that's a serious red flag for reassignment or tampering, and reason to walk away from the purchase and check with the DMV or police.
