A full-resolution download costs one credit and goes up to 50 megapixels, according to remove.bg's API documentation. Credits come in subscriptions or pay-as-you-go packs. Third-party pricing trackers list subscriptions starting at around $9 a month for 40 credits in 2026, which puts each full-size image at roughly 20 cents or more. Developers also get 50 free API calls a month at preview size.

Why look for a remove.bg alternative?

The size cap. A 612-pixel cutout looks fine in a chat window. Put it on a marketplace listing that zooms, a slide on a projector or anything printed, and it falls apart.

The cost adds up. If you sell online and shoot 50 products a month, that's 50 credits every month for a job a free tool can often handle.

Uploads. remove.bg processes images on its servers. That's how it gets its quality, but it means every photo, including photos of people, leaves your device.

How good are free background removers, really?

Better than they were, with a clear weak spot. Our tool uses MODNet, a portrait matting model, through Transformers.js. The first run downloads the model once, either the 25 MB full-precision version for the best edges or a 6.3 MB quantized one if you're on a slow connection. After that, all the processing happens on your device.

It's strongest on what it was trained for: people. Product shots, pets and objects with clear edges usually come out cleanly too. Where it falls behind remove.bg is the hard stuff: flyaway hair, fur against a busy background, glass and sheer fabric. Expect a softer edge there. Semi-transparent pixels around hair stay semi-transparent, which helps the cutout sit naturally on a new background.

A sensible routine is to try the free tool first. If the edges are good enough for where the image is going, you're done. If you need a perfect cutout of curly hair for a magazine cover, that's when one remove.bg credit earns its keep.

How do you get the cleanest cutout from any tool?

  1. Shoot against a plain, contrasting background. A dark jacket against a dark wall is hard for every model, paid or free.
  2. Light the subject evenly. Hard shadows on the background often get mistaken for part of the subject.
  3. Crop before removing. Less background means fewer things to misjudge. The Image Cropper does this in your browser.
  4. Pick the right output. Transparent PNG for design work, solid white for marketplace listings that require it.
  5. Upscale only at the end. If the source is small, enlarge the finished cutout with the Image Upscaler, not the original photo.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is remove.bg free?

Partly. remove.bg is free for low-resolution images up to 0.25 megapixels, which works out to about 612 by 408 pixels for a typical 3:2 photo. A full-resolution download costs one credit per image, and credits come from a subscription or pay-as-you-go packs.

How do I get a full-resolution background removal for free?

Use a tool that doesn't cap the download. Browser-based removers that run on your device, Apple's Lift Subject on iPhone and Mac, and the Remove Background button in Windows 11 Paint all keep the photo's original size at no cost.

Does remove.bg keep my photos?

remove.bg processes images on its servers, so your photo is uploaded. Its privacy policy covers how long files are kept. If you don't want to upload at all, use an in-browser tool, the built-in options on iPhone, Mac or Windows, or the open-source rembg on your own computer.

Which background remover is best for hair?

Paid server-side services, remove.bg included, are generally strongest on flyaway hair, fur and semi-transparent edges because they run large models. Free in-browser models get people and products right but leave softer edges around fine hair. For a headshot that matters, try the free option first and pay only if the edges bother you.

Who owns remove.bg?

remove.bg was built by Kaleido, an Austrian company. Canva acquired Kaleido in 2021, and remove.bg still runs as its own service with its own pricing.
