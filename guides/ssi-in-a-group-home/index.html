Can You Get SSI in a Group Home or Assisted Living?

📄 6 min readUpdated November 1, 2026

Usually, yes. If you live in a private group home and pay the room and board yourself, your SSI isn't reduced. If a relative pays, SSA presumes $351.33 of support and the 2026 check drops to $662.67. Here's how each case works, with the 2026 numbers.

Can you get SSI while living in a group home?

Usually yes, and often the full federal rate of $994 a month. Three questions decide it: who runs the home, whether it's a medical facility paid for by Medicaid, and who pays your room and board.

First, a definition that matters. Social Security calls a place an "institution" when it provides treatment or services on top of food and shelter to four or more people who aren't related to the owner. A home with three unrelated residents isn't an institution at all. SSA treats it like any shared household, and the rules in our guide to SSI and living with family apply instead.

What happens if the home is run by the government?

If you live in a public institution, meaning one operated or controlled by a federal, state, county or city government, for a full calendar month, you're not eligible for SSI that month. A full month means you were there on the first and stayed through the last day.

The $30 rate is the federal "personal needs" payment. It only reaches facilities licensed for inpatient medical care. A waiver-funded group home or an assisted living building usually isn't one, so the full rate stays in play and the room-and-board test below decides the amount.

Does paying room and board protect the full check?

In a private, for-profit home, yes. SSA treats the amount the home accepts as payment in full as the market value of your food and shelter. If you pay it, or you're legally on the hook for it, you're not receiving in-kind support and your check isn't reduced.

The reduction appears when someone else pays. A parent, a sibling or a family trust covering the monthly fee counts as in-kind support, and SSA applies the presumed value rule. Here's how the common setups shake out at the 2026 rate, assuming no other income:

How much does the presumed value rule take?

SSA doesn't try to price the room. It presumes the support is worth one-third of the federal rate plus $20, which is $351.33 in 2026. That amount is counted as unearned income, and the usual $20 general exclusion comes off, so the net cut is $331.33 when you have no other income. If you already have SSDI or a pension, the $20 is used up on that, and the full $351.33 lands on top:

What if a nonprofit home provides everything?

Nonprofit retirement homes and similar institutions have their own rule. The presumed value applies if the home has an express obligation to provide your full food and shelter, meaning a legally enforceable contract or set of membership rules that promises both and asks for no current or future payment. A lump-sum prepayment for lifetime care doesn't count as a current payment, so a life-care contract triggers the rule.

The rule does not apply when the home has no such obligation and receives no payment for your food and shelter, or is paid by another nonprofit.

Are foster care and adult family care different?

Yes. If a public or private agency places you in a licensed private home under a program such as adult foster care, and you pay the rate the agency sets, SSA treats that rate as the market value and finds no in-kind support. It only comes in if you pay less and someone other than an agency covers the difference.

What about state supplements and Medicaid waivers?

Many states add money on top of federal SSI specifically for people in licensed residential care, and the amount depends on the type of facility and the state. Ask the home and your state's Medicaid agency what applies.

Medicaid home and community-based waiver services paid to the home for your care are not income to you. What matters to SSA is the room-and-board portion and who pays it.

Report the move to SSA within 10 days of the end of the month it happens, and bring the admission agreement showing who pays what. And keep resources under $2,000. Our SSI asset limit guide lists what's excluded. The net worth calculator keeps the tally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get SSI in a group home?

Yes in most cases. A private group home where you pay the room and board doesn't reduce SSI at all. If someone else pays your fee, SSA presumes in-kind support worth $351.33 in 2026 and the check drops to about $662.67 with no other income.

Does SSI pay for assisted living?

SSI is a cash benefit paid to you, not to the facility, and the federal rate is $994 a month in 2026. That rarely covers an assisted living fee on its own. Many states add a supplement for licensed residential care, and Medicaid waiver programs can pay for care services, though not room and board.

How much SSI do you get in a nursing home?

If Medicaid pays more than half the cost of your care for a full calendar month, federal SSI drops to $30 a month, or $60 for a couple both in facilities. A stay expected to last 90 days or less can keep the full rate for up to 3 full months if a doctor certifies it and you show you're paying for a home to return to.

What is the presumed value for SSI in 2026?

One-third of the federal benefit rate plus $20, which is $351.33 for an individual and $517 for a couple. You can rebut it by showing the actual value of the room and board someone else pays is lower.

Does a group home count as a public institution?

Only if a federal, state, county or city government operates or controls it. A publicly operated community residence designed for and actually serving 16 or fewer residents is excluded, so SSI continues there under normal rules. Private homes, nonprofit or for-profit, are never public institutions.
