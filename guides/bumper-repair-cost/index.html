Bumper Repair vs Replacement: What It Costs

🚗 8 min read🧮 National ranges + decision rules

Parking lots eat bumpers. A shopping cart, a pole you misjudged, the neighbor's kid's bike — the plastic cover takes the hit, and the damage always looks worse than the bill (or exactly as bad). This guide sorts bumper work into the four cost tiers shops actually quote, explains when repair stops making sense, and walks the deductible math that decides whether insurance helps or just complicates your renewal.

Every bumper quote starts from what the plastic is doing, not how bad it looks from ten feet.

Most bumper repairs nationally land between $150 and $1,200; most replacements between $500 and $1,500 before you add luxury parts or sensor recalibration. Where your quote sits inside those bands depends on the three factors below.

Repair or replace: the decision

Repair wins whenever the cover is one continuous piece of plastic — scratched, scuffed, creased, it doesn't matter. Shops restore the surface, refinish, and the part keeps doing its flexible, energy-absorbing job. The price gap is real: at typical labor rates, repairing a deep scratch on a sedan runs about $390, while replacing the same cover runs about $1,045 — aftermarket parts, paint, and 5.5 hours of labor.

Replacement becomes the honest answer when the damage goes through the plastic: cracks that open when you press the cover, torn corners, warped sections that no longer hold shape, or broken mounting tabs (the clip points that hold the cover to the car's impact structure). Plastic welding a cracked cover is possible and sometimes appropriate for a beater, but it's a repair on a part that flexes with every driveway approach, and shops that warranty their work mostly won't offer it on late-model cars.

One more layer: the cover is cosmetic. Behind it sit an energy absorber and a metal reinforcement bar. Any impact hard enough to crack the cover — think 10 mph into a post — deserves a check that the structure behind it survived. Cosmetic estimates assume the bones are good.

What moves the price: parts, paint, sensors

  • Aftermarket vs OEM parts. An aftermarket cover for a mainstream sedan runs $200-$450; the OEM equivalent $350-$800. Aftermarket fit on common models is now genuinely good; insurers default to it, and paying OEM money matters mainly for luxury badges, lease returns, and long keeps.
  • Paint finish. Solid colors are cheap to match. Metallic and pearl add roughly $75 to the job; tri-coat whites and reds — the ones with a candy layer — add $175 or more, because matching requires spraying test panels.
  • Sensors and cameras. Parking sensors, radar units behind the cover, and cameras add removal/reinstall labor, and most need recalibration afterward: $150-$550. This is the single biggest sleeper cost on modern cars — a $500 plastic cover becomes a $1,500 job on a sensor-packed EV.
  • Labor rate. Body labor runs $50-$120/hr nationally; big metros push past $150. A 3-5 hour job moves hundreds of dollars with the local rate.

Should you file an insurance claim?

Run the numbers in this order. If the estimate is below your deductible, a claim pays nothing — done, pay cash. If it's above, a collision or comprehensive claim pays estimate minus deductible, but the claim itself can follow you: expect a premium increase at renewal that's largest in year one and fades over about three years. A useful rule of thumb: for bills within a few hundred dollars of your deductible, cash usually nets out cheaper unless you have accident forgiveness in writing.

Can you fix a bumper yourself?

For tier one, usually yes. A $25-$50 kit — polish, compound, and a touch-up pen in your paint code — removes paint transfer and softens light scratches in an afternoon. YouTube makes it look easier than it is; the failure mode is a glossy patch that doesn't match the surrounding factory orange peel. For anything deeper, or any job where plastic is actually cut, DIY economics fall apart: a spray gun, proper filler, and color-matched paint run past $300 in materials before you've sanded anything, and blends across body lines are a trained skill. Buy the kit for scuffs; hire the rest.

A realistic walkthrough

Say you back a 2019 Camry into a pole: a deep scratch across the rear cover, paint cracked at the edges, no dents. Shop quotes come in at $350-$480 — consistent with the $390 model estimate at $90/hr labor, metallic paint. Your deductible is $500, so it's a cash job either way. If the impact had torn the corner instead, the same car jumps to the $900-$1,300 range for an aftermarket cover, paint, and labor — and now a claim against a $500 deductible recovers $400-$800, which starts to be worth the renewal conversation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to fix a scratched bumper?

A light scuff or paint transfer that polishes out runs $50 to $300. A scratch deep enough to expose primer needs sanding, filler, and a paint blend, which runs $300 to $900 at most shops. The jump happens because spot blending metallic paint across body lines takes real hours of refinish labor.

When does a bumper need replacement instead of repair?

Replace when the plastic is cracked through, torn, warped, or when the mounting tabs that clip the cover to the car are broken. Repairing cracked urethane is possible with plastic welding, but it's structural compromise on a part that flexes every drive — most reputable shops quote replacement once the damage goes through the material.

How much does ADAS recalibration add to bumper work?

$150 to $550 at most shops. Parking sensors, radar modules behind the cover, and rear cameras need to be removed, reinstalled, and then recalibrated so lane assists and cross-traffic alerts still aim correctly. On some vehicles recalibration is a static procedure with target boards; on others it's a drive cycle.

Will insurance cover bumper damage?

Collision coverage covers bumper damage from crashes (minus your deductible); comprehensive covers non-collision events like a falling branch. If the repair costs less than your deductible, a claim pays nothing. Above the deductible, weigh the payout against a possible 20-40% premium increase at renewal — small claims often cost more than they pay over three years.

Can I drive with a cracked bumper?

Usually, yes — a cracked cosmetic cover isn't a mechanical failure, and you won't fail inspection for it in most states. Two exceptions: a cover dragging or loose enough to detach is a road hazard police will ticket, and damage that misaligns parking sensors or blocks a camera disables safety systems some states expect to function.
