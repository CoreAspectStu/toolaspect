AI or Not Alternatives: AI Image Detectors, Compared

📄 7 min readUpdated October 10, 2026

AI or Not is one of the better-known AI image detectors, and its free plan is fair: 20 image checks a month. Run out, or want to check something without uploading it, and you need alternatives. The useful thing to know is that "AI detectors" do three quite different jobs. Some guess from the pixels. Some read a signed label inside the file. Some look for an invisible watermark. Each catches different fakes and misses others. This guide compares seven options, shows which one fits which situation, and is upfront about where our own AI Image Detector fits. It reads metadata on your device and makes no pixel-level guess.

What can each kind of check actually prove?

This is where most people get misled. A pixel classifier gives you a probability, not a fact. A label or watermark check gives you a fact, but only when the label is there. Here's how they play out on common cases.

The pattern: proof-based checks are excellent when they hit and silent when they miss. Classifiers always give an answer, but it's an estimate.

How accurate are AI image detectors?

Less accurate than their marketing suggests, especially on images found in the wild. A 2025 study by Li and colleagues, "Is Artificial Intelligence Generated Image Detection a Solved Problem?", found detectors suffer significant performance drops on real-world data. Another paper, "Fake or JPEG?", showed some detectors had learned to spot JPEG compression and image size patterns from their training data rather than signs of AI. That means a resized or re-saved image can fool them in either direction.

In practice, a high "AI" score on a heavily compressed image from a group chat is weak evidence. Run two different classifiers. If they disagree, you don't know yet.

Which check should you run first?

Start with what you have. If you've got the original file, check its EXIF data or run our detector: a full set of camera details is a good sign, though not proof, since metadata can be edited. Then try Content Credentials Verify and SynthID, because a positive from either is close to definitive. If the image came from social media, skip straight to a reverse image search to find the earliest copy and its source. Use a pixel classifier last, and treat it as a tiebreaker. Frequently Asked Questions

Is AI or Not free?

There's a free plan with 20 AI image checks a month plus text detection, as listed on aiornot.com/pricing in October 2026. Pro costs $5 a month for 500 image checks and adds video, voice, music and deepfake detection. Business is $100 a month.

Which AI image detector is the most accurate?

No independent test has crowned one, and accuracy drops for every detector on real-world images that have been compressed, resized or screenshotted. A 2025 study found detectors suffer significant performance drops outside their test sets. Use a detector as one signal and look for proof, like Content Credentials or a SynthID result, where you can.

Can SynthID detect images from Midjourney?

No. SynthID only finds the invisible watermark added by Google and the partners using its technology, which Google listed as OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao in October 2026. Images from other generators won't carry it, so a negative result tells you nothing about them.

Does a missing Content Credentials label mean an image is real?

No. Social media sites and messaging apps commonly strip metadata, including Content Credentials, when images are uploaded. A real photo and an AI image can both come back with no credentials. Only a credential that is present tells you something.

Does the ToolAspect AI Image Detector upload my image?

No. It reads the file's metadata in your browser and nothing is sent to a server. That also means it checks camera data, software tags and timestamps, not the pixels themselves, so treat its result as one clue rather than a verdict.
