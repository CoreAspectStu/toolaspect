Debt-to-Income Ratio Explained

💡 90K searches/mo💰 CPC: $8⏱️ 7 min read

Your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio is one of the most important numbers lenders look at when you apply for a mortgage, car loan, or credit card. It measures how much of your monthly income goes toward debt payments. Here is how to calculate yours and what it means.

What Is DTI?

DTI is the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward debt payments. It includes rent/mortgage, car loans, student loans, minimum credit card payments, child support, and any other recurring debt.

Ad

Lenders use DTI to assess your ability to take on additional debt. A lower DTI means you have more room in your budget for a new payment.

How to Calculate Your DTI

Add up all monthly debt payments, then divide by your gross (pre-tax) monthly income:

  • Mortgage or rent payment
  • Car loan payment(s)
  • Student loan payment(s)
  • Minimum credit card payments
  • Personal loan payments
  • Child support or alimony
  • Any other recurring debt obligations

DTI Thresholds

Here is how lenders interpret your DTI ratio:

  • Under 20%: Excellent — very strong financial position
  • 20-35%: Good — manageable debt, likely to qualify for new credit
  • 36-43%: Caution — the maximum most mortgage lenders allow
  • 44-50%: High — may struggle to qualify for new loans
  • Above 50%: Critical — half your income goes to debt, high risk

Front-End vs Back-End DTI

Mortgage lenders calculate two types of DTI. Front-end DTI (also called the housing ratio) includes only your housing payment. Back-end DTI includes all debts. Most lenders want front-end under 28% and back-end under 36%.

How to Lower Your DTI

If your DTI is too high for a mortgage, here are strategies to lower it:

  • Pay down credit card balances to reduce minimum payments
  • Refinance high-rate loans to lower monthly payments
  • Increase your income (side hustle, raise, new job)
  • Avoid taking on new debt before applying for a mortgage
  • Consider a co-signer with a lower DTI
  • Extend your loan term to reduce monthly payments (costs more in interest)

Frequently Asked Questions

What DTI do I need for a mortgage?

Conventional mortgages typically require a back-end DTI of 43% or less. FHA loans allow up to 50% in some cases. VA loans look for 41% or less. Lower DTI gives you better rate options.

Does rent count toward DTI?

If you are currently renting, rent payments are included in your DTI. If you are buying a home, the projected mortgage payment replaces rent in the calculation.

Is DTI more important than credit score?

Both matter equally. A high credit score with a high DTI can still result in a loan denial. Lenders want to see both a history of responsible borrowing (credit score) and the ability to repay (DTI).
