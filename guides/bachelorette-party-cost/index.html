How Much Does a Bachelorette Party Cost, Really?

💡 Trending upward💰 CPC: $1.5-4⏱️ 8 min read

The bachelorette party has quietly become a second wedding. The Knot's Bach Study puts the average attendee spend at $1,300 per person for a one-to-two-day party and $1,650 once it stretches to three or four days, up about $600 since 2019. Nobody sets out to spend that; it happens one line item at a time, and the person doing the planning usually fronts the money and absorbs the awkwardness of collecting it. Here's the honest structure of the cost, three complete budgets, and the split mechanics that keep friendships intact.

Where the $1,300 goes

A bachelorette budget has five lines, and only one of them scales with the whole group: lodging. The rest are per-person costs that quietly stack: travel to get there, food and drinks once you've arrived, activities, and the extras pile (decorations, matching outfits, bride gear, favors).

Three budgets, same eight friends

  • Local one-night — $2,715 total, $339.17 each. A $600 house or suite split one night, $30 of rides and gas, $120 of food and drinks, a $60 activity, $25 of decor and favors. Everyone sleeps in their own bed Sunday morning.
  • Weekend getaway — $5,265 total, $658.13 each. The table above: a $1,800 two-night rental and $385 a head of spending. This is the modal modern bachelorette.
  • Destination blowout — $11,295 total, $1,411.88 each. A $3,600 three-night house, $350 flights, $260 of food, $200 of booked activities, $45 of extras. Right at The Knot's destination average.

All three assume the crew covers the bride. Drop that courtesy and each person's number falls by about $73 on the weekend trip ($585 each instead of $658).

The bride question, settled politely

In most groups the attendees absorb the bride's share, which is pure arithmetic: divide by 8 instead of 9. The fairness debate starts with the beds. A three-bedroom rental has one master suite, two decent doubles, and sometimes a bunk, and splitting the house evenly means the pair in the master pays the same as the pair on the bunk. The fix is charging by room: price the master at a premium (a 1.5 weight against 1.0 for standard rooms), and the split self-corrects. Same logic for the friends who arrive Thursday and the ones who leave Sunday.

If someone genuinely can't afford the trip, the earlier that's said the better. The kind cut is shrinking the party, not the person: a local night at $374 a head includes everyone, while quietly subsidizing a $1,412 weekend breeds resentment that outlasts the wedding video.

Collect the money before you spend it

The maid of honor usually books the house and the activities, which means floating thousands on a credit card. Protect the friendship and the credit score: collect a non-refundable deposit when dates lock (it filters the maybes), the balance two weeks before travel, and let bar tabs and shopping stay personal. Book refundable rates wherever possible, because one pregnancy announcement or job change can shrink the crew by two. And get the money by payment app the day it's requested; the person still owing $658 the week after the wedding is a trope because it keeps happening.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost of a bachelorette party?

About $1,300 per person for a one-to-two-day party and $1,650 per person for three-to-four days, according to The Knot's Bach Study, up roughly $600 since 2019. Those are averages across everything from a local bar night to a flight-and-hotel weekend, so treat them as a midpoint rather than a requirement.

Who pays for the bride at a bachelorette party?

Usually the attendees. The common arrangement splits the full trip cost, bride included, across everyone else: on an 8-person, $5,265 weekend that's $658 per attendee versus $585 each if the bride paid her own way. Some groups cover only her dinners and activities, or the maid of honor gifts her share.

How do you split a bachelorette house rental fairly?

Price by bed quality, not per head. The master suite with an ensuite rents for more than the twin bunk, so charge the couple or pair in the nice room accordingly. A simple method: total the house cost, assign each room a weight (nice room 1.5, standard 1.0, bunk 0.7), and divide by the sum of weights.

When should you collect bachelorette money?

Before anything is booked. Collect a deposit per person when the dates lock, the balance two weeks before travel, and personal spending (bar tabs, shopping) stays on each person's own card. Money collected during the trip is money you will chase for a month afterward.
