Why look for an iLovePDF alternative at all?

iLovePDF is good. It's fast, the tools are simple, and unlike some rivals it doesn't stop you after a couple of tasks a day. So people usually leave for one of three narrower reasons.

The first is the per-task caps. The free Basic plan limits how many files you can drop into one job and how large they can be. Its pricing page lists Merge at 25 files on the free plan versus 500 on Premium, with free file-size limits that range from 15 MB to 200 MB depending on the tool. Premium goes up to 4 GB. If you're combining a year of scanned receipts, 25 files runs out fast.

The second is features behind the paywall. OCR, the desktop and mobile apps, team workflows and the AI credits are Premium only. When we checked in October 2026, the European pricing page showed Premium at €9 a month, or €60 a year billed annually. Prices in other regions differ, so check the page in your own currency.

The third is privacy. iLovePDF processes files on its servers, which it says are covered by European data protection law, and it deletes uploads within two hours. That's a reasonable policy. But some documents, like a passport scan or a medical letter, are ones people would rather never upload at all.

How do I merge more than 25 PDFs for free?

Use a merger that doesn't count files. Our Merge PDF reads every file in your browser and copies the pages into one new document. It doesn't re-render them, so text stays selectable and the quality doesn't drop. There's no file limit because there's no server to protect. The ceiling is your device's memory, and a modern laptop handles a few hundred ordinary pages without fuss.

If you're stuck on a phone with a huge batch, do it in two passes. Merge the first half, merge the second half, then merge the two results. It's clunky, but it works on anything.

On a desktop, Preview and PDF24 Creator both merge offline with no cap. In Preview, open the first file, show thumbnails, and drag the other files into the sidebar.

Is there an iLovePDF alternative that doesn't upload files?

Yes. Every PDF tool on ToolAspect runs inside the browser tab. The page loads its code once, JavaScript reads your file from your own disk, and the result downloads straight from memory. You can check it yourself: open the browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab and run a merge. You won't see your PDF going anywhere.

There are honest limits. Very large scans lean on memory, so a 600-page document on an old phone may stall. And converting a PDF back into an editable Word file is hard to do well locally, so we don't pretend to. Word itself can open a PDF and convert it, and that's usually the better route.

Is PDF24 better than iLovePDF?

For free use, it's at least as generous and often more so. PDF24 doesn't state a task or file-count limit on its online tools, doesn't need an account, and says it deletes files from its EU servers after an hour. PDF24 Creator gives Windows users the whole kit offline, and it's free for business use too.

iLovePDF wins on polish and on mobile. Its interface is cleaner and its phone apps are better known. PDF24's site is busier, and the desktop app is Windows-only, so Mac and Chromebook users are limited to the website.

What about editing the text that's already in a PDF?

This is where most free tools stop. A PDF stores text as positioned fragments, not flowing paragraphs, so changing a word without breaking the layout is genuinely hard.

Sejda handles it best among free options, within three tasks an hour. LibreOffice Draw opens PDFs for editing and copes with simple single-column pages. If you just need to add something, like a date or an answer on a form with no fillable fields, PDF Add Text is quicker.

When is iLovePDF Premium worth it?

If PDFs are part of your daily job, you need OCR at volume, or you want the desktop app with batch workflows, Premium is fairly priced. Teams that need shared workflows get real value from it too.

For everyone else, a free mix does the job: browser tools for merging, splitting, compressing, OCR and redaction, Preview or PDF24 Creator on the desktop, and Sejda when you have to change existing words. Frequently Asked Questions

Is iLovePDF free?

The basic web tools are free, but each one caps how many files you can add to a task and how large they can be. iLovePDF's own pricing page lists a 25-file limit for Merge on the free plan against 500 on Premium, and free file-size caps that vary by tool. The desktop and mobile apps, OCR and AI features need Premium.

How long does iLovePDF keep my files?

iLovePDF says uploaded files are deleted automatically within two hours, and you can delete them yourself straight after processing. They're still uploaded to its servers first, so for sensitive documents a tool that works in your browser avoids that step completely.

What's the best free alternative to iLovePDF for merging lots of files?

A browser-based merger like ToolAspect's Merge PDF has no file-count cap, because the work happens on your device. The practical limit is your device's memory. PDF24 Creator on Windows and Preview on a Mac also merge large batches offline.

Can I run OCR on a scanned PDF without paying?

Yes. ToolAspect's OCR PDF runs Tesseract in your browser and gives you the recognized text to copy or download, and PDF24 offers a free online OCR tool as well. Accuracy depends mostly on scan quality, so a clean 300 DPI scan works far better than a phone photo taken at an angle.

Is iLovePDF Premium worth paying for?

If you work with PDFs daily, need its desktop app, or regularly hit the batch and size caps, it's one of the cheaper subscriptions around. If you only merge or compress a few files a month, free tools cover that without an account.
