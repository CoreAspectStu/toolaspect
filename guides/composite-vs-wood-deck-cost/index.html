Composite vs Wood Deck Cost: The 20-Year Math

💡 ~12K searches/mo💰 CPC: $15⏱️ 8 min read

Pressure-treated decking costs $18–$42 per sq ft installed; composite runs $25–$55. But wood needs cleaning and sealing every 2–3 years at $250–$600 a pop of annualized upkeep. Run the clock to 20 years and the totals flip: about $17,500 for wood versus $13,400 for composite on the same 300 sq ft deck.

What does maintenance really cost?

Wood decks aren't install-and-forget. To keep the warranty and the color, pressure-treated pine needs washing and sealing or staining every 2 to 3 years: $250 to $600 a year annualized if you hire it, cheaper but sweatier if you roll it yourself on a weekend. Skip it and the boards gray, check, and splinter, and the 15-to-20-year lifespan shrinks toward 10.

Composite asks for soap, water, and a soft brush: $50 to $150 a year in practice. It won't rot, splinter, or need resealing, and most brands carry 25-to-30-year fade and stain warranties. The trade is heat (darker composites get hot in direct sun) and price.

The crossover lands around year 8 to 10, when cumulative wood upkeep finally passes the composite premium. After that, every year widens the gap, and there's a decent chance you'd be re-sealing a 20-year-old wood deck right around the time the composite one still has warranty left.

When is wood the better call?

  • You're selling within a few years. Wood's lower upfront plus stronger resale recoup (~75–85% vs ~50–65% in Remodeling's Cost vs. Value Report) wins on short horizons.
  • The budget is rigid. A 300 sq ft wood deck at $9,000 fits where $11,400 doesn't; no financing beat maintenance math.
  • You like the look and the work. Real grain takes stain beautifully, and some owners genuinely enjoy the upkeep ritual.
  • Full sun exposure is extreme. Dark composite boards can run too hot for bare feet in the Deep South; lighter boards and shade mitigate it.

When is composite worth the premium?

You'll stay 10 years or more, you'd otherwise pay someone to seal the deck, the surface gets barefoot traffic (kids, pool), or you simply never want to think about it again. Also worth it for second-story decks, where tearing out a failed surface later means working over a finished area below.

Middle path worth knowing: cedar splits the difference at $25–$55/sq ft with natural rot resistance, though it still wants periodic oil. And if your frame is sound, re-decking an existing structure with composite boards runs 40% to 60% of a full rebuild, since the substructure (about a third of deck cost) stays. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a composite deck pay for itself?

Over a long horizon, yes. On a 300 sq ft deck, composite costs about $2,400 more up front ($11,400 vs $9,000 for pressure-treated), but wood needs $250 to $600 a year of cleaning and sealing. Around year 8 to 10 the cumulative upkeep passes the upfront difference, and by year 20 the totals are about $13,400 for composite versus $17,500 for wood.

Why is composite decking so expensive?

Composite boards are engineered wood-plastic or PVC extrusions sold with 25-to-30-year fade and stain warranties, and they require hidden fastener systems and tighter joist spacing (often 12 inches on center) than wood. Material alone runs $9 to $18 per sq ft before a single post is set.

Can you resurface a wood deck with composite boards?

Yes, if the framing is sound. Re-decking over a solid pressure-treated frame costs roughly 40% to 60% of a full rebuild, since footings, posts, beams, and joists (about a third of a deck's cost) stay in place. Have the structure inspected first: rot at the ledger or posts means a full rebuild.
