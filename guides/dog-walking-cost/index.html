How Much Dog Walking Costs per Walk (and What a Weekday Schedule Really Runs)

🦮 7 min read📊 National rates + schedule math

A dog walker looks like a $25 decision. It's usually a $7,000-a-year decision wearing a small price tag. The per-walk rate is easy to compare; what hits the budget is the schedule — walk length, days per week, dogs per walk, market — stacked on top of it. Here's what walkers charge nationally, and how to price the whole commitment before you book.

Who's on the leash moves the number too. Within the $20-$35 solo band, app marketplaces cluster near the bottom — Rover's 2025 national average for a 30-minute walk was $21.45 — while independent professional walkers run $24-$34 with a US average near $29. Market matters as much: the same national platform averaged $21.21 in Chicago while independents there charged $25-$35, and the big coastal metros run 20-40% over national bands. Around Thanksgiving and Christmas, expect roughly 1.5× holiday pricing from walkers who book out.

What does a weekday walker cost per month and per year?

Five 30-minute walks a week at the $27.50 national typical is $137.50 a week, about $596 a month, and $7,150 a year. In a major metro at 1.3× national pricing, each walk runs $35.75, the week $178.75, and the year $9,295 — $774.58 a month averaged out. The budget path is a group walk at $16, three afternoons a week: $48 a week and $2,496 a year, and the dog still comes home tired. The premium path — a daily one-hour walker at $40 — runs $10,400 a year.

Is Rover or Wag cheaper than an independent walker?

On sticker price, the marketplaces look cheaper — both land at the low end of the band. But platforms take a cut from both sides: Rover takes roughly 20-25% from the sitter and adds an owner-side booking fee around 11%, while Wag sells a monthly pass that covers its walk fees. Walkers price those fees in, so an independent with a standing weekly schedule often beats the apps' all-in price by 10-20%. What platforms buy back is vetted reviews, insurance, and support when something goes wrong — which counts for something when a stranger is entering your home. Do walkers charge for a second dog? Are packages worth it?

  • Second dog: +$5 to $10 per walk, nearly universal, since the walk is already happening. At the $8 midpoint, two dogs on one 30-minute walk cost $35.50 against $55 for two solo walks.
  • Prepaid packages: most independents discount 10-15%. At 12% off, a $27.50 walk becomes $24.20 — a 20-walk package costs $484 instead of $550, saving $66 every 20 walks, about $858 a year for a five-day-a-week dog. Buy the small package first; expiration windows and walker turnover are the catch.
  • Tips: 15-20% is standard — $4 to $6 on a $27.50 walk — and a holiday bonus of a week's tip is a common thank-you for a daily regular.
  • Puppies and meds: midday puppy visits price like short walks; medication administration adds $5-$10 at many services.

Is a walker cheaper than daycare or a sitter?

Usually. Five walk-in daycare days a week run $700-$1,200 a month against $596 for daily walks — though daycare is a full day of supervised play, not a midday break, and its packages and monthly passes close much of the gap. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a dog walker per walk?

Nationally, $15-$25 for a 20-minute walk, $20-$35 for 30 minutes (typical $27.50), and $30-$50 for a full hour. Group walks with other dogs run $12-$20 per dog. Within the 30-minute band, app marketplaces cluster near $21 (Rover's 2025 national average was $21.45) and independent professionals near $29. Major metros run 20-40% over these bands, small towns under them.

How much does a weekday dog walker cost per month?

In a major metro at 1.3x national pricing, the same schedule runs $178.75 a week and about $9,295 a year. The budget path is a group walk three afternoons a week at $16: $48 a week, $2,496 a year.

On price alone, independents usually win by 10-20%. Do dog walkers charge more for a second dog?

Usually $5-$10 extra per walk for a second dog from the same household, since the walk is already happening. Two dogs on one 30-minute walk at the $27.50 typical plus $8 works out to $35.50, versus $55 for two separate solo walks.

Are prepaid walk packages worth it?

Most independents and many platforms discount packages 10-15%. At 12% off a $27.50 walk you pay $24.20, so a 20-walk package costs $484 instead of $550 and saves $66. For a five-day-a-week dog, that annualizes to about $858 a year. The catch: expiration windows and refunds if you move or the walker quits, so buy the smaller package first.

Is a dog walker cheaper than doggy daycare?

Usually. A daily 30-minute walk averages about $596 a month against $700-$1,200 a month for five walk-in daycare days at $35-$60. But they are different products: daycare is a full day of supervised group play, a walk is a midday break. Daycare packages and monthly passes narrow the gap, so run both numbers for your dog.
