How Much Do Small Pets Actually Cost? Rabbits to Bearded Dragons

🐹 10-20K searches/mo💰 CPC: $1-2⏱️ 8 min read

Small pets get marketed as starter animals with starter prices: $20 hamsters, $40 guinea pigs, $30 lizards. The real numbers land differently. A rabbit runs $1,580 in its first year and nearly $10,000 over its life. Even the hamster's honest first-year bill is $480. Here's the full arc — acquisition, proper setup, monthly food, exotic vet — for the four most common small pets, with the math you can point to before the pet store checkout.

How much do rabbits cost?

Adoption is $25-$75 (shelters and house-rabbit rescues are full of them, often already spayed — a $400 surgery you skip). The setup that actually works is an exercise pen, not a cage: $150-$300 with litter box, hides, and heavy dishes. Spay or neuter is $200-$500 and isn't optional — unspayed females have very high uterine-cancer rates, and intact rabbits spray. Food is the quiet constant: hay, pellets, and fresh veggies run $50-$80 a month.

  • First year: $50 adoption + $250 setup + $350 spay + $780 food/litter (12 × $65) + $150 vet = $1,580
  • After that: $930 a year ($65/month + $150 vet)
  • Lifetime (10 yrs): 1,580 + 930 × 9 = $9,950

Rabbits live 8-12 years. The "cheap classroom pet" is a decade-long commitment that prices like a cat — and needs an exotics vet, which charges $50-$150 a visit and up to four figures for an emergency GI-stasis episode.

How much do guinea pigs cost?

Double what you'd guess, because guinea pigs need company: they're herd animals, and solo pigs stress. Budget a same-sex pair at $20-$50 each. A C&C-grid cage big enough for two runs $120-$250 — pet-store "guinea pig cages" are almost always too small — and hay, pellets, veggies, and bedding run $50-$70 a month for the pair.

  • First year (pair): $70 adoption + $180 cage setup + $120 initial vet + $720 food/bedding (12 × $60) + $150 vet = $1,240
  • After that: $870 a year
  • Lifetime (6 yrs): 1,240 + 870 × 5 = $5,590, about $78 a month

How much do hamsters cost?

The genuine budget pick: $15-$30 for the animal, $60-$120 for a 40-gallon tank or equivalent bin cage (the pet-store "starter habitats" are too small and get replaced), a proper $20-$40 wheel sized so the back stays straight, and about $25 a month in food and bedding. One hamster only — Syrians are solitary and cagemates fight.

  • First year: $20 + $110 setup + $300 food/bedding (12 × $25) + $50 vet reserve = $480
  • Lifetime (3 yrs): 480 + 350 × 2 = $1,180 — the cheapest real pet budget there is

How much do bearded dragons cost?

The reptile surprise: the lizard is $40-$100, but the sun is expensive. A 120-gallon adult enclosure, UVB and heat fixtures, thermostat, and decor run $400-$700; reptile keepers widely recommend against the 40-gallon "starter kits" for adults. Then food: live insects plus greens run $35-$55 a month, and UVB bulbs need replacement about twice a year ($25-$50 each) even when still glowing.

  • First year: $70 + $550 setup + $90 initial vet + $540 insects/greens (12 × $45) + $120 vet + $60 UVB = $1,430
  • After that: $720 a year
  • Lifetime (12 yrs): 1,430 + 720 × 11 = $9,350 — rabbit territory, because they live so long

Three costs the pet store won't mention

Exotic vet visits. Small pets are "exotics," and exotic exams run $50-$150 — usually more than a cat's. Not every vet sees rabbits or reptiles, so find your exotics vet before the emergency, and check the pet insurance cost calculator, since some exotics plans exist and price out surprisingly well against a four-figure rabbit surgery.

Chew replacement. Rabbits and guinea pigs are rodents-adjacent chewing machines: hides, tunnels, litter boxes, and eventually baseboards and charger cables all need replacing. Budget $100 a year and bunny-proof the room.

Electricity (dragons). Heat lamps and UVB run a real, permanent kilowatt bill — part of why the dragon's steady state is $720 a year while the hamster's is $350. Frequently Asked Questions

Are small pets cheap to own?

The animal is cheap; ownership isn't. A $20 hamster still needs a $110 proper setup and runs about $480 in its first year and $1,180 over its 2-3 year life. A $50 shelter rabbit runs $1,580 the first year and close to $10,000 over a 10-year lifespan — cat territory. Price the decade, not the sticker.

What's the best small pet for a budget?

A hamster for total dollars (about $480 first year, $1,180 lifetime), or guinea pigs if you want interactive pets for a middle budget ($1,240 first year for a pair, $5,590 lifetime). Rabbits and bearded dragons both clear $9,000 over their lifetimes — fine pets, wrong choice if the budget was "under $500 all-in."

Do guinea pigs really need to live in pairs?

Yes. Guinea pigs are herd animals, and solo pigs chronically stress and vocalize more. The standard guidance from welfare organizations is same-sex or neutered pairs — which is why they're budgeted as two ($70 adoption, two mouths of hay and veggies) in every honest cost table.

Why can't you put two hamsters together?

Syrian hamsters are strictly solitary; two adults in one cage fight, often to death, and even dwarf species need careful introductions with a plan to separate. The pet-store image of a cluster of hamsters is a staging choice, not husbandry advice. One hamster, one habitat.
