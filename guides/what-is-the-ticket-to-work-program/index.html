What Is the Ticket to Work Program, and Is It Worth It?

📄 7 min readUpdated October 23, 2026

Ticket to Work is Social Security's free, voluntary program that connects people on SSDI or SSI with job services. If you're 18 to 64 and getting benefits, you already have a ticket. Using it gets you help finding work and, while you make timely progress, protection from medical disability reviews. Here's who qualifies, how to use it, and the year-by-year rules that keep the protection in place.

What is the Ticket to Work program?

It's Social Security's free employment program for people on disability benefits. You get a "ticket" you can take to an approved provider: either an Employment Network (EN), which can be a private company, nonprofit or local agency, or your state's vocational rehabilitation (VR) agency. They help with things like job search, training, accommodations and benefits planning. You don't pay them. SSA pays the provider later, when you hit work milestones.

It's voluntary. Nothing happens to your benefits if you ignore the ticket, and assigning it doesn't commit you to anything you can't undo.

Those rules come from 20 CFR 411.125. You don't need to apply for a ticket separately. If you meet them, you have one.

How do you use your ticket?

  • Find providers. The Ticket to Work Help Line (1-866-968-7842) and the directory at choosework.ssa.gov list ENs that serve your area, including ones that work remotely.
  • Talk to more than one. You're allowed to interview as many as you like. Ask what they'll actually do, how often you'll hear from them, and whether they help with benefits counseling.
  • Sign a plan. With an EN it's an Individual Work Plan; with state VR it's an Individualized Plan for Employment. The provider sends it to the program manager, and your ticket counts as assigned from the day the plan is signed while you're eligible.

If a provider isn't working out, you can take the ticket back in writing and reassign it. You get up to 90 days to do that while your protection from medical reviews stays in place.

Does Ticket to Work stop disability reviews?

This is the benefit most people are really after. While your ticket is assigned and you're making timely progress, SSA won't start a medical continuing disability review. That's real protection if you're nervous that working, or simply being reviewed, could cost you your benefits. The CDR process itself is in what happens at an SSDI continuing disability review.

What it doesn't stop is a work review. SSA can still check whether your earnings are over the substantial gainful activity (SGA) limit, $1,690 a month in 2026, and stop checks on that basis. The ticket also doesn't change any work rules. The trial work period, the extended period of eligibility and the SGA limit work exactly as they do without one; can you work while on SSDI walks through them.

What counts as timely progress?

Every 12 months the program manager checks whether you're moving toward self-supporting work. You can meet each year's goal through work or through school. Here's the schedule from 20 CFR 411.180, with 2026 dollar amounts:

The $1,210 figure is the 2026 trial work month amount, and $1,690 is the 2026 SGA amount; both change every January. You can also mix the two routes. If you do a third of the year-one work goal (one month at $1,210) and two-thirds of the school goal (40% of a full-time year), the percentages add to 100 and you've passed.

Months when your ticket isn't assigned, or is in inactive status, don't count toward these years.

What if you can't keep up?

Life happens: a flare-up, a hospital stay, a job that falls through. You have two options before a review goes against you:

  • Put the ticket in inactive status. Ask the program manager in writing. From the next month, the clock on timely progress stops, but medical reviews can happen again. You can reactivate the same way.
  • Challenge a failed review. If the program manager decides you're not making timely progress, the notice explains why. The decision takes effect 30 days after it's sent unless you ask SSA to review it.

Failing a progress review doesn't throw you out of the program. You lose the protection from medical reviews, but your provider can keep working with you. If you later show you've met the missed year's requirements, you can ask to be put back in in-use status.

Is Ticket to Work worth it?

If you're thinking about working at all, usually yes. The services are free, the protection from medical reviews is valuable, and benefits counseling alone can stop costly mistakes, like going over the SGA limit without realizing it. Ask your provider, or the Help Line, about a Work Incentives Planning and Assistance (WIPA) counselor, who can map out how your earnings would affect your checks month by month.

The main risk isn't the ticket. It's working without understanding the limits. Frequently Asked Questions

Does using a Ticket to Work affect my SSDI benefits?

Not by itself. The ticket doesn't change your benefit amount or the work rules. Your checks are affected by what you earn, under the usual trial work period and substantial gainful activity rules, whether or not you use a ticket.

Who is eligible for the Ticket to Work program?

People aged 18 to 64 who get SSDI in current pay status, or SSI that isn't suspended. You aren't eligible to assign a ticket while you're receiving continued benefits during an appeal of a cessation decision.

Does Ticket to Work protect me from a continuing disability review?

From medical reviews, yes, as long as your ticket is assigned and you're making timely progress. It doesn't stop SSA from reviewing your earnings to see whether they're over the substantial gainful activity limit, $1,690 a month in 2026.

What happens if I don't make timely progress?

You become subject to medical reviews again, but you can keep working with your provider. The decision takes effect 30 days after the notice unless you ask SSA to review it, and you can ask to be reinstated once you show you've met the missed requirements.

Can I switch Employment Networks?

Yes. Tell the program manager in writing that you're taking your ticket out of assignment, then sign a new plan with another provider. If you were making timely progress, you get up to 90 days to reassign it while keeping your protection from medical reviews.
