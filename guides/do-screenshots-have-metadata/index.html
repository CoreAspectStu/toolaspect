Do Screenshots Have Metadata?

📄 6 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Screenshots carry much less metadata than photos. There's no GPS position and no camera information, just the image size, a timestamp and sometimes a label saying it's a screenshot. That's why screenshotting a photo strips its location. The real privacy risk in a screenshot is what's visible: the status bar, notifications, URLs and badly hidden text. Here's what's stored, what leaks, and how to share safely.

Do screenshots have EXIF data?

Very little. A screenshot isn't a photo taken by a camera, so there's no camera to describe. The major platforms don't write GPS coordinates into screenshots by default, and there's no lens, exposure or camera model to record. What you usually get is the image size, a date and time, and on Apple devices a small tag that labels the image as a screenshot. That's how the Photos app sorts them into their own album.

Here's how that compares with a photo straight off a phone camera:

The pixel size is the sneaky one. A screenshot that's exactly 1179 by 2556 pixels came from one of a handful of recent iPhone models. It won't identify you, but it narrows down the device.

Does taking a screenshot of a photo remove its location?

Yes. The screenshot is a new file built from the pixels on your screen, so it doesn't inherit the original photo's GPS position, camera details or capture time. People use this trick all the time before posting a picture, and it works.

It costs you a lot of quality, though. A 12-megapixel phone photo is 4032 by 3024 pixels. Shown full width on an iPhone screen that's 1179 pixels wide, it takes up about 1179 by 884, and a screenshot keeps only those pixels. That's under 9% of the original. Fine for a quick share, poor for printing or zooming in.

Almost everything a screenshot gives away is in the picture, not the metadata. That's where people slip up:

The URL bar is a common one. Links to shared documents, password resets and order pages often carry a token that works like a key. Anyone who can read it from your screenshot may be able to open the page.

Can a cropped screenshot be uncropped?

Normally no. But in 2023, researchers Simon Aarons and David Buchanan showed that two popular tools didn't throw the cropped part away. Google Pixel's Markup editor overwrote the start of the original file without shortening it, so the leftover bytes of the full image stayed behind. That's CVE-2023-21036, fixed in Google's March 2023 update. They found the same problem in Windows 11's Snipping Tool and Windows 10's Snip & Sketch (CVE-2023-28303), which Microsoft patched the same month. On Windows it only happened when you reopened a saved screenshot, edited it and saved over the same file.

The bugs are fixed, but screenshots cropped and shared before the fix may still hold the uncropped image. And the lesson still applies: when you crop something sensitive, save it as a new file rather than overwriting the old one.

Is blurring or pixelating enough to hide text?

Not reliably. Pixelated text can sometimes be reconstructed, because each block's color depends on the letters under it and a program can try fonts until the blocks match. Open-source tools have shown this working on pixelated passwords. Mild blur has the same weakness, and a see-through highlighter often reveals what's under it if someone adjusts the brightness.

Use a solid black or white box, then save a flat image so the box can't be lifted off as a separate layer. For PDFs, the same rule applies, and a black rectangle drawn on top isn't enough. Use a real PDF redaction tool that removes the text underneath.

Do apps strip metadata when you send a screenshot?

Some do and some don't. Big social networks generally strip EXIF from images you upload, but email, cloud drive links and file transfer send the file exactly as it is. Messaging apps vary by app and by whether you send it as a photo or as a document. Since a screenshot carries so little metadata anyway, the bigger risk is always what's visible. Before you send one, look at all four corners and the very top of the image. That's where the personal stuff hides.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do screenshots show your location?

Not in their metadata. iPhone, Android and desktop screenshots don't store GPS coordinates by default. Your location can still show up in what's visible, such as a map, a weather widget, a street sign or an address in the image.

Can someone tell what phone took a screenshot?

Sometimes, roughly. The pixel size of a full-screen screenshot matches specific screen resolutions, and the status bar style differs between iPhone and Android. Neither identifies you personally.

Does a screenshot remove EXIF data from a photo?

Yes. A screenshot is a new image, so the original photo's GPS, camera details and timestamp aren't copied. It also lowers the resolution, so a metadata remover is better if you need the full-size photo.

Can a cropped screenshot be recovered?

Normally not. The 2023 aCropalypse bugs in Google Pixel Markup and the Windows Snipping Tool left the uncropped image inside some edited files. Both were patched in March 2023, but older edited screenshots may still contain hidden data.

Is it safe to blur sensitive info in a screenshot?

Not reliably. Pixelation and light blur can sometimes be reversed for text. Cover sensitive details with a solid box and save a flat copy of the image.
