How the Selection Index works

Your PSAT report shows two section scores, each from 160 to 760. The Selection Index doubles the verbal one:

SI = (2 × R&W + Math) ÷ 10

The doubling rewards verbal balance in a way that surprises strong math students. A 760 Math / 640 R&W profile indexes (1,280 + 760) ÷ 10 = 204. Flip it — 760 R&W / 640 Math — and the same 1,400 total indexes 216. Twelve points for the same composite, which in cutoff terms is the difference between Commended and Semifinalist in a dozen states.

Why your zip code changes your cutoff

NMSC allots Semifinalists roughly in proportion to each state's share of graduating seniors, then sets each state's cutoff to hit that allotment. Result: the same 218 index that makes Semifinalist in Indiana or Arizona falls short in New Jersey. It also means the spread is stable-ish year to year — the high-cost, high-education states cluster at the top — but individual states bounce a point or two annually with the score distribution.

One quirk worth knowing: eligibility follows the state where you take the test as a junior, so a senior-year move can't game it. Boarding school students are pooled into a special national group rather than their school's state.

What happens after you qualify

  1. October (junior year): take the PSAT/NMSQT and submit entry info through your school
  2. April-September: roughly 50,000 high scorers hear from NMSC; Commended thresholds circulate in spring
  3. September (senior year): about 16,000 Semifinalists are named via their high schools
  4. October: Semifinalists submit the Finalist application — grades, coursework, essay, recommendation
  5. February: about 15,000 Finalists named; roughly 7,250 win scholarships from March onward

The money comes in three flavors: the $2,500 single-payment National Merit Scholarship, corporate-sponsored awards, and college-sponsored packages that at some sponsoring schools grow into the largest line of a financial aid letter. Finalist status travels well even where the official scholarship doesn't.

How to read a score that's close

If your index sits within three points of your state's recent cutoff, you're in live territory: cutoffs moved as much as five points in some states between 2025 and 2026. If you're a sophomore reading this, your qualifying test is next October — and since the index doubles verbal, the highest-payoff prep is reading pace and grammar conventions, not another round of math drills. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good Selection Index for National Merit?

It depends entirely on your state. For the class of 2026, anything from 210 (North Dakota, West Virginia, Wyoming, New Mexico) to 225 (DC, Massachusetts, New Jersey) made Semifinalist, and the Commended cutoff was 210 nationally. A practical target: 220+ puts you in Semifinalist range in most states; 223+ covers all but the toughest (MA, NJ, DC); 228 is perfect. For the class of 2027, estimates run 208-223 with most states expected to drop a point or two.

How do I calculate my PSAT Selection Index?

Selection Index = (2 × Reading & Writing + Math) ÷ 10, using your two PSAT section scores (each 160-760). The index runs 48-228. Verbal is doubled because the PSAT folds two verbal sections into one score. Example: 720 R&W and 700 Math give (1,440 + 700) ÷ 10 = 214. And the doubling bites: a 760 Math / 640 R&W profile indexes 204, while the flipped 760 R&W / 640 Math profile indexes 216 — twelve points apart on the same composite.

Is Commended Student a big deal?

Commended Students (roughly 34,000 a year) don't continue in the National Merit Scholarship competition, but the letter is a genuine credential for applications, and some corporate-sponsored scholarships are reserved for Commended students specifically. It signals top-3%-ish scoring nationally. The recognition is announced in late September of senior year along with Semifinalists.

How much money can you win as a National Merit Finalist?

NMSC awards roughly 7,250 scholarships: $2,500 single-payment National Merit Scholarships, corporate-sponsored awards (often renewable, sometimes tied to a parent's employer), and college-sponsored awards that can run to full tuition or more at sponsoring schools. Many colleges outside the official program also offer large guaranteed aid for Finalists. The score itself opens doors; the essay and application decide the money.

When should I start preparing for the PSAT for National Merit?

The qualifying test is the October PSAT/NMSQT of junior year, so the summer before junior year is the practical runway. Because the Selection Index doubles verbal, students chasing cutoffs usually profit most from grammar, rhetoric, and reading-pace work. And take the test seriously even beyond National Merit: the PSAT is also the qualifying route for other programs and a dry run for the SAT.
