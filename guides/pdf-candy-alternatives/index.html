PDF Candy Alternatives, Compared (Free and No Upload)

📄 6 min readUpdated October 10, 2026

PDF Candy is a decent all-rounder: a long list of tools, a clean interface and no ads. The catches turn up once you use it a lot: free use is limited per hour, files above a certain size need a paid plan, there's no batch processing, and every file you drop in gets uploaded to its servers. Below are eight free alternatives, a table of exactly what PDF Candy charges for, and a tool-by-tool swap list. Most of the jobs people use it for, like merging PDFs and compressing PDFs, can be done right in your browser without uploading anything.

What are PDF Candy's free limits?

PDF Candy describes its free plan in words rather than numbers. As of October 2026, its pricing page lists "page and hourly restrictions", "no batch processing", "limited file size" and an operations limit on the desktop app. You don't need an account to start, but once you hit the limit it asks you to sign in or subscribe. Reviews from TechRadar and PCWorld describe the free web tier as about one task an hour. PDF Candy doesn't confirm that figure itself, so treat it as a rough guide.

Is PDF Candy safe to use?

For ordinary documents, yes. PDF Candy is run by Icecream Apps, a software company registered in Cyprus. Its security page says uploads are encrypted with 256-bit AES in transit and at rest, kept on servers in the EU and USA, and deleted automatically within 2 hours. You can also ask for earlier deletion.

The issue isn't trust in PDF Candy in particular. Any upload-based tool puts a copy of your file on someone else's server for a while. For a lease, a payslip or a medical letter, a tool that runs on your device removes that question entirely. When is PDF Candy worth paying for?

The yearly plan works out to about €48 a year. That's good value if you're on Windows, process PDFs most days, and like having one interface for everything, including the desktop app. It's also a fair pick if you regularly handle files bigger than free tools manage comfortably, since paid tasks go up to 500 MB.

It's harder to justify if your work is mostly merge, split and compress. Those are exactly the jobs free browser tools do without limits. If you're thinking about the lifetime plan, check how often you've actually hit the hourly cap in the last month. Twice? Wait an hour. Twice a day? The €99 might pay for itself.

How do I check a PDF tool doesn't upload my file?

Open the tool, then turn off Wi-Fi or unplug your network cable. If the tool still processes your file, it's working on your device. For a closer look, press F12, open the Network tab and drop in a PDF. An upload shows up as a request roughly the size of your file. Browser-based tools load their code once and then work offline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PDF Candy free?

Partly. Every tool works without an account, but free use comes with hourly and page limits, a smaller file size cap and no batch processing. PDF Candy's pricing page doesn't publish the exact numbers. Paid plans start at €6 or $6 a month depending on where you are, and remove the hourly limit.

Is PDF Candy safe?

It's a legitimate service run by Icecream Apps, a company based in Cyprus. Its security page says files are encrypted in transit and at rest, stored on servers in the EU and USA, and deleted automatically within 2 hours. That's reasonable for everyday files, but for tax, medical or ID documents a tool that never uploads is the safer choice.

Does PDF Candy have a Mac version?

Not of the desktop app. PDF Candy Desktop is for Windows only, from Windows 7 to 11. Mac and Linux users can use the website or switch to a browser tool that works offline, or Preview on a Mac.

Is the PDF Candy lifetime plan worth it?

Only if you're on Windows and use PDF Candy most weeks. The €99 lifetime plan covers the desktop app and the website. If you mainly merge, split and compress, free tools do the same jobs without a limit, so you'd be paying for convenience rather than features.

Do I need an account for PDF Candy?

Not to start. PDF Candy says every tool works without registration, and the free tier needs no account. Once you reach the free usage limit, it asks you to sign in with Google, Apple or email, or to subscribe, before you can carry on.
