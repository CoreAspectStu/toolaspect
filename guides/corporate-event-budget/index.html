How to Build a Corporate Event Budget

💡 8-15K searches/mo💰 CPC: $4-12⏱️ 7 min read

Company events price per head, and the head-count math is brutal and simple. A holiday party runs $75–$150 a person (a full stack lands near $160). Team building: $65–$380. One-day meetings about $300–$450 per guest. Multi-day offsites $2,000–$3,000 each with travel. Build the budget per attendee, add 5–10% contingency, and finance will actually read it.

Why per-person is the only unit that works

Event vendors quote totals; finance approves comparables. The bridge between them is cost per attendee, because it survives headcount changes and lets you benchmark your $21,000 holiday party against an industry band instead of against last year's memory.

One rule keeps the math honest: every line is either per-person (food, drinks, swag, activities) or a fixed cost you divide across heads (keynote, production, custom build). Mix them up and your per-person number lies in both directions — too expensive per head at low attendance, deceptively cheap at high attendance.

The anatomy of a holiday party budget

120 employees, standard metro, 10% contingency — the worked example:

  • Venue and setup: $30 × 120 = $3,600
  • Catering: $55 × 120 = $6,600
  • Bar package: $25 × 120 = $3,000
  • Entertainment/DJ: $12 × 120 = $1,440
  • Decor, AV, transport, swag: $26 × 120 = $3,120
  • Planning and on-site coordination: $12 × 120 = $1,440
  • Subtotal $19,200 → +10% contingency = $21,120 all-in ($176 a head)

Two levers dominate: the bar (a consumption bar instead of a package saves $10–$15 a head) and the date (a Thursday in early December runs 15–25% under the peak two weekends).

Where offsite money actually goes

The $2,000–$3,000 per-person offsite band makes sense once you see the lines: lodging $700, flights $500, ground transport $100, F&B $300 — travel is about 61% of a $1,950 stack. That's why the hybrid schedule (one travel day, meetings that could've been a call held virtually) has become the default compromise, and why "can we drive?" is the highest-leverage question in offsite planning.

For the meeting itself, don't skip the AV line. Production that looks basic — screens, mics, recording, a tech on site — runs $40 a head for a one-day meeting and $120+ at conference scale, and it's the line that eats understaffed budgets.

Getting the budget approved

Present three numbers, in this order: per-person cost, total, and the benchmark band. "120 employees at $176 a head all-in, industry band $75–$200" is an approval conversation. "$21,000 for a party" is a denial. Then show the contingency explicitly (5–10%) — finance trusts budgets that admit surprise exists — and note where you'd cut if the number gets trimmed: the bar tier and the plus-one policy, not the food quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a corporate event cost per person?

Depends entirely on the format. 2025 planning benchmarks: holiday party $75-$150 a head (ezCater's standard; a full stack with venue, bar, entertainment, and coordination runs closer to $160), team building $65-$380, a one-day meeting or conference day $300-$450 per guest, a multi-day offsite $2,000-$3,000 all-in with travel (BoomPop), and client-facing conferences landing near $465 a head before production extras.

What percentage of an event budget should be contingency?

5-10%, toward the high end for outdoor events, custom production, or first-time vendors. The line that moves most is food and beverage - it rose about 6% in 2025, the steepest increase in corporate event budgets, while overall meeting costs rose 4.3%. A 10% contingency on a $21,000 holiday party is $2,100: roughly what an extra bar hour, a surprise vegan station, and overtime servers cost.

What are the biggest lines in a corporate event budget?

Food and beverage first, then venue, then AV and production. For a one-day meeting, F&B runs about $110 of a $290 per-person stack; for a conference, AV and production adds $120 a head and is the line first-time planners underestimate. For multi-day offsites, travel dominates: lodging ($700 for two nights) plus flights ($500) is about 61% of a $1,950 per-person budget.

How do you get a corporate event budget approved?

Present three numbers: per-person cost, total, and the benchmark band. Finance approves comparables, not vibes - 'holiday party for 120 at $176 a head all-in, against an industry band of $75-$200' reads completely differently from '$21,000 for a party.' Build it per attendee, add the contingency visibly, and show the cost of skipping it: turnover and hiring costs dwarf a holiday party for most teams.

How can we cut the corporate event budget without cutting the event?

Attack the travel and format lines, not the experience. A one-day offsite instead of two nights removes 61% of the per-person cost. Weekday and non-December dates cut venue and catering quotes 15-30%. Virtual or hybrid formats run 60-75% cheaper per attendee where the event's purpose allows it. And cater by headcount reality, not the invitation list - catering for the 85% who actually attend saves 15% on the biggest line.
