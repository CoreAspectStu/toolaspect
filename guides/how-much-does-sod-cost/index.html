How Much Does Sod Cost?

⏱️ 6 min read

Sod sells for $0.35 to $0.85 per square foot in the common grasses, which works out to $160 to $380 a pallet. Zoysia runs the table at $0.60 to $1.10 a square foot — $330 to $560 a pallet. Laid by a crew with prep included, budget $1.00 to $1.80 a square foot. The classic order, a 1,000-square-foot front yard, is two pallets and $350 to $850 in material.

How much is sod per square foot and per pallet?

Sod is priced like produce, not like lumber: it's cut fresh, it's regional, and the grass type sets the band. Fescue and Bermuda anchor the affordable end; zoysia carries a premium because it's slow to grow on the farm. Here's the 2026 picture:

The gap between farm-gate and delivered pricing explains most of the spread. North Carolina State's sod producers report has farm-gate Bermuda at $0.30 to $0.62 a square foot (averaging $0.41), rising to near $0.99 delivered. Big-box pallets sit at the top of the band — a 500-square-foot pallet of Harmony Zoysia at Home Depot runs about $559, right at $1.12 a foot. You're paying for convenience and a pallet you can pick up today.

One caveat before you compare any two quotes: pallet coverage isn't standardized. Most pallets hold 450 or 500 square feet depending on the farm and region, and mini pallets and contractor big rolls exist too. A $400 pallet of 450-square-foot coverage is pricier per foot than a $400 pallet of 500 — always divide price by coverage, never compare pallet to pallet.

How many pallets does your lawn need?

The math is area divided by pallet coverage, rounded up, plus a waste cushion. Rolls follow the same pattern: the contractor roll covers about 10 square feet, small rolls 6 to 9. Add 5 percent for a clean rectangle, 10 percent for curved borders, slopes, or a first-time laying crew. Sod is cut fresh to order and returns aren't accepted, but a pallet short is worse — a mismatched seam from a different harvest shows for a season.

What does installed sod cost per square foot?

Turn-key installation runs $1.00 to $1.80 per square foot — roughly double the material price on the common grasses. For the 1,000-square-foot yard, that's $1,000 to $1,800 installed versus $350 to $850 in pallets. The labor line covers more than laying: killing or stripping the old turf, tilling and grading, about a cubic yard of compost worked into every 1,000 square feet, the laying itself, a roller pass, and the first soak.

DIY shifts those costs rather than deleting them. You'll add a tiller rental, a starter fertilizer (cheap, and worth it), and delivery — often free once you're past a few pallets, a flat fee below that. What you can't skip is timing. Fresh sod should go down within 24 hours of harvest; a stacked pallet generates internal heat that can kill the grass in hours on a hot day. Schedule delivery for the morning you install, and if the day slips, break the pallet down into shaded single layers and keep it moist.

Sod or seed — which is cheaper?

Seed wins on price, and it's not close — a fraction of sod's cost per square foot for the same square footage. But the honest comparison is cost against what you get. Sod is an instant lawn: erosion control the day it's laid, no mud season, no weed competition from the seedbed, and usable turf in weeks. Seed asks for months of watering, germination patience, and a leash on foot traffic, and washouts on slopes mean doing parts twice.

The usual split: sod the front yard and any slope you can't afford to lose, seed the back if the budget is tight. And whatever goes down, the prep underneath is the same job — that's where the survival of either one is decided.

When's the best time to buy and lay it?

Farms harvest roughly April through October, and the grass's own calendar matters more than the deal. Cool-season grasses like fescue and bluegrass root best in fall, second-best in spring. Warm-season grasses like Bermuda and zoysia want late spring through summer heat — laying zoysia in October in most zones means it goes dormant before it roots. Buying in season is buying on sale; end-of-season pallets are sometimes discounted, but the establishment risk eats the savings.

Logistics move the price a little and the outcome a lot. Pallets weigh 1,500 to 3,000 pounds, so delivery needs a truck with a forklift or a pallet jack — coordinate that before the truck shows up. Lay the rolls staggered like brickwork, butt edges tight without stretching, pin rolls on slopes steeper than about 2:1, and water daily for two weeks. First mow at the two-to-three-week mark, when a corner resists lifting, taking no more than a third of the blade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a pallet of sod?

Common grasses run $160 to $380 a pallet: fescue $160-$315, Bermuda $180-$380. Zoysia is the expensive one at $330-$560 a pallet. Confirm coverage before ordering - pallets hold 450 or 500 square feet depending on the farm.

How many square feet does a pallet of sod cover?

Standard pallets cover 450 or 500 square feet - the two common sizes vary by region and farm. Mini pallets and contractor big rolls also exist, so the coverage number on your quote is the one that matters.

How much does it cost to sod a quarter acre?

A quarter acre is 10,890 square feet - about 23 pallets at 500 square feet each with a 5 percent waste cushion. At $0.35-$0.85 per square foot, material runs roughly $3,800 to $9,300, which is why large lawns often go to seed or sprigs instead.

How much does a pallet of sod weigh?

Between 1,500 and 3,000 pounds depending on soil moisture, sod thickness, and roll size. Fresh-cut sod is heavy and perishable - plan on a forklift or pallet jack at delivery, not a wheelbarrow and hope.

How long does sod take to root?

Sod knits to the soil in two to three weeks - the test is resisting lifting when you tug a corner, which is also the green light for the first mow. Full root establishment takes about six weeks. Keep the top inch of soil moist for the first two weeks, watering daily in heat.
