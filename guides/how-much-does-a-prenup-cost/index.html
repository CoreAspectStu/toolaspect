How Much Does a Prenup Cost? What Couples Actually Pay

💍 10-20K searches/mo💰 CPC: $8-25⏱️ 8 min read

A prenup costs $599 on an online platform (about $649 with e-signature and notarization), $1,000-$3,000 as an attorney flat fee for a simple agreement, and $5,000-$20,000+ when a business or family money is in play — a family-law survey puts the average attorney-drafted prenup near $8,000 per couple. The line most quotes skip: the other spouse's independent review, commonly $500-$1,500.

What actually drives the bill up

Complexity, roughly in this order:

  • Business ownership or a professional practice — valuations and buyout terms add attorney hours faster than anything else
  • Real estate beyond the shared home — rental properties, family cabins, property bought before the marriage
  • Stock options, RSUs, and equity compensation — vesting schedules have to be divided conceptually before they're divided legally
  • Expected inheritance or family trusts — trust structures vary enough that templating them is risky
  • Spousal support terms — waivers and caps, the most negotiated clause in the document
  • Debt allocation — student loans brought into the marriage, business debt

A two-income couple renting an apartment with no business and no trust money sits in the simple band. Every box you tick moves you one band up. A moderate case — say, a rental property plus spousal support terms — prices at about $4,000 typical from a flat-fee attorney, plus $799 for the other spouse's review: $4,799 all-in, with an honest range of $3,000-$7,500.

Do both spouses need lawyers?

Most states don't strictly require independent counsel for a prenup to be valid, but many weigh it heavily when a spousal-support waiver is involved, and nearly every family lawyer recommends it. Practically, the drafting attorney works for one spouse; the other spouse hires a review attorney, commonly at a flat $500-$1,500 or hourly. That's why real totals run about double the headline quote: the $2,000 flat fee becomes $2,800 once the other side is represented. Skipping the review saves $799 on a platform prenup and gives a future challenger its easiest argument.

Timing rules that protect the agreement

Prenups get challenged on process more than substance. The recurring vulnerabilities: incomplete financial disclosure, signing under deadline pressure (wedding-week signings are a cliché for a reason), and terms a judge reads as unconscionable. Some states codify timing — California gives parties at least seven calendar days between receiving the agreement and signing. A majority of states follow the Uniform Premarital Agreement Act or its 2012 revision; the rest apply their own case law. The takeaway for budgeting: paying for unhurried process is part of what makes the document durable.

What about postnups?

Same document category, negotiated after the wedding instead of before. Postnups are commonly quoted at or above prenup rates — the drafting is similar, but finances are already commingled, which adds negotiation time. If you're pricing both, run the same complexity checklist and add a premium at the typical-to-high end.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a prenup cost in 2026?

Online platforms charge $599 per couple flat (about $649 with e-signature and notarization), with full attorney representation through the platform at $2,197. Attorney flat fees run $1,000-$3,000 for simple agreements, $2,500-$6,000 at moderate complexity, and $5,000-$20,000+ for complex ones; a family-law survey puts the average attorney-drafted prenup near $8,000 per couple. Hourly billing runs $150-$500 an hour depending on market.

Is an online prenup as good as an attorney prenup?

Different products. Online prenups are genuine state-specific legal documents and cost a fraction of attorney fees, but enforceability turns on voluntariness, full financial disclosure, and fairness — and an agreement neither spouse had reviewed by independent counsel is easier to challenge. Platforms sell attorney review at $799 per partner for this reason. If a business, equity compensation, trusts, or contested terms are involved, attorney-drafted is the safer money.

What is the most expensive part of a prenup?

Disclosure and negotiation, not the paperwork. Both sides document assets, debts, and income; attorneys negotiate terms; revisions follow. Business valuations and stock-option schedules add hours fast, which is why complex prenups reach $5,000-$20,000+. The other spouse's independent review commonly runs $500-$1,500 on top of the drafting fee.

Does a postnup cost the same as a prenup?

Postnups are commonly quoted at or above prenup rates. The legal work is similar, but postnups are negotiated after marriage, when finances are already commingled — that tends to add negotiation time rather than drafting time. Expect pricing in the same bands as prenups, with a premium at the typical-to-high end in most markets.

Can a prenup be thrown out?

Yes, and the reasons are procedural more than substantive: incomplete financial disclosure, signs of pressure or rushed signing (California, for example, requires at least seven calendar days between presentation and signature), terms a judge finds unconscionable, and missing independent counsel where a state weighs it heavily. A majority of states follow the Uniform Premarital Agreement Act or its 2012 revision; the rest apply case law. This is general information, not legal advice.
