Dog Pregnancy Week by Week: The 63-Day Timeline (2026)

🐕 ~18K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2⏱️ 8 min read

Dog gestation is 63 days on average from breeding, with 58-72 normal — nine weeks flat. The milestones are just as fixed: embryos implant around day 16-18, ultrasound confirms pregnancy days 25-30, an X-ray can count puppies from day 45, and a temperature drop below 99°F means whelping inside 12-24 hours. Here's the whole timeline on real dates, what to do at each stage, and the signs that mean call the vet.

Weeks 1-3: quiet, and that's normal

Days 1-14 look like nothing. Fertilized eggs travel and don't implant until days 16-18, and until the placenta forms she has no hormonal reason to act different. Some dogs show a brief appetite dip or mild nesting behavior around week 3 as progesterone stays high, but plenty show zero signs. This is the window to stop any flea/tick or deworming treatments that aren't labeled safe for pregnancy, and to confirm with your vet which ones are. Keep exercise normal; don't "rest" her yet.

Weeks 4-5: confirmation and the diet shift

Day 25-30 ultrasound is the reliable confirmation: fetal heartbeats are visible, and a good sonographer can estimate viability. Palpation works days 28-32, but it's operator-dependent, so leave it to the vet. A relaxin blood test also turns positive around day 28-30 if you want lab confirmation. At day 35, food becomes the project: fetal growth accelerates hard from here, and she'll move to roughly 30-50% more calories by term, in progressively smaller, more frequent meals as the uterus crowds the stomach. Switch to a quality puppy or performance formula — the calcium and calorie density do the work; skip calcium supplements unless your vet prescribes them.

Weeks 6-7: count the puppies

From day 45 the skeletons mineralize enough to count on an X-ray, and by day 50-55 the count is dependable. This number is worth knowing twice: once now, so a stuck puppy at 3 a.m. means "three more inside, go now," and once during whelping, so you know when she's actually finished. Her abdomen is obviously distended by this point, nipples enlarged, and you may see puppy movement late in week 7. Set the whelping box up at day 50 and encourage her to sleep in it — mothers who choose the box early don't deliver under your bed.

Weeks 8-9: the watch

From day 57, take her rectal temperature morning and evening. Normal is 100.5-102.5°F; a drop below 99°F predicts labor within 12-24 hours. Whelping itself runs in stages: stage 1 (6-12 hours, occasionally up to 24) looks like restlessness, panting, nesting, and refusing food; stage 2 is active delivery, ideally a puppy every 30-60 minutes with rest between; stage 3 passes placentas, which she'll likely eat — that's fine, in moderation. Puppies should nurse within the first couple of hours for colostrum.

Emergencies, not milestones: hard straining 30-60 minutes with no puppy, more than two hours between puppies when the X-ray said more are coming, green-black discharge before the first puppy, or a puppy stuck partially out. Those are dystocia, and they're surgical problems. The earlier X-ray count earns its keep exactly here.

First litters run smaller than the breed's typical, litter size peaks at ages 2-5, and both extremes carry risk: single-puppy litters (common in toys) can grow too large for easy delivery, and very large litters in small mothers raise the odds of a C-section. Brachycephalic breeds like Frenchies and English Bulldogs deliver by scheduled C-section as standard practice — plan that with your vet from day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many weeks is a dog pregnant?

Nine weeks — 63 days on average from breeding, with 58-72 days normal. Week 1-2: fertilization and implantation. Week 3-4: ultrasound confirms. Week 5: appetite climbs. Week 6-7: X-ray can count puppies. Week 8-9: whelping box ready, temperature watch, delivery.

Can a dog be pregnant for 70 days?

Yes. Counted from breeding, 72 days is still within normal range because sperm can survive several days before fertilization; counted from ovulation, gestation is tighter, around 63 days. Past 72 days from breeding with no labor, call the vet — an overdue litter can mean a stuck puppy or uterine inertia.

Do pregnant dogs eat more right away?

Not usually. Early pregnancy often dips appetite slightly around week 3. The real increase starts around day 35 (week 5) and by the final week she may eat 30-50% more than normal in multiple small meals. Then appetite often drops off in the last day or two before whelping.

What does a dog's temperature drop to before labor?

A normal dog's rectal temperature runs 100.5-102.5°F. Within about 12-24 hours of labor it drops below 99°F, usually to 98-99°F. Take her temperature twice daily from day 57; a single reading under 99°F means whelping is close, so get the whelping kit to the box.
