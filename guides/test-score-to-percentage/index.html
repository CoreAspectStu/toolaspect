What Grade Is 21 Out of 30? Test Scores to Percentages, Decoded

📝 High search volume⏱️ 6 min read

The short answer: 21 out of 30 is 70%, a C- on the standard 10-point scale with plus/minus grades. But "what grade is X out of Y" questions are really three questions wearing one coat: what's the percentage, what letter is that on my school's scale, and how many points would I have needed for the letter above? This guide answers all three, with the arithmetic shown, plus the quick-grade trick teachers use to mark a whole stack of tests without a calculator.

How do you convert a test score into a percentage?

One division and one multiplication: percentage = points earned ÷ points possible × 100. For 21 out of 30, that's 21 ÷ 30 = 0.70, times 100 gives 70%. A few more, worked the same way:

Raw scoreArithmeticPercentageLetter (10-pt, +/−)
21 / 3021 ÷ 30 = 0.7070%C−
18 / 2518 ÷ 25 = 0.7272%C−
17 / 2017 ÷ 20 = 0.8585%B
45 / 5045 ÷ 50 = 0.9090%A−
19 / 2019 ÷ 20 = 0.9595%A
24 / 3024 ÷ 30 = 0.8080%B−

Notice how the same letter shows up at different raw scores depending on the total. That's why "I got a 21" means nothing without the "out of 30" attached — a 21 out of 25 is 84%, a whole letter and a half higher.

What letter grade is 70 percent, exactly?

It depends on the scale, and the scale is set by your school, not by math. The three you'll run into:

  • 10-point with plus/minus (most common in college): A− starts at 90, B− at 80, C− at 70, D− at 60. So 70% is a C−, worth 1.7 on the 4.0 GPA scale.
  • 10-point, plain letters: 70% is a C, full stop. No plus/minus means no GPA penalty for landing at the bottom of the band.
  • 7-point scale (common in parts of the South and some private schools): A starts at 93, B at 85, C at 75, D at 65. A 70% squeaks by as a C−, and a 76% that would be a C elsewhere is a C here too — the bands are just narrower.

When in doubt, the syllabus wins. The conversion from letter to GPA is also school-specific, but the standard mapping runs 4.0 for an A down to 0.7 for a D−, with F at 0.

How many points buy the next letter up?

Flip the formula: points needed = cutoff percentage × points possible, rounded up. On a 30-point test with the 10-point scale, the letter boundaries land on clean numbers:

Letter goalCutoffPoints on a 30-point testResulting %
D−60%18 points60%
C−70%21 points70%
C73%22 points73.3%
B−80%24 points80%
A−90%27 points90%

So a student sitting at 21 out of 30 is exactly one point from a C, three from a B−, and six from an A−. On odd totals the rounding matters more: for a C at 73% on a 45-point test you need 73% × 45 = 32.85, rounded up to 33 points, which scores 73.3%.

Does one C- actually hurt your GPA?

Less than you'd think, more than you'd hope. GPA is credit-weighted, so a C− (1.7) on a test usually isn't the whole course grade — it's one score inside a category. If tests are 60% of the course and this is one of three, that single 70% drags the course grade down by a couple of points before other work pulls it back. How do teachers grade a stack of tests fast?

They pre-compute the division table and never touch a calculator again. Points possible in, percentages out, one row per raw score — a 30-point test has 31 possible scores and you only need the rows near the letter boundaries marked. Post-test, grading becomes lookup: 27? A−. 24? B−. The test grade calculator builds this quick-grade table for any point total up to 200, in any of the three scales, which beats rebuilding it in a spreadsheet every time a quiz has 23 questions.

Two marking habits worth stealing from experienced graders: always double-check the points possible before you divide (the classic error is dividing by the last question number when one question was worth 2 points), and grade the same question across all papers rather than one paper at a time — it keeps partial-credit judgments consistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

What grade is 21 out of 30?

21 out of 30 is 70%, a C- on the 10-point scale with plus/minus grading (70-72.9% is the C- band). On a plain letter scale it's a C. One more point, 22 out of 30, is 73.3% and a solid C.

How do you convert a test score into a percentage?

Divide points earned by points possible, then multiply by 100. For 18 out of 25: 18 ÷ 25 = 0.72, so 72%. The division works for any point total, including decimals on partial credit.

What letter grade is 70 percent?

On the 10-point scale, 70% is the floor of the C range: a C- with plus/minus grading (1.7 GPA points) or a plain C without it. On a 7-point scale where a C- starts at 70, it's also a C-, but a 73% would be a C there instead of a plain-C territory letter.

How many points do I need on a 30-point test for a B?

On the 10-point scale a B- starts at 80%, which is 24 out of 30 exactly. A solid B at 83% needs 24.9 rounded up to 25 points, and a B+ at 87% needs 27 (26.1 rounded up).
