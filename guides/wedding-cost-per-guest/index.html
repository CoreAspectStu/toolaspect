How Much Does a Wedding Cost Per Guest? The $292 Question, Answered

🧾 6K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3.00⏱️ 7 min read

The average US wedding costs $292 per guest — $34,200 across 117 guests, per The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study. But only about $133 is truly per-person (catering $80, bar $25, cake $7, rentals $12, favors $5, invitations $4). The other $159 is each guest's share of $18,600 in fixed costs. Once you see the split, guest-list math stops being guesswork.

Where the $292 comes from

The Knot surveys couples right after their weddings — 10,474 of them for the 2026 study — and the per-guest average falls out of two numbers: $34,200 total and 117 guests. The calculator-grade move is splitting that $292 into what an extra invitation actually costs versus what the day costs whether anyone shows up.

Notice the U-shape. Small weddings don't win on per-guest cost because venue and vendor minimums put a floor under the fixed pool; huge weddings lose because the variable stack compounds. The sweet spot is the middle — big enough to dilute the fixed pool, small enough that the per-person lines haven't taken over.

The two halves: variable stack vs fixed pool

The variable stack — what each guest truly costs:

  • Catering — $80 per person average, $50-$150 by service style
  • Bar and drinks — $25 typical, $10-$50 by package
  • Cake and dessert — $7 typical, $3-$15
  • Rentals (tables, linens, china) — $12 typical, $6-$25
  • Favors — $5 typical, $0-$12
  • Stationery and postage — $4 typical, $2-$8

That's $133 per head typical, spanning $71 to $260. Every name you add to the list costs roughly this — nothing more, nothing less.

The fixed pool — what the day costs regardless: venue site fee (~$4,600), photography and video (~$3,400), florals and decor (~$3,100), music (~$2,050), attire and beauty (~$2,050), rings (~$1,710), planner (~$1,370), officiant (~$340) — about $18,600 all-in at the national average. Divided across 117 guests, that's $159 a head. Across 60 guests, $310. Across 150, $124.

Three weddings, same math

The average, reproduced: 117 guests, typical lines. $133 × 117 = $15,561 variable + $18,600 fixed = $34,161 — $292 a head, within a dollar of the published average. The lean-to-lavish band spans $16,357 to $68,220.

Upscale at 100 guests: $100 plates and a $35 bar push the stack to $163 a head. $16,300 + $18,600 = $34,900 — $349 a guest. Same headcount as plenty of average weddings, an extra $10,000 of atmosphere.

Lean at 60 guests: budget lines ($71 a head) and a trimmed $9,900 fixed pool. $4,260 + $9,900 = $14,160 — $236 a guest. And the fixed pool is now 70% of the bill, so the marginal guest costs just $71. That's the quiet argument for small weddings: the tenth-guest savings are trivial, but the floor is lower too.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost per wedding guest?

$292 per guest, per The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study: an average $34,200 wedding across 117 guests. About $133 of that is truly per-person — catering, bar, cake, rentals, favors, invitations — and the remaining $159 is each guest's share of roughly $18,600 in fixed costs like the venue fee, photographer, and band.

Is it cheaper per guest to have a bigger wedding?

Per guest, often yes — up to a point. The fixed pool divides across more heads, so guest 150 costs less than guest 50. But the total still climbs by the full variable stack (about $133) for every name added, and The Knot's tiers show the curve: under-50-guest weddings average $340+ a head, 51-100 average $270-$530, and 100-plus average about $370 because the variable lines take over.

How much should I budget for food and drink per guest?

Catering averages $80 per person before bar and cake; per-plate menus run $50-$150 by service style. Add a bar at $10-$50 a head, dessert at $3-$15, rentals at $6-$25, favors at $0-$12, and stationery at $2-$8. The full stack spans $71-$260 per guest, with $133 typical at the national average.

What costs the most per guest at a wedding?

The venue-and-catering combination: 29% venue and rentals plus 24% catering, cake, and drinks is more than half the average wedding. Per guest that's about $85 of venue share and $70 of food and drink. Neither is fully negotiable, but the venue site fee is fixed — which is why the same $18,600 pool feels cheap at 150 guests and expensive at 60.

Does cutting the guest list actually save money?

It cuts the total, not the per-guest average — and the two get confused constantly. Cutting from 150 to 100 guests saves 50 × the variable stack, about $6,650 at typical lines, dropping the total from $38,550 to $31,900. But the per-guest figure rises from $257 to $319 because the same fixed pool now splits across fewer people. You save real money; you just don't look 'cheaper per head.'
