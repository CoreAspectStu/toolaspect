Is DocuSign free to use?

Signing something someone sends you through DocuSign is free. You click the link in the email, sign, and you're done. Sending documents out for signature is what costs money.

DocuSign's pricing page doesn't list a free tier. What it offers is a 30-day free trial with no credit card required. After that, the entry plan is Personal, which includes 5 envelopes a month. US reviews in 2026 put it around $10 to $15 a month depending on whether you pay yearly or monthly. The Standard and Business Pro plans are priced per user and include 100 envelopes per user per year when billed annually. That works out to about 8 a month, which surprises people who assumed a team plan meant unlimited.

So the real question isn't "what's a free DocuSign?" It's which of two jobs you're doing.

Do you need to sign, or send for signature?

This split decides everything. Signing is putting your own signature on a document. Sending is asking other people to sign, tracking whether they have, and ending up with a record that proves who signed what and when.

For signing, you don't need a signing platform at all. A lease addendum, a school permission form or an NDA someone emailed you as a PDF can be signed with a free tool, then sent back as an attachment. That covers most people, most of the time.

For sending, the audit trail is the point. A platform records email addresses, timestamps, IP addresses and the exact version each person saw, then seals the final file. If the signature is ever disputed, that certificate is your evidence. That's the part free tools that run on your own device can't provide.

Is a free electronic signature legally binding?

In the US, usually yes. The federal ESIGN Act of 2000 and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, adopted by nearly every state, say a signature can't be denied legal effect just because it's electronic. What matters is that the person meant to sign and the parties agreed to deal electronically. A drawn or typed signature on a PDF can meet that bar.

The weak point is proof, not validity. If the other side later says "I never signed that," a platform's certificate of completion is strong evidence. A signature image on a PDF is much weaker evidence on its own. In the EU, eIDAS sets up three levels: simple, advanced and qualified signatures. Simple ones, the kind any free tool makes, are valid but carry the least evidential weight.

Some documents have extra rules regardless of the tool. Wills, many notarized documents, certain court filings and some real estate paperwork often need wet ink, witnesses or a specific process. This is general information, not legal advice, so for anything high-stakes, check what the receiving party or your local rules require.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does DocuSign have a free plan?

No. DocuSign's pricing page lists no free tier. There's a 30-day free trial that doesn't need a credit card, and the cheapest paid plan, Personal, includes 5 envelopes a month. You can always sign documents other people send you through DocuSign without paying.

What's the best free alternative to DocuSign?

For sending a few documents a month to other people, Dropbox Sign and SignWell both give you 3 free requests a month with an audit trail. For signing a PDF yourself, a free tool like ToolAspect's Sign PDF, Adobe Acrobat Reader or Mac Preview is enough, and none of them limit how often you sign.

Is a signature added with a free PDF tool legally binding?

In the US, the ESIGN Act and UETA give electronic signatures the same standing as ink for most documents, as long as the parties intend to sign and agree to do business electronically. The harder part is proving who signed and when, which is what a signing platform's audit trail is for. Wills, some court filings and certain real estate documents have extra rules, so check before relying on any e-signature for those.

What's the difference between an envelope and a signature request?

They're the same idea under different names. DocuSign calls each send an envelope, Dropbox Sign calls it a signature request, and SignWell counts documents. One send can usually include several files and several signers and still count as one.

Can I sign a PDF without uploading it anywhere?

Yes. ToolAspect's Sign PDF runs in your browser, so the document never leaves your device. Adobe Acrobat Reader and Mac Preview also sign offline. The trade-off is that there's no third-party record of the signing, which is fine for most forms and agreements you're signing yourself.
