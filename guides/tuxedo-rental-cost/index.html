Tuxedo Rental Cost: How Much to Rent or Buy in 2026

💡 15-25K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2-5⏱️ 7 min read

The formalwear question has a clean answer most of the time: rent for $150–$250 a person for a wedding, $100–$180 for prom, and buy only when you'll wear it five times or more. Here's the retailer-by-retailer pricing, the promos nobody advertises, and the break-even math for the buy decision.

How much does a wedding tux rental cost?

Per person, plan on $150 to $250 all-in — package price plus the ~$12 damage-and-handling fee every chain charges. The Knot's industry average runs $150-$300, and the package tiers look like this:

Basic tiers mean lighter fabrics and fewer accessories; premium adds shoes and better wool. For photos and one evening, the standard tier is where most parties land.

What does the whole party pay?

Groomsmen customarily cover their own rentals — the standard ask is $150-$250 each. The party math at the typical $197 per person: a groom plus five groomsmen totals $1,182, or $985 with the groom comped. Run your own headcount in our suit and tux rental cost calculator.

What discounts can you actually get?

  • The free groom. Most national chains comp the groom's rental at five paid groomsmen rentals — sometimes at four, and the slot can often cover a father of the bride instead. Ask at registration; it's rarely on the price card.
  • Group discounts. $30-$40 off per tux once four or more register together.
  • Off-season timing. Prom season (March-May) is peak; January and late-fall rentals face less size-run competition.
  • Local shops. Independent formalwear stores routinely undercut chains by $30-$60 per package and remember your name at the second fitting.

When does buying beat renting?

Divide the buy cost by the rental price. A mid-range tuxedo at $850 plus $75 of alterations is $925; at $197 a rental, that's 4.7 rentals to break even — the fifth formal event is where buying wins, which matches the 4-5 wear rule Jos. A. Bank itself publishes. The surprise is how fast a suit breaks even: $400 plus alterations is under 3 rentals.

What else should you budget?

Beyond the package: budget $20-$50 for a shirt upgrade if the included one is polyester, real shoes if the premium tier's rentals look worn, and nothing else — fittings are included, and out-of-town groomsmen can get measured at any branch of the same chain then pick up locally. Timeline: reserve styles and measure everyone two months out; final fittings land one to two weeks before the wedding, when alteration queues are short. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a tux?

Wedding rentals run $150-$250 per person including the ~$12 damage-and-handling fee; the full market spans $99-$300. Men's Wearhouse packages run $99-$249 and Jos. A. Bank lists $169.99 for jacket and pants. Prom rentals typically run $100-$180.

Who pays for groomsmen tux rentals?

By convention, groomsmen pay their own way — $150-$250 each is the standard ask. Many couples cover the cost as a groomsmen gift, and rental chains commonly comp the groom's own tux when five groomsmen rent, with $30-$40 group discounts on top.

How many wears until buying a tux is cheaper?

About five. A mid-range tuxedo at $850 plus $75 of alterations totals $925, which equals 4.7 rentals at the typical $197 — so the fifth formal event is where buying wins. A $400 suit breaks even at under 3 wears, which is why the standard advice is rent the tux, buy the navy suit.

Do you tip for a tuxedo rental?

No tip is expected for the fitting and pickup itself. If a store goes beyond — same-day turnaround on an alteration, open-box delivery, emergency exchange the week of the wedding — a $10-$20 gesture to the person who fixed it is noticed and remembered.

What happens if a rented tux gets damaged?

The ~$12 damage-and-handling fee you already paid covers ordinary wear and minor damage like a popped button or a loosened hem. Major damage — burned, torn, or lost pieces — is billed separately per the rental agreement, which is why the deposit card stays on file.
