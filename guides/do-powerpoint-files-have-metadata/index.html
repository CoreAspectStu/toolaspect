Do PowerPoint Files Have Metadata?

📄 6 min readUpdated November 4, 2026

Yes. Every .pptx carries properties such as the author and dates, and it can also hold content most people forget about, like speaker notes, hidden slides and objects parked off the slide. We built a test deck and cleaned it. Clearing the properties, including with our own tool, left all of that in place.

What metadata does a PowerPoint file store?

A .pptx is a ZIP package of XML files, like a Word or Excel file. Some of it describes the file, and plenty of it is content people forget is there. Speaker notes are the classic example. Decks get sent to clients with "don't mention the price rise" still sitting under slide 4.

Slide titles deserve a second look. They're listed in the file's properties as well as on the slides, and that list can include slides you've hidden. If you've reused an old deck, its master slides and layouts may also hold a former client's name or logo.

How do you remove metadata in PowerPoint?

Document Inspector goes much further than a property cleaner. Save a copy first, since Microsoft warns you might not be able to undo it. Then go to File > Info > Check for Issues > Inspect Document, and choose Inspect. We didn't run it ourselves, but here's what Microsoft's documentation says about it:

ItemCan the inspector remove it?
Comments and ink annotationsYes
Document properties and personal infoYes
Revision tracking dataYes, on versions new enough to detect it
Invisible on-slide objectsYes, but not objects merely covered by others
Off-slide contentYes, unless the object has an animation effect
Presentation notesText yes, pictures in notes no
Custom XML dataYes
Embedded files and objectsFinds only, fix by hand
Macros, VBA and ActiveX controlsFinds only, fix by hand

Look at the gaps. Hidden slides aren't on Microsoft's list, so unhide them and decide for yourself, or delete them. An animated object parked off the slide won't be detected at all, and that's a common trick for fly-in effects. Charts count too: if one holds a full workbook behind it, pasting it back as a picture cuts the link to that data. For cropped photos, use Picture Format > Compress Pictures and tick "Delete cropped areas of pictures".

Does saving as PDF remove the metadata?

Mostly, but check the output. A PDF export drops the animation, the off-slide objects and the XML parts. It doesn't drop everything, though. A PDF has metadata of its own, and PowerPoint can copy the document properties across unless you turn that off under Options when you export. Export options can also include hidden slides and notes pages, so look over the settings before you save. We cover what to check in Do PDF Files Have Metadata?, and our PDF metadata editor lets you see and change those fields. Be aware that its Strip All button can leave the file's XMP data behind, as that guide explains.

A PDF also can't be edited as a deck, which is often the point. If the other person doesn't need your slides, send them something they can read and not much else. The file can't reveal what you never put in it.

What should you check before you send a deck?

  1. Go through the slide panel and look for crossed-out slide numbers.
  2. Read the notes for every slide, or remove them all with the inspector.
  3. Zoom out in Normal view and look for anything sitting beyond the slide edges.
  4. Open the Comments pane, including resolved threads.
  5. Right-click each chart, and see what opens under Edit Data.
  6. Open View > Slide Master and look for old names and logos.
  7. Run Document Inspector on a copy, or clear properties with a tool.
  8. Reopen the copy and click through it once, since a removed object can break an animation.

The same habits work for other Office files, as we show for Word documents and Excel workbooks. If you're under a legal hold or answering a records request, ask your attorney before you clean anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you tell who made a PowerPoint?

Often, yes. File > Info shows an author and a last-modified-by name, usually taken from the Office account of whoever saved it. Those fields are easy to change and can be inherited from a template, so treat them as a clue and not as proof.

Do speaker notes show when you send a PowerPoint?

They don't show during a slide show, but anyone who opens the .pptx can read them in the Notes pane. They stay in the file until you delete them. A property cleaner won't remove them, as our test showed.

Can someone see hidden slides in a PowerPoint?

Yes. Hidden slides are skipped when presenting, but they're still in the file, and anyone with the deck can unhide them. Delete any you don't want shared. Don't assume Document Inspector will catch them, since Microsoft's list doesn't include them.

Does PowerPoint keep a history of edits?

A .pptx mostly stores who saved it last, along with a revision count and dates. Decks in OneDrive or SharePoint also have a version history on the server, but that isn't inside a copy you download and email. Newer versions can store revision data too, which the inspector can remove.

Does Document Inspector remove everything?

No. Microsoft says it only reports embedded objects and macros, and it won't detect objects covered by other objects, animated objects off the slide, or pictures in notes. Those need checking by hand. Always run it on a copy.
