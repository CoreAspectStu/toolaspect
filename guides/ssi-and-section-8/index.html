SSI and Section 8: Will a Voucher Cut Your Check?

📄 6 min readUpdated November 4, 2026

A Section 8 voucher won't reduce your SSI. SSA excludes HUD housing help from income and resources, so your 2026 check can stay at $994. SSI does count when the housing authority sets your rent, though, and that usually comes to about $284 to $288 a month. Here's how both sides work, with the numbers.

Does a Section 8 voucher reduce your SSI?

No. SSI usually counts free or cheap housing as income, which is what trips people up when they move in with family. Federal housing help is the exception. SSA's operations manual says the value of assistance paid under the U.S. Housing Act of 1937 is excluded from both income and resources, and it notes that "Section 8" is HUD housing assistance.

Under 20 CFR 416.1130, rent below the presumed maximum value (about $351.33 in 2026) normally leaves the gap counted as support, and plenty of voucher holders pay less than that. But SSA's manual tells staff to assume any HUD or Rural Housing Service assistance is excluded, and to accept your statement unless there's a reason to question it. Tell SSA it's Section 8.

How much rent do you pay on SSI with Section 8?

HUD starts with your total tenant payment. It's the highest of 30% of your monthly adjusted income, 10% of your monthly income, any welfare amount set aside for housing, or the minimum rent, which can be up to $50 in the voucher program. On SSI, the 30% figure almost always wins.

Adjusted income is annual income minus deductions. Any disabled or elderly family gets a flat deduction: $550 for 2026 where the housing authority has moved to HUD's newer rules, and $400 where it hasn't. HUD set January 1, 2027 as the compliance deadline for most agencies, so you may see either figure this year. SSI counts as income here.

For 2027, SSI rises to $1,029 and the deduction to $575, which puts the payment at about $294. So $35 more a month from SSA means about $10 more in rent once your housing authority applies it.

What if the apartment costs more than the payment standard?

Your voucher pays up to a local limit called the payment standard. Below that limit, you pay your total tenant payment and the voucher covers the rest of the rent and utilities. Above it, you pay the whole difference yourself.

There's also a brake. When you first move in with a voucher, your share can't be more than 40% of your adjusted monthly income if the unit is over the payment standard. On $994 of SSI under the newer rules, that's $379, so you've got only about $95 of room. Here it is with an example $1,200 payment standard. Can you get a voucher if SSI is your only income?

Income isn't usually the barrier. Voucher income limits are set by local area, and federal law steers most new vouchers to households at or below 30% of area median income, which SSI alone will nearly always fall under. Getting off the waiting list is the hard part. Lists can run for years and many close to new names, so apply wherever you'd live, and ask about Mainstream vouchers, which are set aside for non-elderly people with disabilities.

HUD's newer rules cap net family assets at $105,574 for 2026 and $109,797 for 2027, though only where those rules are in force. SSI's own limit of $2,000 for one person will stop you long before that. A net worth calculator shows where you stand.

What happens if you live in someone else's Section 8 home?

The housing authority needs to approve you as a household member, and adding your SSI will usually raise the family's payment. Moving in without approval can put their voucher at risk.

SSA then looks at what you pay. The HUD subsidy isn't counted, but help from the people you live with can be. Section 8 by itself isn't on SSA's list of payments that make a public assistance household, though that exception still applies if someone else in the home gets SNAP, TANF or SSI. If nobody does, paying your share of the household's costs is the safer route. Confirm it with SSA, since we didn't find a manual example for this exact setup.

What do you need to report, and to whom?

Report to both. Tell SSA when you move, when someone moves in or out, and when anyone starts paying your bills. Tell the housing authority about changes in income or household members. If you also get SSDI, our disability benefits calculator gives a rough figure for the table above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Section 8 count as income for SSI?

No. SSA excludes the value of Section 8 assistance from income and resources, and its manual tells staff to accept your statement that you get HUD help unless there's a reason to question it.

Does SSI count as income for Section 8?

Yes. SSI goes into your annual income when the housing authority sets your payment. On $994 a month in 2026, that's about $284 to $288 toward rent and utilities, depending on which HUD rules your agency uses.

Can I pay my rent directly if I get SSI and a voucher?

Yes, and you should. Pay your share from your own account so there's a clear record. If a relative pays it for you, SSA can count that as support and reduce your check by up to about $331 in 2026.

Will my rent go up when SSI goes up?

Usually, at your next rent review. Your payment is about 30% of adjusted income, so the $35 SSI increase for 2027 adds about $10 a month.

Does the $2,000 SSI limit change if I have a voucher?

No. The limit stays at $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple. The voucher itself isn't a resource, but any money you've saved still counts, including cash left over at the start of a month.
