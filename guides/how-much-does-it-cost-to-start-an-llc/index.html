How Much Does It Cost to Start an LLC? Fees by State

🔎 90K searches/mo💰 CPC: $28⏱️ 7 min read

Starting an LLC has exactly one mandatory cost: your state's filing fee, which runs from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts and averages about $132 nationwide. Everything else you see advertised — registered agent services, operating agreement templates, expedited handling — is optional. The bills that surprise people come later: recurring annual fees that range from zero to California's $800 minimum franchise tax, charged every single year whether the business makes money or not.

A useful paranoia: any service charging $50 or more for an EIN is reselling something the IRS hands out free in about ten minutes at irs.gov. That's the single most common upsell in the formation industry.

What's the New York publication trap?

New York looks cheap at $200 to file — and then the state requires new LLCs to publish a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks. In most counties that runs $300 to $600. In New York County, it can top $1,500. The counties with the cheapest newspapers are upstate, which doesn't help a business based in the city. Budget roughly $1,000 on average if you're forming a New York LLC, and know that some upstate counties let it go for a fraction of that.

What does an LLC cost per year after that?

The annual bill is where states really differ. Nine states charge LLCs nothing recurring. At the other end, California's $800 minimum franchise tax applies to every LLC, every year, including year one — the first payment comes due about three and a half months after formation, before most businesses have their first customer. Delaware's flat $300 annual tax and Nevada's $350 are the other big ones. Most states land between $0 and $150.

Is it cheaper to form an LLC in another state?

Usually not, and the marketing that says otherwise is selling you something. Delaware and Wyoming earn their reputations with courts and privacy laws that matter to investors and holding companies. But if you live in Texas and form in Wyoming, you still have to register as a foreign LLC in Texas the moment you operate there — which means a second filing fee and a second registered agent. You've doubled your paperwork to save nothing. The rule that holds for almost everyone: form in the state where the business physically operates. The exceptions are multi-state businesses and companies raising institutional money.

The bottom line

Expect $35 to $500 to file — the only required cost — then check the recurring column before committing, because that's what compounds. A Montana or Ohio LLC can live five years on about a hundred dollars in state fees, while the identical California business pays $3,350. Formation services are convenient, not necessary, and your EIN is always free. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest state to form an LLC in?

Montana has the lowest filing fee at $35, with Kentucky at $40 and Arkansas at $45 close behind. For ongoing costs, Arizona, Missouri, New Mexico, and Ohio charge no annual report fee at all. But the cheapest state for you is almost always the one you live in, because forming elsewhere means registering as a foreign LLC at home and paying fees in both states.

Why does California charge $800 a year for an LLC?

It's the minimum franchise tax, and it applies to every California LLC every year, including the first, whether the business has revenue or not. The first $800 comes due within about three and a half months of formation. Add the $20 Statement of Information and the ongoing bill is $820 a year — roughly $4,170 over five years in state fees alone.

Can I form an LLC for free?

No state files articles of organization for free, so the filing fee — $35 to $500 — is unavoidable. What you can get for free: your EIN from the IRS (minutes online), your operating agreement (write it yourself), and the registered agent role (act as your own if you have an in-state street address). DIY filing means your total cost is the state fee and nothing else.

Do LLCs have to renew every year?

Most states require an annual report or franchise tax payment, ranging from $0 in states like Ohio and New Mexico up to California's $800 minimum. A handful of states bill every two years instead. Miss the filing and most states charge late fees, and eventually administratively dissolve the LLC.

Is it cheaper to form an LLC or an S corporation?

An LLC is usually cheaper to run. An S corporation is a tax election that typically adds payroll processing and accounting costs — often $1,000 or more a year — in exchange for self-employment tax savings once profit is meaningful. Below roughly $40,000 to $60,000 of net profit, the extra overhead usually eats the savings.
