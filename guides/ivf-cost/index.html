How Much Does IVF Cost?

🧬 Fertility treatment costs⏱️ 8 min read

The clinic's advertised price is the start of the math, not the end. A base IVF cycle runs $12,000–$18,000 before medications; add standard stimulation drugs (≈$5,500) and ICSI (≈$1,800) and one fresh cycle costs about $20,800. With PGT-A genetic testing and a frozen transfer, a full cycle stack reaches ~$27,400 — and most patients need to budget for the possibility of more than one cycle.

The line items, honestly

IVF bills like a hospital stay: a base fee plus everything that happens around it. Here's the national picture for 2025:

Two things jump out of that table. First, medications are the least predictable line — the same drugs can cost $3,500 or $7,000 depending on your dose and where you fill them. Second, the add-ons are where two "similarly priced" clinics diverge by $10,000: one bundles anesthesia into the base fee, another bills it separately; one pushes PGT-A for every patient over 35, another reserves it for specific indications.

The medication stack, decoded

Stimulation drugs are dosed to your ovaries, not to a price list:

  • Minimal or oral-plus protocols: $2,000–$4,000. Letrozole or clomiphene with limited injections — fewer eggs, gentler cycle.
  • Standard injectable cycles: $4,500–$6,500. FSH products (Gonal-f, Follistim, Menopur) plus a trigger shot. This is the median patient.
  • High-dose, antagonist, or poor-responder protocols: $8,000–$10,000. More vials, more days, sometimes additions like Omnitrope or Luveris.

The quiet lever is the pharmacy. Mail-order fertility pharmacies routinely fill the same prescription 30–50% cheaper than a clinic's in-house dispensary, and some manufacturer programs discount for self-pay patients. If meds land at the top of your quote, price them independently before you accept the bundled number.

A worked example

A typical single cycle at a $13,500 clinic: standard meds $5,500 + ICSI $1,800 + PGT-A $3,000 + anesthesia $600 + one frozen transfer $2,500 + a year of storage $500 = $27,400. With $5,000 of confirmed insurance coverage, out-of-pocket drops to $22,400. Financed at 11.99% over 48 months: $589.77 a month, $28,309 total repaid — $5,909 of that is interest. That's why the HSA conversation below matters more than any coupon.

Stripped down — no ICSI, no testing, minimal meds, fresh transfer only — the same clinic bills about $16,000. Which stack is right is a medical question, but knowing the gap exists makes you a better participant in that conversation.

Insurance: the state map and its asterisk

As of 2025, roughly two dozen states require some form of fertility coverage, and about a dozen of those include IVF, typically with lifetime caps between $15,000 and $100,000. The list has been growing — several states added mandates in 2024–25 — and it includes both comprehensive states (Massachusetts, Illinois, Connecticut, New Jersey, Maryland, New York) and preservation-only states.

The asterisk that voids most of the map: state mandates bind fully insured plans, not self-funded ones, and most large employers self-fund under federal ERISA. So the same employer in the same mandated state can cover everything or nothing depending on how the plan is structured. A benefits check with your insurer — one phone call, ask for the fertility benefit in writing — beats every chart.

Multi-cycle packages and refund programs

Success rates being what they are, most patients should price the possibility of a second cycle before the first one starts:

  • À la carte: one fresh cycle with meds and ICSI ≈ $20,800; three of them ≈ $62,400.
  • Multi-cycle packages: commonly 2–3 cycles bundled for $20,000–$30,000. You win if you need repeats; you lose the difference if the first cycle works.
  • Refund/shared-risk programs: unlimited cycles (usually 3 fresh plus transfers) until a live birth or a large partial refund, $25,000–$40,000. Read the fine print on what counts as "success" and which exclusions apply.

Roughly, packages are insurance against the expensive tail — worth it when the clinic's success numbers for your age and diagnosis suggest more than one attempt is realistic.

Paying for it: HSA first, loans last

IVF is a qualified medical expense, so HSA and FSA dollars pay for it pre-tax — an effective 22–32% discount on whatever you can route through them. Beyond that, clinic payment plans are sometimes interest-free (but short), employer fertility benefits are increasingly common and worth an HR question, and fertility loans run from about 8% APR for strong credit to 20%+ at the margin. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does IVF cost out of pocket?

One fresh cycle runs $18,000-$27,000 all-in: a $12,000-$18,000 clinic base fee plus $2,000-$10,000 of medications and $1,000-$2,500 for ICSI if used. Add PGT-A ($2,500-$4,000) and frozen transfers ($1,000-$3,500 each) and a full cycle with testing lands near $27,400. Real-patient invoice tracking puts a typical single cycle around $23,500.

Why do IVF prices vary so much between clinics?

Because the advertised price rarely includes the same things. One clinic's $12,000 quote may exclude anesthesia, meds, and ICSI while another's $16,000 includes them. Regional cost-of-living matters too — major metros run 15-30% higher. The only comparable number is a written, itemized quote for your exact protocol.

Does insurance cover IVF in my state?

As of 2025, roughly two dozen states require some fertility coverage, but only about a dozen include IVF, with lifetime caps commonly between $15,000 and $100,000. State mandates bind fully insured plans, not self-funded ERISA plans — the kind most large employers run. A benefits check with your insurer tells you in a day what a state map can only hint at.

How can I lower my IVF costs?

Five levers that actually move the number: use mail-order fertility pharmacies (drugs often cost 30-50% less); pay with HSA/FSA dollars for the pre-tax discount; compare multi-cycle packages if you may need repeats ($20,000-$30,000 for 2-3 cycles vs $62,400 for three a la carte); check clinical trials and teaching-hospital programs; and if your state mandates coverage, verify whether a plan switch during open enrollment applies to your treatment year.

Is IVF financing a good idea?

It can be, with eyes open. Fertility loans run from about 8% APR for strong credit to 20%+ at the margin. A $22,400 balance at 11.99% over 48 months costs $589.77 a month and $5,909 in interest — real money on top of an already expensive treatment. Exhaust HSA dollars, 0% clinic plans, and employer benefits before signing a loan.
