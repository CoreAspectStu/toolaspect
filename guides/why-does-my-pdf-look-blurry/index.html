Why Does My PDF Look Blurry? 6 Causes and How to Fix Them

📄 6 min readUpdated October 23, 2026

A blurry PDF almost always means the page is stored as an image instead of text: a low-resolution scan, an over-compressed file, or a document that was turned into a picture somewhere along the way. Real PDF text stays sharp at any zoom. Here's how to tell which one you have, how much resolution a page actually needs (we tested five settings), and what you can and can't fix.

Why does my PDF look blurry?

A PDF can hold two very different things. Text and vector drawings are stored as instructions ("draw this letter here, at this size"), and they're redrawn sharply at any zoom. Images are a fixed grid of pixels. When a page is an image, zooming in just makes the pixels bigger, and everything goes soft.

So the question is really how the page got turned into a picture. These are the usual culprits:

How do I tell if my PDF is text or an image?

  • Try to select a word. If you can highlight single words, it's text. If dragging draws a box around the whole page, it's an image.
  • Search for a word you can see. Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on a Mac) finds nothing on an image page, unless it's been through OCR.
  • Zoom to 400%. Real text stays razor-sharp. Image text shows fuzzy edges or visible pixels.

If you're comfortable with a command line, pdfimages -list file.pdf shows each embedded image with its resolution in pixels per inch, which tells you exactly how much detail a scan holds.

Below about 8 pixels of x-height, letters start losing their shapes, which is why 72 and 96 DPI look blurry and why footnotes go first. The other column is the surprise. The original text PDF was 3.6 KB. As an image it was 38 times bigger at 72 DPI and about 300 times bigger at 300 DPI. Turning text into a picture makes a file both blurrier and heavier, which is a strong hint to keep text as text wherever you can.

Can you make a blurry PDF sharp again?

Not really. Once the detail is gone, nothing can put it back; "sharpening" just increases contrast at edges, and AI upscalers guess at what letters should look like, sometimes wrongly. Your realistic options:

  • Go back to the source. The original Word file, the original photo, or the sender's copy will be sharper than anything you can rescue.
  • Rescan it. 300 DPI for documents, black-and-white or grayscale for plain text pages. That keeps files reasonable.
  • Add a text layer. OCR won't sharpen the image, but it makes the words searchable and copyable, which is often what you needed anyway.

How do I compress or convert a PDF without blurring it?

A lot of blurry PDFs were sharp until someone shrank them. In our PDF compressor, try the lossless option first, which doesn't touch image quality at all. If you need the rerender option for a big scan, 150 DPI is the right floor for reading on screen, and 200 to 300 DPI is safer for anything that will be printed or has small print. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my PDF look blurry but print fine?

Usually because the viewer shows a quick low-resolution preview while you scroll or zoom, then redraws it. Browser and email previews do this a lot. If it stays blurry when you stop, the page is probably an image that holds enough detail for print but looks soft when enlarged on screen.

What DPI should a scanned PDF be?

300 DPI is the standard for documents you might print or zoom into. 150 DPI is fine for reading on screen and makes much smaller files. Below 150 DPI, small text and footnotes start to blur.

Does compressing a PDF make it blurry?

It can. Lossless compression doesn't change image quality, but rerendering pages at a lower DPI or stronger JPEG compression does. Rerendering at 72 or 96 DPI makes small text noticeably soft, so stay at 150 DPI or above for documents.

Can I unblur a PDF?

Not truly. Detail that was thrown away can't be recovered. The best fix is to get the original file or rescan the paper at 300 DPI. OCR can add searchable text to a fuzzy scan, but it won't make the image sharper.

Why is the text in my PDF blurry when the images are sharp?

The text has probably been turned into an image, for example by a scan, a print-to-image step or a flatten, while photos kept their own resolution. Try selecting a word. If you can't, the text is part of a picture.
