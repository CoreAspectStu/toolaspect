iLoveIMG Alternatives, Compared (Free and No Upload)

iLoveIMG is the image-side sibling of iLovePDF, and it's a handy one-stop shop: compress, resize, crop, convert, watermark, remove backgrounds. People go looking for an alternative when they run into the per-task caps, when they'd rather not upload client or family photos, or when the tool they need is one of the limited ones. The task caps sound generous until you do the math on a real job. A 400-photo product shoot at 30 images per task is 14 separate rounds of uploading and downloading. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's slow.

Why do people look for an iLoveIMG alternative?

Everything gets uploaded. iLoveIMG says processed files are deleted automatically within two hours, and it holds ISO/IEC 27001 certification. That's a solid policy. Still, ID scans, kids' photos, unreleased product shots and client work often shouldn't leave your device at all, and some contracts forbid it.

Big batches. 30 per task is fine for a blog post and tedious for a catalog.

The 6 MB ceiling on AI tools. A straight-from-the-camera photo can be bigger than that, so background removal and upscaling refuse it until you shrink it first.

Ads and upsells. Free pages carry ads, and some features nudge you toward Premium.

How do you check that a tool doesn't upload?

Don't take any site's word for it, ours included. Open the page, press F12 and click the Network tab, then drop in a photo. If a request appears that's roughly the size of your file, it was uploaded. If the only traffic is the page itself and its scripts, the work is happening on your machine.

A quicker test: load the tool, switch off Wi-Fi, then use it. A true in-browser tool keeps working. The one catch is that some tools download a model on first use (our background remover and upscaler both do), so run them once while you're online.

Should you just stick with iLoveIMG?

For a handful of non-sensitive images, iLoveIMG is quick and well made. Its batch-of-30 tools are easier than most desktop apps for a one-off. Switch when the photos are private, when you're dealing with hundreds at once, or when you keep bumping into the 6 MB cap on the AI tools. For most people the sensible setup is a browser tool for one-offs and XnConvert or IrfanView for the big jobs. Frequently Asked Questions

Is iLoveIMG free?

Yes, the basic plan is free. As listed on its pricing page in October 2026, free users can compress, resize or convert up to 30 images per task with 200 MB per task, remove backgrounds from 3 images at a time and upscale 1. Premium costs €5 a month billed yearly (€60) or €9 billed monthly.

Does iLoveIMG keep my photos?

iLoveIMG says processed files are deleted automatically and permanently within two hours. Your images are still uploaded to its servers to be processed, so if a file shouldn't leave your device, use a tool that works in the browser or a desktop app.

Is iLoveIMG the same company as iLovePDF?

Yes. Both are run by the same Europe-based company, share the same security page and point to the same ISO/IEC 27001 certification. A Premium account also gives you mobile access through the iLovePDF apps.

What's the best free alternative for resizing lots of images?

A desktop batch tool. XnConvert runs on Windows, Mac and Linux and can resize, convert, rotate and watermark a whole folder in one pass, though it's only free for private and educational use. IrfanView's batch mode does the same on Windows.

Can I blur faces without uploading a photo?

Yes, but you'll need an editor. Select the face in GIMP or Photopea and apply a Gaussian blur or pixelate filter. Pixelating with large blocks is harder to reverse than a light blur, and painting a solid box over the face is the safest option of all.
