How Much Does Loyalfans Pay?

⏱️ 6 min read

Loyalfans keeps the subscription-platform math familiar: creators keep 80% of what fans spend on subscriptions, tips, paid messages, live streams, and store items, and the platform takes 20%. Payouts run twice a month on the 1st and 16th with a $50 minimum. What actually sets the platform apart is a 5% lifetime referral program that pays on top of your own page. Here's the per-subscriber math, the payout mechanics, and the honest comparison against OnlyFans and Fansly.

What percentage does Loyalfans take?

Twenty percent, on every revenue stream the platform offers. Unlike some platforms that tier their cut by feature or payment method, Loyalfans applies one 80/20 split across the board:

When does Loyalfans pay you?

Withdrawals are processed on the 1st and 16th of each month, and you need at least $50 accumulated to cash out. Bank transfer is the standard method, with options varying by region. Plan around that floor in your first months: a new page earning $30 a month waits two cycles before its first payout clears, and the paperwork — tax forms, identity verification — is worth completing at signup rather than at first withdrawal, when it becomes the thing standing between you and your money.

Compared with the neighbors, that cadence sits in the middle of the pack. OnlyFans holds funds roughly seven days before release with a $20 minimum, and Fansly also runs a seven-day pending period with minimums that vary by method — $20 for Paxum, Cosmo, and ACH, $50 for SEPA, and $100 for wire or crypto. Twice-monthly on the 1st and 16th is predictable if not fast.

How does the 5% referral program work?

This is Loyalfans' actual differentiator. Refer another creator — with your link, from your socials, from your fanbase — and you earn 5% of everything they make, for as long as they're on the platform. Loyalfans pays it on top; the referred creator still keeps their 80%, so referring costs your fellow creator nothing.

How much should you expect from a second platform?

A fraction of your primary. Smaller platforms send far less native discovery traffic, so the same content that carries a busy page elsewhere lands quieter here. The playbook that works: import your best-performing catalog first — depth converts better than a fresh start — point your social bios and link pages at the new page from day one, price at or slightly below your established page for the first cohort, then normalize. Calendar a ninety-day review. If the second platform isn't paying meaningfully by then with content that works elsewhere, the audience genuinely isn't there, and your effort belongs on the primary page.

Treat the whole exercise as insurance plus margin rather than a growth bet. Platform risk is the category's shared weakness — policy changes, payout interruptions, and account bans have hit every subscription platform at some point — and a ready second page, an owned mailing list, and a funnel you control are the standard hedges.

The bottom line

Loyalfans pays 80% across every revenue stream, cashes out on the 1st and 16th with a $50 minimum, and adds a 5% lifetime referral program on top — the one line item its bigger competitors don't match as cleanly. Model it as portfolio income with a floor: run the Loyalfans calculator with your real numbers, weight the referral stream if your audience refers well, hold the ninety-day review honestly, and keep the strategic assets on infrastructure you own. The platforms are payment rails; the business is the audience.
