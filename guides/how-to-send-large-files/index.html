How to Send Large Files for Free

📄 7 min readUpdated October 16, 2026

Email gives up somewhere around 20 or 25 MB, and a single phone video can be ten times that. The fix is nearly always one of two things: send a link instead of an attachment, or make the file smaller before it goes. Below are the free limits for the services you probably already have, how long uploads really take, and real measurements of which files shrink when you zip them. Spoiler: photos and video don't.

Why won't my email send a big file?

Email was designed for text, and attachments ride along encoded as text. That encoding inflates a file by roughly a third, which is why a 20 MB video can bounce off a 25 MB limit. Most providers cap the total message somewhere between 10 MB and 25 MB, and the recipient's server has its own cap that you can't see. Our guide to email attachment size limits has the provider-by-provider numbers and the encoding math.

Anything past about 15 MB, stop fighting the attachment limit. Either send a link, or make the file smaller. Often the answer is both.

What's the easiest free way to send a large file?

Upload the file somewhere and send a link. You probably already have a service that does this, built into your email account. Here's what the common free options allow, from each provider's own help pages as of October 2026:

For a one-off file to someone outside your organization, a transfer service like WeTransfer or Dropbox Transfer is the least fuss: the recipient doesn't need an account, and the link expires on its own. For anything you'll update or share with the same people again, a cloud drive folder is better, because you send the link once and keep replacing the contents.

How long will a large file take to upload?

Upload speed is usually the bottleneck, and it's often far slower than your download speed. Cable plans commonly upload at a tenth of their download rate. These times assume you get the full speed, which you won't on busy Wi-Fi:

File size10 Mbps50 Mbps100 Mbps500 Mbps
100 MB1.3 min16 sec8 sec2 sec
1 GB13.3 min2.7 min1.3 min16 sec
5 GB1.1 hours13.3 min6.7 min1.3 min
20 GB4.4 hours53 min27 min5.3 min

The math is simple: multiply megabytes by 8 to get megabits, then divide by your upload speed. A 5 GB video over a 10 Mbps connection is 40,000 megabits ÷ 10, a bit over an hour. Run a speed test before you start a big upload, plug into Ethernet if you can, and keep the laptop awake until it finishes. Browser-based uploads usually die when the machine sleeps.

Will zipping a file make it smaller?

Sometimes a lot, often barely at all. It depends on whether the file is already compressed. We ran real files from our own machine through deflate, the same algorithm a standard .zip uses, at its strongest setting:

File typeSize reductionWhy
XML sitemap (573 KB)95.5%Repetitive text compresses brilliantly
JSON data file (204 KB)84.2%Same story: structured text
HTML page (17 KB)69.1%Text again
PNG screenshots (21 files)14.9% medianAlready compressed losslessly
PDFs (21 files)4.0% medianMost PDFs compress their own contents
Camera JPEGs (17 files)1.3% medianJPEG is already compressed
MP4 videos (25 files)0.3% medianVideo codecs leave nothing to squeeze

So zip a folder of spreadsheets, logs or CSV exports and it might shrink to a fifth of its size. Zip a folder of phone photos or videos and you'll save almost nothing. The real benefit there is packaging: one file instead of 300, which survives upload and download without anything going missing.

How do I shrink a file before sending it?

When zipping won't help, you need to reduce the content itself:

  • Scanned or image-heavy PDFs: our PDF compressor has a lossless mode that tidies the file's structure and a rerender mode that redraws each page at 72 to 300 DPI. Rerendering is where scans drop from 40 MB to 4 MB, but it also turns text into pictures, so keep it for scans and photos.
  • Only part of a PDF is needed: split the PDF and send the pages that matter, or break a huge file into chunks that each fit under the email limit.
  • Photos: the image compressor converts to JPEG or WebP and resizes. A 12-megapixel phone photo resized to 2,000 pixels wide at 80% quality is usually a few hundred kilobytes, plenty for a screen.
  • Video: re-encoding at 1080p instead of 4K cuts the file to roughly a quarter. HandBrake does this free on any desktop. Trimming dead air at either end helps too.

All three of our tools run in your browser, so the file isn't uploaded to us while you shrink it.

Is it safe to send large files through a link?

A shared link is only as private as the link itself. Anyone who has it can usually open the file, and links get forwarded, pasted into chats and indexed by mail scanners. A few habits close most of the gaps:

  • Pick an expiry. Transfer services expire by default; on cloud drives, remove access when the job's done.
  • Restrict access to named accounts when the service allows it, rather than "anyone with the link."
  • Encrypt sensitive files before they leave your machine. 7-Zip can make an AES-256 encrypted archive, and a PDF can carry its own password (see how to password-protect a PDF). Send the password by text or phone, never in the same email as the link.
  • Strip what you don't mean to share. Photos can carry GPS coordinates; our guide to removing location from photos shows how.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best free way to send a large file?

For a one-off file, a transfer service such as WeTransfer (3 GB free) or Dropbox Transfer (2 GB free) is simplest because the recipient needs no account. For bigger files, upload to Google Drive (15 GB free) or OneDrive (5 GB free) and share a link.

How do I email a file larger than 25 MB?

You can't attach it directly in most email services. Gmail swaps it for a Google Drive link automatically, Outlook offers a OneDrive link, and Apple Mail offers Mail Drop for files up to 5 GB. Or compress the file first and see if it fits.

Does zipping a file reduce its size?

Only for files that aren't already compressed. In our test, text files such as XML, JSON and HTML shrank 69% to 96%, but camera JPEGs shrank about 1% and MP4 videos about 0.3%.

How long does it take to upload 1 GB?

About 13 minutes at 10 Mbps upload, under 3 minutes at 50 Mbps and around 80 seconds at 100 Mbps. Real-world speeds are usually a bit slower than the plan's headline figure.

Why can't I copy a large file to my USB drive?

The drive is probably formatted as FAT32, which can't hold any single file of 4 GB or more. Reformatting it as exFAT removes that limit, but it erases the drive, so copy anything you need off it first.
