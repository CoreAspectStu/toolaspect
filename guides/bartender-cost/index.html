How Much Does a Bartender Cost for a Party or Wedding?

🍸 6K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3.00⏱️ 7 min read

A professional bartender runs $30-$70 an hour ($50 typical), and the crew size follows one rule: one bartender per 50-75 guests, tighter for mixed drinks. A 100-guest wedding with a full bar, 4 hours of service, a mobile bar package, and 18% gratuity comes to about $1,031 — two bartenders at $450 labor, $500 package, $81 tip. Here's the staffing math, the fees that surprise hosts, and how the bill really builds.

The staffing rule: one per 50-75 guests

Every bartender quote starts with crew size, and crew size is a division problem. One bartender per 75 guests when the bar is beer and wine only — people open their own bottles, so the bartender is really a cashier and ice manager. One per 50 once every drink involves a shaker. The in-between — beer, wine, plus one signature cocktail batched ahead — works at one per 60.

Why not save money on one bartender for 100 guests? Because the queue doesn't average — it spikes. The half hour after the toast, the cocktail hour entrance, the last call: a single bartender facing 100 simultaneous orders turns your bar into a waiting room. The second bartender isn't for volume, it's for peaks.

What's on the invoice

Beyond the hourly rate, four line items regularly surprise first-time hosts:

  • Two-hour minimums — a 90-minute cocktail reception still bills two hours
  • Setup and teardown — usually 30-60 minutes per bartender, billed at the same rate
  • The mobile bar itself — $300-$800 for the bar, ice, mixers, garnishes, and cups when your venue doesn't have one
  • Gratuity — 15-20% of labor prepaid, or a tip jar, and some contracts auto-add a service charge

Some staffing companies flatten all of it into a $200-$700 flat event fee. That's fine — just compare quotes on the all-in number, because a "$40/hour" shop with mandatory packages can outrank a "$60/hour" shop that brings everything.

A worked bill, three ways

The wedding: 100 guests, full bar, 4 hours plus 30 minutes of setup. Crew: 100 ÷ 50 = 2 bartenders, each billing 4.5 hours. Labor: 2 × 4.5 × $50 = $450. The venue has no bar, so add the mobile bar package: $500. Gratuity at 18% of labor: $81. Total: $1,031, range $619-$1,543 — squarely inside the $480-$1,200 band Insurance Canopy publishes for 100-150-guest events where hosts supply the alcohol.

The backyard party: 40 guests, beer and wine, 3 hours. Crew: 40 ÷ 75 = 1 bartender. Labor: 3.5 × $50 = $175. The kitchen island is the bar, so no package. Tip jar, no prepay. One line on a credit card, and nobody waits nine minutes for a lager.

The big reception: 150 guests, full bar, 5 hours plus an hour of setup, 20% gratuity. Crew: 3 bartenders. Labor: 3 × 6 × $50 = $900. Package: $500. Tip: $180. Total: $1,580. Notice the labor line tripled while the guest count only grew 50% — hours and crew compound.

Bring-your-own vs. the package deal

The cheapest legal path in most states: hire labor only, buy your own alcohol at retail, rent nothing. That's the $175 backyard scenario. One step up: add the mobile bar package when the venue is a field, a loft, or a backyard — $300-$800 buys the physical bar and everything disposable. The full open-bar package through a caterer or venue bundles alcohol, staff, and liability at per-drink or per-guest pricing ($30-$45 a head is typical for limited and full open bars) — convenient, insured, and priced accordingly.

Two rules regardless of path. First, confirm licensing and liability: many venues require proof of liquor liability insurance, and agency bartenders carry it — part of why they cost more than your cousin. Second, don't guess the bottles: the wedding alcohol calculator converts your guest count and hours into an actual shopping list, so the bartender line and the beverage line stay separate in your budget. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bartender cost per hour?

Professional bartenders through staffing agencies and mobile bar companies run $30-$70 an hour, with $50 typical. Thumbtack's national band runs $40-$60 an hour, and major-metro specialists quote up to $80. Two-hour minimums and billed setup time are standard, so a four-hour reception realistically bills 4.5 hours per bartender.

How many bartenders do I need for my event?

Follow the one-per-50-75-guests rule: one bartender per 75 guests for a beer-and-wine-only bar, one per 60 with a signature cocktail added, and one per 50 for a full cocktail menu. At 100 guests that means one or two bartenders; at 150 on a full bar, three. The extra bartender matters most in the 30 minutes after the toast, when everyone orders at once.

What's included in a mobile bar package?

The typical $300-$800 package brings the physical bar (a portable counter, skirting, sometimes a vintage trailer or tow-behind), ice, mixers, garnishes, cups, and small equipment. You supply the alcohol, which keeps it cheaper than a venue's open-bar package. Labor is often billed on top at the hourly rate, so compare quotes on the all-in total.

How much does a wedding bartender cost total?

When you supply the alcohol, expect $480-$1,200 total for a 100-150-guest wedding, per Insurance Canopy's published band. A typical build: 100 guests, full bar, 4 hours plus setup, two bartenders at $50 an hour ($450 labor), a $500 mobile bar package, and 18% gratuity ($81) — about $1,031 all-in, with a realistic range of $619-$1,543.

Do you tip a private event bartender?

Yes. Prepaid gratuity of 15-20% of the labor bill is standard when you're the one paying, and some contracts auto-add it as a service charge — read the line items so you don't double-tip. The tip jar is the accepted alternative at casual events; either route lands in the same place, and hosts who'd rather not see a jar can pre-pay instead.
