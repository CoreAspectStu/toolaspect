How Much Does It Cost to Ship a Dog (or Cat) in 2026?

🐶 ~35K searches/mo💰 CPC: $4–8⏱️ 7 min read

Four modes, wildly different prices: shared ground transport at $0.50–$1.60 per mile, private VIP ground at roughly double, in-cabin flight at $95–$150 each way, and air cargo from $500 to $2,500+. Here's what drives each one, what paperwork costs, and how to spot the lead-gen trap this industry is known for.

Ground transport: the per-mile math

Ground pricing follows the same falling per-mile curve as car shipping, because the economics rhyme: fixed pickup and route-building costs amortize over distance. Published 2025–26 transporter rates run $2.00–$3.50 per mile under 150 miles, $1.10–$1.80 at 500 miles, $0.60–$1.00 at 1,250, and $0.45–$0.80 beyond 2,500. Size multiplies the rate: roughly 0.90× for a pet under 20 pounds, 1.00 for 20–50, 1.15 for 50–80, and 1.30 for the big ones, because crate size eats van capacity.

Run the numbers on the classic case — a 65-pound dog moving 1,250 miles: 1,250 × $0.60 × 1.15 = $862.50 at the low end; 1,250 × $1.00 × 1.15 = $1,437.50 at the top. Coast-to-coast for the same dog lands between $1,294 and $2,300. A private, door-to-door run where your pet is the only stop doubles that: expect roughly $1,725 to $3,306 for the 1,250-mile move.

Flying: cabin vs cargo

In-cabin is the bargain of the industry if your pet qualifies: under about 20 pounds, inside a soft carrier that fits under the seat, booked in advance because airlines cap pet slots per flight. At $95 to $150 each way, a small dog crosses the country for less than the gas would cost to drive it.

Cargo is different machinery. Your pet flies in an IATA-compliant hard crate in a pressurized hold, usually arranged through a professional shipper for anything complicated. Domestic cargo runs roughly $500–$900 for a small pet, $700–$1,300 medium, $900–$2,000 large, and $1,200–$2,500 extra-large, higher with full-service shippers, with route driving as much of the number as size. Two hard limits: most airlines ban flat-faced breeds from cargo outright, and summer heat embargoes close hot-weather routes. Hawaii and international moves require cargo regardless of size.

The paperwork and equipment bill

  • Health certificate (CVI): $100–$300 from a USDA-accredited vet, valid 10–30 days depending on the state; airlines use the 10-day window.
  • Travel crate: $50–$220 depending on size; air cargo requires IATA-compliant hardware, which ground transport doesn't.
  • Rabies and vaccination records: usually already in your file; ask your vet for a stamped copy.

Why quotes vary so wildly

Pet transport shares its worst habit with auto transport: it's a lead-generation vertical. Many "get a quote" forms exist to sell your contact information to a stack of transporters, and the lowest opening number routinely excludes the crate, paperwork, or door delivery, or assumes your pet joins a route that hasn't been built yet. The price corrects the week of pickup, when you've run out of time to re-shop. The defense is procedural: three to five itemized quotes, USDA registration checked for ground transporters, IPATA membership checked for air, no large deposit before the route and driver are confirmed, and payment tied to pickup.

How to choose without overpaying

Default to shared ground with flexible dates booked two to four weeks out; it moves the overwhelming majority of US pets safely and costs half the alternatives. Upgrade to private only for genuinely anxious, senior, or medical-needs pets. Fly in-cabin whenever the pet is small enough and you're flying anyway. Reserve cargo for size, Hawaii, or international. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest way to move a pet long distance?

For pets under about 20 pounds, flying in-cabin with you: $95 to $150 each way on major US airlines. For bigger pets, shared ground transport booked two to four weeks ahead on a popular route: $0.50 to $1.60 per mile, which puts a 1,250-mile move for a large dog at roughly $863 to $1,438. Flexibility is the discount; last-minute dedicated runs are the premium.

How much does it cost to fly a large dog in cargo?

Domestic air cargo for a large dog typically runs $900 to $2,000 arranged through a shipper, with extra-large dogs $1,200 to $2,500, before the IATA crate ($50 to $220) and health certificate ($100 to $300). Route matters as much as size, and summer heat embargoes can close the option entirely on some routes.

Can brachycephalic breeds fly?

Usually not in cargo. French Bulldogs, English Bulldogs, Boxers, Pugs, and Persian cats are banned from cargo holds by most airlines because their airways make heat and stress dangerous. Flat-faced pets move by ground transport; some airlines allow small brachycephalic breeds in the cabin with a veterinary letter.

What paperwork does a pet need to cross state lines?

A certificate of veterinary inspection (health certificate) issued by a USDA-accredited vet, typically valid 10 to 30 days depending on the state, plus current rabies vaccination records. Airlines add IATA-compliant crate requirements and their own health-certificate windows. Budget $100 to $300 for the CVI and plan the vet visit within the validity window of your travel date.

Why did my cheap pet transport quote double?

Because pet transport is a lead-generation vertical: many quote forms sell your contact information to transporters, and the opening number often excludes crate, paperwork, or door delivery, or assumes your pet joins a route that is not yet built. The market then corrects the price the week of pickup. Collect three to five itemized quotes, verify USDA registration or IPATA membership, and do not pay a large deposit before the route and driver are confirmed.
