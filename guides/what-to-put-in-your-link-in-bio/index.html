What to Put in Your Link in Bio

⏱️ 6 min readUpdated September 24, 2026

Your link in bio should hold three to six links, with the one thing you most want people to do right now at the top. That's usually your newest video or product, followed by the page that earns you money, an email signup, and your other active channels. Here's what each platform allows, what to include for different kinds of accounts, and how to word buttons so people actually tap them.

Why do you need a link-in-bio page at all?

Social apps want people to stay inside them, so they're stingy with clickable links. Captions on Instagram don't turn URLs into links. TikTok hides the website field on personal accounts until you hit a follower threshold. YouTube stopped making links clickable in Shorts descriptions in 2023. The one place every platform reliably lets you link out is your profile.

A link-in-bio page turns that one link into a menu. It's a single mobile page with your name, a line about what you do, and a short stack of buttons. You put its URL in every profile, and when something changes you update the page once instead of editing five apps.

What should you put in your link in bio?

Fewer things than you think. A page with 15 buttons makes visitors read, and most of them won't. Aim for three to six links, ordered by what you most want someone to do right now:

  1. Your one current priority. The new video, this week's product drop, the webinar signup, the booking page. It goes first and it can look different from the rest.
  2. The thing that makes you money. Your shop, services page, or paid community. If it's already #1, move on.
  3. A way to keep in touch off-platform. An email list signup is the usual choice. Followers are rented; email addresses are yours.
  4. Your other main channels. One button each, only for platforms where you actually post.
  5. Contact or FAQ. Especially if you keep getting the same DMs about pricing, sizing, or collabs.

How should you word the buttons?

Say what happens when someone taps. "Watch the new episode" beats "YouTube." "Book a free 15-minute call" beats "Contact." "Shop the fall drop" beats "Store." A verb plus the outcome tells people why they'd bother, and it quietly filters out taps from people who were never going to buy.

Keep labels short enough to fit on one line on a phone, roughly 25 to 30 characters. If a button needs a sentence to explain it, it probably shouldn't be on the page.

What makes a link page look trustworthy?

People land on your page from an app, usually on a phone, often with a slow connection. A few small things decide whether they stay:

  • A face or logo up top, matching your social profile picture, so they know they're in the right place.
  • Your name and one line that says what you do. "Weeknight recipes under 30 minutes" is more useful than an inspirational quote.
  • Fast loading. A plain page with a few buttons should appear almost instantly. Autoplay video and heavy background images slow it down on mobile data.
  • No dead links. A sold-out product or last spring's giveaway makes the whole page look abandoned. Check it every time you post something new.
  • A readable URL. A custom domain like yourname.com/links looks better on a business card than a long string, and you won't lose it if you switch tools.

How do you know if your link page is working?

Look at taps, not just visits. Most hosted tools show clicks per button, and on your own site you can add UTM tags to each link so your analytics shows where traffic came from. If the top button isn't getting the most taps, the label or the order is the problem, not the audience.

The bottom line

Put your single most important link first, follow it with the one that pays you and a way to reach people off-platform, and stop at around six buttons. Use verbs on every label, keep the page fast, and prune dead links whenever you post something new.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I put in my link in bio?

Three to six links, in priority order: your current top priority (a new video, product, or booking page), the page that makes you money, an email signup, your other active channels, and a contact or FAQ link.

How many links can you have in an Instagram bio?

Up to five. Only the first appears on your profile; the others show when someone taps it, which is why many people use one link pointing to a link-in-bio page.

Why can't I add a link to my TikTok bio?

Personal TikTok accounts need 1,000 followers before the website field appears. Switching to a business account unlocks it right away, but business accounts only get the Commercial Music Library.

Is a link-in-bio page better than a website?

It does a different job. A link page is a quick menu for social visitors on phones, while a website holds deeper content. Many people host their link page on their own website domain to get both.

How often should I update my link in bio?

Whenever you publish or launch something new, and at least check it monthly. Remove sold-out products, expired offers and old giveaways so the page doesn't look abandoned.
