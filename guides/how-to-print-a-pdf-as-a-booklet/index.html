How to Print a PDF as a Booklet, With or Without Acrobat

📄 6 min readUpdated October 27, 2026

To print a PDF as a booklet, open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader, choose Booklet in the print dialog, set it to print both sides with left binding, then fold and staple. Without Acrobat, put the pages in booklet order first (for 8 pages that's 8, 1, 2, 7, 6, 3, 4, 5) and print 2 per sheet, double-sided, flipped on the short edge. Below are the orders for 8 to 24 pages, plus how small the text will get.

What does printing a PDF as a booklet actually do?

It puts two pages side by side on each side of a landscape sheet, in an order that only makes sense once you fold. Stack the printed sheets, fold them down the middle, staple the spine, and the pages read 1, 2, 3 in order. Each sheet of paper carries four pages, so a 16-page PDF needs 4 sheets.

The order is the clever part. For an 8-page booklet, it looks like this:

SheetFront (left | right)Back (left | right)
Sheet 18 | 12 | 7
Sheet 26 | 34 | 5

Page 8 sits next to page 1 because they're the back and front covers of the same outer sheet. Every page pair on a sheet adds up to the page count plus one (8 + 1, 2 + 7, 6 + 3, 4 + 5), which is a handy way to check a layout.

How do I print a booklet in Adobe Acrobat Reader?

Acrobat Reader is free and has a proper booklet mode, so it's the easiest route on Windows or Mac:

  1. Open the PDF and press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac).
  2. Under Page Sizing & Handling, click Booklet.
  3. Set Booklet subset to Both sides if your printer prints double-sided by itself. If it doesn't, print Front side only, put the stack back in the tray, then print Back side only.
  4. Leave the Sheets from boxes alone. Reader works out the sheets for you.
  5. Pick a Binding. Left is normal for English. Right is for right-to-left languages, and the Tall options bind along the long edge of the page.
  6. Print one sheet as a test, fold it, and check the page numbers before you print the rest.

If your page count isn't a multiple of 4, Reader fills the gap with blank pages, so you don't need to pad the file yourself.

How do I make a booklet without Acrobat?

Chrome, Edge and macOS Preview don't have a booklet setting, but you can get the same result by reordering the pages first and then printing two per sheet. It takes three steps:

  1. Pad to a multiple of 4. If you need blank pages, make a one-page blank PDF (print an empty document to PDF) and add as many copies as you need to the end with Merge PDF.
  2. Put the pages in booklet order. Paste the sequence from the table below into Extract PDF Pages with "Keep document order" unticked. It builds a new PDF in exactly the order you type, in your browser.
  3. Print 2 pages per sheet, double-sided, flipped on the short edge. In Chrome that's under More settings: Pages per sheet 2, then Print on both sides with Flip on short edge. Set Scale to Fit to printable area.

Flipping on the short edge matters. A landscape sheet with two pages on it gets turned over left to right, which is the short edge of the paper. Flip on the long edge and every back side prints upside down. Print one sheet first to check, because some printer drivers name the options differently.

How small will the text get?

Each page shrinks to fit half a sheet. A Letter page printed as a Letter-size booklet comes out at most 64.7% of its original size, so 12-point text prints at about 7.8 points. That's readable, but small for a long document. Printing on a bigger sheet fixes it:

Those are the best-case numbers. Printers can't print to the very edge, so the real scale is usually a few percent smaller. If you're designing the document yourself, set it up at the finished size (5.5 × 8.5 in for a Letter booklet, A5 for an A4 booklet) and nothing has to shrink at all.

Why are my booklet pages in the wrong order?

Three things cause almost every mixed-up booklet:

  • The page count isn't a multiple of 4. In the manual method, a 10-page PDF without 2 blanks will shift every page. Acrobat handles this, other tools don't.
  • Wrong flip edge. Long-edge flipping turns every back side upside down.
  • Reprinting from the middle. If a sheet jams, reprint that whole sheet, both sides, not a page range. In Acrobat, use the Sheets from boxes; for a 16-page booklet sheet 2 carries pages 3, 4, 13 and 14.

For thick booklets, split into groups of a few sheets each and print each group as its own booklet. Past a dozen or so sheets, the inner pages stick out at the fold and won't line up when you trim. If you're numbering pages before you impose them, add numbers first with PDF page numbers; once the pages are reordered, the numbers would come out in booklet order instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I print a PDF as a booklet?

In Adobe Acrobat Reader, open the print dialog, choose Booklet under Page Sizing & Handling, set Booklet subset to Both sides, pick Left binding and print. Then fold the stack in half and staple the spine.

Why does a booklet need a multiple of 4 pages?

Each sheet of paper holds four booklet pages, two on each side. A 10-page PDF needs 12 page slots on 3 sheets, so 2 pages end up blank. Acrobat adds them automatically; if you reorder pages yourself, add them first.

Should I flip on the long edge or short edge for a booklet?

Short edge. Booklet pages sit side by side on a landscape sheet, and the sheet turns over along its short edge. Flipping on the long edge prints every back side upside down.

Can I print a booklet from Chrome?

Not directly, because Chrome has no booklet setting. Reorder the pages into booklet order first (for 8 pages: 8,1,2,7,6,3,4,5), then print 2 pages per sheet, double-sided, flipped on the short edge.

How many pages can I fold into one booklet?

There's no hard limit, but past a dozen or so sheets (about 48 pages) the inner sheets push out at the edge and the booklet won't close flat. For longer documents, print several smaller booklets and bind them together.
