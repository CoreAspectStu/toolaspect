How Much Does Drywall Installation Cost?

⏱️ 7 min read

Drywall installation costs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot for a full hang, tape, and finish — most jobs land near $2. In room terms, that's roughly $400 to $650 for a small bathroom, $900 to $1,500 for a standard room or two, and $8,000 to $30,000 all-in for a 2,000-square-foot house. The three levers that move your quote: finish level, ceilings, and who buys the board. Here's the full breakdown.

How much does drywall installation cost per square foot?

Contractors quote drywall by the square foot of board installed, and the 2026 bands look like this:

ScopePrice per sq ftNotes
Full install, Level 4 (paint-ready)$1.50-$3.50Most common quote; national average near $2-$2.50
Labor only (hang + finish)$1.75-$3.75Materials excluded — you buy the board
Hang only$0.65-$1.90Board up, nothing finished
Tape and finish only (from Level 1)$1.15-$2.15Existing board, joints closed up

Ceilings run 10-20% over walls — overhead cutting, lifting, and a sag-resistant board requirement in many codes. Garages and fire-rated assemblies need 5/8-inch Type X board, which prices above half-inch. And a Level 5 skim coat, the glass-smooth finish that shows no flaws under raking light, adds meaningfully to the finish line.

Quotes swing thousands between contractors on identical plans, which is why three bids minimum is the standing advice — the labor line is where the money hides.

What do drywall materials cost?

Board runs $12 to $20 per standard 4x8 half-inch panel, with a national average near $15. The other line items are cheap but they add up on big jobs:

  • Joint compound: about 0.05 lb per square foot of board — a 20 lb box covers roughly 400 sq ft of hung wall. Budget $15-$25 per box.
  • Screws: about one per square foot at a 12-inch field schedule; a 1-lb box holds roughly 300.
  • Tape and corner bead: a 500-ft roll of paper tape covers about 16 sheets' worth of seams; bead runs one length per corner plus 10%.

For a 12x12 room, all-in materials sit near $260-$350 — $210 in board plus $50-$100 in consumables. The drywall calculator converts any measured area into sheets, mud pounds, screw counts, and a board cost in one step.

What are drywall finish levels — and why do they change the price?

The industry grades finish from Level 0 to Level 5, and every extra level is labor someone has to pay for:

LevelWhat it meansWhere it's used
0No taping — board fastened onlyTemporary construction
1Tape embedded in joint compound, no extra coatsAttics, plenums, concealed areas
2One coat over tape and fastener headsGarages, storage, behind tile
3Two coats — ready for heavy textureTexture-covered walls
4Three coats, flat paint or light textureStandard residential finish
5Full skim coat over everythingGloss paint, severe lighting, commercial

Most residential quotes assume Level 4. If you plan glossy paint or have wall-washing light that highlights every imperfection, Level 5 is the insurance policy — expect it priced as an adder per square foot rather than a new line item.

How many sheets will your project need?

Quick check with a 12x12 room, 8-foot ceiling: four walls at 96 square feet each is 384 square feet, plus 10% waste is 422, divided by a 32-square-foot 4x8 sheet rounds up to 14 sheets — about $210 of board at $15. Four-by-twelve sheets drop that to 9 sheets with fewer butt seams, at the cost of a heavy board that needs a second person or a lift. Ceilings deserve their own calculation at a 12-15% waste factor instead of 10, since overhead work breaks more board.

Should you hang drywall yourself or hire it out?

Hanging is honest work but learnable — measure twice, screw to the schedule, keep seams on studs. Finishing is the craft. Three coats of compound, feathered edges, and sanding dust settle every argument about who should do what: bad tape work telegraphs through paint forever, and a pro finisher works fast enough that the hybrid play (you hang, they finish) is common. Field planning numbers: roughly one labor hour per sheet to hang and about 1.5 per sheet to tape and finish — multiply your sheet count by local rates to sanity-check any quote before you sign it.

The bottom line

Figure $2 per square foot as the working number — $575-$1,350 for a room, $8,000-$15,000 for a standard whole house — then adjust for finish level and ceilings. Get three bids, confirm the finish level in writing, and check whether board is in the quote or on you. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to drywall a 12x12 room?

A 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has 384 square feet of wall. At $1.50-$3.50 per square foot installed, that is roughly $575-$1,350 for hang, tape, and finish, or about $210 in board if you hang it yourself (14 sheets at $15). Add the ceiling and the price climbs — overhead work runs 10-20% more.

How much does it cost to drywall a whole house?

A 2,000-square-foot house carries roughly 3,500-4,500 square feet of wall and ceiling board. HomeAdvisor brackets whole-house jobs at $8,000-$30,000; standard Level 4 finishes with normal ceiling heights usually land between about $8,000 and $15,000, with high ceilings, demo, and premium finishes pushing toward the top.

What is the labor cost to hang and finish drywall per square foot?

Labor alone typically runs $1.75-$3.75 per square foot for hanging plus finishing, depending on region and finish level. Breaking it down, hanging alone quotes near $0.65-$1.90 per square foot in many markets, and taping-only (Level 1) starts around $1.15-$2.15.

How much do drywall sheets cost?

Standard half-inch 4x8 panels run about $12-$20 each, with a national average near $15. Five-eighths-inch fire-rated board costs noticeably more, and 4x10 or 4x12 sheets carry a premium per sheet but cover more area with fewer seams.

Is it cheaper to drywall yourself?

Material-only costs are modest — about $210 in board for a 12x12 room plus $50-$100 in mud, tape, and screws. Hanging is within reach of a careful DIYer with a helper; finishing is where the skill lives, and bad tape work shows under paint forever. Many owners hang the board themselves and hire a finisher for the coats.
