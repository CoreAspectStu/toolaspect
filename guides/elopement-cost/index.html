How Much Does It Cost to Elope in 2026?

💍 ~12K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3⏱️ 8 min read

Eloping costs what you decide it costs — somewhere between a Tuesday at the courthouse and a helicopter on a ridge. The honest bands: $150-$2,500 for a bare elopement (license, officiant, dinner), $5,000-$10,000 for the typical full day with photography and celebration (The Knot's range), and more once travel enters. An itemized typical elopement totals about $2,260 — roughly 93% less than the $34,200 average US wedding. Here's the line-by-line math.

Courthouse, adventure, package, or destination?

The courthouse is the floor: a $25-$125 license, a $25-$100 civil ceremony fee where charged, and dinner after — $100-$400 all-in before attire and rings. Las Vegas is the same idea with better stories: chapel officiants start around $50-$100 and licenses are issued same-day.

The adventure elopement is the $2,260 middle — a state park, an officiant, two hours of photography, a permit, and a celebration dinner. It's the version most people picture now, and the one our calculator models by default.

The package hands the logistics to a photographer or planning company: officiant, 1-3 photography hours, florals, and permit help bundled at $1,500-$8,000. Worth comparing against roughly $1,350 of separately-booked typical-tier vendors — you're paying for coordination, and sometimes that's a bargain on a remote location.

The destination elopement adds travel and lodging, pushing past $7,000 at the top of the typical band. The same math applies, just with flights: keep the guest count at zero and even Iceland prices out under a mid-size hometown wedding.

Do you need a permit to elope on public land?

Often, yes, on the scenic spots — national parks and many state parks require a special-use permit for ceremonies, commonly $50-$200, applied for weeks ahead. Many national forests allow small ceremonies without one. Group size, arches, and setups change the answer, and rules differ parcel by parcel — check with the land manager, not a blog (including this one; it's general information, not legal advice). Who counts as a witness?

Most states require one or two adult witnesses. Your photographer and officiant do this constantly and rarely charge — a $20-$50 tip each is the norm if they step in. A handful of states require none at all, which is how the two-person elopement works legally.

Is eloping really that much cheaper than a wedding?

The arithmetic is lopsided because the expensive wedding lines are guest-driven: venue, catering at roughly $292 per guest, bar, rentals. Eloping deletes the guest list and keeps the vendors you'd actually miss. Even the marry-now-party-later version — private ceremony, big reception next year — comes out ahead, because a reception is one line to budget instead of a full ceremony-day stack. What shouldn't you cut?

  • The photographer. Two hours at $400-$800 is the floor for usable images of both of you; this is the line couples regret trimming.
  • The license logistics. Waiting periods, residency rules, and county clerk hours end more elopement plans than money does.
  • Weather margin. A backup spot and a flexible officiant save the day more often than any rental.

What you can cut with impunity: the "bridal" markup on attire (a non-bridal dress and an off-the-rack suit halve that line), printed anything, and flowers beyond one bouquet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to elope?

A bare courthouse or mountain-top elopement runs $150-$2,500 once the license, a modest officiant fee, and a celebration dinner are counted. A typical full elopement with photographer, officiant, and celebration lands in The Knot's $5,000-$10,000 range, and destination elopements with travel and lodging run above it. Against the $34,200 average US wedding (The Knot, 2025), an itemized typical elopement totals about $2,260 — roughly 93% less.

What is the cheapest way to elope?

A courthouse ceremony: a $25-$125 license (state-dependent), a $25-$100 civil ceremony fee where charged, and dinner after — $100-$400 all-in before attire and rings. Free public land (many national forests allow small ceremonies without a permit, or a $50-$200 special-use permit) keeps the location line near zero. The photographer is the line most couples refuse to cut, at $400-$800 for a short shoot.

What does an elopement package include?

Most packages bundle an officiant, a photographer for 1-3 hours, florals, and help with location permits; many add hair and makeup or a picnic dinner. Published bands run about $1,500-$8,000 depending on hours and destination. A package competes with roughly $1,350 of separately-booked typical-tier vendors (officiant, two-hour photographer, florals, location), so compare inclusions hour by hour.

Do you need witnesses to elope?

Most US states require one or two adult witnesses, though a handful require none. Elopement officiants, venues, and photographers routinely serve as witnesses for free or a small tip ($20-$50 each).
