How Much to Pay a Pet Sitter

🐾 7 min read📊 2025-26 rates

Pet sitting prices are refreshingly consistent nationally — and surprisingly stackable once you add a second pet, a holiday week, or a platform service fee. Here's what each service type actually costs, how the add-ons pile up, and how to tell whether the quote in front of you is fair.

What do the base rates look like?

Drop-in visit, 30 min$18 – $30$25 – $40
Drop-in visit, 60 min$28 – $45$35 – $60
Dog walk, 30 min$20 – $35$25 – $45
Overnight (sitter stays)$75 – $150$100 – $175
House sitting, light care$50 – $100$75 – $125

A drop-in is the workhorse: the sitter comes, feeds, waters, scoops or litters, gives meds if needed, brings in mail, and texts you a photo. The 60-minute tier adds real playtime. Overnights mean the sitter sleeps at your place, which pets with separation anxiety or medical needs do dramatically better with.

How do the add-ons stack?

Three multipliers matter, and they compound:

  • Extra pets: +$5-$10 per visit, per additional animal. The first pet is in the base rate.
  • Holidays: 1.5x on Rover's major-holiday dates; independents run 1.25x-2x.
  • Platform fees: Rover takes roughly a 20% service fee from sitters (25% in California) and an owner-side booking fee of around 11%.

Concrete stack: two 30-minute drop-ins a day, three days, two cats. Per visit: $18-$30 plus $5-$10 for the second cat = $23-$40. Per day: $46-$80. Weekend total: $138-$240. Book the same thing over Christmas at 1.5x: $207-$360. Through Rover with the service fee on top, that holiday booking plausibly clears $430 on the high end.

Is Rover, Wag, or a private sitter cheaper?

On paper, platform listings often look lower. In practice, the service fee eats the difference: a $22 drop-in on Rover costs the owner $26-$28 all-in, right at the top of the private-sitter range in most cities. What you're buying with the fee is background checks, a review history, the Rover Guarantee (insurance-ish coverage), and a replacement pipeline if a sitter cancels the night before your flight.

Private sitters — found through your vet, neighbors, or local Facebook groups — frequently charge the same or a bit less, keep every dollar, and can be more flexible with keys and last-minute changes. The trade is you do the vetting: ask for references, confirm they're insured if they're a business, and do a paid meet-and-greet before the trip. For recurring weekly work, both platforms and privates will usually settle at a flat weekly rate 10-15% under per-visit pricing — it's worth asking.

Do cats really cost less to sit?

Not on the rate card — a drop-in is a drop-in — but cats need fewer of them. Most cats are fine with one visit a day (fresh food, water, scooped litter), so a cat weekend runs $18-$30 a day while a dog needing three visits and a walk runs $75-$100. Over a week, that's $126-$210 for the cat versus $525-$700 for the dog. That's also why cat owners overpay for boarding: a kennel charges $25-$45 a night for what a drop-in does for less, with none of the stress of hauling the cat anywhere. The same logic applies to the cat food calculator world generally — cats are the budget pet almost everywhere except the vet.

Exotics (birds, reptiles, small mammals) go the other way: fewer sitters will take them, and many charge a $5-$15 flat surcharge per visit for specialized care like insulin shots, subcutaneous fluids, or live feeding. If your pet needs medical care, ask whether the sitter is a vet tech — many moonlight, and their rates are comparable despite the credentials.

What about tipping?

Standard practice: 15-20% of the booking, more at the holidays, in cash or through the app. On a $150 weekend that's $22-$30. Sitters remember good tippers and regulars — in a business where the best people are booked solid, that memory is worth real money to you next Thanksgiving.

How do I know if a quote is fair?

  1. Run it through the pet sitter rates calculator — if it's inside the band, it's fair.
  2. Outside the band isn't automatically a rip-off: medication, young puppies, multiple dogs, or genuinely hard jobs price up. Ask why rather than negotiating blind.
  3. Watch for the too-cheap quote ($10 drop-ins) — it signals someone new, someone unreliable, or someone who will cancel when a better booking appears.
  4. Everything recurring is negotiable; everything one-off mostly isn't.

Rates here are national ranges from 2025-26 listings and rate cards; your metro, pet count, and holiday calendar move the number. Get the agreed rate and scope in writing — even a text thread — before the first visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a fair rate for a pet sitter?

$18 to $30 for a 30-minute drop-in visit, $28 to $45 for an hour, $20 to $35 for a dog walk, and $75 to $150 per overnight stay in 2025-26. Add $5-$10 per extra pet per visit. Two drop-ins a day for a weekend with a second pet lands at $138-$240, which is the fair band most quotes should fall inside.

Is it cheaper to board a pet or hire a sitter?

For one pet, boarding ($40-$75 a night for dogs) often edges out a single daily drop-in, but sitting wins on stress and price multiplies with pets: a sitter charges $5-$10 per extra animal while boarding charges nearly full rate per animal. Cats almost always come out ahead with drop-ins, since boarding a cat runs $25-$45 a night for a pet that mostly needs a clean litter box and fresh food.

Why do pet sitters charge more around Christmas?

Supply and demand: everyone travels the same ten days of the year. Expect 1.5x on Rover for major holidays and 1.25x-2x from independents, and note that the best sitters book out 4-6 weeks ahead. Booking early costs nothing extra; booking late costs either the multiplier or whoever's left.
