How Much Does YouTube Pay Per View?

⏱️ 7 min read

YouTube pays through advertising revenue shared with creators, and there is no published per-view rate. What creators actually earn lands somewhere between about $1 and $20 per 1,000 views depending on niche and audience, which works out to roughly $0.001 to $0.02 per view. That spread is the whole story: the same view count pays ten times more in personal finance than in vlogs. Here is how the math works and how to estimate your own number.

How does YouTube pay creators?

Ads on your videos run through Google AdSense, and YouTube keeps 45 percent of long-form ad revenue while you keep 55 percent. The number that matters for planning is RPM, revenue per mille: your total ad revenue divided by your views in thousands. RPM is not a rate YouTube sets. It falls out of an auction among advertisers who bid on your audience, so it moves with niche, season, viewer geography, and how many of your views actually showed an ad.

Two things quietly shrink RPM below what newcomers expect. First, not every view is monetized: skippers, ad-blockers, and age-restricted placements earn nothing, and a 60 percent monetized-playback share is a reasonable planning figure for many channels. Second, geography dominates. A US-heavy audience clears advertiser rates several times higher than a global-average one; our RPM by country guide shows just how wide that gap runs.

How many views and subscribers do you need to get paid?

Before any money moves, you join the YouTube Partner Program. The standard door is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in the past 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. A lower tier at 500 subscribers and 3,000 watch hours unlocks fan funding, memberships, and Super Chat before full ad revenue.

Once you are in, AdSense pays monthly once your balance crosses $100. That threshold matters for small channels: at a $2 RPM, you need roughly 50,000 monetized views to see a deposit.

What is the difference between CPM and RPM?

CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what lands in your pocket per 1,000 video views, after YouTube's 45 percent share and after discounting the views that never showed an ad. RPM is always lower than CPM, and it is always the better planning number because it maps to the view count you can actually see on your channel.

What makes RPM rise or fall?

  • Audience geography. Advertisers pay by market; where your viewers live sets your ceiling.
  • Video length. Videos over eight minutes can carry mid-roll ads, which typically lift revenue per view without adding a single viewer.
  • Season. Q4 holiday budgets reliably raise RPMs; January reliably drops them. Budget on your worst quarter, not your average.
  • Monetized share. Skips, ad-blockers, and limited-ads placements all earn zero. Content that keeps advertisers comfortable keeps the share high.

One more line item people forget: YouTube Premium. Subscribers pay a monthly fee that YouTube pools and distributes by watch time, so Premium minutes show up in your revenue report at pooled rates rather than auction rates.

How much do YouTubers make a month?

Run the arithmetic on a mid-size channel at 100,000 monthly views. Entertainment at $2 RPM: about $200 a month from ads. Finance at $8: $800. Same effort, different audience value.

But ads are usually less than half of a working creator's income. Channel memberships split roughly 70/30 in the creator's favor on a $4.99 tier, Super Chat and Super Thanks add live revenue, affiliate links and sponsorships scale with trust rather than view count, and products tend to out-earn everything else once an audience is loyal. A channel earning $500 a month from ads might clear $2,000 all-in once the non-ad streams are working.

The bottom line

YouTube pays about $1 to $20 per 1,000 views, or $0.001 to $0.02 per view, with the niche and the audience's geography deciding where in that band you land. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views?

There is no fixed rate. Creator-reported RPMs run from about $1-$3 per 1,000 views in entertainment and gaming to $5-$15 in finance and $8-$20+ in insurance and legal topics. Your own trailing 90-day RPM in YouTube Studio is the only number that applies to your channel.

How much money is 1 million views on YouTube?

Roughly $1,000 at a $1 RPM to $15,000 or more at a $15 RPM, from ads alone. A million entertainment views might pay $1,000-$3,000; a million finance views $5,000-$15,000. Sponsorships, affiliates, and memberships sit on top of that.

How many views do you need to get paid on YouTube?

Join the YouTube Partner Program first: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. AdSense then pays out once your balance crosses $100.

What is the difference between CPM and RPM?

CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what you earn per 1,000 video views after YouTube's 45 percent cut and after views that showed no ad at all. RPM is always the more useful number for estimating income.

Does YouTube pay for Shorts?

Yes, but through a separate pooled revenue model rather than the long-form ad auction, and creators consistently report Shorts RPMs well under $1 per 1,000 views. Shorts work best as discovery that feeds long-form viewing.
