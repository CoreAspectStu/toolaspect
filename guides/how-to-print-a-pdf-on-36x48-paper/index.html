How to Print a PDF at 36 x 48 (Arch E)

📄 6 min readUpdated November 6, 2026

A 36 × 48 inch sheet is Arch E, and in a PDF it's 2592 × 3456 points. If your file's already that size, print it at 100%. If it isn't, here are measured scales from every common page size, the sizes shops confuse it with, tile counts and pixel needs.

Is 36 x 48 the same as A0 or E size?

Not quite, and the differences are big enough to matter. Arch E is the architectural series' largest standard sheet. ANSI E is the engineering one, two inches smaller across and four inches shorter. A0 is the metric sheet the rest of the world uses, and it's a little narrower and longer than either.

"E size" on its own can mean Arch E or ANSI E, so give the shop inches. Arch E1, 30 × 42, is a common in-between size for drawings. Arch E is also exactly two Arch D sheets side by side, which matters if you're tiling. Going down, Arch E to Arch C at exactly 50% is the one clean half-size reduction for a scaled drawing. Arch C is the only common size that fills the sheet with nothing left over, since it's the same 3:4 shape. Everything else leaves a strip. Tabloid and ANSI D leave almost five inches blank. If you'd rather fill the sheet, enlarge until the short side fits and trim the rest. A tabloid page filling 36 × 48 needs 327.3% and loses 7.64 inches of length. Will my slides or photos fit the shape?

Arch E is 3:4, the same shape as an old 4:3 slide. That's handy for a poster built in PowerPoint or Keynote, if the slide was set to 4:3.

SourceScale to 36 × 48Unused space
4:3 slide (10 × 7.5 in)480%None, it fills the sheet
16:9 slide (13.33 × 7.5 in)360%9 in on the short side (48 × 27 printed)
2:3 photo or poster133.3%4 in on the short side (32 × 48 printed)
Letter flyer423.5%1.41 in on the long side

Widescreen slides are the common mistake. A 16:9 slide fits at only 360%, and it prints 48 × 27 with a 9-inch band of blank paper. Change the slide size to 36 × 48 inches before you lay it out, not after, since resizing later squashes or shifts the content. Photos and posters at 2:3 lose four inches the other way. If the sheet's landscape and the file's portrait, fix it with Rotate PDF first, or the shop may print it small in the middle.

Can you print 36 x 48 at home?

Only in pieces, unless you've got a printer that takes a 36-inch roll. We worked out tile counts with a quarter-inch margin on each edge and no overlap, trying both orientations:

Overlap for trimming can add a row. Two Arch D prints make a clean Arch E only if they're truly borderless, which is rare outside a shop. Tiled prints are fine for checking a layout, not for measuring. Our guide to printing a poster across several pages covers the settings.

How many pixels does a 36 x 48 print need?

More than most phones produce. Here are the pixel counts at a few common print resolutions, and how big each would be as an uncompressed 8-bit RGB image:

PPIPixelsMegapixelsUncompressed RGB

As a rule of thumb, 100 to 150 PPI looks fine from a few feet away, which is how most people see a poster, and 300 is for close inspection. A 12-megapixel phone photo is about 4,000 × 3,000 pixels, so it lands near 83 PPI on the 48-inch side. Vector text and linework stay sharp at any size.

JPEG compression makes real files far smaller. If yours is still too big to send, Compress PDF can help, but check the result, since it can soften photos.

What should you ask the print shop?

  • Arch E or ANSI E? Say 36 × 48 inches, and confirm portrait or landscape.
  • Which roll? Wide-format printers commonly take 36, 42 or 44 inch rolls. A 36-inch roll can only reach the full width if the printer can print edge to edge, so ask whether they'll use a wider roll and trim.
  • Bleed. For color to the edge, a common request is 1/8 inch a side, making the file 36.25 × 48.25 inches. Ask what they want.
  • Paper. Plans usually go on bond and posters on poster stock. We haven't checked any shop's prices or stock.
  • Which pages. Send only the sheets you need. Extract PDF Pages pulls them out of a full set.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size is Arch E paper?

Arch E is 36 × 48 inches, about 914 × 1219 mm. In a PDF it's 2592 × 3456 points. It isn't the same as ANSI E, which is 34 × 44 inches, or A0, which is 841 × 1189 mm.

How much do I enlarge a Letter PDF to get 36 x 48?

Use 423.5%. That fills the 36-inch width and leaves 1.41 inches blank on the long side. Text at 12 pt prints at about 50.8 pt.

Can I print an A0 PDF on 36 x 48?

Yes. At Fit it scales to 102.5% and leaves about 2 inches unused across the width. At 100% it prints true size with a border of blank paper, which is what you want if someone will measure it.

Why does my 16:9 poster have a blank band?

A 16:9 slide only reaches 360% on a 36 × 48 sheet, so it prints 48 × 27 inches and leaves 9 inches blank. Set the slide size to 36 × 48 before you design, or use a 4:3 slide, which fills it exactly.

What resolution does a 36 x 48 poster need?

As a rule of thumb, 150 PPI is plenty for viewing from a few feet, which is 5,400 × 7,200 pixels. A 12-megapixel photo gives about 83 PPI at this size, fine from across a room but soft up close.
