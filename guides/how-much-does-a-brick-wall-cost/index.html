How Much Does a Brick Wall Cost?

⏱️ 6 min read

A cube of face brick runs $350 to $900 per thousand, the labor to lay it adds $300 to $800 per thousand more, and a finished wall lands somewhere between $10 and $45 per square foot once scaffold, ties, and mortar are in the bill. A homeowner-scale garden wall can come in under that; a full veneer job on a house runs higher. Here's the piece-by-piece math so you can sanity-check any quote.

How much does brick cost per square foot?

Brick is priced per thousand, but walls are measured in square feet, so the conversion is what matters. A modular brick bedded with a ⅜ inch joint covers enough layout for 6.86 bricks per square foot, which means the per-1,000 price translates like this:

Price per 1,000Material cost per sq ftWhere you land
$350$2.40Economy modular, picked up at the yard
$500$3.43Mid-range face brick, the common case
$900$6.17Architectural and specialty colors
Glazed / oversizedup to ~$15Wider and specialty shapes command a premium

Current cost guides put standard face brick materials at roughly $2.50 to $6.25 per square foot, which is the same math approached from the other direction. Anything quoted outside that band deserves a question: below it, you're probably looking at concrete brick or a seconds grade; above it, you're paying for color or texture, not structure.

What does the labor to lay it cost?

Masons price by the thousand too. Expect $300 to $800 per 1,000 bricks laid on straightforward work, before the extras. On a per-square-foot basis that's another $2 to $5.50 of labor against 6.86 bricks per square foot, stacked on top of materials.

Finished-wall quotes bracket the whole job. Basic brick walls run $10 to $45 per square foot installed depending on region and complexity, and Homewyse's mid-2026 numbers for a full basic veneer installation sit at roughly $38 to $51 per square foot — that higher figure is a real wall on a real house: scaffold, flashing, ties, corners, and joint tooling, not just brick stacked on a slab. If you want the look for less, thin brick veneer installs at $8 to $18 per square foot because there's no structural masonry behind it.

What moves a labor quote: wall height (scaffold instead of a step ladder), openings (every window adds a lintel and cut bricks), arches and radiused work, and site access. A four-foot garden wall you can reach over is the cheap end of the curve. A two-story gable with scaffolding is not.

How many bricks and bags of mortar does a wall need?

The count comes from one formula: bricks per square foot = 144 ÷ (bedded width × bedded height), using the face dimensions plus the mortar joint. For modular brick bedded at 8 × 2⅝ inches, that's 144 ÷ 21 = 6.86. Multiply by wall area, add 10 percent for cuts and blending, and figure roughly 37 bricks per 80 lb bag of premix mortar. The installed price of that same wall at $38-$51 per square foot is $19,000 to $25,500 — labor, scaffold, flashing, lintels, and everything the mason brings. That gap is why brick is priced by the square foot installed, not by the cube.

Does a bigger brick like a King size cost less overall?

Somewhat, but not in the way the sticker suggests. King and Queen brick cost more per thousand, but they cover more wall per piece:

Brick sizeBedded face (⅜ in joint)Bricks per sq ft
Modular8 × 2⅝ in6.86
Queen8 × 3⅛ in5.76
King10 × 3⅛ in4.61

Run the totals and material cost usually lands within a few percent either way — fewer bricks, each one pricier. The real difference is joints. A King wall has a third fewer joints to fill and tool than a modular wall, and joint tooling is where masons spend their day. On a big job that shows up in the labor line. One caution: King and Queen don't lay to the same module as standard lintels and coursing, so confirm the whole wall system is designed around the brick you picked, not just the price.

What else belongs in a brick wall budget?

The brick and the mason are the big numbers, but a veneer wall carries a supporting cast that adds real money on a house job:

  • Ties and anchorage. Veneer over wood framing needs corrugated wall ties, typically every 16 inches vertically and 24 horizontally — cheap parts, expensive to retrofit.
  • Flashing and weeps. Through-wall flashing above openings and weep holes at the base keep water out of the wall cavity. Skipping them is how brick walls rot the frame behind them.
  • Lintels. Every door and window opening needs steel support. Each one is a fabrication line item, not a detail.
  • Mortar type. Type N for veneer and above-grade work, Type S where higher strength is specified — same bag count, verify which one the spec calls for.
  • Delivery and pallet deposits. Brick arrives on pallets by the cube; yards typically want the pallets back.
  • Waste. 5-10 percent standard, 15 percent for curved work where every brick is a tapered cut.

The bottom line

Budget $350-$900 per thousand for brick, $300-$800 per thousand to lay it, and $10-$45 per square foot for the finished wall — plan around $38-$51 if it's full veneer on a house. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a brick wall cost per square foot installed?

Finished brick walls run $10-$45 per square foot installed, and a full veneer job runs higher — Homewyse pegs basic brick veneer at roughly $38-$51 per square foot in mid-2026. Materials alone work out to $2.40-$6.17 per square foot at typical $350-$900 per-1,000 brick prices.

How many bricks are in a square foot of wall?

A modular brick bedded with a ⅜ inch joint lays at 144 ÷ 21 = 6.86 per square foot, so order 7 per square foot before waste. Queen size runs about 5.7 and King about 4.6 per square foot.

How many bricks do I need for a 100 sq ft wall?

About 686 modular bricks before waste, so 755 with a 10 percent allowance, plus 19-20 bags of 80 lb mortar at roughly 37 bricks per bag. At $500 per 1,000 that is about $378 of brick.

How much waste should I order for a brick job?

5-10 percent is standard, covering cuts, breakage, and blending between cubes. Push to 10 percent on walls with lots of openings, and 15 percent is not unreasonable for curved garden walls where every piece tapers.

Does a bigger brick like a King size cost less overall?

Usually a wash on materials — King brick costs more per 1,000 but you need only 4.6 per square foot instead of 6.9, so the totals come out close. The real saving is labor: fewer bricks to lay and fewer joints to tool.
