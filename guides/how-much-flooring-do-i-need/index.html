How Much Flooring Do I Need? Square Feet and Boxes

🧮 6 min readUpdated September 28, 2026

To work out flooring, multiply the room's length by its width, add 10% for waste, and divide by the square feet in one box. A 15 × 12 ft bedroom is 180 sq ft. With 10% waste you buy 198 sq ft, and at 23.8 sq ft per box that's 8.3 boxes, so you need 9.

How do you measure a room for flooring?

Flooring is sold by the square foot, so you need the room's floor area. For a plain rectangle, that's length × width. A 15 × 12 ft bedroom is 180 sq ft.

Most rooms aren't perfect rectangles, and that's fine. A few habits keep the measurement honest:

  • Measure wall to wall at the widest point. Round each measurement up to the next half foot. The cushion is small and it's on the safe side.
  • Split odd shapes into rectangles. An L-shaped living room might be a 20 × 14 section plus a 10 × 8 nook. That's 280 + 80 = 360 sq ft. Add up the pieces.
  • Include closets and doorways. If the new floor runs into the closet or under a door to a transition strip, those square feet count.
  • Don't subtract fixed cabinets or a kitchen island unless they're large. The cut pieces around them eat most of the saving anyway.

If you already know the square footage from a floor plan, type it straight into our flooring calculator. It will skip the length and width.

How much extra flooring should you buy?

You'll lose material to end cuts, pieces that don't fit the pattern and the odd damaged plank. The standard fix is a waste allowance added on top of the room's area.

Material and layoutCommon waste allowance
Vinyl plank or laminate, straight lay, simple room5% to 10%
Hardwood or engineered wood, straight lay10%
Any plank in a room with lots of angles, closets or hallways10% to 15%
Diagonal layout15%
Herringbone or chevron15% to 20%

These are working rules of thumb, and the manufacturer's installation guide may say otherwise for a specific product. The calculator defaults to 10%, which suits most straight-lay jobs. How many boxes of flooring do you need?

Divide the square footage you're buying by the coverage on the box, then round up.

Boxes = room area × (1 + waste) ÷ square feet per box

For the 180 sq ft bedroom at 10% waste: 180 × 1.10 = 198 sq ft, and 198 ÷ 23.8 = 8.3, so you buy 9 boxes. Coverage per box varies a lot. It depends on the plank's width and length and on how thick it is, so always use the number printed on the box you're buying, not a typical figure.

Do you need underlayment?

It depends on the product. Laminate usually needs a foam underlayment unless the planks have a pad attached. A lot of vinyl plank comes with the pad already attached, and many makers say not to add a second layer under it. Doubling up can make the locking joints flex and fail.

Over concrete, a moisture barrier is usually required for wood and laminate. Some underlayments have one built in. Read the installation guide before you buy, because using the wrong underlayment can void the warranty.

If you do need it, underlayment covers the same area you're buying flooring for. In the calculator, untick the underlayment box if your planks already have a pad.

Do you need to leave a gap around the edges?

For floating floors, yes. Laminate, most vinyl plank and floating engineered wood need an expansion gap around the edge of the room, commonly about ¼ to ⅜ in, so the floor can move with changes in temperature and humidity. Baseboard or quarter-round covers it.

The gap doesn't change how much flooring you buy. It does mean you shouldn't cut planks tight to the wall, and it's one reason to leave the baseboards off until the floor is down. If you're painting the room while it's empty, do the walls first. The paint calculator covers gallons by room size.

Some products also need time to acclimate in the room before you install them. Solid wood and laminate usually do. Many rigid-core vinyl planks need little or none. Check the box.

What about tile or a bathroom?

Tile works differently. You count individual tiles, and you also need thinset and grout. Use the tile calculator for bathrooms, entryways or anywhere you're laying tile instead of planks.

Why keep a spare box?

Flooring styles are discontinued all the time, and new production runs can be a slightly different color. Keep at least one unopened box in the garage after the job. If a plank gets gouged or water-damaged, you can swap it for a perfect match.

The bottom line

Multiply length by width, add 10% for a straight lay (15% or more for diagonal and herringbone), and divide by the square feet on the box. Round up, buy from one lot, and hold one box back for repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much flooring do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 × 12 room is 144 sq ft. With 10% waste you should buy about 158 sq ft. At 23.8 sq ft per box that's 6.7, so 7 boxes. Use the coverage printed on your own box, since it varies by product.

How much extra flooring should I buy?

Buy 5% to 10% extra for vinyl plank or laminate in a simple room, about 10% for hardwood, 10% to 15% for rooms with lots of angles, 15% for diagonal and 15% to 20% for herringbone.

How do I calculate flooring for an L-shaped room?

Split the room into rectangles, work out length × width for each and add them together. An L-shaped room made of a 20 × 14 section and a 10 × 8 nook is 280 + 80 = 360 sq ft. Then add your waste allowance.

How many square feet are in a box of flooring?

It depends on the plank size and thickness. Many vinyl plank and laminate boxes cover roughly 15 to 25 sq ft, but some are much smaller or larger. Always use the figure printed on the box you're buying.

Do I need underlayment for vinyl plank flooring?

Often not. Many vinyl planks come with a pad already attached, and manufacturers usually say not to add another layer on top. Planks without a pad, and laminate, usually need one. Check the installation guide for your product.
