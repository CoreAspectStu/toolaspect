Inheriting Money While on SSI: What Happens and What to Do

📄 6 min readUpdated October 27, 2026

An inheritance counts as SSI income in the month it becomes available to you, minus any funeral or last-illness costs you paid. Whatever's left on the 1st of the next month counts toward the $2,000 asset limit. You can protect your SSI by spending it down at fair prices or moving it into an ABLE account or a special needs trust. Refusing it or giving it away triggers a penalty of up to 36 months.

Does an inheritance count as income for SSI?

Yes. SSA treats an inheritance as a death benefit, a type of unearned income. It counts in the month it actually becomes yours to use, not the day the person died. While the estate is tied up in probate and you can't touch the money, it isn't income or a resource. If it's unclear when you got it, SSA uses the earlier of the date you say you received it or the date the estate closed.

A big inheritance almost always means no SSI for that month: $1,014 or more of unearned income wipes out the 2026 maximum of $994, since SSA ignores only the first $20. And because SSA usually figures each check from your income two months earlier, the hit can land on a later payment too.

Do funeral costs reduce what counts?

They do, and this one's easy to miss. A death benefit only counts as income to the extent it's more than what you paid toward the person's last illness and burial. That covers the funeral, the burial plot and interment, related hospital and medical bills, and smaller things SSA's own manual lists, like new clothes for the funeral, food for visiting relatives and taxi fare to the hospital or funeral home.

Say your mother leaves you $30,000 and you pay $4,000 of her funeral bill. SSA counts $26,000, not $30,000. Keep the receipts.

What happens the month after you get it?

Whatever you haven't spent by the first moment of the next month becomes a countable resource, and the SSI limit is still $2,000 for one person ($3,000 for a couple). If you're over that on the 1st, you get no SSI for that month. SSA suspends benefits rather than ending them, and they restart without a new application once you're back under the limit. After 12 months in a row of suspension, though, SSI ends and you'd have to reapply.

Can I just refuse the inheritance?

You can, but it won't protect your SSI. SSA treats turning down an inheritance you were entitled to the same as receiving it and giving it away. That triggers a transfer penalty: the amount divided by the monthly SSI rate, rounded down, is the number of months you can't get SSI, up to 36. Refusing $10,000 costs 10 months; refusing $50,000 costs the full 36.

Giving it to your kids or a sibling gets the same treatment, even in the month it arrives. Selling something you inherited at fair market value is fine.

Spending down is what most people do, and it's allowed as long as you buy things for yourself at normal prices. Prepaying rent, replacing a car, paying off a credit card and setting up a burial fund of up to $1,500 all work. Buying your daughter a car doesn't; that's a gift.

An ABLE account is the simplest long-term option if your disability began before 46, and withdrawals for housing, transportation, health care and living costs don't count. The $20,000 yearly cap includes anything family put in this year. For larger amounts, a special needs trust is usually the answer. Since 2016 you can set one up yourself if you're under 65, but the drafting rules are strict, so pay a lawyer. A trustee who pays your rent can reduce your SSI by up to $351.33 a month in 2026, so trustees usually pay for other things.

Do I have to report an inheritance to SSA?

Yes, by the 10th day of the month after you receive it. Late reports lead to overpayments you'll have to repay, plus penalties of $25 to $100 each. Tell SSA how much you got, when, and what you paid toward funeral and last-illness costs.

If someone plans to leave you money, the best fix happens before they die. A parent can leave your share to a third-party special needs trust in their will. That money never belongs to you, it doesn't count for SSI, and unlike a first-party trust there's no Medicaid payback when you die. If you also get SSDI, an inheritance doesn't affect that at all; see SSDI vs SSI for how the two programs differ.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will an inheritance stop my SSI?

Usually for at least a month. It counts as income in the month you receive it, and anything left on the 1st of the next month counts toward the $2,000 limit. Spend it down at fair prices or move it to an ABLE account or special needs trust and SSI can resume.

How long do I have to spend an inheritance on SSI?

There's no grace period. Whatever you have at the start of the next month counts. Over $2,000 means no SSI that month, and after 12 months in a row it ends.

Does an inheritance count before probate is finished?

No, not while you can't use it. If the date isn't clear, SSA uses the earlier of the day you say you got it or the day the estate closed.

Can I give my inheritance to family to keep SSI?

Not without a penalty. Giving it away or refusing it counts as a transfer, and you lose one month of SSI for every full $994 (the 2026 rate), up to 36 months.

Does an inheritance affect SSDI?

No. SSDI is based on your work record, not your savings, so an inheritance doesn't change it.
