Why Does My PDF Print Blank or With Missing Text?

📄 7 min readUpdated October 19, 2026

A PDF that looks perfect on screen and comes out of the printer blank, or with text missing, is almost always a translation problem between the file and the printer: fonts that aren't embedded, layers set not to print, form fields that need flattening, or a printer out of memory. Here's how to tell which one you've got, and the one setting that fixes most of them.

Why does my PDF print blank when it looks fine on screen?

Because printing and displaying are two different jobs. Your screen shows whatever the viewer can draw. Your printer gets a translated version, built by a driver, with fonts, layers and transparency converted on the way. When something doesn't survive that translation you get a blank sheet or a page with holes, while the screen looks perfect. Match the printout to the fix:

Nine times out of ten, "Print as Image" solves it on the spot. The viewer renders each page to a picture and sends that to the printer, so nothing gets re-interpreted. The cost is a slower print job and slightly softer text. In Acrobat and Acrobat Reader it's behind the Advanced button in the print dialog. If your viewer lacks the option, convert the pages with PDF to JPG and print the images. Same result.

Why does the text print in the wrong font, or not at all?

Fonts are the number one culprit. A PDF either carries its fonts inside the file (embedded) or just names them and hopes the other end has them. If not, the printer swaps in something similar, and the stand-in's different widths can make lines overlap, run past the margin or lose characters.

We ran Poppler's font check over 40 PDFs on our own machines, a mix of reports, brochures, invoices and papers, to see how common the problem is:

Fonts embedded but without a character map6 of 40Prints fine, but copied or searched text comes out as gibberish

The three files with missing fonts were a technical report set in Comic Sans, which the author had and we didn't, and two files that named Helvetica without embedding it. Helvetica is one of the 14 base fonts every viewer must substitute for, so those print, just not in Helvetica. The Comic Sans file is the risky kind: a font the printer has never heard of.

Most viewers list a file's fonts under File, Properties, Fonts, with "(Embedded)" or "(Embedded Subset)" after the name. Anything without that label is borrowed from your machine. Re-export your own documents with fonts embedded; for someone else's, Print as Image is the fix you control.

Why don't my comments or form answers print?

Because viewers treat them as a separate layer on top of the page, and the print dialog decides whether to include it. In Acrobat it's the Comments & Forms dropdown: "Document" prints the page only, "Document and Markups" adds comments, stamps and form fields. Browser and mobile viewers vary.

The permanent fix is to flatten. Flattening turns typed answers, signatures and annotations into ordinary page content that every printer sees. Our Flatten PDF tool does it in the browser, and the flattening guide explains what you give up (the fields stop being editable). Flatten a copy, not your only original.

Why does a layer show on screen but not on paper?

PDFs can have optional content groups, usually called layers. Each layer has separate switches for viewing and printing. Designers use it for a DRAFT watermark that shows on screen but stays off the printed proof, or crop marks that print but don't display. Receive one and you'll swear the printer is broken.

In Acrobat, open the Layers panel, right-click the layer, choose Properties, and set Print to "Always Prints." Print as Image ignores the switches entirely, which is another reason it fixes so much. A related case is the job that prints page one and then blanks: a page with a huge photo can exhaust an older printer's memory. Print as Image at 300 dpi, or shrink the images with the PDF compressor first.

How do I check whether the file or the printer is the problem?

Three quick tests, in order:

  1. Print from a different program. If Acrobat prints blank but Chrome prints fine, the file is OK and Acrobat's settings or a damaged installation are the problem.
  2. Print to PDF. Choose "Save as PDF" or "Microsoft Print to PDF" as the printer. If the new file looks right, your physical printer or its driver is at fault. Update the driver or switch it to a generic PCL or PostScript version.
  3. Open the file in a second viewer. Blank everywhere means it's the file. See why a PDF opens blank; PDF Repair can rebuild a damaged one.

And if the text prints perfectly but copies as nonsense, that's a different problem: fonts embedded without a character map. Six of our 40 test files had it. OCR PDF gives you a copy with real, selectable text.

If the page prints but the top or bottom is cut off, the problem is paper size, not the file: see how to print an A4 PDF on Letter paper.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my PDF print blank pages?

Usually a font, transparency or driver problem between the file and the printer. Turn on Print as Image in the print dialog, which renders each page to a picture first. If every viewer also shows blank pages, the file is damaged or is an XFA form.

What does Print as Image do?

It makes the viewer draw each page as a bitmap and send that to the printer instead of the PDF's fonts and vector instructions. It fixes most blank-page and missing-text problems at the cost of a slower job and slightly softer text. In Acrobat it's under Advanced in the print dialog.

Why are my form answers missing when I print the PDF?

The print dialog is set to print the document only. Choose Document and Markups in Acrobat, or flatten the PDF so the typed answers become part of the page. Flattening is the reliable fix when the file will be printed by someone else.

How do I know if a PDF has embedded fonts?

Open File, Properties, Fonts in Acrobat or most other viewers. Fonts marked Embedded or Embedded Subset travel with the file. Any font without that label is borrowed from the machine you're printing from, and a different machine may substitute something else. In our check of 40 PDFs, 3 had at least one unembedded font.

Why does the PDF print but the text copies as gibberish?

The fonts are embedded without a character map, so the viewer can draw the shapes but can't tell which letters they are. It prints fine. To get usable text, run the file through an OCR tool, which reads the page as an image and writes a searchable text layer.
