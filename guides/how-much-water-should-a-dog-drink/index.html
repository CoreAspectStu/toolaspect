How Much Water Should a Dog Drink a Day?

🐾 15–25K searches/mo💰 CPC: $1–2.5⏱️ 6 min read

The number veterinarians actually use is 50–60 mL per kilogram of body weight per day (Merck Veterinary Manual) — roughly 0.8–1 oz per pound. That's 38–46 oz for a 50-lb dog, 15–18 oz for a 20-lb dog, and about a cup for a 10-lb cat counting food moisture. Here's the full table, the adjustments that matter, and the threshold where drinking becomes a symptom.

What moves the number

  • Heat (85°F+): ×1.5. Dogs cool themselves by panting, and panting evaporates water all day. A mild-day number can be half of a July number for the same dog.
  • Exercise: ×1.25 or so. A daily runner or working dog drinks a quarter more at minimum. Offer water after exertion, in moderation, rather than unlimited mid-session.
  • Puppies: ~1 oz/lb. Growth runs at the top of the range, roughly 20% above adult baseline per pound. A 20-lb puppy needs about 544–653 mL (18–22 oz).
  • Wet food: −170 mL per cup. Wet food is ~75% moisture, kibble ~10%. Two cups of wet food covers roughly a third of a mid-size dog's day.
  • Nursing mothers: 2–3× maintenance. Milk production is mostly water; a lactating female's bowl should never run dry, and your vet should be in the loop.

A worked example: the 50-lb dog, three ways

Baseline: 22.7 kg × 50–60 mL = 1,134–1,361 mL (38.3–46.0 oz, 4.8–5.8 cups). Make her an active dog and the band rises to 1,417–1,701 mL (47.9–57.5 oz). Feed two cups of wet food and about 340 mL arrives in the food, leaving 1,077–1,360 mL in the bowl. Same dog, three honest answers — which is why "how much does your dog drink?" at the vet's office needs context, not just a number.

When drinking too much is a symptom

Polydipsia — sustained intake above roughly 100 mL/kg (1.5 oz per lb) — is one of the earliest flags for diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, Cushing's disease, hypercalcemia, and pyometra in unspayed females. For the 50-lb dog that threshold is 2,268 mL (77 oz) per day, every day, for several days. One beach day doesn't count. A week of mysteriously empty bowls, especially paired with house soiling, weight change, or appetite swings, is a same-week phone call — and measuring how much the bowl actually drops per day is genuinely useful information to bring.

The reverse matters too. A dog drinking far less than its range may have nausea, dental pain, or another reason to avoid the bowl, and decompensates faster than owners expect. Any abrupt change in either direction, lasting more than a couple of days, is worth a call.

Cats are the same math with a twist

Cats use the same 50–60 mL/kg band, but for them it's total intake: a 10-lb cat needs about 227–272 mL (roughly one cup) from all sources. Cats descended from desert hunters with a weak thirst drive — they simply don't chase the bowl the way dogs do. A wet-food cat might meet most of the target from food and drink almost nothing; a kibble-only cat must get all of it from water, which is why chronic mild dehydration and urinary crystals cluster in dry-food cats, and why so many vets push wet food for feline urinary health.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water should a dog drink in a day?

The veterinary maintenance range is 50-60 mL per kg of body weight per day — about 0.8 to 1 oz per pound. A 50-lb dog needs 1,134-1,361 mL (38-46 oz, roughly 5-6 cups); a 20-lb dog needs 454-544 mL (15-18 oz); a 10-lb dog about half that. Hot weather and hard exercise can add 25-50%.

Can a dog drink 1 oz of water per pound?

Yes — the popular 1-oz-per-pound rule equals about 65 mL/kg, just above the veterinary band of 50-60 mL/kg, and it matches the top of the range for active dogs and puppies. As a daily baseline it's a touch generous for a sedentary adult on wet food, but healthy kidneys handle it easily. The number to watch isn't a single day's total anyway — it's a sustained change.

How much water should a puppy drink?

Puppies drink at or slightly above the adult range per pound — about 1 oz per pound per day. A 20-lb puppy needs around 544-653 mL (18-22 oz). Keep fresh water available through the day rather than one large filling, and expect house-training to track drinking closely: what goes in on a schedule comes out on a schedule.

Why does my dog drink less on wet food?

Wet food is roughly 75% water, so a cup delivers about 170 mL of moisture; dry kibble is around 10%. Two cups of wet food can cover a third of a dog's daily requirement, so bowl-drinking drops accordingly. That's normal. What's not normal is a dry-food dog suddenly drinking far less — that can signal nausea or illness.

How much water is too much for a dog?

Sustained intake above roughly 100 mL per kg — about 1.5 oz per pound — is called polydipsia and deserves a vet visit. For a 50-lb dog that's more than about 2,268 mL (77 oz) every day for several days. Combined with accidents, weight loss, or appetite changes, excess drinking is an early flag for diabetes, kidney disease, Cushing's, or pyometra in unspayed females.

Should I leave water out all day for my dog?

For healthy adults, yes — free access to fresh water is the standard advice, and dogs self-regulate well. Exceptions are managed conditions (congestive heart failure, some kidney and bladder protocols) where your vet prescribes measured intake, and young puppies overnight during house-training. If your vet has set a water restriction, follow it; otherwise keep the bowl full and clean.
