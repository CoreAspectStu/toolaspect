Apply to a US program from an Indian university and you'll hit the same wall: the form wants a GPA on a 4.0 scale, your transcript says CGPA out of 10. The conversion everyone uses is one multiplication — but the honest version has footnotes, and this guide walks through all of them: the linear formula, the CBSE ×9.5 percentage rule, university-specific formulas that quietly give different answers, and what admissions readers actually do with your number.

What is the CGPA to GPA formula?

The standard approximation maps one scale onto the other: GPA = (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4, which is the same as multiplying by 0.4. Divide by the maximum on your scale, multiply by the maximum on theirs. An 8.5 becomes 3.40, an 8.0 becomes 3.20, a 7.5 becomes exactly 3.00.

Why isn't there one official conversion?

Because the scales don't reward the same performance. India's 10-point scale grades each course against absolute marks, and an 8.0 at a top engineering college means something different than an 8.0 elsewhere — which is exactly the context a formula can't see. So the official answer is that US admissions offices and credential evaluators (WES, ECE, and company) apply their own methodology, often producing results that differ from ×0.4 by a tenth or two.

Practically: when a form demands a 4.0-scale number, use ×0.4 and note "converted from 10-point CGPA" if there's room. When a form accepts your native scale, give the CGPA directly. Never let a converted number replace the real one on official paperwork.

How does CGPA convert to percentage?

Three formulas circulate, and they don't agree:

  • CBSE: CGPA × 9.5. CBSE derived the 9.5 multiplier by analyzing actual Class X averages against grade points, and it became the de facto national rule of thumb. An 8.2 CGPA is 77.9%.
  • Simple: CGPA × 10. Some universities just move the decimal point. The same 8.2 reads as 82%.
  • Technical universities: (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. Several engineering schools print this on the transcript itself. An 8.2 becomes 74.5% — a full percentage class lower than the simple method.

The differences are not academic trivia: 74.5% and 77.9% can straddle a Distinction cutoff. If your university specifies a formula, that one wins, full stop. If you're estimating, say which method you used.

What's a good CGPA for US admissions?

Rough anchors for graduate programs: 8.0+ (3.2) clears most minimum filters, 8.5–9.0 (3.4–3.6) is competitive at strong programs, 9.0+ (3.6+) travels anywhere. Below 8.0 and other parts of the application do more lifting — but committees read transcripts, not just endpoints. An upward trend across semesters, hard courses in your major, and a project-heavy final year all get noticed, and many programs weight the last two years of a bachelor's degree most heavily.

CGPA vs GPA: what else differs?

Beyond the scale, two systems diverge in ways worth knowing before you fill in forms:

  • Pass marks. Many Indian universities treat 4.0 or 5.0 CGPA as the pass line (and 40% in a course); a US D is 1.0 on the 4.0 scale. The bottom of the scales maps worse than the top — another reason linear conversion is an approximation.
  • Grade inflation direction. US GPAs cluster high (the average at many state universities sits near 3.3); Indian CGPAs cluster in the 6.5–8.5 band. Evaluators know this and adjust — which is why a 7.8 from a rigorous program can read stronger than a converted 3.12 suggests.
  • Credit vs marks weighting. US GPA weights each course by credit hours; many Indian CGPAs weight by course marks or credits per the university's own scheme. Any converter that ignores weighting is estimating.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula to convert CGPA to GPA?

GPA = (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4 — divide by the top of the Indian 10-point scale and multiply by the top of the US 4.0 scale. An 8.0 becomes 3.20 and a 9.0 becomes 3.60. It's the standard approximation; no single official conversion exists.

Is CGPA x 0.4 accepted by US universities?

As a self-reported estimate, usually. For official evaluation, universities and credential evaluators like WES apply their own methodology to your transcript, and many graduate schools simply ask for the CGPA on the 10-point scale directly. Use x 0.4 when a form demands a 4.0-scale number and offers no alternative.

Why does CBSE multiply CGPA by 9.5?

CBSE analyzed actual Class X results and found the average mark corresponding to each grade point worked out to 9.5, so CGPA × 9.5 became the published conversion. It's specific to CBSE's system but widely borrowed as a rule of thumb for other 10-point scales.

What percentage is First Class?

Typically 60% and above, with Distinction at 75%+ and Second Class from 50%. Exact cutoffs vary by university, so check your institution's own rule — some set Distinction at 70%.

Does a low CGPA ruin US admissions chances?

No. Committees read transcripts in context: an upward trend, strong courses in your major, research, work experience, test scores, and recommendations all carry weight. Many programs also care more about the final two years. A CGPA below 8.0 makes standardized tests and statement of purpose work harder, but it's one factor, not the verdict.
