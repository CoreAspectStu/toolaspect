How Much an Invisible Dog Fence Costs (DIY Wireless vs Buried Wire vs Pro)

⚡ 8 min read📊 National 2025 ranges + per-acre wire math

Four products get sold under the name "electric dog fence," and they cost wildly different amounts: a $150 wireless unit, a $460 do-it-yourself buried-wire kit, a $700 GPS collar with a monthly bill, and a $1,500-plus professional install. The right question isn't which is cheapest — it's which one fits your acreage, your dog count, and how much of the work you'll actually do. The wire math: what acreage actually costs

Buried-wire systems scale with the perimeter of the contained area, and perimeter scales with the square root of acreage. A square 1-acre lot has an 835-foot perimeter; 10 acres needs 2,640 feet.

DIY wireless: the $200 shortcut and its ceiling

A stationary wireless system — base unit plus collar, $95-$350 — takes an afternoon: plug in the transmitter, set the radius, train the dog. No wire, no burial, and it moves with you, which renters love. The ceiling is coverage: a circular boundary of roughly half to three-quarters of an acre, edges that wobble near large metal objects and slope, and a delay that lets a running dog carry past the boundary before the correction lands. For a small, flat, open yard and a dog who isn't a bolter, it's the best $200 in dog containment. For anything else, bury wire.

GPS collars: no wire, but a bill forever

SpotOn- and Halo-class collars draw boundaries from satellites at $300-$1,500 per collar, no installation at all, and they travel — the same collar contains the dog at a cabin or a campsite. The catch is the subscription: $5-$30 a month on most brands ($60-$360 a year), required for Halo and optional for SpotOn's basic fencing. Over five years a $700 collar with a mid-tier plan is quietly a $1,500-$2,500 system. On large or irregular property where wire is impractical, they win anyway; on a fixed yard, buried wire beats them on lifetime cost.

The training is the system

Every tier above fails the same way: an untrained dog runs through the boundary, takes the correction, and either keeps going or refuses to come back in. Flag training — two weeks of graduated lessons with visible flags at the boundary — is what actually teaches the dog where the edge is, and it's why professional packages include training sessions and why DIY kits include a flag set. Budget your 14 days of walks along the flags, or budget for the pro. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an invisible fence cost installed?

Professionally installed buried-wire systems run $1,200-$2,000 for up to an acre (typically $1,500), plus $200-$500 for each additional acre and $100-$200 per extra dog collar. A 3-acre property with two dogs lands near $2,250 at typical pricing, training sessions included.

Is a DIY invisible fence as good as a professional one?

The hardware is similar — transmitter, buried wire, receiver collar. The differences are wire quality, a clean burial job, and training. DIY kits run $150-$500 and fail most often on cheap stranded wire and skipped flag training. Do the two weeks of flag training and use direct-burial 14-18 AWG solid-core wire and a DIY install holds up fine.

How much wire do I need for an underground dog fence?

A square 1-acre lot needs about 835 feet of perimeter wire: half an acre 590 feet, 2 acres 1,181 feet, 5 acres 1,867 feet, 10 acres 2,640 feet. Most kits include 500 feet, so 1 acre needs one extra spool, 2 acres two, 5 acres three. Add 5-10% for slack and driveway crossings.

Do GPS dog fences like SpotOn and Halo need a subscription?

Most do. Units run $300-$1,500 per collar with no wire or installation, but the GPS and boundary features typically carry a $5-$30 monthly subscription — $60-$360 a year forever. Halo requires one; SpotOn's basic fencing works without. For one or two dogs on big or irregular land they're often the practical choice; for a fixed yard, buried wire wins on lifetime cost.

Is an electric dog fence cheaper than a wood fence?

Dramatically. A wood privacy fence around a square 1-acre lot runs about $20,871 at the $25-per-foot national typical ($17-$36/LF) — roughly 14 times a professional electric install and 45 times a DIY buried-wire kit. The wood fence buys a visible barrier that contains kids and blocks wildlife; the electric fence only contains the dog.
