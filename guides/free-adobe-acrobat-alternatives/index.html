What can the free Acrobat Reader do?

More than people expect. Reader opens, prints and searches PDFs. You can highlight, add comments and sticky notes, fill in form fields and add your signature. If your PDF work stops there, you don't need anything else.

What Reader won't do is change the document itself. Creating PDFs, editing existing text, combining files, converting to Word or Excel, running OCR on scans and redacting are all paid features. That's where the alternatives come in.

What's the best free desktop alternative to Acrobat?

It depends on your computer.

  • Windows: PDF24 Creator. It's free, works offline, adds a virtual PDF printer so any program can make a PDF, and its license covers business use too. The site and app aren't pretty, but they're thorough.
  • Mac: Preview, which comes with macOS. Drag pages between sidebars to merge files, drag thumbnails to reorder, sign with the markup toolbar, and use the Reduce File Size filter when exporting.
  • Any system, including Linux: LibreOffice. Writer exports to PDF, and Draw opens existing PDFs for editing. It's best on simple layouts.

Pair any of these with browser tools for the gaps. A Mac user, for instance, might use Preview for daily merging and signing and our OCR PDF when they need the text out of a scan.

Can you edit text in a PDF without Acrobat?

Yes, but this is the feature that's hardest to replace. A PDF stores text as positioned fragments, not as flowing paragraphs. Changing a sentence means rebuilding those fragments without wrecking the spacing around them.

Sejda's online editor does a good job of it. You can click straight into existing text and change it, as long as you stay within the free tier's three tasks an hour, 200 pages and 50 MB. LibreOffice Draw also edits PDFs and runs offline, though columns, tables and embedded fonts can come out shifted.

The other route works better more often than you'd think. If you have the original Word or Google Docs file, edit that and export a fresh PDF. You'll get a cleaner result than editing the PDF ever gives you.

Is browser-based redaction as safe as Acrobat's?

It can be, as long as the tool removes the content and doesn't just cover it. Drawing a black box over text in a normal editor hides it visually, but the words are still in the file. Anyone can copy them out.

Acrobat's redaction removes the underlying text and keeps the rest of the page as text. Our Redact PDF takes a blunter approach. It rebuilds each page as an image with your boxes burned in, so nothing under the box survives. The cost is that the whole document loses its selectable text. If you need the remaining text as text, run the redacted file through OCR PDF afterward to get a transcript. Our guide on how to redact a PDF covers the details.

When is Acrobat Pro still worth paying for?

Some work really does need it. Law firms and agencies that send contracts out for signature need a tracked e-signing workflow. Accessibility work for public-sector documents leans on Acrobat Pro's accessibility checker. Print shops use its preflight tools. And if you edit existing PDF text every day, a paid editor saves real time.

If none of that is you, the free mix is enough: Reader for reading and form filling, a desktop app for daily jobs, and browser tools for OCR, redaction and comparison. Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a completely free version of Adobe Acrobat?

Yes, Acrobat Reader is free. It views, prints and searches PDFs, and lets you comment, fill in forms and sign. Creating, editing, combining, converting, OCR and redaction need a paid plan or a free alternative.

How much is Adobe Acrobat Pro per month?

In the US, Acrobat Pro is $19.99 a month on an annual plan billed monthly, or $29.99 a month with no annual commitment, according to Adobe's pricing page in October 2026. Acrobat Standard is $14.99 and $24.99 on the same terms.

What is the best free alternative to Adobe Acrobat?

There isn't one tool that does everything. On Windows, PDF24 Creator covers the most ground offline. On a Mac, Preview handles merging, reordering and signing. Browser tools fill the gaps for OCR, redaction, comparison and password protection without uploading files.

Can I convert a PDF to Word without Acrobat?

Yes. Microsoft Word can open a PDF directly and convert it into an editable document, and LibreOffice can open PDFs too. Simple text documents convert well, but complex layouts with columns, tables or unusual fonts will shift.

Can I sign a PDF for free without Acrobat?

Yes. Acrobat Reader itself lets you fill and sign for free, Mac Preview has a signature tool, and ToolAspect's Sign PDF lets you draw or type a signature in your browser. What free tools don't give you is a tracked e-signature workflow for sending documents to other people.
