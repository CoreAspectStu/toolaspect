How Much Should My Dog Weigh?

🐕 8 min read🧮 Target-weight formula included

Every owner asks the vet this, and every good vet gives the same annoying answer: "it depends on the dog." But "depends" has structure. Breed gives you a range. A 60-second hands-on check gives you a condition score. And those two things together give you a number — plus, if the news is bad, a target weight, a daily calorie budget, and a timeline. Here's the whole method.

Notice how wide those bands are — 25 lb of legal Labrador. That's why the scale reading alone can't answer "how much should my dog weigh," and why two dogs of the same breed can both be perfect at very different numbers.

Then score the body condition

Vets use a 9-point body condition score (BCS), and you can run it at home in a minute:

  • Ribs: run your flat hand along the ribcage. At a healthy 5/9 you feel ribs with light pressure, like knuckles through a sleeve. Pressing hard to find them means 6+/9. Ribs visibly ridging out means 3/9 or less.
  • Waist: stand over your dog. Behind the ribs there should be a visible tuck-in before the hips. No waist from above = 7/9 territory.
  • Abdominal tuck: from the side, the belly rises from the chest toward the hind legs. A sagging, straight, or barrel belly pushes the score up.
  • Coat caveat: on thick-coated dogs (Huskies, Pomeranians, Goldens), feeling beats seeing. Trust your hands.

Score 5/9 = ideal. 4 or less = underweight. 6+ = increasingly overweight.

How do I calculate the target weight?

Here's the piece most owners have never seen. The 9-point scale has arithmetic attached: each point above 5 is about 10% of ideal body weight. So the ideal-weight formula is:

ideal weight = current weight ÷ (1 + 0.1 × (BCS − 5))

A 90 lb Labrador at 7/9: ideal = 90 ÷ 1.2 = 75 lb. Fifteen pounds to lose, which is about 17% of current body weight. Cross-check against the breed range — 75 lb sits inside 55–80, near the top, plausible for a big male. If the math lands outside the breed range entirely, re-run the BCS check or ask your vet; the score can be off, or "Labrador" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

How much should I feed to get there?

Feed the dog you want, not the dog you have. The standard veterinary weight-loss protocol is 1.0 × RER at the target weight, where RER = 70 × (kg)^0.75. For the 75 lb target above (34.0 kg): RER ≈ 986 kcal, so roughly 986 kcal a day. Compare that to the ~1,578 kcal a neutered dog maintains 75 lb on — the cut is big, which is why vets prescribe measured meals, not free-feeding and hope.

Practical rules that make the plan work:

  • Pace: 1–2% of body weight per week. Faster risks muscle loss. 90 → 75 lb takes about 9–18 weeks.
  • Weigh-ins: every 2 weeks, same scale, same time of day. No movement after two checks → cut calories another 10%.
  • Food choice: a weight-loss formula (high protein, low calorie density) gives more volume per calorie — the bowl looks less cruel. Count treats inside the budget; they cap at ~10%.
  • Weight-loss dog food: therapeutic diet foods from your vet are the densest version of this, designed so dogs keep eating muscle-mass-preserving protein while calories drop.

The dog food calculator turns the calorie target into cups using your bag's kcal-per-cup (usually 350–450 for dry), and the raw dog food calculator does the same for fresh diets at 2–3% of target weight.

Why bother? The Purina lifespan study

The best-known answer comes from Purina's 14-year study of 48 Labrador Retrievers, where littermates were fed either maintenance portions or 25% less from 8 weeks old. The lean-fed dogs lived a median of 13.0 years versus 11.2 — about 1.8 extra years — and crossed the threshold for arthritis medication 3 years later. Not life-extension science fiction: just not overfeeding.

Overweight dogs also face higher rates of diabetes, cruciate injuries, breathing trouble (a real issue for flat-faced breeds already working uphill), and anesthetic risk. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my dog is a healthy weight?

Use your hands and eyes, not just the scale: you should be able to feel the ribs with light pressure (like knuckles through a sleeve), see a waist behind the ribs from above, and see an abdominal tuck from the side. That's a body condition score of 5 out of 9. If you have to press to find ribs, or the waist is a memory, the score is 6 or higher.

How much overweight is a dog at BCS 7?

Roughly 20% above ideal weight. Each point above 5 on the 9-point scale equals about 10% of ideal body weight, so BCS 7 is ~20% over, BCS 8 is ~30%, and BCS 9 is ~40%. Working backwards from current weight: ideal = current ÷ (1 + 0.1 × (BCS − 5)).

How much should a Labrador weigh?

The AKC breed standard spans 55-80 lb: females 55-70, males 65-80. Where your Lab lands in that band depends on build and sex. A 90 lb Lab at body condition score 7 is about 20% over, making their ideal weight around 75 lb - near the top of the range but legitimate for a big male.

How much should I feed my dog to lose weight?

Feed to the target, not the current weight: 1.0 x RER at ideal weight, where RER = 70 x (ideal kg)^0.75. For a 75 lb target that's about 986 kcal/day. Aim for 1-2% of body weight lost per week, re-weigh every two weeks, and adjust by ~10% if progress stalls.

Why can't I just follow the feeding chart on the bag?

Bag charts are calibrated for typical dogs at typical activity, and they round up - pet food companies lose nothing when you overfeed. A weight-loss dog's needs can be 30-40% below the chart row for their current weight. Calculate calories for your dog's target and convert to cups using the kcal-per-cup printed on the bag.
