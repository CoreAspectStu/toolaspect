How Much Is Catering Per Person? Every Style, Priced

💡 10-20K searches/mo💰 CPC: $3-8⏱️ 8 min read

Catering costs $15 to $150 per person depending on how the food hits the table — and that's just the food. The bar, the cake, a mandatory 18–25% service charge, and sales tax routinely push a "$100 a head" plated dinner to $188.71 all-in. Here's the full matrix by service style, what stacks on top, and the two questions that protect you from the most common quote surprise.

What stacks on top of the per-head price

The brochure number covers food and basic service. Your final invoice adds:

  • Bar packages: beer and wine $15–$25 per person, limited or signature-cocktail bars $20–$30, full open bar $30–$45.
  • Service charge: 18–25% of the food and bar subtotal (22% is typical). It covers staffing and setup — and it is not automatically a tip that reaches the servers.
  • Sales tax: catering is taxable in most states, and the tax usually applies to the service charge too.
  • Cake or dessert: $6–$12 per person, or less with a dessert bar or pies.
  • Rentals: $8–$20 per person if your venue doesn't include plates, glassware, flatware, and linens.

A worked example: the $100 plate that costs $189

Take 120 guests, a plated dinner at the typical $100 per person, a $38 full open bar, an $8-per-person cake, 22% service charge, and 7% tax:

  • Food: 120 × $100 = $12,000. Bar: 120 × $38 = $4,560. Cake: $960.
  • Service charge: 22% of $16,560 = $3,643.20.
  • Tax: 7% of $21,163.20 = $1,481.42.
  • Total: $22,645 — $188.71 per person.

Run the same guest list with food trucks at $22 and no bar package and you land near $4,473, including cake, service charge, and tax. Two receptions, same headcount, $18,000 apart. Neither is "wrong" — they're different products.

Why the styles differ by 7×

The price spread isn't ingredient quality — a taco truck uses the same chicken as a plated service. You're paying for staffing: plated dinners run one server per 10–12 guests plus a captain, buffets one per 25, and food trucks zero. China, glassware, and flatware rentals follow the same gradient, and so does kitchen infrastructure — venues without a production kitchen charge the caterer for temp equipment and portable ovens, and that passes through to you.

Where styles converge: cocktail receptions with heavy passed apps look cheap per head until you count pieces. Caterers plan 12 pieces per guest for a 2-hour grazing event plus 3–4 per extra hour; at $3–$5 a piece, the math climbs toward buffet money fast. Five questions to ask before you sign

  1. Does the service charge go to staff? Policies vary by caterer and state. If not, budget a separate 5–10% tip.
  2. What's the food-and-beverage minimum? A $15,000 F&B minimum quietly turns a Tuesday brunch into a plated-dinner budget.
  3. Is the tasting credited toward the balance? Many caterers apply the $100–$300 tasting fee to your final invoice if you book.
  4. When is the final headcount due? You're billed for the guaranteed count, usually 7–14 days out, not the bodies that show up.
  5. How are plate-count increases and overtime billed? Day-of adds typically price at the top tier plus service charge, and overtime runs $75–$150 per staffer per hour.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget for catering per person?

Budget $100 per person for a typical plated dinner, $60 for a buffet, $50 for a cocktail reception, and $22 for food trucks — those are the food-only midpoints of national 2025 ranges. Once you add a bar ($15-$45), cake ($6-$12), a 22% service charge, and sales tax, plan on $110-$190 all-in per head for full-service events.

Why is my catering quote higher than the per-person price?

Because the per-person price is food only. A mandatory service charge of 18-25% applies to food and bar, sales tax applies in most states, and rentals ($8-$20 per person) appear when the venue doesn't include plates, glassware, and linens. A $100 plated dinner with a $38 open bar becomes $188.71 per person all-in for a 120-guest wedding.

Is a buffet cheaper than a plated dinner?

Usually by $20-$70 per person. Buffets need less service staff (one server per 25 guests versus one per 10-12 for plated), though food costs per head can creep up because guests take more. Family style and stations land in between, at roughly $10 above buffet prices.

How much food do caterers make per guest?

Standard caterer math: 6 hors d'oeuvres per guest when a meal follows, 12 plus 3-4 per extra hour for grazing receptions, 8-10 oz of protein per adult for a main, and 6-10 oz each of two sides. Kids under 12 count as about half an adult. The party food quantity calculator turns these rules into an exact shopping list.

What questions should I ask a caterer before booking?

Ask whether the service charge goes to staff, what rentals are included, whether there's a food-and-beverage minimum, what the tasting costs and whether it's credited, how overtime and plate-count changes are billed, and the final payment schedule. Get the per-person price, service charge, tax treatment, and rental policy in writing on the proposal — verbal "all included" claims cause the biggest budget surprises.
