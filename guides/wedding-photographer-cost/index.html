💡 40K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2.80⏱️ 7 min read

Couples pay about $3,000 on average for a wedding photographer (The Knot's Real Weddings Study), but that average mixes Tulsa with Manhattan. The real answer is hours times market: the standard 8-hour package runs $1,880 in a small town, $2,600 in a mid-size metro, and $4,000+ in NYC, LA, or San Francisco. Here's the full grid, the add-ons worth their price, and the levers that actually move the number.

The engagement session deserves its reputation. You learn how your photographer directs, they learn how you two photograph, and the risk of wedding-day awkwardness drops. The album is the opposite: deferred gratification at the same price, so take the option instead of the bundle.

A worked quote, two ways

A Chicago couple wants 10 hours, a second shooter, and an engagement session. Coverage: 10 × $500 = $5,000 typical for a major metro, plus $450 and $400 for the add-ons — $5,850 typical, with a realistic range of $4,800 to $7,400. That's 95% above the $3,000 national average, which is exactly what you'd expect: the average mixes in thousands of small-town weddings.

Flip it: 6 hours, small town, no add-ons — $1,410 typical ($1,200 to $1,650 range). Both quotes are fair. The national "average" frustrates everyone on both sides of it because it's a blend of markets, not a price for one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a wedding photographer cost?

The national average is about $3,000, per The Knot's Real Weddings Study. The standard 8-hour package runs roughly $1,880 in a small town, $2,600 in a mid-size metro, and $4,000+ in New York, LA, or San Francisco. WeddingWire's national range runs $1,150 to $3,000, and major-metro premium quotes clear $6,000.

What drives the price of wedding photography most?

Hours and market, in that order. Every extra hour of coverage adds $235 to $500 depending on the market, so trimming 8 hours to 6 saves $470 to $1,000. A major-metro wedding costs roughly 2x the same coverage in a small town because photographers there carry higher cost of living, insurance, and demand.

Do I need a second photographer?

At $300 to $800, a second shooter is worth it above 150 guests or when the couple gets ready in two locations — two shooters cover parallel moments one camera can't. Under 80 guests at a single venue, a skilled solo photographer usually handles the day fine.

Is an engagement session worth adding?

Yes, and many planners call it the highest-value add at $250 to $600. It's a rehearsal: you learn how your photographer directs, they learn how you pose, and you get save-the-date photos. If you already love your existing photos together, it's skippable.

When should I book my wedding photographer?

8 to 12 months out for peak-season Saturdays — the best photographers in any market book right after venues. Off-season (November-April) and Friday or Sunday dates give you 3 to 6 months of breathing room, and many photographers discount them 10 to 15 percent.

How can I spend less on wedding photography?

Cut coverage before quality: 6 hours from a great photographer beats 10 from a mediocre one. Book a talented photographer in their first 2-3 years, marry Friday or Sunday, skip the engagement session if you have photos you love, and defer the album — most studios let you buy it after the wedding at the same price.
