How Much Does a Divorce Cost?

💡 30-60K searches/mo💰 CPC: $10-30⏱️ 6 min read

The honest answer is a range that runs from $150 to six figures, and the variable isn't your state or your lawyer, it's how much the two of you agree on. The courthouse charges the same for an amicable filing and a war. Everything expensive about divorce is billed by the hour, and every hour traces back to a disagreement.

Nolo's survey found a median of about $7,000 and an average near $11,300 in attorney fees, with uncontested cases averaging roughly $4,100 and contested ones climbing per party from there. In Texas and similar states, contested cases involving children commonly run couples $15,000-30,000. The ladder is steep at exactly one point: the line between agreement and its absence.

Filing fees, the fixed floor

The one cost that doesn't care about your conflict level: California $435 (highest), Florida $300-410, Texas $250-350, North Carolina $225-235, Georgia $200-300, New York $210, down to around $50 in Mississippi. Responding spouses often owe their own $50-300 answer fee. Low-income filers can usually get the fee waived outright (California's form FW-001 is the template most states copy).

The add-ons nobody budgets

  • QDRO ($500-1,500): the order that actually divides a 401(k) or pension. Skipping it can cost you the tax treatment or the money.
  • Custody evaluation ($1,500-5,000): contested custody's signature expense, often followed by a guardian ad litem.
  • Appraisals ($300-600 each): house, business, collectibles.
  • Forensic accounting ($5,000-20,000+): for self-employed spouses whose income is a mystery.

Where the money really goes

Hourly attorney time, mostly on two activities: discovery (financial disclosure fights) and motion practice (temporary orders for support and custody while the case runs). Family lawyers in metros bill $250-500 an hour, and a single contested custody week can consume several thousand dollars of retainer per side. Nolo's data shows contested cases settling before trial more often than not, which means the money usually buys pressure, not verdicts.

The practical corollary: settle the two or three issues you actually disagree on, using tools before lawyers. Four levers that actually cut the bill

Narrow the fight before you hire. Every issue you and your spouse settle privately is an issue nobody bills you to argue. Couples who walk into a lawyer's office with the house, the retirement accounts, and the parenting schedule already agreed pay uncontested rates; couples who arrive with a list of grievances pay hourly. This single choice is worth roughly the difference between $4,100 and $19,500 per party.

Use mediation as the default, not the fallback. A mediator at $150-500 an hour, split between you, resolves in a few sessions what litigation bills for over months. Pair it with an hour or two of independent attorney review each, which most mediators recommend anyway, and you get a drafted agreement plus a second set of eyes for a fraction of a contested track.

Unbundle the lawyer. Many family attorneys will take limited-scope work: draft the settlement agreement, review a document, or coach you for a hearing you attend yourself. You pay for the expensive judgment and skip the expensive clerical time.

Ask about the fee waiver. Filing fees of $100-450 are waivable in most states for low-income filers, and approval is close to automatic if you receive SNAP, SSI, or similar assistance. Ask the clerk for the packet when you file rather than discovering it afterward.

One caution against over-optimizing: pure DIY is the wrong economy when there are minor children, real estate, or retirement accounts in play. A botched QDRO or a vague parenting provision costs far more to fix later than the lawyer would have cost to draft correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost of a divorce in the US?

Nolo's reader survey found a median of about $7,000 and an average of roughly $11,300 in attorney fees. But the average is nearly meaningless without the path: DIY runs $150-500 plus filing, online services add $150-500, mediation runs $3,000-8,000 shared, attorney-handled uncontested averages about $4,100, and contested cases with disputes average $19,500 per party, with kids pushing couples to $15,000-30,000 in states like Texas.

How can I get divorced cheaply?

Agree before you file. An uncontested divorce filed yourselves costs a few hundred dollars all-in; adding mediation ($3,000-8,000 shared) still beats one contested motion cycle. The expensive inputs are disagreement and billable hours, not paperwork: couples who arrive at the courthouse with a signed settlement agreement pay a fraction of couples who arrive with demands. Independent attorney review of any agreement costs a couple hundred dollars an hour and protects both sides.

Who pays attorney fees in a divorce?

The default is each side pays their own. Courts can shift fees when one spouse controls the money or earns far more, and community-property states often treat fees incurred during marriage as shared debts. Practically, the spouse with access to funds usually advances both retainers, which itself is an argument for settling: every contested month bills both sides from the same pool.
