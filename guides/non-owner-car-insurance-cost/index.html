How Much Is Non-Owner Car Insurance?

🚗 Auto insurance⏱️ 6 min read

Non-owner car insurance — liability-only coverage for drivers who don't own a car — averages $486 a year, about $41 a month. The state you live in swings the price more than fivefold, from $216 in South Dakota to $1,141 in New Jersey, and age does the rest: a 16-year-old averages $1,660 a year while a 55-year-old averages $460. It's the cheapest way to stay legally covered and keep your insurance history clean between cars. Here's who needs it, what it covers, and what it costs.

What non-owner insurance actually is

It's a liability policy that follows you, not a vehicle. You cause an accident while driving a borrowed or rented car, and your policy pays the other side's medical bills and property damage up to its limits. What it never pays: damage to the car you were driving. No collision, no comprehensive — that's the entire reason it's cheap.

Four situations make it worth buying:

  • You rent cars a few times a year. A $486 annual policy can undercut the rental counter's liability add-on over a year of trips.
  • You borrow cars regularly. The owner's policy covers you as a permissive driver in most cases, but your own liability layer protects you both when it runs out.
  • You're between cars. A lapse in coverage is a red flag insurers price against. Continuous coverage, even cheap coverage, protects your future rates.
  • You need an SR-22 without a car. After a DUI or suspension, many states require proof of insurance to reinstate your license — a non-owner SR-22 is the standard solution when there's no vehicle to insure.

Age does the second-biggest share of the work

Rates start brutal and settle fast: $1,660 a year at 16, $884 by 19, $551 at 25, and a floor near $460 through your 50s. Past 60 they creep back up — $509 at 70, $556 at 75 — as insurers price in slower reaction times. If you're a parent adding a teen who occasionally drives a relative's car, brace for the top of that curve; if you're 50-something between vehicles, you're in the cheapest cohort on the road.

Carrier choice: the $619 swing

Among major national carriers writing non-owner policies, averages run from $207 (USAA, military community only) and $458 (GEICO) up to $826 (Allstate). That's a $619-a-year spread for the same liability product — and it's the one cost factor entirely in your control. Not every carrier writes non-owner policies at all, and availability varies by state, so the practical move is to quote three to five carriers that do. The SR-22 wrinkle

Two prices get confused here. The SR-22 form itself costs $15 to $50 — a one-time filing fee for paperwork. What wrecks the premium is the violation behind it: a DUI can double or triple whatever you'd otherwise pay. The filing usually must stay active about three years, and a lapse resets the clock in many states, which is the worst possible place to save money. If a non-owner SR-22 is your situation, price the total across carriers rather than shopping the filing fee.

Before you buy: check what already covers you

Two free phone calls can make the policy unnecessary. If you borrow the same person's car regularly, their insurer may already list you as an occasional driver. If you rent and pay with a credit card, the card may include rental liability coverage. Paying for a non-owner policy on top of protections you already have is the classic version of the problem this product solves — duplicated coverage sold to someone who didn't check first.

One boundary worth knowing: non-owner policies exclude business use, which rules them out for rideshare and delivery driving. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is non-owner car insurance per month?

About $41 a month on average — the national annual average is $486. Cheap states like South Dakota, Wyoming, and Maine run $18-$23 a month, while New Jersey averages $95 a month and Connecticut $88. Your age shifts it: teens average over $100 a month, drivers in their 50s around $38.

Is non-owner insurance cheaper than regular car insurance?

Yes, usually much cheaper. A non-owner policy carries liability only — no collision, no comprehensive — so it costs a fraction of an owned-car policy. Nationally, $486 a year versus roughly $2,000-plus for full coverage, a saving of about 75%. Even against a liability-only owner policy it tends to come in slightly cheaper.

Does non-owner insurance cover the car I'm driving?

No. It pays for injuries and damage you cause to other people and their property — that's what liability means. Damage to the borrowed or rented car itself is not covered, which is why rental counters still sell collision damage waivers and credit-card rental coverage still matters.

How long should I keep a non-owner policy between cars?

Keep it active the whole gap. A lapse in coverage is a rating factor insurers penalize, often for years — buying your next car after three uninsured months can cost more in surcharges than the non-owner premiums would have. Continuous coverage is one of the few cheap signals you can send the market.

Can I use non-owner insurance for rideshare or delivery driving?

No. Non-owner policies exclude using a vehicle for business purposes like rideshare or delivery, and the coverage gap during period 1 (app on, no passenger) is exactly when rideshare drivers get burned. Rideshare needs a commercial or rideshare endorsement on a regular policy — a non-owner policy won't fill it.
