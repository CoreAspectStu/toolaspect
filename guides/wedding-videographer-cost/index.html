How Much Does a Wedding Videographer Cost? 2026 Pricing by Market

💡 12K searches/mo💰 CPC: $2.50⏱️ 7 min read

Couples who add a videographer pay about $2,300 on average (The Knot's Real Weddings Study), and only around a third of couples book one at all. That average blends markets and add-ons. Priced directly, the standard 8-hour package runs $1,400 in a small town, $2,000 in a mid-size metro, and $3,000+ in NYC, LA, or San Francisco. Here's the full grid, the add-ons worth their price, and the bundle math that saves $200-$500 when it's right for you.

The second videographer deserves special attention because it matters more in video than in photography. With one camera, your editor must choose between your face and your partner's during the vows. With two, they cut between them, and reactions get their own footage. Above 120 guests or a full Catholic Mass, treat it as part of the real price.

The photo + video bundle math

Hybrid studios that shoot both crafts typically discount the combined booking 10-25%. On a mid-size-metro 8-hour video package ($2,000 typical), that's $200-$500 back. The catch is quality control: one studio means one style on both products, and the best photographer in your market rarely employs its equal in video.

A worked example. A San Francisco couple pricing 10 hours with a second videographer and drone lands at $4,550 typical ($3,750-$5,700 range). Booking photo and video together at 15% off — the common midpoint — takes that to about $3,868, a $683 saving, with 10% ($4,095) and 25% ($3,413) as the outer bounds. That's real money, worth taking only when the studio's photo portfolio matches what a dedicated photographer would deliver for the same dollars.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a wedding videographer cost?

Couples who add videography spend about $2,300 on average, per The Knot's Real Weddings Study, and only around a third of couples book one. The standard 8-hour package runs roughly $1,400 in a small town, $2,000 in a mid-size metro, and $3,000 in New York, LA, or San Francisco-class markets.

Is a wedding videographer worth it?

For most couples who book one, yes. Video is the only vendor product that captures vows, toasts, and speeches with sound, and industry surveys consistently flag skipped videography as one of the most common post-wedding regrets. A 6-hour highlight package at $1,350-$1,650 in a mid-size metro keeps the essentials on film for a third of what a premium package costs.

How much does the photo and video bundle discount save?

Hybrid studios typically discount combined photo-video bookings 10-25%, which on a mid-size-metro 8-hour video package saves $200-$500. Take the bundle only when the studio's photography portfolio matches what a dedicated photographer would deliver for the same money — otherwise book the best specialist in each craft separately.

What does drone wedding video cost?

Drone coverage typically adds $200-$500, with $350 common. It pays off at outdoor venues, estates, and mountain or coastal ceremonies where the establishing shot does real work; indoors or in dense downtown airspace it's usually money skipped, and some venues prohibit flights outright.

Why is wedding videography so expensive?

The shoot is the smaller half of the job. An 8-hour wedding becomes 30-60 hours of editing: syncing audio from ceremony microphones, color correcting multiple cameras, cutting the highlight film, and exporting delivery formats. Gear loads are heavier too — multiple bodies, audio recorders, lights, and often a drone.

How can I spend less on wedding videography?

Trim coverage before quality: 6 hours covering prep through toasts beats 10 that run into open dancing. Skip raw footage ($200-$800) and the full-length documentary edit ($400-$1,200) unless grandparents will watch it, book a newer studio building its reel, and marry Friday or Sunday when many studios discount 10-15%.
