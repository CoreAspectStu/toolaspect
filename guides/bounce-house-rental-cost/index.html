How Much Does a Bounce House Rental Cost? Day Rates and Every Add-On

🏰 22K searches/mo💰 CPC: $4.00⏱️ 7 min read

Renting a bounce house costs $100-$275 for a standard dry unit ($175 typical), $250-$550 for a wet/dry combo ($350), and $400+ for obstacle courses — for a 4-8 hour day with setup included. The bill climbs on add-ons: delivery $25-$150, attendant $25-$45/hr, generator $50-$150. A wet combo fully loaded lands at $640 typical. And if you're the bounce-every-summer family, a $400 residential unit pays for itself in 2.3 rentals.

Local pricing is all over the map, legitimately: Salt Lake City yards advertise basic units at $114-$134, Miami full days around $150, Dallas-area day packages $200-$700. This is a hometown industry — the yard ten minutes away often beats the national directory on both price and setup punctuality.

The three fees that move the bill

  • Delivery, $25-$100 — often free within 15-20 miles of the yard; extended distances price by the mile
  • Attendant, $25-$45/hr — mandatory at most parks and with some insurance policies; smart above 10 kids at once
  • Generator, $50-$150 — needed when no outlet sits within about 50 feet; Las Vegas-class markets quote $150-$200

The generator is the fee nobody budgets and everybody needs at a park. Blowers draw real amperage continuously; a 100-foot household extension cord sags the voltage enough to starve one. Rental yards know this, which is why the generator line appears on their fee pages before you ask.

A worked quote, two ways

The loaded summer party: wet/dry combo at $350, local delivery $50, a four-hour attendant at $35 ($140), generator $100. Total: $640 typical, range $425-$955. Add the weekend keep — many yards let you hold the unit until Monday for half the day rate again (+$175) — and you're at $815, which only makes sense when Sunday's second party exists.

The lean birthday: standard dry 13x13 at $175, delivery included by the yard down the street, your own exterior outlet, no attendant. Total: $175 ($100-$275 range). That number is the anchor for the buy-vs-rent question below.

Buy vs. rent: the 2.3-rental break-even

A residential bounce house from the big-box aisle costs $250-$600, about $400 typical. Against the $175 typical rental, that's 2.3 rentals to break even — a family doing one birthday a year plus a cousin's party each summer crosses the line in year two or three. The caveats are real: residential vinyl carries lower rider limits (usually 2-3 kids, weight-capped), ages faster in UV and storage, and has near-zero resale. Commercial units ($1,500-$3,500) are a business purchase, not a parenting one.

Renting still wins when usage is once-a-year, storage is nonexistent, or you want the wet combo and obstacle-course sizes that cost more to own than a used sedan. Buying wins on year three, assuming the garage corner holds.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a bounce house for a day?

A standard dry 13x13 rents for $100-$275 a day ($175 typical), a bounce-and-slide combo for $200-$450, a wet/dry combo for $250-$550 ($350 typical), and obstacle courses from about $400. The day rate covers a 4-8 hour window with setup and teardown; delivery, attendants, and generators are extra.

What adds to the price of a bounce house rental?

Delivery runs $25-$150 by distance (often free within 15-20 miles), a staffed attendant runs $25-$45 an hour and is mandatory at most parks, and a generator rental adds $50-$150 when no outlet sits within about 50 feet. A wet/dry combo with local delivery, four attendant hours, and a generator totals $640 typical, with a $425-$955 range.

How much is a water slide or wet bounce house rental?

Wet/dry combos run $250-$550 a day ($350 typical) — about $100-$200 more than the dry equivalent, since water hookup, drainage, and sanitizing are involved. A roughly 20-foot standalone waterslide runs about $250. Expect the top of the range in hot-weather metros like Houston, where water combos quote $300-$550.

Is it cheaper to buy a bounce house?

Break-even is 2.3 rentals: a residential unit costs about $400 ($250-$600) against the $175 typical rental, so a family that bounces at least twice a year comes out ahead by the third rental. Commercial units ($1,500-$3,500) only pay off if you're the one renting out. Residential vinyl is lighter, holds fewer kids, and ages faster — respect the weight label.

Do I need an attendant or generator for a bounce house?

An attendant ($25-$45 an hour) is usually required at parks, required by some insurance policies, and smart above roughly 10 kids at once. A generator ($50-$150) is only needed when the blower can't reach an outlet within about 50 feet — which describes most park pavilions and far-corner backyards. Extension cords beyond that distance drop voltage the blower notices.
