Vet Tech Salary by State: What Technicians Really Earn

🩺 Careers⏱️ 8 min read

Veterinary technicians earned a national median of $47,380 in May 2025, which is $22.78 an hour, according to the BLS's Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics. The state spread is enormous: DC and California pay $61,270 and $60,630 medians, while Louisiana pays $35,530, a $26,000 gap for the same credential. The job is growing fast, 9% projected through 2035, with about 13,400 openings a year. This guide walks the full table, the assistant-and-veterinarian comparison, and the levers that actually raise a tech's pay.

The national picture first

Percentiles say more than a single median. In May 2025, the 10th percentile of vet techs earned under $35,710, the 25th under $38,910, the median was $47,380, the 75th $57,650, and the 90th $63,180. In other words: a quarter of the field works for less than $19 an hour, and the top decile, research and specialty hospital roles, mostly, clears $30. For context, the median across all US occupations was $50,980, so vet teching pays slightly below the national middle for a job with one of the highest injury rates in the country. Everyone in the field knows this arithmetic; it's why the raise conversation matters.

The assistant-to-tech gap is $9,230 a year for a two-year degree, which is a solid return as those things go. The tech-to-vet gap is $82,720 for four more years of school and, at many programs, $200,000-plus of debt; plenty of techs run that ROI and stay put. A fair comparison the other direction: human-hospital roles with similar technical depth. Phlebotomists median $45,230, roughly parity. Radiologic and MRI technologists, the people doing for humans exactly what imaging techs do for animals, median $81,390. That gap is the strongest argument for the specialty and research paths below.

Five levers that actually raise pay

  1. Get credentialed if you're working uncredentialed. Most states require the credential for the full technician role anyway, and uncredentialed staff cluster near the 10th-25th percentiles, assistant pay for technician work.
  2. Specialize. NAVTA-recognized VTS academies cover anesthesia, dentistry, emergency and critical care, internal medicine, zoo medicine, and more. Specialists command the upper band, and anesthesia/ECC sit at the top of those.
  3. Consider research and industry. University veterinary programs ($52,200 median industry) and pharmaceutical or laboratory roles pay above clinical practice, with better hours and lower bite risk.
  4. Relief work. Per-diem techs at emergency hospitals commonly earn a premium hourly rate over staff positions; the tradeoff is schedule instability and no benefits.
  5. Geography, honestly assessed. Moving from Louisiana's median to California's is +$25,100 on paper, and the cost-of-living adjustment eats much of it, but DC-to-Northern-Virginia style moves within a metro often capture real gains. The projection math on a small raise

Because the field's raises are small, they compound quietly. A Florida tech at the state median, $46,380, earning 4% annual raises lands at $52,171 in year four and $144,780 cumulative over the three years. That final salary passes the current medians of 22 states, including Texas and Ohio. A tech negotiating 6% instead of 3% for the same period ends nearly $3,300 a year ahead by year four, permanently. Annual-percentage differences this small are why switching employers, which resets you to market, beats waiting for step raises in most of this field.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do vet techs make in 2025?

The national median was $47,380 a year, $22.78 an hour, in May 2025 per the BLS. The 10th percentile earned under $35,710 and the 90th over $63,180. About 131,400 people held the job, and the field is projected to grow 9% through 2035 with roughly 13,400 openings a year.

Which state pays vet techs the most?

Washington, DC at a $61,270 median, then California ($60,630), Washington state ($59,740), New York ($59,010), and Minnesota ($57,270). Louisiana is lowest at $35,530. The $26,000 top-to-bottom spread shrinks considerably once cost of living is applied, but not to zero.

What's the difference between a vet tech and a vet assistant?

Credentialing and about $9,230 a year. Technicians complete an AVMA-accredited degree (usually an associate) and pass a state credentialing exam; assistants typically learn on the job with a high school diploma. Medians in May 2025: assistants $38,150, technicians $47,380. Assistants feed, clean, restrain, and support; techs run anesthesia, take radiographs, perform lab tests, and administer treatments under a veterinarian's supervision.

Can you make a living as a vet tech?

It's tight in low-paying states and workable in high ones. The national median of $47,380 sits below the $50,980 all-occupation median, and the bottom decile earns under $35,710. The levers that move pay without a DVM: specialty credentials (VTS) in anesthesia, emergency, or dentistry; research and industry roles, which pay above clinical practice; relief work at premium hourly rates; and geography, worth up to $26,000 a year at the extremes of the state table.

How long does it take to become a vet tech?

Two years for an associate degree in veterinary technology from an AVMA-accredited program, followed by your state's credentialing exam (the VTNE in most states). Bachelor's-level veterinary technologist programs take four years and open more research doors. Compare that against the veterinarian path: eight or more years and $200,000-plus of education for a $130,100 median.
