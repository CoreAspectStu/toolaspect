Why do people look for Sejda alternatives?

Almost always it's the hourly limit. Three tasks sounds like plenty until you're merging a scan, compressing the result, then realizing you need to drop a page and compress again. That's three tasks gone and the job isn't finished.

The 200-page and 50 MB caps come next. A scanned contract, a thesis with images or a year of bank statements will hit one of them. And because Sejda Web sends your file to its servers, some people would rather not use it for tax returns or medical records at all, even though Sejda says files are deleted after processing.

Finally, the desktop app's free tier is tighter than the website's: 3 tasks a day instead of 3 an hour. It's a good app, but it isn't a free way around the limit.

Is it safe to use online PDF tools for private documents?

Most reputable services, Sejda included, encrypt uploads and delete them after a set time. That's a reasonable setup. But "deleted after processing" is still a promise rather than something you can check, and the file did leave your hands.

The simple rule we use: for menus, flyers and public reports, use whatever's fastest. For anything with an account number, a signature or a Social Security number on it, use a tool that never uploads. That means Sejda Desktop, a desktop app like LibreOffice, or browser tools that process the file locally. You can test the last kind yourself. Load the page, switch off Wi-Fi, and run the job. If it still works, nothing was sent anywhere.

Should you just pay for Sejda?

Sometimes, yes. If you edit existing text in PDFs every week, Sejda's editor is better than most free options, and €71 a year for web plus desktop is cheap next to Adobe Acrobat. The week pass also makes sense for a one-off crunch like a visa application or a house purchase.

If your work is mostly merging, compressing and signing, paying doesn't buy you much. Those jobs are fully covered by free tools, and the ones that run in your browser are faster for small files because there's no upload or download wait. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sejda free?

Sejda has a free tier for both its web tools and its desktop app. The free web plan is widely listed as 3 tasks per hour, with documents up to 200 pages or 50 MB, while the free desktop app allows 3 tasks per day. Paid plans remove those limits.

How much does Sejda cost?

When we checked Sejda's pricing page in October 2026, a Web Week Pass was €5.50 for 7 days, Web Monthly was €8.50 a month, and Desktop+Web Annual was €71 a year. Prices are quoted in euros, so what you pay in dollars depends on the exchange rate. Paid plans raise uploads to 500 MB and allow OCR on documents up to 100 pages.

What's the best free alternative to Sejda for editing existing text?

That's Sejda's real strength, and few free tools match it. LibreOffice Draw opens a PDF and lets you change existing text for free on Windows, Mac and Linux, though layouts with unusual fonts can shift. If you only need to add new text or cover a typo, a browser tool such as ToolAspect's PDF Add Text is quicker.

Does Sejda upload my files?

Sejda Web does. Its own homepage says its servers process the files and that they're permanently deleted after processing. Sejda Desktop works offline, so files never leave your computer. Browser-based tools that run locally are the other way to keep a file on your device.

How do I get around Sejda's 3 tasks per hour limit?

Wait out the hour, use Sejda Desktop for occasional jobs, or switch to a tool with no task limit. In-browser tools such as ToolAspect's Merge PDF, Compress PDF and Split PDF don't count tasks at all, because the work happens on your own device.
