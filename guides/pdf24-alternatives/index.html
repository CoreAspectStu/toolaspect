PDF24 Alternatives, Compared (Mac, Linux and No Upload)

📄 7 min readUpdated October 7, 2026

PDF24 is one of the few PDF services that's genuinely free, so people rarely leave it over price. They leave because they're on a Mac or Linux, where there's no PDF24 desktop app, or because they don't want to upload a private document. For merging, compressing, splitting, password-protecting and organizing pages, our in-browser Merge PDF, Compress PDF and Organize PDF do the job on any system without sending the file anywhere.

What is PDF24, and what's the catch?

PDF24 comes from Geek Software GmbH. It has two halves: a website full of online PDF tools, and PDF24 Creator, a free desktop program. Neither has a task limit, a page cap or a watermark, which makes it unusual next to Smallpdf, iLovePDF and Sejda. Here's how it looked when we checked in October 2026.

So the catch isn't money. It's the platform gap, the upload, and the ads on the website. For plenty of people none of those matter, and PDF24 is a fine choice.

Why would you want an alternative to a free tool?

You're not on Windows. PDF24 Creator is the offline half, and it doesn't exist for macOS or Linux. Mac and Linux users only get the website, which means every job is an upload.

The document is private. PDF24's one-hour deletion policy and EU servers are better than average. But a lease, a payslip or a passport scan still leaves your device, and some workplaces don't allow that at all.

You need to change existing text. PDF24 can add text boxes, but it can't rewrite a sentence that's already in the PDF. For that you need a real editor.

The ads get in the way. They're labeled "discreet" by PDF24, and they mostly are, but on a phone they can push the tool below the fold.

How can you tell whether a PDF tool uploads your file?

There's a quick test that works for any website. Open the tool, wait for the page to finish loading, then turn off Wi-Fi or switch on airplane mode. Now run the job. If the tool still produces a file, the work happened on your device. If it stalls or throws an error, it was sending your document to a server.

PDF24's online tools upload, and PDF24 is upfront about it. Our PDF tools pass the airplane-mode test, as do Preview and LibreOffice. On Windows, PDF24 Creator passes too, which is why it's still a good pick for Windows users who don't need anything else.

Should Windows users bother switching?

Probably not, if PDF24 Creator already does what you need. It's free, it works offline and it doesn't watermark. The reasons to look elsewhere on Windows are narrower: you can't install software on a work laptop, you want something lighter than a full desktop suite, or you need real text editing.

For a laptop you can't install on, browser tools are the obvious answer because there's nothing to install. For text editing, LibreOffice Draw is the free option, and Sejda's editor is worth a look if you only have a few edits. Our best free PDF tools roundup covers the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PDF24 really free?

Yes. PDF24 says its tools are free for everyone, companies included, with no usage restrictions. It's paid for by advertising on the website. The desktop app, PDF24 Creator, is also free, but it only runs on Windows.

Is there a PDF24 for Mac?

No. PDF24 Creator is Windows only, and PDF24 points Mac users to its online tools instead. On a Mac, Preview covers merging, reordering, signing and basic markup offline, and browser-based tools that run locally cover compressing, splitting, redacting and page numbers without an upload.

Is PDF24 safe to use?

PDF24 says its processing servers are in EU data centers and that files uploaded to the online tools are deleted automatically after one hour. That's a sensible policy. If a document is sensitive enough that you don't want it uploaded at all, use PDF24 Creator on Windows or a tool that processes files in your browser.

Does PDF24 add a watermark?

No. PDF24 doesn't watermark output from its free tools, which is one reason it's popular. Most of the alternatives in this guide don't watermark either, including ToolAspect's PDF tools, Mac Preview and LibreOffice.

What's the best PDF24 alternative for Linux?

Linux users have good offline options. LibreOffice Draw opens and edits PDFs, and command-line tools such as qpdf and Ghostscript handle merging, splitting and compression. For a point-and-click option with no install, browser-based tools work the same on Linux as anywhere else.
