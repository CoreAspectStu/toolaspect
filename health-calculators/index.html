Free Health & Fitness Calculators for Body, Nutrition & Pregnancy

Health numbers are personal, and they deserve better than one-size-fits-all guesses. This collection of free health and fitness calculators covers the measurements people actually track: body composition, daily calories and macros, hydration, training zones, and pregnancy milestones. Each calculator takes your inputs — height, weight, age, activity level, due date — and applies a published, named formula, so you always know which method produced the number. Everything runs instantly in your browser, free and without an account, and nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

The directory is organized into three groups. Body composition holds the ten core tools: BMI, body fat, ideal weight, BMR, calories, macros, deficits, protein, and hydration. Fitness & training covers macro splits and heart-rate zones. Pregnancy & health collects the week-by-week, ovulation, due date, and weight-gain tools, plus BAC, A1c conversion, and two medical cost estimators.

Body Composition: The Ten Tools People Track Most

The composition group begins with the BMI Calculator, the standard screening index: weight in kilograms over height in meters squared, classified as underweight, normal, overweight, or obese. BMI is fast and population-tested, but it cannot tell muscle from fat — which is why the collection also includes the Body Fat Calculator, which uses the US Navy circumference method (neck and waist, plus hips for women) to estimate body fat percentage with nothing more than a tape measure. The Ideal Weight Calculator completes the picture with a healthy weight range for your height and frame.

Energy comes next. The BMR Calculator estimates the calories your body burns at complete rest — the baseline everything else builds on. The Calorie Calculator applies your activity level and goal (lose, maintain, gain) to that baseline to produce daily calorie needs. The Calorie Deficit Calculator goes one step further, pairing a daily target with the timeline it implies. Nutrition detail is handled by the Macro Calculator (protein, carbs, fat targets), the Protein Intake Calculator (daily target by body weight and goal), and the Macro Split Calculator (converting percentage splits into grams). The two hydration tools — Water Intake and Hydration by Weight — translate body size into a daily water target.

Fitness & Training: Training at the Right Intensity

Training tools are few but precise. The Heart Rate Zone Calculator uses the Karvonen formula, which computes zones from heart rate reserve — your maximum heart rate minus your resting heart rate — rather than maximum heart rate alone. That personalization matters: two people of the same age with different resting rates get different fat-burning and cardio thresholds. If you train with a heart-rate monitor, this calculator turns its raw numbers into a plan.

Pregnancy & Health: Dates, Ranges, and Decisions

The pregnancy group covers the full timeline. The Due Date Calculator estimates delivery from your last menstrual period or conception date; the Pregnancy Week by Week tool walks all 40 weeks with fetal sizes and symptoms; the Ovulation Calculator identifies the fertile window from cycle data for those trying to conceive. Weight management tools include the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator, which applies IOM ranges by pre-pregnancy BMI and week, and the Twins Weight Gain Calculator for twin-specific ranges.

General health is served by the BAC Calculator, which estimates blood alcohol from drinks, body weight, sex, and elapsed time — a planning tool, never a driving test. The A1c Converter translates between HbA1c and average glucose, the two ways diabetes numbers are reported. Two cost estimators — Vasectomy Reversal and Invisalign — round out the group with procedure cost bands, insurance, and FSA/HSA considerations.

Using These Tools Responsibly

  • Calculators estimate from population formulas — individual bodies vary; use results as starting points, not diagnoses
  • BMI alone can misclassify muscular people; pair it with the Navy-method body fat estimate
  • For calorie targets, re-run the calculator after significant weight change — a stale baseline quietly defeats the plan
  • Pregnancy tools use standard clinical rules (Naegele's rule, IOM ranges); your provider's dating scan overrides an LMP estimate
  • BAC estimates are rough and individual; never use one to decide whether to drive
  • For anything medical — diet changes, glucose targets, pregnancy concerns — treat these tools as homework for the conversation with your clinician, not a substitute for it
