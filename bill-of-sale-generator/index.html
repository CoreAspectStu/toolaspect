How the Bill of Sale Generator Works

A bill of sale is a simple contract, but the simple ones are the ones people botch — missing VINs, no price in words, an as-is clause nobody wrote down until after the argument. This tool assembles the document from your fields, formats the price in words the way banks and DMVs expect, and prints clean on one page.

How to use it

Pick the item type, fill in the details, and read the preview as you type. For vehicles, include the full 17-character VIN and the true odometer reading — both are what a DMV clerk checks first. The print button produces a one-page document (use your browser's Save as PDF if you'd rather email it). Sign two originals: one for the buyer to take to the DMV, one for the seller's records.

A worked example

Selling a 2016 Toyota Camry with 98,240 miles for $11,500 in Ohio: the generated document reads the price as "Eleven thousand five hundred and 00/100 dollars," includes the odometer disclosure line, the as-is clause, and two signature blocks. Ohio doesn't require notarization, so no notary block prints. Change the state to Montana and one appears automatically, because Montana titles require a notarized signature. That one field is the difference between a document the county clerk accepts and one that sends you home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a handwritten bill of sale legal?

Yes. No state requires a special form — a bill of sale is a contract, and a handwritten one with the required elements (parties, item description, price, date, signatures) is enforceable. What matters is completeness and, in a handful of states, notarization. A printed form just makes sure nothing gets left out.

Does a bill of sale need to be notarized?

Only in some states. Louisiana, Montana, Nebraska, and West Virginia generally require a notarized bill of sale, and Maryland requires notarization for vehicle purchases. Most states accept a plain signed document. Rules change and vary by item type, so confirm with your DMV before the sale — this generator adds the notary block automatically for those states.

Is a bill of sale the same as a title?

No, and the difference matters. The title transfers legal ownership of a vehicle; the bill of sale documents the transaction — price, date, condition, and parties. Never accept a bill of sale in place of a missing title when buying a car. Some states do accept a bill of sale plus other paperwork for very old vehicles, but that's the exception, not the rule.

Who keeps the bill of sale after the sale?

Both parties. The buyer needs it to register and title the vehicle and to document what was promised; the seller keeps a copy as proof of transfer date, mileage, and — critically — that the vehicle was sold, which matters for parking tickets, tolls, and liability until the title transfer is filed. Sign two originals or make copies.

What does "sold as-is" mean on a bill of sale?

It means the seller makes no warranties about condition — the buyer accepts the item with all faults, visible or not. It does not license fraud: misrepresenting the mileage, hiding a salvage title, or lying about accidents can still unwind the sale. Sellers should disclose known defects in writing even on an as-is sale.

Do I need a bill of sale to pay taxes on a private car purchase?

Often, yes. Many states compute sales or use tax on private vehicle purchases from the price on the bill of sale, and tax collectors are entitled to be skeptical of "$500" Camrys — some states penalize declared amounts far below book value. Keep the document truthful and keep a copy.
