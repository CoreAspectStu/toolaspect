How the Concrete Block Calculator Works

Enter your wall length and height and any door/window openings to subtract. The calculator works from the standard nominal 8×8×16 CMU: each block face covers 16" × 8" = 128 sq in, which works out to exactly 1.125 blocks per square foot of wall (9 blocks per 8 sq ft, or 1.125 × area). It returns the block count with waste, the number of 80lb mortar bags, and a material cost. The "blocks per LF" field lets you double-check coursing: 0.75 blocks per linear foot at 16" module.

Formulas Used

  • Wall area: length × height − openings
  • Blocks: ceil(area × 1.125 × (1 + waste%)) — 1.125 blocks/sq ft for nominal 8×8×16
  • Mortar: ≈ 1 bag of masonry cement (or Type S 80lb mix) per 33 blocks with standard ⅜" joints
  • Coursing check: courses = height ÷ 0.667 ft (8" per course); blocks/course = length × 0.75

Worked Example

A 30 ft × 8 ft wall with no openings: 240 sq ft × 1.125 = 270 blocks, plus 3% waste = 279 blocks (buy 280). Coursing check: 8 ft ÷ 8" = 12 courses × 30 ft × 0.75 = 270 blocks — matches. Mortar: 279 ÷ 33 ≈ 9 bags. At $2.25/block the block package runs about $628.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many concrete blocks per square foot? Exactly 1.125 for standard nominal 8×8×16 block with ⅜" mortar joints — that's 9 blocks per 8 square feet.

How many blocks in a bag of mortar? One 80lb bag of premix Type S mortar lays about 33 standard blocks; pure masonry cement bags (75lb) go further — about 36 blocks per bag when mixed 1:3 with sand.

Do I need bond beam blocks? At wall tops, over openings, and at 8–10 ft vertical intervals in seismic/wind zones — typically every other course at the top. Ask your engineer; this calculator covers stretcher blocks only.

What about filling cells with concrete? Solid-grouted walls need roughly 1 yd³ of grout per 250 sq ft of 8" wall (each 8×16 core ≈ 0.02 yd³ fill). Reinforced vertical cells are typically filled at 4 ft on center.
