How the EV Battery Replacement Calculator Works

Out-of-warranty pack replacement is the EV's version of an engine rebuild — the single repair that can exceed the car's value. It's also increasingly common as the first mass-market EVs (2011-2012 Leafs and Volts) age past their 8-year terms. This calculator prices the repair three ways and tells you whether you should be paying at all.

The formula

Pick a model to load its parts band and pack size, or choose custom and enter kWh, priced at $150-$250 per kWh installed ($200 typical). Parts are multiplied by 0.60 for remanufactured (the middle of the 30-50% discount band) or 0.40 for used/salvage. Add labor hours × shop rate, then sales tax on the subtotal. The warranty box compares model year and odometer against the 8-year/100,000-mile federal-minimum term — the factory coverage that applies across every major US-sold EV.

How to use it

Start with the warranty check. If the vehicle is inside 8 years and 100,000 miles, the likely cost of a failed pack is $0 under warranty (or a deductible under some terms) — verify with a dealer before pricing anything. Out of warranty, run all three pack conditions: the spread between new and used often decides the repair-versus-replace question by itself. Adjust the shop rate to your metro; EV-certified independent shops quote $120-$210 an hour.

A worked example

A 2019 Leaf with a degraded 40 kWh pack, out of warranty at 118,000 miles. New dealer parts at the typical $9,000 plus 5 hours at $150 ($750) is $9,750; with 7% sales tax the installed total is $10,432.50 — $260.81 per kWh, inside the $166-$271 installed band Recurrent reports for Leaf packs. A remanufactured pack at 40% off drops parts to $5,400 and the total to $6,580.50 with tax. A Tesla Model 3 follows the same math at higher stakes: $13,500 parts typical, $8,100 remanufactured, $9,469.50 installed after tax and labor.

The decision rule is residual value: if the car is worth less after the repair than the repair costs, the pack only makes sense on a car you'll keep. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an EV battery replacement cost?

$4,000 to $20,000 installed depending on model and pack size. Nissan Leaf packs run $4,000-$10,000 by capacity (24/30/40 kWh), Tesla Model 3 and Model Y average around $13,500 ($12,000-$16,000), and GM lists the Chevy Bolt pack near $15,734 before labor. Generic pricing runs $150-$250 per kWh installed. Remanufactured and aftermarket packs cut 30-50% off dealer parts prices.

Is EV battery replacement covered by warranty?

Probably, if your EV is under 8 years old and 100,000 miles. Every major manufacturer selling EVs in the US covers the battery at least 8 years/100,000 miles - Tesla extends to 120,000 miles on some trims, and Hyundai and Kia go 10 years/100,000 miles - and the warranties typically guarantee capacity retention to about 70% of original range. Per Recurrent's fleet data, only about 2.5% of EVs have ever needed a replacement outside degradation.

Can you put a used or remanufactured battery in an EV?

Yes. Remanufacturers like GreenTec Auto sell refurbished Tesla Model 3 packs for $7,999-$9,999 against $12,000-$16,000 new - roughly 30-50% off dealer parts pricing. Used packs from salvage cars cost less still but carry unknown state-of-health and usually no warranty. Have the pack's measured capacity verified before paying, and expect labor to be similar either way.

How long do EV batteries last?

Longer than the warranty in most cases. Recurrent's fleet data puts average degradation near 1.8% of range per year, which means a 250-mile pack still holds about 205 miles after ten years. The earliest mass-market EVs - 2011-2012 Nissan Leaf and Chevrolet Volt - are now past their 8-year terms, which is why battery replacement searches and out-of-warranty replacements are rising.

Does insurance cover EV battery replacement?

For damage, often yes: collision coverage pays for crash damage and comprehensive covers flood, fire, and theft-related pack damage. Degradation and out-of-warranty failure are not insurable events - that is what the battery warranty is for. Some insurers now offer battery-specific add-ons; read the exclusions about pack handling and salvage titles.

Is it worth replacing an EV battery or scrapping the car?

Run the residual-value math: if the installed pack costs more than the car will be worth with a new pack, the repair rarely makes sense unless you plan to drive it for years. A $10,433 replacement in a $9,000 car is a hard no; the same repair in a car worth $20,000 post-repair pencils out, especially against a new-EV payment. Remanufactured packs move the math the most.
