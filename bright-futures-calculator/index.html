How the Bright Futures Calculator Works

Bright Futures is Florida's merit scholarship, funded by the lottery and paid by the Florida Department of Education. Two levers decide your tier — weighted GPA in the 16 core credits, and hours of service or paid work — and the tier decides what percentage of tuition and fees the state covers. This calculator checks both levers and prices the result.

The eligibility logic

FAS requires a 3.50 weighted GPA and 100 hours. FMS requires a 3.00 weighted GPA and 75 hours. Both thresholds must be met — a 4.0 GPA with 60 hours still lands you short of FMS until the hours catch up. Since the test-score requirement was dropped for Florida public school graduates, GPA and hours are the entire decision for most students. The verdict box tells you exactly what's missing and which tier is already locked in.

A worked example

A senior with a 3.60 weighted GPA and 100 logged hours clears both FAS bars. At UF's published rate of $214.54 per credit taking 30 credits a year, that's $6,436.20 per year, and $25,744.80 across a 120-credit degree — tuition and applicable fees fully covered, plus summer terms at the same rate.

Now a 3.20 GPA with 80 hours. That clears FMS (3.00 + 75) but not FAS: the student qualifies for 75% coverage worth $4,827 a year at UF rates, and needs +0.30 GPA in the remaining core courses to convert to FAS. That gap is the whole game — use the weighted GPA calculator to see what next semester's grades do to the average.

How to use it

Enter your weighted GPA exactly as your school reports it (Bright Futures computes it unrounded across the core 16, so don't round up optimistically). Count all approved service hours plus documented paid work hours. Pick an institution rate or enter your own from your college's fee sheet — private and out-of-state schools get a fixed per-credit amount instead of a percentage. Then read the renewal rules below, because the check that keeps the money flowing in college is separate from the one that earns it.

Full rules, the application timeline, and the renewal-and-restoration details are in the Bright Futures guide. This is an independent estimator, not affiliated with the Florida Department of Education — award rules change by legislative session, so always verify at floridabrightfutures.gov before making enrollment decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for Florida Bright Futures in 2025-26?

Florida Academic Scholars (FAS) needs a 3.50 weighted GPA in the 16 required core credits plus 100 hours of volunteer service, paid work, or a combination. Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS) needs a 3.00 weighted GPA plus 75 hours. For Florida public high school graduates, SAT, ACT, and CLT scores are no longer part of the requirement. Gold Seal awards need a 3.00 weighted GPA plus 30 hours on a career-technical path.

Do you still need SAT or ACT scores for Bright Futures?

Not for Florida public high school graduates — test scores were removed from the FAS and FMS requirements, so weighted GPA and service or work hours carry the decision. Home education, private school, and out-of-state students follow separate evaluation rules that can still involve testing, so those families should confirm their path with the Florida Department of Education.

How much does Bright Futures pay?

FAS pays 100% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public institutions; FMS pays 75%. At recent per-credit rates that's about $6,400 a year for FAS and $4,800 for FMS at a public university taking 30 credits — roughly $25,700 and $19,300 over a 120-credit degree. Both tiers now fund summer terms too.

Can paid work hours count for Bright Futures?

Yes. The 100 hours for FAS (75 for FMS) can be volunteer service, paid work hours, or a combination of the two. Paid work must be documented by the employer — a pay stub record or letter showing hours — just as service hours are logged and approved through the school.

What GPA do you need to renew Bright Futures in college?

FAS renews with a 3.00 cumulative college GPA; FMS renews with a 2.75. A FAS student who lands between 2.75 and 2.99 doesn't lose everything — they renew at the Medallion level. Full-time students must also earn at least 24 credit hours per academic year, and dropped or withdrawn hours generally have to be repaid to keep renewal. There's a one-time restoration if you lose the award to GPA during your first funding year.

Is there a deadline to apply for Bright Futures?

You must submit the Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA) by August 31 of your high school graduation year — it doesn't renew automatically with the FAFSA, and the FAFSA itself is not required for Bright Futures. Students who miss the window can appeal, but approval is not guaranteed, so file in spring of senior year while counselors can still fix transcript errors.
