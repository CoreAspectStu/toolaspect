How the Pet Sitter Rates Calculator Works

Pet sitting pricing is per-visit with two stackable add-ons: extra pets and holiday dates. This calculator applies the national ranges to your exact booking shape so you can sanity-check a quote or set a fair one.

The formula

Per-visit rate = service base rate ($18-$30 for a 30-minute drop-in, and so on) + extra-pet fee × additional pets. Trip total = per-visit rate × visits per day × days × holiday multiplier. A 15% tip lands on top if you're tipping, and most owners do.

How to use it

Match the service to what you actually booked, drop-ins for cats and low-maintenance pets, walks for dogs that need midday exercise, overnights when pets do badly alone at night. Set additional pets to the number beyond the first, since sitters usually include one pet in the base rate. If your quote came through a platform, remember the fees: Rover takes roughly 20% from the sitter (25% in California) and adds an owner-side booking fee of around 11%.

A worked example

Two 30-minute drop-in visits a day for a 3-day weekend, two cats (one additional pet at the calculator's default $7.50/visit fee; the common national range runs $5-$10). Per visit: $18-$30 base plus $7.50 for the second cat gives $25.50-$37.50. Per day: two visits runs $51-$75. Trip total: $153-$225. Over the same weekend a single overnight sitter runs $225-$450 for all three nights, and a 1.5x holiday multiplier pushes the drop-in package to $230-$338.

Where these numbers move: New York, SF, Seattle, and similar metros add 20-40%; rural areas sit 10-20% below; medication administration sometimes carries a flat $5-$15 per visit; and recurring weekly work usually earns a 10-15% discount off the per-visit rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I pay a pet sitter?

National ranges for 2025-26: $18 to $30 per 30-minute drop-in visit, $28 to $45 for a 60-minute visit, $20 to $35 for a 30-minute dog walk, and $75 to $150 per overnight stay. Two drop-in visits a day for three days with a second pet runs $138 to $240 total at typical add-a-pet fees of $5 to $10 per visit. Big metros run 20 to 40 percent over these numbers, small towns under them.

How much is overnight pet sitting?

$75 to $150 per night nationally for a sitter who stays in your home, with $100 as a common midpoint in mid-size cities and $150-plus in expensive metros. A week of overnights for one pet runs $525 to $1,050. Some sitters charge a flat housesitting rate ($50 to $100 a night) when pet care is light, and most add $5 to $10 per additional pet per night.

Do pet sitters charge more for holidays?

Usually yes. Expect a 1.5x multiplier on Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's dates on platforms like Rover, and 1.25x to 2x from independent sitters around major holidays. A $25 drop-in becomes $37.50 at 1.5x. Book holiday coverage 4 to 6 weeks out, because the good sitters are gone by early December.

How much extra for a second pet?

Most sitters add $5 to $10 per additional pet per visit, or $10 to $20 per night for overnights. Two drop-in visits a day for three days for two pets totals $138 to $240, versus $108 to $180 for one pet. Exotics and animals needing medication often carry a flat $5 to $15 per-visit surcharge instead.

Is Rover cheaper than a private pet sitter?

Often similar or slightly higher once you count Rover's fees: sitters pay Rover roughly a 20 percent service fee (25 percent in California), and owners pay a booking fee of around 11 percent on top of the listed rate. What you get for it is background checks, reviews, insurance coverage, and replacement guarantees. Private sitters may quote less but you carry the vetting yourself. For recurring work, many owners negotiate a flat weekly rate with either, roughly 10 to 15 percent under the per-visit price.

Should I tip my pet sitter?

Yes, tips are normal and appreciated: 15 to 20 percent for a job well done, more around holidays. On a $150 booking that's $22 to $30. Cash or an app tip both work, and on Rover the tip goes entirely to the sitter. Tipping isn't obligatory for a medicated-care professional like a vet tech doing medical boarding, but for everyday sitters and walkers it's the norm.
