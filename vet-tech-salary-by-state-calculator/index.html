How the Calculator Works

Three tools on one page. The state lookup reads the BLS OEWS May 2025 table above. The percentile finder places your hourly wage against the national 10th/25th/median/75th/90th bands and your state's spread. The projector compounds an annual raise over your horizon, the same arithmetic a step schedule uses.

The formula

Hourly equivalent = annual median ÷ 2,080, which is exactly how the BLS publishes the two figures, so $47,380 becomes $22.78. Rank orders the 51 areas (50 states plus DC) by median. The projector uses salary × (1 + raise%)^years, summing each year for cumulative earnings.

How to use it

Pick your state for the published bands, then drop your actual wage into the finder to see which percentile you occupy; anyone below the 25th percentile ($38,910 nationally) is being paid like an assistant. For negotiation, the state's 75th percentile is a realistic target at a well-run hospital, and the projector shows what a 3-4% annual raise compounds to over a stay of a few years.

A worked example

A Florida tech earning the state median, $46,380, projects 4% annual raises for three years: $48,235, then $50,165, then $52,171 in year four, for $144,780 cumulative. The hourly rate moves from $22.30 to $25.08. Against the national table, that year-four salary passes 22 states' current medians, including Texas ($39,410) and Ohio ($47,990).

Spread is worth reading too. New Jersey's band runs $37,870 to $75,310, a 1.99× ratio from 10th to 90th percentile, which says the same license pays wildly differently by hospital within one small state. Compare South Dakota, where the band is $35,780 to $50,440, a 1.41× spread: less upside, but less downside. Where the band is wide, the employer you pick matters as much as the state.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which state pays vet techs the most?

The District of Columbia leads at a $61,270 median (May 2025 BLS), followed by California at $60,630, Washington at $59,740, New York at $59,010, and Minnesota at $57,270. Louisiana is lowest at $35,530, with Mississippi ($36,380) and Alabama ($36,590) just above it. The top-to-bottom spread is nearly $26,000 a year, though high-paying states also carry higher living costs.

What is the average vet tech salary in the US?

The median annual wage for veterinary technologists and technicians was $47,380 in May 2025, which is $22.78 an hour. The 10th percentile earned under $35,710 and the 90th percentile over $63,180. The field employed about 131,400 people in 2025 and is projected to grow 9% through 2035, much faster than the 3% average across occupations, with roughly 13,400 openings a year.

How much do vet techs make compared to vet assistants and veterinarians?

The May 2025 medians: veterinary assistants and laboratory animal caretakers $38,150, veterinary technologists and technicians $47,380, and veterinarians $130,100. So credentialed techs out-earn assistants by about $9,230 a year, while vets make about 2.75 times what techs make. Adjacent human-healthcare roles pay differently too: phlebotomists median $45,230 and radiologic technologists $81,390.

Do credentialed vet techs (CVT/RVT/LVT) earn more?

Generally yes: most states require credentialing (registered, licensed, or certified, depending on the state) for the full technician role, and uncredentialed assistants cluster near the lower percentiles. Specialization adds more: NAVTA-recognized academies exist in anesthesia, dentistry, emergency and critical care, internal medicine, and others, and specialists typically command the upper part of the pay band. The percentile finder above shows where your current wage sits nationally and in your state.

Can vet techs earn more without becoming veterinarians?

Yes, though the ceiling is real. The levers that work: credentialing if you're working uncredentialed, specialty certification (anesthesia and emergency/critical care pay best), moving into research or pharmaceutical industry roles (scientific research and pharmaceutical manufacturing pay well above clinical practice), relief work with premium hourly rates, and practice management. Geography matters too: the same credentials are worth nearly $26,000 more a year at the top of the state table than at the bottom.
