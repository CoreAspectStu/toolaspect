How the Marriage Name Change Cost Calculator Works

A name change isn't one fee — it's a relay of documents, each feeding the next. The good news: the anchor step is free, most of the stack is state-level pocket change, and the biggest line is usually the passport. This calculator adds up the whole relay so nothing surprises you at a counter.

The math

Total = certified copies (count × per-copy fee) + $0 Social Security + your state's license fee + the passport fee for your situation ($0 within a year of issuance, $130 renewing by mail, $165 in person) + optional photo, vehicle title fee, and an online kit. Every input is editable because the state-to-state spread is wide.

How to use it

Pick your state if it's listed — that loads typical DMV and title fees — then adjust counts and fees to match your situation. Skip the passport line only if you don't travel; a passport in your old name stays valid until it expires, though booking tickets gets confusing. The kit toggle adds the $50–$100 service price on top, since no kit replaces a government fee.

A worked example

A Florida newlywed doing the full stack: 3 certified copies at $15 each = $45. Social Security card: $0. License: $25. Passport renewal with the new name: $130, plus a $15 photo. Vehicle title: $15. Total: $45 + $0 + $25 + $130 + $15 + $15 = $230. The same stack with a $75 online kit is $305 — the kit fills in forms you could fill in yourself, which is exactly the trade to eyeball. A Californian running the same errands pays closer to $190, since the DMV charges nothing.

The Order That Actually Works

  1. Certified copies first — buy 3 or 4 when you get the license; everything downstream wants one and most keep it.
  2. Social Security — free, by mail or in person, 2–4 weeks to process. Everything checks this record.
  3. Driver's license — usually within 30 days of the name change; Florida wants the SSA update done 48 hours before your visit.
  4. Passport — free under DS-5504 if issued within the last year; otherwise renew at $130 before any international travel.
  5. Vehicle title and registration — then banks, payroll, insurance, and voters registration, all free.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to change your name after marriage?

Doing it yourself usually runs $150 to $300. The Social Security card update is free, the DMV charges $0 to $45 depending on the state, a passport name change is $0 to $165 depending on how long ago it was issued, and certified copies of the marriage certificate run $5 to $25 each — you'll want 2 to 4. Online name-change kits add $50 to $100 but don't replace any government fee.

Is the Social Security name change free?

Yes — Form SS-5 has no fee, ever. You file it with your marriage certificate and ID, and the new card arrives in 2 to 4 weeks. Do this first: most DMVs and passport agencies check that the SSA record already shows your new name before they'll update their own.

How much does a passport name change cost?

Nothing if your current passport was issued within the last year — Form DS-5504 is free. Otherwise you renew by mail with Form DS-82 at $130, or file Form DS-11 in person at $130 plus the $35 execution fee if you're not eligible to renew by mail. Add a passport photo, about $15, unless the old one still looks like you.

How many certified copies of the marriage certificate do I need?

Plan on 2 to 4 at $5 to $25 each. Social Security, the DMV, passport agencies, insurers, and banks each want to see a certified copy, and most keep them or return them slowly. Ordering extras later means a wait of several weeks by mail, so buy them upfront.

Are online name-change services worth it?

They cost $50 to $100 and don't replace a single government fee — they pre-fill your paperwork and ship instructions and envelopes. If your stack is Social Security plus DMV plus passport, the DIY route is a few forms and one afternoon. The kits earn their price when you're also updating a dozen banks, loyalty programs, licenses, and titles.

How long do I have to change my name after marriage?

There's no single national deadline, but pieces have clocks. Many states expect a driver's license update within 30 days of the name change (some allow 60), and you shouldn't book travel under a name that doesn't match your passport. Since everything chains off the Social Security record, starting within a few weeks keeps the documents from drifting apart.

What order should I change documents in?

Social Security first — it's free and everything else checks it. Then the driver's license (Florida, for example, wants the SSA update done at least 48 hours before your DMV visit), then the passport, then the vehicle title and registration, then banks, employer payroll, insurance, and voters registration. Certified copies are the fuel for all of it.
