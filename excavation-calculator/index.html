How the Excavation Calculator Works

Enter the pit, trench, or pool dimensions and the calculator returns the volume in cubic feet, bank cubic yards (in-place soil), and loose cubic yards after the soil swells during digging. It then divides by your truck capacity to give truck loads and multiplies by the per-load haul-off price for a removal cost estimate. If you need backfill, keep in mind compacted backfill takes about 10–20% more volume than the hole it came from.

Excavation Formulas

  • Volume (ft³) = length × width × depth (trenches: length × width × average depth)
  • Bank cubic yards (BCY) = ft³ ÷ 27
  • Loose cubic yards (LCY) = BCY × swell factor (earth swells 20–40% when dug)
  • Truck loads = ceil(LCY ÷ truck capacity)
  • Weight check: loose soil ≈ 2,400 lb/yd³ — a 15 yd³ truck hauls ~18 tons at the legal limit in most states

Worked Example

Excavating a 30 ft × 20 ft pool shell 5 ft deep: 3,000 ft³ = 111 BCY. With 25% swell that's 139 loose yards — 10 fifteen-yard truck loads. At $150/load, haul-off costs $1,500, plus machine time and disposal tipping fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a swell factor? Soil occupies more volume loose than in the ground. Common earth swells about 25%, clay 35%, blasted rock 40–50%. Truck counts must use loose yards.

How many cubic yards are in a dump truck? Small dumps carry 10 yd³, standard single-axle/tandem dumps 12–16 yd³, tri-axles up to 20, and semi end-dumps 24–28.

How much does excavation cost? Machine excavation typically runs $50–$200 per hour or $1–$5 per bank cubic yard depending on access and soil; haul-off and disposal add $15–$60 per loose yard in many markets.

Do I need to keep soil for backfill? Yes — stockpile clean topsoil and suitable backfill on site. Hauling it away and buying it back can double your cost.
