Understanding Compound Interest

Compound interest is the most powerful force in finance — it's interest earned on both your principal and previously accumulated interest. Our calculator shows exactly how your money grows over time with optional monthly contributions.

The Compound Interest Formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

  • A = Final amount
  • P = Principal (initial investment)
  • r = Annual interest rate (as decimal, so 7% = 0.07)
  • n = Compounding periods per year (12 = monthly, 365 = daily)
  • t = Number of years

With monthly contributions (C), the formula adds: C × [((1 + r/n)^(n×t) - 1) / (r/n)]

The Power of Starting Early

Thanks to compound interest, starting early matters more than investing more. Consider two people:

  • Person A: Invests $200/month from age 25-35 ($24,000 total), then stops. At 65 at 7%, they have ~$245,000.
  • Person B: Invests $200/month from age 35-65 ($72,000 total). At 65 at 7%, they have ~$244,000.

Person A invested 1/3 as much but ended up with the same amount — because their money had 10 extra years to compound.

The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your interest rate to estimate doubling time: 72 ÷ 7% ≈ 10.3 years. At 10%, money doubles every 7.2 years. Use this quick mental shortcut to understand compound growth.

Real-World Investment Returns

  • S&P 500 (historical avg): ~10% annually (before inflation)
  • Bond market: ~4-5% annually
  • High-yield savings: ~4-5% (2024 rates)
  • Real estate: ~8-12% (including appreciation)

Always factor in inflation (~3% historical average). A 7% return with 3% inflation gives a real return of about 4%.
