🐕 Dog Crate Size for a German Shepherd

The 42-inch answer, when a 36 fits, and the long puppy runway

An adult German Shepherd needs a 42-inch crate. The typical adult measures 33 inches nose to tail base and stands 25 inches tall; 33 + 4 = 37 inches of interior length, which steps past the 36 to the 42, and the 42's roughly 29-inch interior height clears the 28 inches a tall Shepherd needs ears-up. Smaller females at 30 inches of length can use a 36. For a puppy, buy the 42 with a divider and expand monthly until 12 to 18 months. Flying: a 500-series kennel for the typical 75–85 pound adult.

Why the Height Check Matters for This Breed

German Shepherds are the breed where crate height, not just length, decides the size. A 25-inch-tall dog needs about 28 inches of interior height to stand without ducking, and 36-inch crates typically offer 26. So even a shorter-bodied Shepherd often lands in the 42 for headroom alone — the same reason tall working-line males sometimes need an extra-height 42 rather than the standard model. If your Shepherd's ears touch the roof when standing, the crate is a size small no matter what the floor tape says.

For travel, the 500-series kennel (about 40 inches of interior length, rated 71 to 90 pounds) fits the typical adult, with the same height caveat: verify ears-up clearance against the kennel's interior before flying, because IATA rules require the dog to stand fully erect.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size crate does a German Shepherd need?

A 42-inch crate for most adults. A typical German Shepherd measures about 33 inches from nose to tail base and stands 25 inches tall; 33 plus 4 is 37 inches, which steps past the 36-inch crate to the 42. The 42's roughly 29-inch interior height also clears the 28 inches a 25-inch-tall dog needs to stand comfortably.

Should I get a 36 or 42-inch crate for my German Shepherd?

Measure nose to tail base. At 32 inches or less — usually a smaller female — the 36 fits. At 33 inches or more, buy the 42. American show lines and bigger working males run 34 to 36 inches of body and are outright cramped in a 36; when in doubt with this breed, go 42, because Shepherds also sprawl flat when they sleep and use every inch you give them.

What size crate for a German Shepherd puppy?

Buy the adult 42-inch crate with a divider panel and allow length plus 4 inches of floor: about 20 inches at 8 weeks, 24 at 3 months, 28 at 5 months, 33 at 8 months, and the full crate by 12 to 18 months. Shepherds are slow to finish growing, so expect the divider to come out later than with a Lab — many males keep filling out past 18 months.

What airline kennel does a German Shepherd need?

A 500-series kennel (about 40 inches of interior length, rated for 71 to 90 pounds) fits most adult German Shepherds at 71 to 85 pounds. Verify interior height against your dog's ears-up height plus a few inches — IATA rules require standing fully erect without touching the roof, and tall working-line males sometimes need the extra-height version of the 500.
