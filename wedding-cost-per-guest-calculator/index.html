What each guest truly costs: the per-person stack plus the fixed pool behind it

Quick answer: the average US wedding costs $292 per guest — $34,200 across 117 guests, per The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study. Only about $133 is truly per-person (catering $80, bar $25, cake $7, rentals $12, favors $5, invitations $4); the rest is each guest's share of ~$18,600 in fixed costs. That's why cutting the list from 150 to 100 guests drops the total from $38,550 to $31,900 while the per-guest figure climbs from $257 to $319.

How the Cost Per Guest Calculator Works

"Cost per guest" is really two numbers wearing one label. The variable stack — catering, bar, cake, rentals, favors, stationery — is what each additional guest actually costs you. The fixed pool — venue site fee, photography, music, florals, planner, attire, rings — exists whether 40 or 400 people show up. This calculator splits them, then shows how the blend moves as you change the headcount.

The formula

Cost per guest = (variable per-guest stack × guest count + fixed pool) ÷ guest count. The low and high estimates apply the lean and lavish ends of each line's published band: $71-$260 variable per head, $8,050-$37,800 fixed. Editing a line uses your number as the typical; the band stays published.

How to use it

Start with the average preset and your real guest count, then edit the lines you've actually priced — a caterer quote replaces the $80 default the day you get it. For the fixed pool, the $18,600 default mirrors the Knot-average wedding; the reference table above itemizes where it comes from if you'd rather build your own. Try your count at 150, then 100: the total falls, the per-guest number rises, and you'll see exactly why venues push minimums.

A worked example, three ways

The national average reproduced: 117 guests, all default lines. Variable $133 × 117 = $15,561, plus $18,600 fixed = $34,161, or $292 a guest — within a dollar of the Knot's published figures. The lean-lavish band runs $16,357-$68,220.

Upscale at 100 guests: swap catering to $100 a plate and the bar to $35. Variable climbs to $163 a head; total = $16,300 + $18,600 = $34,900, or $349 a guest. Same headcount as the classic wedding, $10,000 more in feel.

Lean at 60 guests: budget lines ($71 variable) and a trimmed fixed pool — smaller venue, emerging photographer, DIY planner — of $9,900. Total = $4,260 + $9,900 = $14,160, or $236 a guest. Same math, opposite direction: the fixed pool is now 70% of the bill, so the next ten guests cost only $710 more.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost per wedding guest?

About $292 per guest. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study (10,474 US couples married in 2025) puts the average wedding at $34,200 across 117 guests. Roughly $133 of that $292 is genuinely per-person spending — catering, bar, cake, rentals, favors, invitations — and the rest is each guest's share of about $18,600 in fixed costs like the venue fee, photography, and music.

How much does catering cost per wedding guest?

Catering alone averages $80 per person nationally, before bar, cake, and rentals. Per-plate menus run about $50-$150 depending on service style and market: plated dinner at the top, buffet and family-style in the middle, and food stations or brunch lower. With bar, dessert, rentals, favors, and invitations added, the full variable stack runs $100-$260 per guest.

Does the cost per guest go up or down with more guests?

Both, which is the trap. Each guest truly costs the variable stack (about $133 at average), so a bigger list raises the total. But the fixed pool — venue fee, photographer, band, planner, rings, attire — gets divided across more people, so the per-guest average can fall. The Knot's tiers show the effect: under 50 guests averages about $340 a head, while 100-plus averages about $370 — luxury minimums push small weddings back up.

What wedding costs are fixed no matter the guest count?

About $18,600 of the average wedding: the venue site fee (~$4,600 of it), photography and video (~$3,400), music and entertainment (~$2,050), florals and decor (~$3,100), attire and beauty (~$2,050), wedding rings (~$1,710), the planner (~$1,370), and the officiant (~$340). None of these move when you add or cut ten guests — which is why trimming the list lowers the total but often raises the per-guest number.

How much does a 100-guest wedding cost?

The Knot's tier for 51-100 guests averages $27,200, and the default grid in this calculator puts a typical 100-guest wedding near $31,900, or $319 a head once the fixed pool is spread across 100 people. An upscale 100-guest version — $100-a-plate catering and a $35 bar — runs about $34,900. The swing between lean and lavish at the same headcount is bigger than most couples expect.

How do I calculate my wedding budget per guest?

Add up your per-person lines (catering, bar, cake, rentals, favors, stationery — about $133 typical), multiply by the guest count, then add the fixed pool (venue, photo, video, music, florals, planner, attire, rings — about $18,600 typical) and divide by guests. The formula exposes the lever: the fixed pool divided by headcount is what makes guest 150 cost less than guest 50.
