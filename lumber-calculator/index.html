How the Lumber Calculator Works

Enter your lumber's nominal dimensions and length, and this calculator computes board feet per piece, how many boards you need to cover a given run of linear feet, and the total cost. It uses nominal dimensions (e.g., a "2×4"), which is how lumber is both sold and priced by the board foot.

Formulas Used

  • Board feet (per piece): BF = thickness(in) × width(in) × length(ft) ÷ 12
  • Pieces needed: ceil(linear feet ÷ board length)
  • Total board feet: pieces × BF per piece
  • Cost: pieces × price per board

Worked Example

For 100 linear feet of 2×4 in 8-foot lengths: each board covers 8 linear feet, so pieces = ceil(100 ÷ 8) = 13 boards. Board feet per board = 2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 5.33 BF, so 13 boards = 69.3 board feet total. At $6 per board, that's $78 plus tax.

Nominal vs. Actual Dimensions

A 2×4 actually measures 1.5" × 3.5" after milling and drying. Board feet and lumber pricing use the nominal size, but always use actual dimensions when calculating framing spacings, spans, or finished openings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a board foot? A volume unit equal to a piece 12" × 12" × 1" thick — 144 cubic inches. Hardwood is priced per board foot; dimensional softwood is usually priced per piece.

How many board feet in a 2x4x8? 2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 5.33 board feet.

How do I estimate framing lumber for a wall? One stud per linear foot of wall at 16" on-center (plus plates), so a 12 ft wall needs about 10–12 studs plus top and bottom plates.

Should I add waste? Yes — add 10–15% for cuts, culls, and mistakes on any framing or trim order.
