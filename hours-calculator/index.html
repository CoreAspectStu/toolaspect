How to Calculate Hours and Minutes

This hours calculator finds the duration between two times, calculate weekly work hours for payroll, and add or subtract time periods. Track employee hours, plan shifts, or calculate billable time without manually counting minutes.

Calculating Time Duration

To find hours between two times: convert both to 24-hour format, subtract start from end. If the result is negative, the shift crossed midnight — add 24 hours. Example: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute break = 8.5 hours − 0.5 hours = 8.0 net hours.

Converting Minutes to Decimal Hours

For payroll, minutes must be converted to decimal hours: divide minutes by 60.

  • 15 minutes = 0.25 hours
  • 30 minutes = 0.50 hours
  • 45 minutes = 0.75 hours
  • 7 hours 30 minutes = 7.50 decimal hours

Timesheet Tips

  • Track time daily — don't try to reconstruct at the end of the week
  • Round to the nearest 15 minutes for clean calculations (7:53 → 8:00)
  • Include break times separately to get accurate net work hours
  • For overtime, calculate anything over 8 hours/day or 40 hours/week
  • Keep timesheets for at least 3 years for FLSA compliance

Working Hours Facts

  • Full-time work week: 40 hours (US standard)
  • Annual hours: ~2,080 hours (40 hrs × 52 weeks)
  • After holidays: ~2,000 hours (10 holidays)
  • Overtime threshold: >40 hours/week (US FLSA)
  • EU work week limit: 48 hours max (including overtime)

Worked Examples: Real Shifts Through the Calculator

A split shift with a lunch break: clock in at 8:42 AM, out at 6:17 PM, with a 45-minute unpaid lunch. From 8:42 to 18:17 is 9 hours 35 minutes; subtract 45 minutes for a net 8 hours 50 minutes, which is 8.83 decimal hours. At $20/hour that shift pays $176.67. The duration tab above does all three steps in one pass.

An overnight shift: 10:15 PM to 6:45 AM. The end time reads smaller than the start, which means the shift crossed midnight — add 24 hours to the difference and you get 8 hours 30 minutes, or 8.50 decimal hours. Night-shift premiums stack on top where employers pay them; the base arithmetic doesn't change.

A weekly pattern: five 9:00-to-17:30 days with 30-minute breaks gives 8.0 net hours a day, 40.0 for the week — right at the federal overtime line with zero margin. Add one Saturday of the same shift and the week reads 48 hours, 8 of them overtime. That's the number the timesheet tab totals for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate hours between two times?

Convert both times to 24-hour format, subtract the start from the end, and add 24 hours if the result is negative (an overnight shift). Subtract any unpaid break to get net work hours. 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute break is 8.0 net hours.

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours for payroll?

Divide the minutes by 60: 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, 45 minutes is 0.75, and 7 hours 30 minutes is 7.50 decimal hours. Multiply decimal hours by the hourly rate to get pay — 8.83 hours at $20/hour is $176.67.

Do breaks count as hours worked?

Short rest breaks of 5 to 20 minutes are paid time under federal rules; bona fide meal periods of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if the employee is fully relieved of duty. Subtract unpaid breaks in the calculator's break field so net hours match the paycheck.

How does overtime work after 40 hours?

For non-exempt employees, federal law requires at least 1.5× the regular rate for every hour past 40 in a workweek. Some states add daily overtime — California pays 1.5× after 8 hours and 2× after 12 in a day, on top of the weekly rule.

How do I calculate hours for an overnight shift?

When the end time is earlier than the start time, the shift crossed midnight: add 24 hours to the raw difference. A 10:15 PM to 6:45 AM shift works out to 8 hours 30 minutes, or 8.50 decimal hours before premiums.
