How to Use the Word Unscrambler

Enter any combination of scrambled letters and instantly find all valid English words that can be made from those letters. This tool finds both full anagrams (using all letters) and partial words (using some letters). Perfect for word games like Scrabble, Words With Friends, and anagram puzzles.

Use the length filter to show only words of a specific length, or the starts-with and contains filters to narrow down results. Click any word to copy it. Works entirely in your browser with no server needed.

Tips for Finding More Words

Look for common prefixes (re-, un-, dis-) and suffixes (-ing, -ed, -tion, -ly) within your letters. Try rearranging letters around common bigrams like TH, CH, SH, QU, and ST. Shorter words are often overlooked but can be high-value plays in word games.

Scrabble Letter Values Reference

Most word unscramblers exist to serve crossword solvers and tile games, so it helps to know what each letter is worth when you play a result. The standard English Scrabble tile distribution contains 100 tiles, and the letter values below follow that standard set. When you unscramble letters here, you can score your candidate words yourself using this table — high-value letters like Q, Z, J, and X are worth 8 to 10 points each, while common vowels are worth just 1.

Strategy follows directly from the values. A word that dumps a Q or a Z on a double-letter square usually beats a longer word made only of one-point letters, and unscrambling a way to use the J in your rack is often better than saving it. How the Unscrambler Works

Under the hood, the tool compares your letter multiset against a large English word list using letter-frequency counting: a candidate word matches only if every letter it needs is available in the quantities you entered. That means repeats matter — entering two E's finds words with up to two E's, not three. Results are sorted longest-first so the full anagrams and highest-scoring plays appear at the top, with alphabetical order breaking ties within each length.

Because the search runs client-side in JavaScript, nothing you type is sent to a server. There is no rate limit, no sign-up, and no delay beyond your own device's processing. The dictionary embedded in the page covers common English words plus many rarer entries, which is why some results may be unfamiliar — tournament word lists include thousands of short, obscure two- and three-letter words that are perfectly legal in competitive play.

Anagrams vs. Partial Words

A full anagram uses every letter you supply exactly once — CINEMA from ICENAM is a classic six-letter example. Partial words use a subset of your letters, which is what makes unscramblers so useful in tile games where you rarely want to empty the whole rack. When you search here, both kinds appear together: a six-letter result from six letters is your full anagram; anything shorter is a partial. For puzzle clues that specify a letter count, use the length filter buttons to jump straight to that size.

The starts-with and contains filters help when you know part of the answer — a crossword's crossing letters, for example, or a Words With Friends board where a specific opening square rewards a word beginning with a particular letter. Combine filters freely; each one narrows the live result set instantly.
