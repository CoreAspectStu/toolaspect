About Percentage Calculations

Percentages are one of the most widely used mathematical concepts in everyday life. From calculating discounts at the store to figuring out tips at restaurants, understanding tax rates, analyzing financial growth, and interpreting statistics, percentages provide a universal way to express proportions and changes. The word "percent" literally means "per hundred"—when you say 25%, you're saying 25 out of every 100. This simple concept underlies countless real-world calculations, and our percentage calculator handles all the common variations instantly as you type.

Percentage Formulas Explained

There are several core percentage formulas, each serving a different purpose. Understanding these formulas will help you solve virtually any percentage problem you encounter:

1. What is X% of Y? — This is the most basic percentage calculation. The formula is Result = (X / 100) × Y. For example, to find 15% of 200: (15 / 100) × 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30. This formula is commonly used for calculating discounts, tips, commissions, and tax amounts.

2. X is what percent of Y? — To find what percentage one number is of another, use Percentage = (X / Y) × 100. For example, to find what percentage 30 is of 150: (30 / 150) × 100 = 0.2 × 100 = 20%. This is useful for calculating test scores, market share, completion rates, and comparing proportions.

3. Percentage change (increase or decrease): — To find how much something changed in percentage terms, use Change% = ((New - Original) / |Original|) × 100. For example, going from 80 to 100: ((100 - 80) / 80) × 100 = (20 / 80) × 100 = 25% increase. This is essential for analyzing sales growth, price changes, and performance metrics.

4. Add or subtract a percentage: — To add X% to a value, use Result = Y + (X / 100 × Y) = Y × (1 + X/100). To subtract, use Result = Y - (X / 100 × Y) = Y × (1 - X/100). For example, adding 10% tip to a $50 bill: 50 + (10/100 × 50) = 50 + 5 = $55. This formula powers tip calculators, tax adders, and discount appliers.

Real-World Percentage Examples

Here are practical examples that demonstrate how percentages are used in everyday situations:

Shopping discounts: A $120 jacket is on sale for 35% off. What's the sale price? First, find 35% of $120: (35/100) × 120 = $42. Then subtract from the original price: 120 - 42 = $78. Alternatively, use the "Subtract %" tab: enter 35 and 120 to get $78 directly.

Restaurant tips: Your dinner bill is $67.50 and you want to leave an 18% tip. Use the "Add %" tab: enter 18 and 67.50. The tip amount is (18/100) × 67.50 = $12.15, making the total $79.65. For quick mental math, 20% is simply moving the decimal point one place left and doubling it: $6.75 × 2 = $13.50.

Sales tax calculation: You're buying a $999 TV in a state with 8.25% sales tax. Use the "Add %" tab: enter 8.25 and 999. The tax is $82.42, making the total $1,081.42. Some states don't tax certain items like groceries or clothing under a threshold amount—always check local regulations.

School grades: You scored 87 points out of 100 on an exam. Use the "What %?" tab: enter 87 and 100. Your score is 87%. If the test was out of 120 instead, enter 87 and 120 to get 72.5%.

Salary increase: Your salary went from $65,000 to $72,000. Use the "% Change" tab: enter 65000 and 72000. The result is +10.77% increase. This helps evaluate whether a raise keeps pace with inflation or market rates.

Cooking and recipes: A recipe calls for 250g of flour but you want to make 150% of the recipe for a larger group. Use the "% of Number" tab: enter 150 and 250. The result is 375g—the amount of flour you need. Scaling recipes by percentage ensures ingredient ratios stay correct.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage?

To calculate X% of a number Y, divide X by 100 to get a decimal, then multiply by Y. For example, to find 20% of 50: 20 ÷ 100 = 0.20, then 0.20 × 50 = 10. You can also convert the percentage to a fraction: 20% = 20/100 = 1/5, so 1/5 of 50 = 10. This calculator does the math instantly—just enter the percentage and the number in the "% of Number" tab.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

To calculate percentage increase, use the formula: ((New Value - Original Value) / Original Value) × 100. For example, if a price increases from $40 to $50: ((50 - 40) / 40) × 100 = (10 / 40) × 100 = 25%. The result is a 25% increase. Use the "% Change" tab on this calculator—just enter the original value (40) and the new value (50).

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

Percentage decrease uses the same formula as increase: ((New Value - Original Value) / Original Value) × 100. If the result is negative, it's a decrease. For example, going from $80 to $60: ((60 - 80) / 80) × 100 = (-20 / 80) × 100 = -25%, meaning a 25% decrease. The "% Change" tab automatically labels it as a decrease when the value drops.

How do I calculate a discount?

To calculate a discount, find the discount amount (X% of the original price), then subtract it from the original price. For example, a $200 item with a 30% discount: the discount is 30% × $200 = $60, so the sale price is $200 - $60 = $140. Use the "Subtract %" tab with values 30 and 200 to get $140 directly. To find what you saved, use the "% of Number" tab: 30% of 200 = $60.

How do I calculate a tip?

To calculate a tip, multiply the bill amount by the tip percentage (as a decimal). For an 18% tip on a $45 bill: 0.18 × $45 = $8.10. The total with tip is $53.10. Use the "Add %" tab with values 18 and 45 to get the total directly. Common tip amounts are 15% (adequate service), 18% (good service), and 20% (excellent service).

How do I calculate sales tax?

Multiply the pre-tax price by the tax rate (as a decimal) to find the tax amount, then add it to the original price. For a $100 item with 7% tax: 0.07 × $100 = $7 tax, total = $107. Use the "Add %" tab with values 7 and 100 to get $107. Some regions have compound tax (GST + PST), so calculate each tax separately if your area requires it.

What is the difference between percentage and percentile?

A percentage represents a part of a whole (e.g., you scored 85% on a test means 85 out of 100). A percentile indicates rank—scoring in the 85th percentile means you performed better than 85% of test takers. Percentages compare to a maximum, while percentiles compare to other people's results.
