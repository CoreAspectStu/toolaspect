What a Texas Speeding Ticket Really Costs

Texas speeding tickets look cheap and aren't. The base fine for a typical 10-15 over citation runs about $200-300 once municipal court costs land on top, and the old Driver Responsibility Program fees are gone since 2019. What's left is the classic structure: a manageable court number, then the insurance consequence, which on a $2,200 premium at 24% adds up to $1,584 across three years of surcharge. That's about seven times the fine.

Texas runs a points system (2 points for a moving violation, 3 if it caused a crash), but points themselves carry no fee anymore; they exist so insurers can see the conviction. That makes deferred adjudication and defensive driving the two levers that matter. Deferred adjudication, roughly $100-150 plus costs, defers the finding of guilt for 90-180 days; complete it clean and the ticket is dismissed, keeping your record and premium intact. The defensive driving option costs about $25-50 in tuition plus roughly $114 in court fees, dismisses one ticket every 12 months, and is available for most non-commercial speeding citations.

The decision rule falls out of the arithmetic above: if the total surcharge ($1,584 at the default premium) exceeds the cost of deferral or driving school (call it $165-175), take the deferral. It almost always does. Only when you've used both options within their windows does paying make sense, and even then, shopping your insurance at renewal, since a 24% hike is an average, not a law, can claw back some of the increase. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a speeding ticket in Texas?

About $200-300 all-in for 10-15 mph over, county dependent; Harris County lists plain speeding at $185 flat. The base fine is small, but municipal court costs and fees roughly double it. The real cost is insurance: a 24% average increase on a $2,200 policy is $1,584 over three years, for roughly $1,807 total.

How do I keep a Texas speeding ticket off my record?

Two main routes: deferred adjudication (about $100-150 plus court costs, dismissed after a clean 90-180 days) or a defensive driving course (about $25-50 tuition plus roughly $114 in court fees, one dismissal per 12 months). Both keep the conviction from your insurer, which is where the money is.

How many points is a speeding ticket in Texas?

Two points for a moving violation, three if the violation caused a crash. Since the Driver Responsibility Program ended in 2019, the points carry no state fee; their impact is on your insurance rating instead.

How much does insurance go up after a ticket in Texas?

Around 22-25% at renewal, per national analyses from ValuePenguin, LendingTree, and The Zebra, for about three years. On a $2,200 full-coverage premium that is roughly $528 a year. Shopping carriers at renewal matters: increases vary meaningfully by insurer.
