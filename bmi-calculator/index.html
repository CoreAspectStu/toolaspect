What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a numerical value derived from your height and weight. It provides a simple screening method for categorizing whether a person is underweight, at a normal weight, overweight, or obese. The formula was developed by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s and is now used worldwide by health organizations including the WHO, CDC, and NHS.

BMI is calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by height in meters squared: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For imperial units, the formula is BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)². Our calculator handles both systems automatically.

How to Calculate BMI Manually

Metric Calculation

  1. Measure your height in meters (e.g., 170 cm = 1.70 m)
  2. Measure your weight in kilograms (e.g., 70 kg)
  3. Square your height: 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89
  4. Divide weight by squared height: 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2

Imperial Calculation

  1. Measure height in inches (e.g., 5'7" = 67 inches)
  2. Measure weight in pounds (e.g., 154 lbs)
  3. Square height: 67 × 67 = 4,489
  4. Multiply weight by 703: 154 × 703 = 108,262
  5. Divide by squared height: 108,262 ÷ 4,489 = 24.1

Limitations of BMI

While BMI is a useful screening tool, it has important limitations:

  • Doesn't measure body fat directly: BMI uses total weight, which includes muscle, bone, and water weight.
  • May overestimate for muscular individuals: Athletes and bodybuilders may have a high BMI but low body fat.
  • May underestimate for elderly: Older adults may have normal BMI but high body fat percentage.
  • Doesn't account for fat distribution: Abdominal fat (visceral fat) is more dangerous than fat stored in hips and thighs.
  • Doesn't account for age, sex, or ethnicity: Body composition varies significantly across demographics.

For a more complete health assessment, combine BMI with waist circumference, body fat percentage, and blood tests. Always consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice.

What is a healthy BMI?

Between 18.5 and 24.9 for most adults. This range has the lowest risk of weight-related health problems.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)².

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so muscular athletes may have a high BMI despite low body fat. Body fat percentage is a better metric for athletes.

What BMI is considered obese?

BMI of 30 or higher. Class I (30-34.9), Class II (35-39.9), Class III (40+, severe obesity).

Should I use BMI for children?

Children use age- and sex-specific percentile charts, not the adult categories. Consult a pediatrician for children's growth assessment.

How often should I check my BMI?

Monthly or quarterly is sufficient. Daily weight fluctuations don't reflect real body composition changes.
