How the Wedding Budget Calculator Works

A wedding budget fails in two directions: a total with no plan, which quietly overspends line by line, or a spreadsheet so detailed nobody maintains it. This calculator takes the middle path: one total, one guest count, and twelve allocations scaled from what couples in The Knot's study paid their vendors.

Where the percentages come from

The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study reports what couples paid each kind of vendor, in dollars. It doesn't publish a percentage split, so the twelve shares here are this calculator's own: the study's vendor averages grouped into twelve categories and scaled to add up to 100%. The reception pair (venue plus catering) gets 53% of the budget, photo and video 10%, and everything else shares the remaining 37%. The same percentages are applied to whatever total you enter.

How to use it

Enter your total budget and guest count. The per-guest number is your reality check against the $292 national average. Under $200, something has to give (usually the list or the venue). Pick your region to see your budget as a share of the typical wedding there: the same $30,000 is 103% of the Midwest average and 62% of the Mid-Atlantic average. Then treat the allocation table as a negotiation grid, not a rulebook.

A worked example

A $30,000 budget with 100 guests allocates as follows: venue and rentals $8,700 (29%), catering cake and drinks $7,200 (24%), photo and video $3,000 (10%), florals and decor $2,700 (9%), music $1,800 (6%), attire and beauty $1,800 (6%), rings $1,500 (5%), planner $1,200 (4%), guest entertainment $900 (3%), transportation $600 (2%), stationery $300 (1%), and officiant $300 (1%). The twelve lines sum exactly to $30,000, and the reception alone claims $15,900.

Per guest, that's $300, which is 3% over the national average, or about $800 total. The fastest rebalance: ten fewer guests at the $80-per-plate catering average frees $800 of food plus the associated bar, rentals, and invitations, without touching a single vendor's price.

Why Are the Category Lines Lower Than The Knot's Vendor Averages?

Because the percentages are this calculator's own split, not a table The Knot publishes. The Knot's cost article lists what couples paid each vendor in dollars. Add up one of everything (with a DJ, not a band, and catering at $80 for 117 guests) and you get $50,660, well over the $34,200 average. The likely reason is that couples hire 13 vendors on average, not every one on the list. The calculator scales those figures down to total 100%, so most lines come out lower than the vendor's own average.

What Does the Calculator Leave Out?

First, a reserve: the twelve categories always add up to 100% of what you enter. The Knot suggests holding back 5%, and Zola says service charges, gratuities and overtime typically add 9% to 15% beyond vendor quotes. On $30,000 that's $1,500 at 5%, or $2,700 to $4,500 at Zola's range, so enter your budget minus the reserve. Our tip calculator handles gratuities once you know each vendor's rate.

Second, the costs The Knot counts outside the wedding total: the engagement ring ($4,600), rehearsal dinner ($2,800) and honeymoon ($5,500). Those three add $12,900, which takes the average to $47,100. The honeymoon budget calculator and engagement ring budget calculator cover two of them. Third, you can't edit the percentages on the page, so rebalance on paper.

Why Does Zola Say $36,000 When The Knot Says $34,200?

They're different surveys. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study covers 10,474 couples who married in 2025: $34,200 across an average of 117 guests. Zola's 2026 First Look Report surveyed more than 11,500 couples getting married in 2026 and reports $36,000 with an average of 145 guests. Divide Zola's two numbers and you get about $248 per guest, under The Knot's $292. This calculator's comparisons use The Knot's figures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average wedding budget in the US?

The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study puts the average at $34,200, or $292 per guest across an average of 117 guests. That average hides a huge spread: $17,100 for weddings of 50 guests or fewer, $27,200 for 51 to 100, and $43,300 for over 100. Region matters too, from about $29,000 in the Midwest to $48,400 in the Mid-Atlantic.

How should I split my wedding budget?

Start from this calculator's default split, which is scaled from the average vendor costs in The Knot's 2026 study: venue and rentals 29%, catering cake and drinks 24%, photo and video 10%, florals and decor 9%, music 6%, attire and beauty 6%, rings 5%, planner 4%, guest entertainment 3%, transportation 2%, stationery 1%, and officiant 1%. Venue plus catering is 53% of the total, so locking those two numbers first anchors everything else.

How much should I budget per wedding guest?

The national average works out to $292 per guest. Catering alone averages $80 per person, and that's before bar, cake, rentals, and invitations, which is why the per-guest total runs three to four times the plate price. If your budget divided by your guest count falls well under $200, trimming the list or the venue tier usually closes the gap faster than squeezing every vendor.

What's the biggest wedding expense?

The reception. This calculator gives venue and rentals 29% of the budget and catering, cake, and drinks another 24%, so the party as a whole takes 53%. The single largest vendor line in The Knot's 2026 study is the reception venue, averaging $12,900.

What costs are not included in the average wedding budget?

The $34,200 average covers the wedding day itself. The Knot reports the engagement ring (average $4,600), rehearsal dinner ($2,800), and honeymoon ($5,500) separately. If you're planning the full engagement-to-honeymoon arc, those three add $12,900 and take the average to $47,100.

Can I plan a wedding on a $15,000 budget?

Yes, but the percentages have to bend. A $15,000 budget at the $292 national average covers about 50 guests, or 65 guests at a leaner $230. Couples who pull it off usually pick an off-peak date, a non-traditional venue (restaurants, parks, backyards), a daytime brunch reception, and a single photographer instead of a photo-plus-video team.

Does changing the guest count or region change the category amounts?

No. The twelve category amounts depend only on your total budget. Guest count drives the budget-per-guest figure and the comparison with the $292 national average, and region drives the percentage-of-typical readout. A $30,000 budget shows $8,700 for venue and rentals and $7,200 for catering, cake and drinks at any guest count and in any region.

How much of a $20,000 wedding budget goes to the venue and catering?

In this calculator, $10,600. That is 53% of the total: $5,800 for venue and rentals and $4,800 for catering, cake and drinks. Photo and video get $2,000. With 100 guests the budget works out to $200 per guest, which is 32% under The Knot's $292 average, so a $20,000 wedding usually means a guest list closer to 68 or a cheaper venue and menu.
