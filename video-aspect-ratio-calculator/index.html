About the Video Aspect Ratio Calculator

Aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between a video's width and height, expressed as width:height. 16:9 is the widescreen standard for YouTube, TVs, and nearly all online video; 9:16 is the vertical format of Shorts, Reels, and TikTok; 4:3 is the legacy television and older YouTube era; 21:9 and wider appear in cinematic and ultrawide contexts; and 1:1 square video serves feed placements on social platforms. This calculator converts between width, height, and ratio so you can resize, letterbox, or crop footage to any target.

The arithmetic is a proportion. Given width and height, the ratio is width ÷ height reduced to lowest terms (1920 ÷ 1080 = 16 ÷ 9). Given a ratio and one dimension, the other is a cross-multiplication: at 16:9, a height of 1080 implies width = 1080 × 16 ÷ 9 = 1920. The tool also computes pixel count (width × height) for quick resolution comparisons and detects the nearest common ratio for any odd dimensions you feed it.

Where the practical confusion lives: containers versus pixels. A 16:9 player can display 4:3 content by pillarboxing (bars left and right) or a 2.35:1 film by letterboxing (bars top and bottom). Anamorphic formats add another wrinkle — the pixels are stored stretched and displayed at a different ratio — which is why a file's stored dimensions can mislead about its display ratio without the metadata.

To use the calculator, enter any two of width, height, and ratio — the third is solved for you, along with total pixel count. A common workflow: type your source resolution, pick the target ratio such as 9:16 for vertical clips, and read off the largest matching dimensions that keep your pixels within bounds.

Check your export settings against the result as well: player-side scaling cannot add detail, so downscaling a 16:9 master to 9:16 in your editor — rather than letting a platform crop the center automatically — keeps control over what stays in frame and what gets cut.

Common Video Resolutions by Ratio

The table pairs each standard ratio with its canonical resolutions. YouTube accepts a range of 16:9 resolutions from 4K down, and its player letterboxes anything non-16:9 automatically; vertical 9:16 uploads to a normal video are shown with pillarbox bars unless published as Shorts.

Resolution names come from the shorter dimension: 1080p means 1080 lines of vertical resolution. 4K UHD is 3840 × 2160 (16:9); DCI 4K is 4096 × 2160 (roughly 1.9:1), a cinema format distinct from consumer UHD — a distinction that matters when matching footage to deliverables.

Cropping and Padding Between Ratios

Converting between ratios means cropping, padding, or distorting — the calculator shows the exact dimensions for each clean path. Going from 16:9 to 9:16 by center-cropping keeps full height but discards the left and right thirds (1920-wide source becomes effectively 607-pixel-wide usable area at 1080 height). Going from 16:9 to 1:1 keeps 1080 × 1080 from the center. Padding preserves all content at the cost of bars. Distorting — stretching to fill — is almost never correct for people and text.

The professional workflow for repurposing horizontal footage vertically is not a blind crop: reframe shot by shot so the subject stays in frame, since a center crop presumes a centered subject. This is why editors treat the calculator's numbers as the geometry constraint and make the framing decision creatively.

Aspect Ratio in Photography and Displays

The same math governs stills and screens. Full-frame and most mirrorless cameras shoot 3:2; Micro Four Thirds is 4:3; smartphones typically capture 4:3 with 16:9 as a cropped option. Computer displays follow the same ratios — 16:9 dominates, 16:10 (as in many laptops) gives slightly more vertical room, and 21:9 ultrawides approximate cinema scope. When a photo's ratio differs from the display's, the same letterbox/pillarbox logic applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the aspect ratio of 1920×1080?

16:9 — divide both numbers by 120 (1920 ÷ 120 = 16, 1080 ÷ 120 = 9). It is the standard widescreen HD resolution used by YouTube and most online video.

How do I convert 16:9 video to 9:16?

Either center-crop to 1080 × 1920 (discarding the left and right of the frame) or pad the full 16:9 image with bars above and below. Cropping keeps full sharpness; padding keeps full content. Reframe per shot if subjects aren't centered.

What resolution should vertical Shorts be?

1080 × 1920 (9:16) is the standard vertical resolution for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and TikTok.

How do I calculate aspect ratio from width and height?

Divide width by height and reduce: 2560 ÷ 1080 = 2.370 ≈ 21:9. Or express it as a decimal multiplier (2.37x) when the exact ratio isn't a common one.

Why do black bars appear on videos?

The video's ratio differs from the display's, so the player letterboxes (bars top/bottom for wider content) or pillarboxes (bars left/right for narrower content) rather than stretch the image.
