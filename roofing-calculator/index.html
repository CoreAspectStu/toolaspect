About the Roofing Calculator

This roofing calculator converts your roof's dimensions and pitch into squares — the trade's unit of measurement — then estimates bundles of shingles, rolls of underlayment, ridge cap, and nails. All math runs locally in your browser.

Roofing material is never quoted in square feet by professionals; it is quoted in squares, and once you speak that language, comparing bids and ordering materials becomes straightforward.

How Roofing Quantities Are Calculated

One roofing square covers 100 square feet. Sloped roof area = footprint area × pitch multiplier (for example, sqrt(144+36) ÷ 12 = 1.118 at a 6/12 pitch). Squares = sloped area ÷ 100. Shingles come three bundles to the square for standard three-tab products, so bundles = squares × 3, then add 10 percent for waste at valleys, hips, and rakes.

Underlayment comes in rolls that commonly cover 400 square feet (four squares) with the required overlap, so rolls = squares ÷ 4 rounded up. Ridge and hips take special cap shingles — figure roughly one bundle of hip/ridge cap per 35 to 40 linear feet. Nails go at about 320 per square for standard four-nail application, which is close to one standard 5-pound coil or 1-pound stick box per square; caps, starter course, and drip edge sit on top of that.

Measuring Your Roof Correctly

Measure each roof plane's length and width from the ground or a ladder, multiply per plane, and add them. For complex roofs with hips and valleys, measure every plane separately — a dormer adds two or three small planes plus valley waste on each.

Do not subtract skylights and chimneys from shingle quantities; the flashing and cuts around them consume nearly as much material as the hole removes. If your existing shingles have three or more layers, removal and dumpster costs may rival the material budget — most codes allow no more than two overlay layers before a tear-off is required.

Budgeting Beyond Shingles

A realistic material list includes starter strip (or shingles reversed as starter) along eaves, ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys in cold climates, drip edge on all edges, pipe boots and step flashing, ridge vent if you are venting, and sealant. These ancillary items commonly total 15 to 25 percent of the shingle cost.

Labor typically runs more than materials on a full tear-off and replace: published industry cost surveys consistently place total professional replacement for an average home in the low-to-mid five figures in the US, varying with region, pitch, and layers removed. Always get itemized bids that list squares, tear-off layers, and flashing work separately so quotes are comparable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bundles of shingles make a square?

Standard three-tab shingles come three bundles per square (100 sq ft). Heavier architectural products may also be three bundles per square in most lines, but always check the bundle coverage printed on the wrapper — a few premium lines require four or more.

How do I calculate how many squares my roof is?

Multiply each plane's length × width in feet, sum all planes, then divide by 100. If you only know the footprint, multiply by the pitch multiplier first — for example, a 1,200 sq ft footprint at 6/12 pitch is 1,200 × 1.118 = 1,342 sq ft ≈ 13.4 squares.

How much waste factor should I add for shingles?

Ten percent is standard for gable roofs; use 12 to 15 percent for roofs with multiple hips, valleys, and dormers, since every intersecting edge produces cutting waste. Round bundles up to full units — leftovers are returnable at most suppliers.

How many nails do I need per square of shingles?

Standard four-nail application for three-tab shingles uses about 320 nails per square (four nails per shingle, with three tabs per strip and 26 strips per bundle). High-wind areas specify six nails per shingle, which raises the count roughly 50 percent.

What is a square in roofing?

A square is 100 square feet of roof area — the universal quoting unit in US roofing. A 2,000 square foot roof is 20 squares, and shingle bundles, underlayment rolls, and labor rates are all priced against it.
