How the Aggregate Base Calculator Works

Every paver patio, concrete slab, and asphalt driveway starts with compacted aggregate base — road base, Class II, crusher run, or 3/4-inch minus. Enter the area and the compacted thickness you need to achieve, and this calculator converts volume to tons using the industry-standard density of about 1.4 tons per cubic yard for compacted base rock, plus cubic yards and a delivered cost. The waste factor covers uneven subgrade and edge loss during spreading.

Formulas Used

  • Compacted volume: ft³ = length × width × (thickness ÷ 12)
  • Cubic yards: yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27
  • Tons: tons = yd³ × density, where compacted road base ≈ 1.4 tons/yd³
  • Rule of thumb: 1 ton of compacted base covers ≈ 100 sq ft at 2 in, 50 sq ft at 4 in

Worked Example

A 20 × 12 ft paver patio with 4 in of compacted base: 20 × 12 × 0.333 = 80 ft³ = 2.96 yd³. At 1.4 tons/yd³ that is 4.2 tons — call it 4.5 with a 5% waste factor. At $35/ton delivered, material runs about $155. Note that you order loose but pay for the compacted quantity: base compacts about 15–20%, so suppliers' "loose ton" coverage differs slightly from this compacted math.

Frequently Asked Questions

How thick should aggregate base be? Paver patios and walkways: 4 in. Residential driveways (pavers or asphalt): 6–8 in. Under concrete slabs on good soil: 4 in. Poor soil or heavy loads: consult an engineer.

How many tons in a cubic yard of road base? Compacted base rock weighs roughly 1.4 tons (2,800 lb) per cubic yard; loose in the truck it's closer to 1.25 tons/yd³. Crushed stone products run 1.35–1.5 depending on gradation.

How much does 1 ton cover? At 4 in compacted, one ton covers about 50 sq ft; at 2 in, about 100 sq ft. For a 2-car driveway at 6 in, expect roughly 14–15 tons per 500 sq ft.

Do I compact in lifts? Yes — compact base in 2–3 in lifts with a plate compactor for each lift. A single deep lift will not achieve the density that gives you a stable surface.
