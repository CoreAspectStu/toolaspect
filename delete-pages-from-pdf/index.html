How to Delete Pages from a PDF

Deleting a page should mean the page is gone, and here it does: the exporter copies only the pages you kept into a fresh document, so anything you removed — the duplicate agenda, the blank scan, the page with last quarter's numbers — never exists in the output file at all.

Step by step

  • Open the PDF. Every page shows up as a numbered thumbnail.
  • Click × on each page to delete. The counter updates ("10 of 12 pages will be in the output").
  • Changed your mind? The strip below the toolbar lists every deleted page with a one-click restore.
  • Download. The file keeps your original name with -organized appended.

A worked example

A 12-page board packet contains a duplicated agenda on page 3 and a blank back-scan on page 9. Open the file, click × on both thumbnails, and the status line reads "10 of 12 pages will be in the output." Download and the new PDF contains pages 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12 in their original order — no page 3, no page 9, and nothing from either one searchable in the copy. The 12-page original stays untouched for the archive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I delete pages from a PDF for free?

Open the file in a thumbnail-based page tool, click the × on each page you want removed, and download the rebuilt PDF. This one runs entirely in your browser — no account, no watermark, and no upload of the document to anyone's server.

Are deleted pages really removed from the file?

Yes. The download is a new PDF assembled by copying only the pages you kept, so the deleted pages — their text, images, and form fields — are not present in the output at all. They're not hidden, commented out, or recoverable from the downloaded copy; keep your original if you might need them back.

Can I undo a deletion after downloading?

Not from the downloaded file — once pages are left out, rebuilding them means returning to the original document. Inside the tool, though, every deleted page sits in a restore strip until you download, so a mis-click costs you one click, not the file.

Why did my file size barely change after deleting pages?

Usually because the pages you removed were text-only while the remaining pages carry images, embedded fonts, or scanned content that dominates the file's size. The output only contains the kept pages' resources, so deleting the heavy pages is what visibly shrinks the file.
