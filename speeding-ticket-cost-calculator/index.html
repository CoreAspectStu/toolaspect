🚨 Speeding Ticket Cost Calculator

The fine is the small part. Price the fine, the assessments, and the insurance surcharge.

The average legal fine runs about $150, but insurers add the real bill: a speeding ticket raises full-coverage premiums about 22-25%, roughly $500-590 a year, for three to five years. On a $1,800 annual policy that's $1,296 of surcharge stacked on a $223 Texas ticket, for a true cost of $1,519. That's why "just pay it" is often the most expensive option, and why a $150 traffic-school course or a plea to a non-moving violation can be the best money you spend all year.

How the Speeding Ticket Cost Calculator Works

Three lines of arithmetic. The fine line is your all-in court number (base fine plus assessments and costs). The surcharge line is premium × increase × years, which is the piece most people never price. Total cost is their sum, and the contest budget is the most useful output: it's how much you could spend fighting the ticket, on an attorney, traffic school, or a day in court, and still come out ahead.

A worked example, checked by hand

Texas, 15 over, $1,800 annual premium. Fine: $223. Surcharge at 24%: 1,800 × 0.24 = $432 a year, × 3 years = $1,296. True cost: $223 + $1,296 = $1,519. The surcharge is 5.8 times the fine, which is why the calculator shows the ratio.

Second example, worse band: California, 25 over, $2,200 premium. Fine about $367 (16-25 over after assessments). Surcharge at 30%: 2,200 × 0.30 × 3 = $1,980. Total: $2,347. In this case, spending up to about $2,000 to keep the conviction off your record, through counsel or a plea to a non-moving violation, would break even.

Why the surcharge dominates

Insurers don't price the fine; they price the risk signal. One speeding conviction tells them you're measurably more likely to file a claim, and they charge for that expectation for the whole time the violation rates, typically three years. A $150 ticket on a $2,400 policy at 25% is $1,800 of premium over the surcharge window: twelve times the fine. Since the hit lands at renewal, you usually have weeks between citation and consequence to act, and that window is where traffic school, deferred adjudication (Texas), or a negotiation lives.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a speeding ticket cost?

The fine itself averages about $150 nationally, but the all-in total for a first offense typically lands between $150 and $400 once penalty assessments and court costs are stacked on. California is the poster child: a $35 base fine becomes roughly $234 after assessments. The bigger number is the insurance surcharge, which averages 22-25%, or $500-590 a year, and runs three to five years. A $223 Texas ticket with a $1,800 premium costs about $1,519 all-in over three years.

How much does insurance go up after a speeding ticket?

Expect a 22-25% bump at renewal, per ValuePenguin, LendingTree, and The Zebra analyses, worth roughly $500-590 a year on a typical full-coverage policy. Going far over the limit pushes it higher: increases up to about 36% are reported for the worst bands. The increase shows up at your policy renewal, not the day of the ticket, and fades after 3 to 5 years depending on state and insurer.

Is it worth fighting a speeding ticket?

Run the math against the surcharge, not the fine. If contesting costs a $100-300 attorney or a day off work but avoids a 24% rate hike worth $1,300 over three years, the economics favor fighting. Options include pleading to a non-moving violation (no insurance points), traffic school where the state allows it, or challenging the officer's speed measurement. Deferred adjudication in states like Texas keeps the ticket off your record for a fee.

Which states have the highest speeding fines?

By typical all-in first-offense cost for 10-15 mph over, California leads at about $234 minimum, followed by Arizona ($231), Nevada and Texas (about $223 each). Georgia adds a $200 Super Speeder fee for 75+ on two-lane roads or 85+ anywhere. Virginia's reckless driving threshold (80 mph or 20 over) turns a ticket into a criminal misdemeanor with fines to $2,500.

Does traffic school remove the insurance increase?

In many states, yes. Completing state-approved traffic school can mask the conviction from your insurer (California is the classic example, where the point stays on the DMV record but hidden from your carrier). It typically costs $25-100 in tuition plus a court fee. Availability, eligibility (usually once every 12-18 months), and the masking effect vary by state, so check both the court and your insurer's rules.

How long does a speeding ticket stay on your record?

The conviction typically stays on your driving record for 3 to 5 years, and insurers in most states can rate you on it the whole time. Some states are stricter: California keeps points for 3-7 years depending on the count, and a serious speeding charge (100+ mph) lasts a decade there. The insurance impact usually shrinks each year you stay clean.
