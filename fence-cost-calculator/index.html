How the Fence Cost Calculator Works

Fences are priced by the linear foot, installed, with gates billed per unit. That makes them one of the easiest projects to estimate well: measure the line, pick the material, and add the extras.

The formula

Total = length × per-foot rate for your material × height factor × terrain factor + walk gates + drive gates + optional opener + optional removal ($2.50–$5.00 per linear foot). Per-foot rates are national installed ranges at a 6 ft height on flat ground; the height and terrain factors scale them from published pricing.

How to use it

Walk your fence line with a measuring wheel or a long tape and add 5% for corners and gate gaps. If you don't know the length yet, use lot geometry: a square quarter-acre has about 417 LF of perimeter, a half-acre 590, a full acre 835, and the house usually closes one side (typical fenced backyards run 150 to 250 LF). Count gates honestly; each one needs two stout posts and more concrete than a run of panel.

A worked example

150 ft of 6 ft pressure-treated privacy fence, flat lot, one walk gate: 150 × $17–$36 per LF gives $2,550 to $5,400 of fence plus a $150–$450 gate, or about $4,025 typical with $2,700 and $5,850 as the realistic bounds.

Now a bigger job: 300 ft of 6 ft vinyl on a gently sloping lot, two walk gates, one double drive gate, and hauling off the old chain-link: 300 × $22–$48 × 1.08 for the slope, plus $300–$900 for walk gates, $450–$1,600 for the drive gate, and $750–$1,500 for removal. That lands near $12,868 typical, ranging $8,628 to $19,552. Slope and gates moved this bid by a third.

Numbers are national planning ranges. Lumber and steel prices swing, and quotes vary 20% or more between installers, so get three.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a 6 foot privacy fence cost per linear foot?

Pressure-treated wood runs $17 to $36 per linear foot installed, with $25 typical. Cedar runs $22 to $45, vinyl $22 to $48, and composite $25 to $55. A typical 150 ft wood privacy fence with one walk gate lands near $4,000.

How many linear feet of fence do I need for my yard?

A square quarter-acre lot has about 417 linear feet of perimeter, a half-acre about 590, and a full acre about 835. Real fences run shorter because the house usually closes one side: typical fenced backyards measure 150 to 250 linear feet. Walk the line with a tape or measuring wheel, adding a few feet for slopes and corners.

What is the cheapest fence to install?

Chain-link at $12 to $26 per linear foot installed for a 6 ft height, or split rail at $10 to $22. Chain-link is also the fastest to install: a crew can hang 100 to 200 ft a day. For privacy on a budget, pressure-treated pine pickets are the least expensive screen.

How much do fence gates cost?

A single walk-through gate runs $150 to $450 installed. A double drive gate for vehicles runs $450 to $1,600, and an automatic opener adds $1,800 to $3,500. Gates are priced per unit, not per linear foot, and they need heftier posts set in more concrete.

Does a fence add value to a home?

A well-built fence typically returns about half its cost at resale, and often more in family neighborhoods and dog-owning buyer pools. Real estate appraisers value a fence by its condition and how standard it is for the block; an unusual fence material can subtract value.

How long does a wood fence last?

Pressure-treated pine lasts 15 to 20 years with cleaning and sealing every 2 to 3 years; neglected, it shows rot in 10. Cedar naturally resists rot and lasts 20 to 30 years, vinyl 20 to 35, and composite 25 or more. Steel and aluminum outlast all of them.
