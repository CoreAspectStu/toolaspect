What do you need on the final exam to get the grade you want?

Direct answer: The score you need on your final = (target grade − current grade × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight. Example: your current grade is 78%, the final is worth 25%, and you want an 80% in the course. (80 − 78 × 0.75) ÷ 0.25 = an 86 on the final. If the answer comes out over 100%, your target is mathematically out of reach; if it comes out at 0% or below, you've already made it.

How the Final Grade Calculator Works

Your course grade has two parts: everything before the final, worth (100% − final weight), and the final itself. Course grade = current × (1 − w) + final × w. Flip that around to solve for the final score you need: needed = (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w.

How to use it

Open the gradebook, type in your current grade, and copy the final's weight from the syllabus (it's usually stated as "the final exam counts 20% of your grade"). Set the target to the letter cutoff you're chasing — 90 for an A, 80 for a B, 70 to pass in most classes. The three small result tiles show your course grade if you ace it (100), do average (70), or bomb it (0), which frames the range before you study a single hour.

A worked example

You're at 78% and the final is worth 25%. You want the B, so 80%. Current contribution: 78 × 0.75 = 58.5 points. The final must supply 80 − 58.5 = 21.5 of its 25 points, and 21.5 ÷ 0.25 = 86. An 86 on the final gets you exactly the 80.

Second example, the other side of the coin. You're at 85% with a 20% final and want the A (90%). Needed: (90 − 85 × 0.80) ÷ 0.20 = 110. Not happening. Even a perfect exam gives 85 × 0.80 + 100 × 0.20 = 88, a B+. The calculator flags this instead of pretending it's possible, so you aim at the grade that's actually on the table.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate what I need on my final exam?

Subtract your current grade times its share of the course from your target grade, then divide by the final's weight. If the final is 25% of the grade, your current grade is the other 75%: needed score = (target − current × 0.75) ÷ 0.25. Sitting at 78% and wanting an 80%? (80 − 58.5) ÷ 0.25 = 86. You need an 86 on the final.

What if the score I need is over 100%?

Then the target grade is mathematically out of reach. With an 85% current grade and a final worth 20%, even a perfect 100 leaves you at 88%, short of a 90. The calculator tells you the highest grade still possible so you can aim for the letter you can actually get.

What if the score I need is zero or less?

You've already locked in the target. If you're at 90% and need a 70 to pass with the final worth 20%, the required score works out to −10% — even a zero on the final leaves you at 72%. A strong final still matters for borderline cases, but the target itself is safe.

Does my current grade include everything before the final?

Yes — homework, quizzes, midterms, labs, participation, all of it. Your current grade is whatever the gradebook shows going into the final, and the final's weight is whatever's left. If your course weights categories instead (homework 20%, midterms 30%, final 50%), compute the weighted current grade first, then run the same formula.

What do I need on my final to pass the class?

Set the target to your class's passing line — usually 60% or 70%. At a 70% pass line with a 30% final, a student at 60% needs a 93.3, and a student at 65% needs an 81.7. At a 60% pass line, someone at 50% with a 30% final needs an 83.3. The gap closes fast as the final gets heavier.

How much will a zero on the final hurt my grade?

Multiply your current grade by one minus the final's weight. An 85% average with a 20% final drops to 68% (85 × 0.80), usually a D+. A 78% average with a 25% final drops to 58.5%, failing. The what-if mode runs this math for any exam score you enter.
