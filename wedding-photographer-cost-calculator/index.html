How the Wedding Photographer Cost Calculator Works

Photography packages are mostly an hours-times-rate business wearing a package label. This calculator prices your combination directly from hourly rates that vary by market, then layers the add-ons on top at their going rates.

The formula

Package = hours × hourly rate for your market, plus each add-on's flat price. Hourly rates: $200-$275 in small towns and rural areas ($235 typical), $275-$375 in mid-size metros ($325 typical), and $425-$600 in major metros ($500 typical). A second shooter adds $300-$800 ($450 typical), an engagement session $250-$600 ($400), and a wedding album $300-$800 ($500). The low and high estimates use the ends of each range.

How to use it

Set the hours to the arc you actually want covered — getting ready through the first two hours of dancing is the standard 8 — then pick your market honestly; pricing in a 45-minute-out suburb of a major metro follows the major metro. Tick the add-ons you're considering and watch which one moves the number most.

A worked example

A Chicago couple wants 10 hours, a second shooter, and an engagement session. Coverage: 10 × $500 = $5,000 typical for a major metro. Add-ons: +$450 and +$400. Typical total: $5,850, with the range running $4,800 (low rates on everything) to $7,400 (top of every band). That's realistic for Chicago — and 95% above the $3,000 national average, which mixes in thousands of small-town weddings.

Flip it: 6 hours, small town, no add-ons. 6 × $235 = $1,410 typical, range $1,200-$1,650. That's a real number for a talented rural photographer, and it's why the national "average" frustrates everyone on both sides of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a wedding photographer cost?

The national average is about $3,000, per The Knot's Real Weddings Study, with most couples spending $1,880-$4,000 for 8 hours depending on market. Small towns and rural areas land at the bottom of that range, mid-size metros in the middle, and major metros like NYC, LA, and San Francisco run $4,000-$6,000+ for the same coverage. WeddingWire's national range runs $1,150-$3,000.

How much does a second shooter cost?

Most studios add a second shooter for $300-$800, with $450 typical. Whether it's worth it depends on guest count and logistics: two shooters cover the couple getting ready in two places, capture guest reactions during the ceremony, and double the candid coverage during cocktail hour. Under 80 guests at one venue, a skilled solo shooter can handle it; 150+ guests or multiple locations makes the second shooter close to mandatory.

How many hours of wedding photography do I need?

8 hours covers the standard arc: getting ready through the first 1-2 hours of dancing. 6 hours works for smaller weddings that skip separate getting-ready coverage or end after dinner. 10-12 hours fits church ceremonies followed by a long gap before an evening reception, or full cultural and multi-event weddings. At roughly $235-$500 per hour depending on market, adding hours is the single biggest price lever.

Why are wedding photographers so expensive?

You're not paying for 8 hours. A full wedding adds 20-40 hours of editing, culling, and gallery work, plus gear, insurance, second-shooter payroll, editing software, and the taxes and retirement a W-2 job would cover. Divided across a typical 20-30 weddings a year, a $3,000 package is a working wage, not a windfall — which is also why the cheap $500 listing rarely shows up on time with finished galleries.

What add-ons are worth it for wedding photography?

An engagement session ($250-$600) is the highest-value add: you learn how your photographer works and they learn how you pose before the wedding day. A wedding album ($300-$800) is worth ordering eventually but rarely worth bundling — most photographers let you buy it after the wedding. Raw files, extra retouching, and printed proofs are usually skip-worthy.

How do I save money on a wedding photographer?

Cut coverage before you cut quality: 6 hours from a great photographer beats 10 from a mediocre one. Other proven levers: book a talented photographer in their first 2-3 years, marry Friday or Sunday (many offer 10-15% off), skip the engagement session if you already have photos you love, and book in the off-season when November-April dates discount.
