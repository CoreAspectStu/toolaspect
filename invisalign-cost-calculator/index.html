How the Invisalign Cost Calculator Works

The calculator prices three variables: what you're buying, where you're buying it, and who's helping pay. Pick the treatment tier your provider suggests, adjust for your market, then layer in your insurance plan's lifetime orthodontic maximum and any FSA or HSA money you can route through pre-tax.

Tier is the biggest lever

Providers quote Invisalign by product tier, and the tier tracks case complexity. Lite handles mild crowding with up to 14 aligners and starts near $2,800 to $3,000. Comprehensive covers full corrections with unlimited aligners, including refinement rounds when stubborn teeth need extra trays, and runs $4,500 to $7,000. You don't choose the tier; your bite does, which is why consultations and scans come first.

Insurance pays the same either way

Dental plans treat clear aligners like braces: if adult orthodontia is covered, the plan pays toward a lifetime maximum, commonly $1,500, at 50% coinsurance. Invisalign's own data says insured patients average $1,772 in coverage, and 92% of insured patients qualified for up to $3,000. Two gotchas: the lifetime max is shared with any childhood braces already claimed, and many plans exclude adult ortho entirely, so verify the line item before you count on it.

FSA and HSA money is a quiet discount

Orthodontia qualifies for both account types, so every pre-tax dollar you route through them is effectively discounted by your marginal tax rate, typically 25% to 35% combined federal and state. That's $500 to $700 of savings on $2,000 of spending. Watch the FSA use-it-or-lose-it rule across a treatment that spans two calendar years, and note HSA 2026 contribution limits of $4,400 self-only and $8,750 family.

A worked example

Take the default case: Comprehensive treatment, $4,500 to $7,000 at the national midpoint, in an average-cost market. Insurance contributes $1,500, bringing the balance to $3,000 to $5,500. Routing $2,000 through an FSA at a 30% marginal rate saves $600, for $2,400 to $4,900 out of pocket. Add one set of retainers at $300 and the total lands at $2,700 to $5,200, or about $113 to $217 a month spread over a 24-month in-house payment plan, which many offices offer interest-free.

Treat the output as a planning estimate, not a quote. Your provider's fee includes or excludes records, scans, and refinements differently than the next office's, and the scan is what pins down the real number. Get two consultations if the first quote surprises you; prices genuinely vary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Invisalign cost?

$3,000 to $8,000 is the typical full range in the US, with most moderate-to-comprehensive cases landing between $4,500 and $7,000 and simple Lite cases starting near $3,000. Invisalign's own published data puts the average insurance contribution at $1,772, so a representative patient pays roughly $2,400 to $5,200 out of pocket after typical insurance and FSA tax savings, plus retainers. Doctor fees vary by market: big-metro offices commonly run about 10% to 15% above the national midpoint, and smaller markets run below it.

Does dental insurance cover Invisalign?

Only if the plan includes adult orthodontia, which many dental plans exclude. When coverage exists, it usually pays up to a lifetime orthodontic maximum of $1,000 to $3,500, with $1,500 the most common figure, at 50% coinsurance. Invisalign reports that among insured patients, 92% qualified for up to $3,000 and coverage averaged $1,772. The lifetime max is shared with any childhood braces history, so check your remaining balance before counting on it.

Is Invisalign more expensive than braces?

Not meaningfully. Metal braces run $2,500 to $7,500 with a national average around $5,000, ceramic braces $3,500 to $8,000, and lingual braces $5,000 to $13,000, while comprehensive Invisalign runs $4,500 to $7,000. For comparable case complexity, clear aligners and braces price within the same band; the choice usually comes down to wear discipline, aesthetics, and which your orthodontist believes will finish your bite faster. Insurers pay the same orthodontic maximum either way.

Can I pay with FSA or HSA funds?

Yes. Orthodontia is a qualifying dental expense for both FSAs and HSAs, which effectively discounts the after-insurance balance by your marginal tax rate, around 25% to 35% for most people. FSA election caps have run $3,200 to $3,300 in recent years; HSA contribution limits for 2026 are $4,400 self-only and $8,750 family. One caution: spend FSA dollars in the plan year they're elected, and know your employer's grace period or carryover rule before routing a multi-year treatment through it.

How long does Invisalign take?

6 to 18 months covers most cases: Lite and Express-style cases finish in about 6 months, Moderate runs 6 to 12, and Comprehensive averages 12 to 18 with no aligner cap if refinements are needed. Aligners get worn 20 to 22 hours a day and swapped every 1 to 2 weeks, with check-ins every 6 to 8 weeks. Missed wear time is the main reason treatments stretch past estimates, and refinements, extra aligner rounds for stubborn teeth, are common enough to ask about up front.

What happened to SmileDirectClub and mail-order aligners?

SmileDirectClub filed for bankruptcy in late 2023 and shut down that December, leaving mid-treatment customers with aligners and no support. Mail-order aligner brands generally cost $1,900 to $2,500 and work without in-person supervision, which is precisely the trade: cheaper, but no hands-on adjustments, no X-ray-based screening for root or jaw problems, and no one to catch a failing case early. Invisalign and other dentist-directed aligners cost more for the supervision, which is also what protects complex bites.
