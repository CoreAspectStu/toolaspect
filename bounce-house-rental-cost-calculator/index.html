How the Bounce House Rental Calculator Works

Rental yards quote a day rate for the unit, then bolt on delivery, labor, and power. This calculator prices the unit class you picked at the day-rate bands above, applies the weekend multiplier, and stacks every add-on at its going rate — then answers the birthday-every-year question: when does buying beat renting?

The formula

Unit subtotal = day rate × rental-length multiplier (1 day ×1, weekend keep ×1.5 typical, two days ×2). Total = unit subtotal + delivery + (attendant hours × $35) + generator. Low and high estimates swap in the ends of every band. Buy-vs-rent break-even = $400 residential purchase price ÷ your typical total — the number of rentals it takes for buying to pay for itself.

How to use it

Pick the unit class first — it's 60-80% of the bill. A wet/dry combo in June costs $175 more than the dry unit it's based on; same party, same kids, hotter day. Then be honest about power: if the party's at a park, tick the generator. If it's in your backyard within extension-cord reach of an exterior outlet, don't. The attendant matters mostly for parks and big groups; in most states it's your call at a private backyard party.

A worked example, both ways

The backyard summer party: wet/dry combo ($350 typical), local delivery ($50), a 4-hour attendant ($140 at $35/hr), generator for the far corner of the yard ($100). Typical total: $640, with the range running $425-$955. Add the weekend keep (+50% on the unit, +$175) and you're at $815 — worth it only if Sunday's party two exists.

The lean birthday: standard dry 13x13 ($175), delivery included by the yard down the street, your own outlet, no attendant. Total: $175 ($100-$275 range). And the buy-vs-rent math on this one: a $400 residential unit pays for itself in 2.3 rentals at this rate — by the third birthday, the family that buys is renting free.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rent a bounce house?

A standard 13x13 dry bounce house rents for $100-$275 a day, with $175 typical across markets. A bounce house with a slide runs $200-$450, a wet/dry combo $250-$550, and obstacle courses start around $400. Most quotes cover a 4-8 hour day with setup and teardown included — the add-ons are what move the bill.

What fees do bounce house rentals add on top?

Three add-ons do most of the damage. Delivery runs $25-$150 depending on distance (some yards include local delivery). An attendant costs $25-$45 an hour — parks and some insurers require one. A generator rental adds $50-$150 when there's no power outlet within about 50 feet, which is most park setups. A wet/dry combo with local delivery, a 4-hour attendant, and a generator comes to $640 typical, range $425-$955.

How much is a waterslide or wet/dry combo rental?

Wet/dry combos rent for $250-$550 a day ($350 typical) — roughly $100-$200 more than the same unit dry, because water hookups, drainage, and extra cleaning are involved. Standalone waterslides around 20 feet run about $250, and big multi-lane slides climb from there. Houston-style markets price water combos $300-$550; drier markets quote the low end.

Is it cheaper to buy a bounce house than to rent one?

For a once-a-year birthday, no — a residential bounce house costs $250-$600 (about $400 typical), versus $175 to rent the basic unit. That's a 2.3-rental break-even, so buying pays off by the third birthday IF you use it that often and store it. Commercial-grade units run $1,500-$3,500 and only make sense if you're the one renting it out. Residential units wear out faster and carry smaller weight limits — check the label.

Can I set up a bounce house at a park?

Usually, but with strings attached: most parks require a permit, proof of insurance from the rental company, stakes instead of sandbags (or the opposite on pavement), and often a staffed attendant. Since parks rarely have power within blower-cord range, budget the generator add-on ($50-$150). Some rental companies won't deliver to parks at all — ask before booking the pavilion.

Why do bounce house prices vary so much between companies?

Local density. This is a hometown industry with thin margins and real delivery costs, so the same 13x13 unit runs $114 in Salt Lake City, $150 in Miami, and $200+ in Dallas. Multi-day and off-season discounts are common and negotiable — many yards let weekend rentals keep the unit until Monday for half the day rate again — so the quote you get on the phone often beats the website price.
