How the Pell Grant Calculator Works

Pell eligibility became an arithmetic problem after FAFSA simplification: your SAI maps to an award through a published schedule, with two income shortcuts layered on top. This calculator runs that schedule — the same logic as our FAFSA SAI tool's Pell module — with enrollment prorating and cost-of-attendance caps added.

The formula

Full-time base award: SAI at or below 0 pays the maximum ($7,395 in 2026-27); SAI from 1 up pays maximum minus SAI, as long as the result stays at or above the 10% minimum ($739.50); SAI above 6,655 pays nothing by formula. Multiply by enrollment intensity — 100% at 12+ credits, 75% at 9-11, 50% at 6-8, credits ÷ 12 below that — and cap at your school's cost of attendance.

The income shortcuts

Parent AGI at or below 175% of the poverty line (225% for single parents) forces maximum Pell even when the formula disagrees. AGI up to 275% (325% single) can force the minimum award when the formula says zero. These exist because SAI can't go low enough for some family shapes — and because Congress wanted a bright line families could plan around.

A worked example

A student with SAI 1,500, enrolled full-time: award = $7,395 − $1,500 = $5,895 for the year, about $2,947.50 per semester over two terms. Drop to half-time and it prorates to $2,948. Now a trickier case: family of four, two parents, AGI $50,000, and the formula produced SAI 7,800 — above the 6,655 line, so formula Pell is $0. But $50,000 is under the two-parent max-Pell cutoff of $54,600, so the shortcut locks in the full $7,395 anyway. This is why checking the income tests matters even when your SAI looks disqualifying.

Lifetime math: each year at a full $7,395 burns 100% of your 600% lifetime allowance, so six maximum years exhaust Pell entirely. Half-time years burn half as much, which is how part-time students stretch eligibility across more calendar time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much Pell Grant will I get?

For 2026-27, your full-time Pell Grant is the $7,395 maximum if your SAI is 0 or below; otherwise it's $7,395 minus your SAI, down to a $740 minimum. So SAI 1,500 gets $5,895, SAI 3,000 gets $4,395, SAI 5,000 gets $2,395, and SAI above 6,655 gets $0 through the formula. Part-time awards prorate by enrollment intensity: half-time is 50% of the full-time award.

What is the maximum Pell Grant for 2026-27?

$7,395 for a full-time student, unchanged since 2023-24 (the max was $6,345 in 2020-21, $6,495 in 2021-22, $6,895 in 2022-23). With year-round Pell, a student can receive up to 150% of the award in one year — $11,092.50 for a maximum-eligibility student who attends summer. Over a lifetime, Pell is capped at 600%, the equivalent of six full-time years.

What income qualifies for the maximum Pell Grant?

Two paths. The income shortcut: parent AGI at or below 175% of the poverty line (225% for single parents) automatically qualifies for maximum Pell — for a family of four that's $54,600 (single parent: $70,200) for 2026-27, using 2024 poverty guidelines. Or the formula path: any financial picture that produces SAI 0 or below, which happens at higher incomes than people expect once allowances are applied.

Can I get a Pell Grant with a high SAI?

Only through the minimum-Pell income shortcut: if parent AGI is at or below 275% of the poverty line (325% for single parents) — $85,800 for a two-parent family of four in 2026-27 — the student qualifies for a minimum grant of about $740 even when the formula's SAI is above the Pell range. Otherwise SAI above 6,655 means no Pell, though institutional and state aid may still use the SAI directly.

Does the Pell Grant amount change part-time?

Yes — awards prorate by enrollment intensity. 12+ credits pays 100%, 9-11 credits 75%, 6-8 credits 50%, and under 6 credits pays credits divided by 12 (3 credits = 25%). A student with SAI 1,500 gets $5,895 full-time but $2,948 at half-time. Year-round Pell also lets full-timers collect an extra award in summer, up to 150% of the annual max.

Is the Pell Grant free money I don't pay back?

Yes — it's a federal grant, not a loan, and doesn't require repayment under normal circumstances. The exceptions all involve withdrawing: if you drop below half-time or leave school mid-term, a Return of Title IV Funds calculation can convert part of your Pell into money you owe. Stay enrolled at least through 60% of the term and the grant is entirely yours.
