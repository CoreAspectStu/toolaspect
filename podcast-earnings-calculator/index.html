About the Podcast Earnings Calculator

This calculator estimates podcast advertising revenue from four inputs: monthly downloads, your CPM rate, the number of ad slots per episode, and how many episodes you publish per month. It applies the standard industry formula — downloads divided by 1,000, times CPM, times ad slots, times episodes — and adds any sponsorship or listener-support income you enter to give a monthly total.

CPM stands for 'cost per mille' — the price an advertiser pays per one thousand downloads of an episode. It is the unit the podcast ad industry inherited from radio and web advertising, and it is how nearly all podcast ad pricing is quoted, whether sold directly by the podcaster or through a network or programmatic marketplace.

What podcast CPMs actually look like

Published industry surveys have consistently placed typical podcast CPMs in the roughly $15–$50 range, with host-read ads above programmatic and dynamically inserted ads. Niche matters a great deal: business, finance, and technology shows command the top of the range, because their audiences are valuable to B2B and financial advertisers, while broad entertainment and true crime shows typically sit lower.

Ad placement matters too. Industry rate cards conventionally quote three positions per episode — pre-roll (before the content, usually 15–30 seconds), mid-roll (inside the episode, usually 60 seconds, the premium slot), and post-roll (after the content, usually discounted since many listeners drop off). The table below summarizes the conventional ranges from published industry rate surveys; your actual rates depend on audience, niche, and how you sell.

The other revenue streams

Advertising is rarely a podcast's only income. Listener support — Patreon and similar memberships — is the most common supplement, and for shows with small but loyal audiences it often out-earns ads: a podcast with 2,000 monthly downloads may struggle to sell ads at all, but 50 patrons at $5 a month is $250 of dependable income with no sales work.

Other streams include direct sponsorships (a flat fee for a set of mentions, negotiated outside CPM pricing), premium subscriptions and bonus feeds, live shows and events, merchandise, books and courses, and services. Diversification matters because ad income scales linearly with downloads — a show needs meaningful scale (commonly cited thresholds run from a few thousand to 10,000+ downloads per episode) before advertisers or networks take an interest.

Reading your estimate honestly

The calculator's answer is only as good as its inputs. 'Downloads' in the CPM formula conventionally means downloads within the first 30 days of an episode — the number advertisers care about — not your all-time back-catalog totals. IAB v2.x certified download statistics are what most ad buyers require, so if your hosting stats are not IAB-certified, advertisers may discount them.

Treat the result as a planning figure, not a promise. If the estimate says ads could pay your production costs, the next step is a small direct-sales test — a handful of episodes sold to a relevant sponsor — which tells you what your specific audience will actually command, something no industry average can. And remember seasonality: ad spending dips predictably in Q1 and rises in Q4, so annualize from several months, not one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is podcast ad revenue calculated?

Monthly ad revenue = (monthly downloads ÷ 1,000) × CPM × ad slots per episode × episodes per month. For example, 10,000 downloads at a $25 CPM with 2 ad slots in 4 episodes = $2,000/month. That is exactly the formula this calculator applies.

What is a good CPM rate for my podcast?

Published industry surveys put typical host-read podcast CPMs in the $18–$50 range, with mid-rolls at the top, pre-rolls somewhat lower, and dynamically inserted programmatic ads lower still. Business, finance, and technology niches sit at the top of the range; broad entertainment typically lower.

How many downloads do I need before advertisers are interested?

There is no hard threshold, but common industry guidance is that direct ad sales become practical somewhere around a few thousand to 10,000 downloads per episode. Smaller shows often earn more per hour of effort from listener support or niche sponsorships than from CPM advertising.

Do downloads mean the same thing as listeners?

No. A download is one episode file fetch, and one listener can generate multiple downloads across devices, while other downloads are never played. Advertisers buy against downloads (typically within 30 days of publish, IAB-certified), so treat downloads as the currency of ad sales, not as a headcount of humans.

What are pre-roll, mid-roll, and post-roll ads?

They are ad positions within an episode: pre-roll runs before the content (typically 15–30 seconds), mid-roll runs inside it (typically 60 seconds and the premium slot), and post-roll runs after it (usually cheapest, since some listeners drop off before the end).
