How the Boarding Calculator Works

The nightly rate is the headline, but the bill is the rate times nights, plus a per-night stack of add-ons, plus a surcharge on whatever nights fall inside a holiday window. This calculator prices the whole thing.

The formula

Base = nights × nightly rate. Add-ons = nights × (walks + playtime + medication, if switched on). Holiday surcharge = holiday nights × rate × surcharge percent. Total = base + add-ons + surcharge, and all-in per night = total ÷ nights.

How to use it

Enter your nights and pick a tier — the rate field auto-fills with that tier's typical mid-range price, which you can override with a real quote. Tick only the add-ons your dog actually needs; the walk and playtime lines are the ones facilities most often bundle into packages that quietly double a kennel bill. If part of the stay overlaps a holiday week, set those nights and the surcharge your facility quoted (10-25% is the industry norm).

A worked example

A 7-night stay at a standard facility charging $55 a night, with daily playtime sessions at $12, daily medication at $7, and 2 nights falling in the Christmas window at a 15% surcharge.

Base: 7 × $55 = $385. Add-ons: 7 × ($12 + $7) = $133. Surcharge: 2 × $55 × 15% = $16.50. Total: $534.50 — $76.36 a night all-in, nearly 40% above the headline rate. The same stay at a $35 kennel with no add-ons would be $245; at a $110 luxury hotel with the same add-ons, $665 plus surcharge. That $400-plus spread is the real decision, and it's why the comparison tiles update live.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does dog boarding cost per night?

National ranges: a basic kennel runs $25-$50 a night, a standard boarding facility $40-$75, a sitter's-home stay through an app like Rover averages about $49, an in-home overnight sitter $45-$85, and a luxury pet hotel $85-$185+. Add-ons are billed per night on top: walks $5-$10, playtime sessions $10-$15, and medication administration $5-$10.

Is a kennel or a pet sitter cheaper?

At the low end, kennels: a $35 kennel night undercuts almost every sitter. But sitter-home boarding through Rover averages about $49 a night nationally, which is often competitive with a mid-range facility once add-ons are counted, and the dog lives in a home with a person rather than a run. Professional in-home overnight sitters ($45-$85) cost the most but come to you. Price the full stay, not the headline rate.

Why does boarding cost more at Christmas and Thanksgiving?

Peak holiday weeks are the scarce inventory of the boarding world, and facilities charge for it: expect a surcharge of roughly 10-25% on holiday nights, sometimes a flat per-night fee instead. Book 2-3 months ahead for Thanksgiving and Christmas — good facilities fill up, and last-minute holiday slots skew to the expensive end of every tier.

Is medication administration extra?

Usually, if it's priced at all. Basic oral meds commonly add $5-$10 a night; some facilities tier it ($3.50 per administration for 1-2 medications, $5.50 for 3-4), others charge per pill, and some include oral medication free while charging $10-$30 for injections. Declaring the medication matters more than the fee — undisclosed meds are a common cause of a stay going wrong.

What vaccinations do boarders require?

Nearly all facilities require current rabies, DHPP, and bordetella (kennel cough); many also want canine influenza, and doggy-daycare-style group play often requires them all plus a temperament test. Costs for overdue boosters land on your bill before the stay does, so check the facility's requirements a month out.

How can I cut boarding costs?

Book the off-season and avoid holiday surcharge windows when you can. Skip bundled add-on packages and pick only what your dog needs — a walk and meds instead of the full activity menu. Compare a 10-night stay across tiers: at $35 a night a kennel run is $350, while the same stay at a $110 pet hotel is $1,100, a $750 difference that buys a lot of dog walker visits.
