How the Dog Bite Settlement Calculator Works

Dog bite settlements start with your economic losses, then multiply. Add up medical bills, expected future care such as scar revision, and lost wages; that total is your specials. Insurers and lawyers then apply a multiplier of 1.5x to 5x for pain and suffering, and the multiplier tracks severity. Punctures that heal clean sit near 1.5x. Reconstructive surgery and nerve damage push toward 5x.

Who actually pays

Almost never the owner personally. Dog bite claims are usually paid by the owner's homeowners or renters insurance, and typical liability limits run $100,000 to $300,000. In 2023, insurers paid $1.12 billion on 19,062 dog-related injury claims, an average of $58,545 per claim, per Insurance Information Institute and State Farm data. That policy is the pot you're negotiating over, which is why a lawyer's first move is confirming coverage exists.

Why scarring and child victims raise the number

The CDC and AVMA estimate about 4.5 million dog bites a year in the US, and roughly 800,000 need medical attention. Children are bitten more often than adults, frequently on the face. Facial scarring is visible, permanent, and expensive to revise, so it pulls both the specials (future surgery) and the multiplier (disfigurement) upward at the same time. That's why the severity setting moves this estimate more than any other input.

Strict liability vs the one-bite rule

In strict-liability states such as California, Florida, Michigan, New Jersey, Arizona, Illinois, and Ohio, the owner is generally on the hook even if the dog had never shown aggression. One-bite states like Texas and Virginia require proof the owner knew the dog was dangerous. New York and Pennsylvania split the difference: medical costs get covered more easily, while full damages take more proof. Treat the table above as orientation, not law.

A worked example

Say a bite leaves $15,000 in medical bills, $5,000 in expected scar-revision costs, and $2,000 in missed work. Specials come to $22,000. At the serious tier (3x to 4x), pain and suffering adds $66,000 to $88,000, for a gross range of $88,000 to $110,000. With no provocation argument, nothing comes off for fault. A 33% pre-suit contingency fee leaves an estimated net of $58,960 to $73,700.

Treat that as a rough educational estimate, not a valuation of any real case. The math runs on national averages and commonly published multiplier ranges, and state law changes everything from liability to filing deadlines. A licensed attorney who can see your medical records and your state's rules will give you a real number.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average dog bite settlement?

$58,545 was the average cost per dog-related injury claim in 2023, per the Insurance Information Institute and State Farm, up from $49,025 in 2022. That's the insurer's average payout across 19,062 homeowners-liability claims, not a court award. Your number depends on medical bills, scarring, and lost wages, so cases range from a few thousand to well past six figures.

Who pays a dog bite settlement?

$100,000 to $300,000 is the typical liability limit on the homeowners or renters policy that pays most dog bite claims. The owner rarely writes a personal check unless damages exceed the policy or they carry no insurance at all. That's why the first question in any dog bite case is whether a policy exists and how much coverage it holds.

What if the dog never bit anyone before?

7 states in the abbreviated table above, including California, Florida, and New Jersey, apply strict liability: the owner pays even for a first bite. In one-bite states like Texas and Virginia, you generally must show the owner already knew the dog was dangerous, through earlier snaps, complaints, or animal-control reports. State law controls this, so check your state's rule before assuming anything.

Does scarring increase a dog bite payout?

3x to 5x is where multipliers land when a bite leaves visible scarring or needs reconstructive surgery, compared with 1.5x to 2x for punctures that heal clean. Scarring also raises the economic side of the claim, because future revision surgery gets priced in before you settle. Facial scars on children push settlements hardest, and kids are bitten on the face more often than adults.

What if I provoked the dog?

25% provocation on a $100,000 gross case cuts it to $75,000 under comparative-fault rules, since your share of blame comes off the top. Insurers raise provocation often, especially when the injured person was teasing, startling, or stepping between fighting dogs. The calculator's fault field models exactly this reduction, and a lawyer can tell you how your state treats it.
