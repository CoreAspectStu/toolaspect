How the Driveway Cost Calculator Works

Paving quotes come down to area times a per-square-foot rate, but the honest comparison has to include what happens after the trucks leave. Asphalt needs sealcoating on a schedule and gets replaced sooner. Concrete costs more on day one and then mostly sits there. This calculator prices both over a horizon you choose.

The formulas

Area = length × width. Installed cost = area × rate per square foot. Sealing count over the horizon = floor((horizon − 1) ÷ seal interval), with the first seal landing after year one and none in the final year of a life. Asphalt cost per year = (install + seals + replacement cost prorated over the horizon) ÷ horizon. Concrete cost per year = install ÷ horizon, since routine maintenance is effectively zero.

How to use it

Enter your dimensions, or type the area directly; the fields stay in sync. The default rates ($10 asphalt, $12 concrete) sit at the national midpoint of the published ranges. If you have a real quote per square foot, use that instead, because regional labor moves these numbers more than material does. Set the horizon to how long you expect to own the home.

A worked example

Take a 50 × 12 ft driveway, 600 square feet, the standard single-car-plus turnaround size. At the $10 midpoint, asphalt comes to $6,000, with a realistic range of $4,200 to $7,800. Concrete at $12 runs $7,200, ranging $4,800 to $10,800 for plain broom finish.

Now the part most quotes skip. Sealing every 4 years at $0.35 per square foot costs $210 a treatment, and 20 years holds four of them: $840. Asphalt totals $6,840 over 20 years, or $342 a year. Concrete totals $7,200 over the same span, $360 a year, but it keeps going: stretched to 30 years the concrete drive costs $240 a year while the asphalt drive, on its second pavement plus seals, runs $12,840 total, or $428 a year. Concrete wins the long game unless you're selling inside a decade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to pave a 600 sq ft driveway?

Asphalt runs $7 to $13 per square foot installed, so a 600 sq ft driveway costs $4,200 to $7,800 with a national midpoint near $6,000. Concrete at $8 to $18 per square foot runs $4,800 to $10,800 for the same footprint. Tear-out of an existing driveway, poor soil, or long haul distances push you toward the high ends.

Is asphalt or concrete cheaper for a driveway?

Asphalt is cheaper upfront, typically by $1 to $5 per square foot. But concrete lasts 25 to 30 years against asphalt's 15 to 20, and it needs no recurring sealcoating. Over 30 years, a concrete drive often wins: 600 sq ft of asphalt with resealing and one replacement totals about $12,840, or $428 a year, against roughly $240 a year for concrete.

How often should an asphalt driveway be sealed, and what does it cost?

Every 3 to 5 years, after the first seal 6 to 12 months after paving. Contractor sealcoating typically runs $0.20 to $0.50 per square foot, so a 600 sq ft driveway costs about $120 to $300 per treatment. Crack filling adds more.

How long does an asphalt driveway last vs concrete?

A well-built asphalt driveway lasts 15 to 20 years, up to 25 in mild climates with regular sealing. Concrete lasts 25 to 30 years and can reach 40 or more in moderate climates. Both numbers assume a properly compacted base: thin pavement over soft ground fails in a fraction of that.

What is the cheapest driveway surface?

Gravel, at roughly $1.50 to $3 per square foot installed. A 600 sq ft gravel drive costs $900 to $1,800, about a quarter of asphalt. The trade-offs are ongoing replenishment, ruts, snow plowing headaches, and no wheeling heavy loads without spreading stone. Chip seal sits between gravel and asphalt.
